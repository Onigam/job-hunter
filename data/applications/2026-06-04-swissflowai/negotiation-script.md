# Negotiation Script — SwissFlow Technical Co-Founder/CTO

## Context

SwissFlow is a very early-stage startup (founded 2026, 5-firm pilot phase, two non-technical founders). This is a co-founding conversation, not a hire negotiation. The primary lever is equity. Cash will be below market until a seed round closes, which is normal and expected at this stage.

The goal: negotiate a meaningful equity stake with standard protective terms, get clarity on the funding timeline, and establish the salary step-up schedule tied to funding milestones.

---

## Switzerland Market Anchors

Use these to anchor the opportunity cost argument if they push back on equity.

| Role | Typical CHF (2026) |
|------|-------------------|
| Staff/Principal Engineer | CHF 150,000-200,000/year |
| VP Engineering | CHF 180,000-240,000/year |
| CTO at funded startup (Series A+) | CHF 200,000-280,000+/year |
| Technical Co-Founder (pre-funding) | Below market base + substantial equity |

Geneva cost-of-living premium: ~15-20% above Zurich. Geneva is the second most expensive city in Switzerland. Use this to resist any geographic discount argument.

---

## Equity Framework

### Target range by stage

SwissFlow has a working product and 5 pilot clients. It's not pre-idea, but it's still pre-revenue at scale.

- **Joining at idea stage:** 25-40% is standard
- **Joining with MVP and first customers:** 15-25% is the right range
- **SwissFlow current stage:** target 20-25%, accept 15% with strong terms

### Non-negotiable terms

1. **4-year vesting schedule** — standard for co-founders; anything shorter benefits only the company
2. **1-year cliff** — protects both sides; don't accept no-cliff
3. **Acceleration clause on acquisition** — full vesting (or double-trigger) if the company is acquired; without this, acquirer inherits unvested shares for free
4. **Anti-dilution protection (broad-based weighted average)** — this is standard in Swiss startup docs; full ratchet is unusual and aggressive
5. **Tag-along and co-sale rights** — if founders sell, you have the right to sell too under the same terms

### What to avoid

- Vesting that starts before your actual start date
- Equity in the form of options priced at a high 409A/strike price (ask for low-priced options or actual shares)
- No shareholders' agreement — insist on one before signing

---

## Opening Anchor (First Conversation)

When they bring up compensation, lead with framing before numbers:

> "Before we talk numbers, I want to name what I'm actually bringing to the table. This isn't a hire. You'd be getting someone who's done this before: built a SaaS platform from zero, scaled it to 50+ enterprise clients, and navigated an acquisition with the technical decisions intact. That kind of track record is the thing that changes the risk profile of an early-stage company.
>
> Given the stage you're at, I'd expect equity in the 20-25% range with a 4-year vest and 1-year cliff, plus an acceleration clause tied to acquisition. On cash, I'm realistic: I know you're pre-funding. I'd want to agree on a salary step-up schedule tied to milestones, probably moving to CHF 80-100k at seed close and CHF 130-160k at Series A."

---

## If They Come in Low on Equity (below 12-15%)

> "I want to be direct: below 15% at this stage, the math doesn't work for me. What you're describing is closer to an early employee package than a co-founder one. I'd be taking on co-founder-level risk, building the entire technical foundation, and forgoing 2-3 years of market-rate compensation in Switzerland. The equity needs to reflect that.
>
> If the cap table is a constraint because of how it's currently structured, let's talk about that specifically. There may be ways to make the structure work. But I'd need to understand what's in the way before I can evaluate whether a lower number makes sense."

---

## Salary Step-Up Schedule (propose this proactively)

Get this in writing before signing anything.

| Milestone | Base Salary |
|-----------|------------|
| Pre-funding (now) | CHF 40,000-60,000/year (founder sacrifice) |
| Seed close (CHF 500k-1M) | CHF 80,000-100,000/year |
| Series A | CHF 140,000-180,000/year |
| Product-market fit / profitability | Market rate (CHF 180,000-220,000+) |

Frame it as: "I'm not asking to be paid like a CTO on day one. I'm asking that we agree now on what 'fair' looks like at each stage, so there's no ambiguity later."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

Even if you don't have a competing offer, you can reference the opportunity cost:

> "For context, I have conversations ongoing with companies at Series A and B stage where the base is CHF 160-180k and the equity is still meaningful. I'm not using that as a threat. I'm using it to show you what I'd be walking away from. The reason I'm talking to you first is that co-founding a company is a different kind of bet, and SwissFlow's approach to the Swiss market is specific enough to be interesting. But the delta has to close somewhere."

---

## Equity Red Flags — Walk Away Conditions

- Equity below 10% for a technical co-founder joining at this stage
- No written shareholders' agreement before start
- No acquisition acceleration clause
- Vesting without a cliff
- Founders have already taken a very large equity share (e.g., 80%+ between two founders) leaving only scraps
- No clear plan to raise funding (if they plan to stay bootstrapped, equity value trajectory is very different)

---

## Swiss-Specific Notes

- **Swiss GmbH vs. AG:** If the company is a GmbH, equity participation is structured differently (Stammanteile, not shares). An AG is easier for VC-backed equity. Ask which legal form they're in and when they plan to convert.
- **Employer social charges:** In Switzerland, social charges on salary can add 12-15% to total cost. Factor this into any salary discussion, especially pre-funding when every franc matters.
- **ESOP pool:** If they're planning to raise, ask whether an ESOP pool is part of the plan and whether it comes from the founders' dilution or all shareholders equally.
