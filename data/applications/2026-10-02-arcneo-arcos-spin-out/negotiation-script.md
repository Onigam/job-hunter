# Negotiation Script: arcneo / arcOS, CTO / Entrepreneur in Residence

_Posting: Berlin hybrid (regular presence), full-time, start immediately. EIR sprint with Roman Grochol (CEO-designate) until December, then a go/no-go on spinning arcOS out. If it's a go, the role becomes technical cofounder with "significant founder equity". The posting gives no salary figure. You'd be based in Geneva._

This isn't a normal salary negotiation. There are two phases with different stakes. The EIR period is mostly about cash, expenses and protecting your time. The spin-out is mostly about equity, vesting and governance. Keep them separate in every conversation.

## 1. Market context

**Geneva / Switzerland (your reference point).** For a CTO or VP Engineering at a funded Swiss scale-up, base pay usually sits around CHF 220k to 300k. Staff and Principal engineers at Geneva and Zurich tech companies are typically around CHF 180k to 240k. Founder CTOs at pre-seed or seed Swiss startups usually take much less, often CHF 100k to 160k, and make up the difference in equity.

**Berlin / Germany (their reference point).** Funded Berlin startups usually pay a CTO around €120k to 170k after a seed or Series A. Founders before a round often take €60k to 100k or defer pay entirely. EIR programmes in Germany usually pay a monthly stipend, often around €6k to 10k a month for an experienced candidate, though some pay nothing and offer equity only.

**What this means for you.** A founder salary in Berlin won't match Geneva living costs. That's normal for a cofounder role, so don't fight it on base alone. The equity has to carry the difference, and the EIR phase has to be paid properly, because you're giving up interview time elsewhere for at least three months.

## 2. Phase 1: the EIR sprint (now until December)

Ask before you commit anything:

> "Before I block out the next three months, how is the EIR phase set up? Is it paid, on what kind of contract, and who covers travel between Geneva and Berlin?"

Opening anchor if they ask what you need:

> "For the sprint I'd be looking at around €10k a month as a freelance contract, plus travel and accommodation for the Berlin weeks. I'm in Geneva, so an equity-only arrangement doesn't work for me, and the sprint is real work, a system handover and customer demos, not an extended interview."

Fallback if they come in low (e.g. €5–6k a month or unpaid):

> "I understand cash is tight before the decision. If the monthly figure has to be lower, I'd want the sprint months to count toward founder vesting if we go ahead, and travel covered on top. That way the risk I'm taking now shows up in the cap table later."

Also settle:
- Contract form: a freelance/consulting agreement is cleanest for a Swiss resident. A German employment contract would raise payroll and social security questions for only three months.
- Travel: flights Geneva–Berlin and accommodation reimbursed, with an agreed number of on-site days per month.
- IP: anything you build during the sprint belongs to arcneo or the future spin-out. Fine, but make sure it's written down and works both ways, so you don't end up owning nothing and also blocked from similar work.
- A clear decision date and what happens to you if it's a "no" (a notice period or a short paid tail).

## 3. Phase 2: technical cofounder (if the spin-out goes ahead)

### The cap table comes first

Before any number, ask how the new company would be split:

> "Walk me through the planned cap table at founding. What does arcneo keep for the IP and the customer relationship, what goes to Roman, is Despot staying in with equity, and how big is the ESOP pool before the round?"

Corporate spin-outs often leave the parent with a large stake, sometimes 30% to 50% or more, which shrinks what's left for the founders. If arcneo holds a majority, the founders are close to employees with large grants, and you should negotiate that way.

### Opening anchor on equity

For a technical cofounder joining alongside a CEO, with a working product already in place, a reasonable target is roughly 15% to 25% of founder shares at incorporation (fully diluted, before the round), depending on how much arcneo keeps.

> "Given that I'd own the whole product and technology side, take over a 30-agent system, build the team, and carry the technical side of the round, I'd expect to be a real cofounder on the cap table, which for me means something in the range of 20% at incorporation, alongside Roman. I'm flexible on salary to make that work."

### If the equity offer is low (e.g. 5–8%)

> "Thanks, I see why the existing work and arcneo's backing count for a lot. At that level, though, it's closer to a senior hire with a grant than a cofounder, and I'd need to be paid like a senior hire. So either the equity moves toward the cofounder range, or the base gets closer to market, which in Geneva is above CHF 200k for this scope. I'd rather have the equity."

### Equity terms to push on

- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for the EIR months to count toward the cliff, or for a shorter cliff (6 months) given the sprint.
- **Acceleration:** double trigger, so if the company is sold and your role ends, unvested shares vest.
- **Leaver clauses:** German GmbH shareholder agreements often have harsh good/bad leaver terms. As a good leaver, vested shares should stay yours at fair market value, not at nominal value.
- **Real shares, not VSOP:** as a cofounder you should hold real GmbH shares, not virtual ones. A VSOP for a cofounder is a red flag.
- **Anti-dilution:** founders rarely get anti-dilution protection, so don't spend your bargaining power on it. Make sure the ESOP pool is created *before* the founder split is fixed, not carved out of you afterwards.
- **Governance:** a seat on the board or advisory board, and veto rights on technical hires and the tech budget. Find out which decisions arcneo can still block as a shareholder.
- **Customer contract with arcneo:** arcneo is the main customer. Ask for a written commercial agreement (pricing, minimum term) between arcneo and the spin-out, so revenue doesn't depend on goodwill.

### Base salary as cofounder

Anchor around €130k, and accept down to around €100k before the round if the equity is right, with a written step-up after the financing closes.

> "Before the round I can work with around €100k, provided the base goes to market level, roughly €140k, once we close the financing."

## 4. Geographic pushback

They will likely argue for Berlin pay and Berlin presence.

> "I'm not asking for a Geneva salary from a Berlin startup. I'm asking that the equity reflects the fact that I'm taking a large pay cut compared with my market, and that travel isn't coming out of my own pocket. If the company needs me in Berlin more than two weeks a month long-term, we should talk about relocation support."

On the cross-border setup, raise it early and get a Swiss tax adviser involved before signing anything:
- If you work mostly from Geneva, Swiss social security usually applies, and a German company employing you would need Swiss payroll or an EOR. Check this with an adviser; don't assume.
- Shares received through employment can be taxed as income in Switzerland at grant or vesting, depending on how they're structured. Founder shares bought at nominal value at incorporation usually avoid this, so push to get your shares at founding, not as a grant later.
- Switzerland charges wealth tax on shareholdings, including unlisted ones, so a high valuation after the round can create a yearly tax bill with no cash behind it. Ask the adviser how Geneva values startup shares.

## 5. Competing offer template (use only if true)

> "To be open with you, I'm in late stages with another company for a Staff/CTO role in Switzerland, at around [X] base. arcOS is the one I want, because I'd own the product from day one, but I need the deal to make sense against that. Is there room on the sprint terms or the founder split?"

Never invent a competing offer. If nothing is live, lean on the market data and the size of the pay cut you're already accepting.

## 6. Walk-away signals

- arcneo keeps a controlling stake and the founder pool is small
- The founder equity is a VSOP instead of real shares
- No written arcneo–spin-out customer agreement
- An unpaid EIR period with no credit toward vesting
- No clear answer on what Despot's role is after the handover
