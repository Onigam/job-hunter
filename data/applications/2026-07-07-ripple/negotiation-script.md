# Negotiation Script: Ripple, Staff Software Engineer Infrastructure (Custody)

## Market anchors (Switzerland, Staff-level infra/platform, 2026)

- Zurich Staff Software Engineer base averages around CHF 282,500/yr (typical range CHF 260,000-307,750). Geneva historically runs 10-20% below Zurich for comparable roles, which puts a generalist Staff base somewhere around CHF 230,000-260,000.
- Fintech and crypto-adjacent infra roles tend to pay a 10-20% premium over generalist SaaS roles for the same level, because of the on-call risk profile and the smaller talent pool that's comfortable with custody-grade reliability requirements.
- Ripple's own US Staff Software Engineer comp (levels.fyi, self-reported) has a base around $241,000, a stock grant around $69,000/yr, and bonus around $27,000, landing total comp near $337,000. That's a US number and Switzerland won't match it dollar for dollar, but it's a useful ceiling reference: Ripple pays Staff engineers well above generic market rate when the company wants them.
- Blockchain-specific developer averages in Geneva (CHF 93,000-165,000) are not the right comparable here. Those numbers track mid-level blockchain app developers, not Staff infrastructure engineers running custody-grade multi-cloud systems. Don't let a recruiter anchor to that band.
- Working anchor range for this role: CHF 230,000-270,000 base, plus bonus, plus equity or RSU-equivalent. Open around the top of that band.

## Opening anchor

Lead with a number, not a range, if pushed to go first. If they ask early, redirect once, then answer with an anchored range:

"I'd rather understand the full band for this level before throwing a number out, what's budgeted for a Staff Infrastructure hire in Geneva?"

If they push back and want a number anyway:

"Based on Staff-level infrastructure comp in Zurich and Geneva, plus the custody domain premium, I'm looking at CHF 250,000 to 270,000 base, with bonus and equity on top. That's where I'd want to start the conversation."

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

If the initial offer lands meaningfully under CHF 230,000 base:

"Thanks for sending this over, I've had a look and I want to be straightforward: the base is below where I expected to land for a Staff Infrastructure role carrying custody reliability. Zurich benchmarks for this level sit in the CHF 260,000 to 300,000 range, and Geneva typically runs maybe 10 to 15% under that, not 25 or 30%. Can we look at closing that gap, either on base or by rebalancing toward bonus and equity if base is fixed on your end?"

If they cite internal banding as a hard constraint:

"I get that banding exists for a reason. If the base is capped, I'd want to see that reflected somewhere else, sign-on, an accelerated equity vest, or a compensation review built into the offer at 6 or 12 months rather than the standard annual cycle."

## Equity notes

Ripple is a private, well-capitalized company, not an early-stage startup, so equity here behaves more like RSUs or private stock units than founder-stage options. Key things to clarify before agreeing to any number:

- Whether the grant is RSUs, options, or phantom equity, and what the strike price and 409A-equivalent valuation basis is if it's options.
- Vesting schedule and cliff. Four-year vest with a one-year cliff is standard; push back if it's longer.
- Liquidity path. Ripple has floated IPO plans publicly for years without executing. Ask directly what the realistic liquidity horizon is and whether there's any secondary sale program for employees, since illiquid equity at a company with no confirmed IPO timeline should be valued conservatively, not at face value.
- Given Ripple's business, ask explicitly whether any part of compensation is denominated in or convertible to XRP or another token, and if so, how that's valued and taxed in Switzerland. Don't assume it's a standard RSU package until confirmed.

## Geographic discount pushback

If the recruiter frames Geneva as a lower cost-of-living market to justify a lower number:

"Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, not a cost-of-living discount market. Rent, health insurance, and taxes here are comparable to or higher than Zurich in most categories. I'd push back on any framing that treats Geneva as a discount to a US or even a Zurich number, the cost base doesn't support that."

## Competing offer leverage template

Use only if there's an actual competing process in flight; don't bluff a number that can't survive a follow-up question.

"I want to be transparent that I'm further along in another process at a comparable stage, with a base in the low CHF 260,000s. I'd rather move forward with Ripple if the offer is close, the custody infrastructure problem is more interesting to me, but I do need the numbers to be in the same neighborhood to make that case internally to myself."

## Close

Once a number is agreed verbally, ask for it in writing before giving a verbal acceptance, and confirm start date flexibility given no relocation is needed from Geneva.
