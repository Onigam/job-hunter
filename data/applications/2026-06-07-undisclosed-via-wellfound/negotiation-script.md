# Negotiation Script — Member of Technical Staff, Principal Engineer (TypeScript)

_Company: Inkeep (identified via cross-reference). Backed by Khosla Ventures + YC. ~19 employees. In-office: SF or NY._

---

## Market Context

**US market benchmarks for Principal Engineer at a well-funded early-stage startup (SF/NY, 2026):**
- Base salary: $240,000 – $310,000
- Equity: 0.25% – 0.75% (principal level, pre-Series B company)
- Total comp target: $350,000 – $450,000+

**Inkeep data point (Senior role, cross-referenced):**
- Senior MTS: $180,000 – $250,000 base + 0.1% – 0.4% equity
- Principal is one level above — expect 20–30% premium on both base and equity

**Switzerland / Geneva reference (if remote arrangement negotiated):**
- Senior Staff / Principal Engineer: CHF 170,000 – 210,000 base
- Remote Swiss hire at US startup: typically USD $160,000 – $220,000 + equity
- Note: Geneva cost-of-living is comparable to SF, so a geographic discount is not justified

**Your anchor:** Principal in SF/NY. Don't pre-discount for your location unless they raise it first.

---

## Opening Position

**Opening ask (verbal, first offer):**

> "For a principal-level role with this scope I'd be looking at $280,000 base and equity in the 0.4%–0.6% range. I know that's at the higher end — it reflects the depth of platform architecture experience I'm bringing, particularly the API/SDK design background and the full-stack TypeScript work at scale."

Why this anchor:
- It's aspirational but defensible for a principal IC with founding-engineer credentials
- Gives room to land at $250,000–$260,000 base + 0.35%–0.5% equity, which is a strong outcome
- Never open with your real floor

---

## If They Come in Low (Below $220,000 Base)

**Script:**

> "I appreciate the offer. The base is below what I'd targeted for a principal role at this stage. Based on what I'm seeing in the SF market for this level, I'd expect something closer to $250,000 – $260,000. Can we work toward that range?"

**If they push back on market data:**

> "Happy to walk through my benchmarks. Khosla-backed principal engineers at this company size are typically in the $230k–$280k range. Given the ownership scope described, I think $250k is a reasonable meeting point."

**Fallback if they genuinely can't move on base:**

> "If the base ceiling is fixed at [X], I'd want to revisit the equity to compensate. What does the principal band look like on equity? I'd want to get to at least 0.4%–0.5% to make the total comp work."

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Inkeep context:** YC + Khosla-backed. This is real equity with exit potential. Push harder here than at a less well-funded company.

**Key terms to nail down:**
- Vesting schedule: standard 4-year with 1-year cliff is the baseline, push for accelerated vesting on acquisition (single trigger preferred, double trigger is common)
- Option type: ISO preferred over NQSOs (tax treatment matters for Swiss residents especially)
- Exercise window: push for 5-year post-termination exercise window, not the standard 90 days
- Cliff waiver: ask if they've considered waiving the cliff given you're a principal-level hire (unlikely but worth asking in a "founding" context)
- Strike price: ask for the current 409A valuation and FMV per share — compare against last funding round price to understand dilution

**Script for equity discussion:**

> "On the equity side — I want to make sure I understand the structure. What's the current 409A and when was the last 409A done? And is the plan to offer ISOs? For someone based in Switzerland the tax treatment matters."

> "I'd also want to ask about the exercise window. The 90-day post-termination window is a real risk at an early-stage company. A 5-year window, or at least 2-year, would be important to me."

---

## Geographic Pushback Defense

If they try to apply a Geneva/remote discount:

> "Geneva is the most expensive city in Europe and one of the most expensive cities in the world — cost of living is comparable to San Francisco. A geographic discount doesn't apply here the same way it would for a hire in a lower-cost market."

> "I'm also committing to in-office time, which has real travel and logistics costs attached. If anything, I'd expect a travel budget or relocation support to be part of the package."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

Even without a formal competing offer, you can frame opportunity cost:

> "I want to be transparent — I'm in late-stage conversations with a couple of other roles at a similar level. The range I'm discussing is consistent across them. I'd rather close this quickly and cleanly if we can get to the right number."

If you do have another offer, use it directly:

> "I have a competing offer at $[X] base with [Y]% equity. I'm more interested in this role because of [specific Inkeep reason], but I'd need you to match on comp for it to be an easy decision."

---

## Negotiation Sequence

1. Get the written offer before negotiating — never negotiate against a verbal number
2. Ask for 48 hours to review, use the time to research and prep
3. Counter once, clearly, with a specific number and rationale
4. If they counter back, accept or ask for one final concession (usually equity or signing bonus)
5. Don't negotiate more than twice — it signals anxiety, not leverage

---

## Signing Bonus as Fallback

If base is stuck:

> "If the base band is firm at [X], would you consider a signing bonus to bridge the gap? Even a one-time $20,000–$30,000 payment would help me make this work without ongoing salary friction."

Signing bonuses are easier for companies to approve because they don't compound into future raises.
