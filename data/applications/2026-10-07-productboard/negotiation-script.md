# Negotiation Script: Productboard, Staff Full-stack Product Engineer, Growth (Prague/Brno/Remote EU)

## The shape of this offer

This one is unusual because the band is published, and it's a Czech band. The posting lists **CZK 2,295,000 to 3,305,000 a year**, split into quartiles:

| Quartile | CZK | Approx. CHF (at ~26 CZK/CHF) | Approx. EUR (at ~25 CZK/EUR) | Share of hires |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 2.30M to 2.55M | 88k to 98k | 92k to 102k | 33% |
| Q2 | 2.55M to 2.80M | 98k to 108k | 102k to 112k | 27% |
| Q3 | 2.80M to 3.05M | 108k to 117k | 112k to 122k | 33% |
| Q4 | 3.05M to 3.30M | 117k to 127k | 122k to 132k | 7% |

**Check exchange rates on the day.** Benefits on top: stock options, 5 weeks' holiday, a learning budget, a home office stipend, and a USD 6k to 13k relocation bonus that only matters if Magino moves to Prague or Brno.

Three things to settle before any talk about numbers:

1. **Can they employ someone in Switzerland at all?** Switzerland isn't in the EU, and the posting says no visa sponsorship and "legal EU work authorization". Ask the recruiter directly whether they hire in Switzerland, and how: a local entity, an employer of record (Deel, Remote, Oyster and so on), or a contractor agreement. Under the EU/Swiss social security agreement, someone living and working in Geneva is normally covered by Swiss AHV/AVS, so a Czech employer would have to register and pay Swiss contributions. Some companies won't. Find out in the first call, not at offer stage.
2. **Which band applies to remote hires?** The posted figures are almost certainly Prague-indexed. Ask whether remote EU hires get the same band or a location-adjusted one, and whether there's a Switzerland adjustment. If it's the Czech band regardless, the top quartile is about CHF 125k.
3. **Is the gap worth it?** Staff-level roles in Geneva outside big tech usually pay CHF 180k to 230k base. Even Q4 here is roughly 35 to 45% below that. The role itself is a good fit (founding-engineer profile, AI agent work, full stack), but Magino should decide upfront what number makes it worth taking, rather than getting drawn in by the interview process.

## Equity: stock options at a late-stage private company

Productboard is a late-stage private company. Its last widely reported round was a Series D in 2022 at a valuation around USD 1.7B (**verify, and ask for the current internal valuation**). Questions to ask:

1. **How many options, and as a percentage of fully diluted shares?** A raw number of options means nothing without the total share count.
2. **Strike price versus the latest preferred price.** If the 2022 valuation has been marked down since, the common stock may be worth less than the headline suggests. Ask what the latest 409A (or equivalent) valuation is.
3. **Liquidation preference stack.** Total preference across all rounds, and whether any is participating or above 1x. In a sale below the last valuation, preferences get paid before common.
4. **Vesting and cliff.** Probably 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
5. **Exercise window after leaving.** The default is often 90 days. Ask for 5 to 10 years; some companies grant it on request, and it costs them very little.
6. **Acceleration.** Ask about double-trigger acceleration (sale plus termination). Unusual for non-executives, but cheap to ask.
7. **Anti-dilution** protects investors, not employees, so there's nothing to negotiate there. What you can ask for is a refresh grant policy for the next round.
8. **Secondary sales.** Has the company ever run a tender offer for employees? That's the only realistic path to liquidity before an IPO or sale.
9. **Swiss tax.** In Switzerland, options are generally taxed as income when exercised (sometimes at grant, depending on the plan and canton). Get a Geneva tax adviser to look at the plan documents before signing. Also check whether the plan is even open to people employed through an EOR.

Value the options at close to zero when deciding whether the cash works. Treat any upside as a bonus.

## Scripts

### First recruiter call: employment set-up and band

> "Before we go further, a practical question. I live in Geneva, which isn't in the EU. Can Productboard employ someone in Switzerland, either directly or through an employer of record? And does the posted band apply to remote hires everywhere, or is it adjusted by location?"

### When they ask for expectations

If there's a location-adjusted band:

> "For a Staff role, in Geneva, I'm looking at around CHF 200k base, plus options. I'm flexible on the mix, but that's the right range for the local market."

If it's the Czech band:

> "I've seen the published band. Given the profile you're describing, a former founding engineer with 0-to-1 experience and agent work, I'd expect to be in the top quartile. I'd also want to understand the option grant properly, because with a Geneva cost of living that's where the gap would have to close."

### If they come in low (e.g. Q2, CZK 2.7M)

> "Thanks, I appreciate it, and I'm keen on the role. The number's the hard part. I'm living in one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and the posting describes almost exactly my background: founding engineer, platform from zero to acquisition, Senior Staff for the last five years, AI adoption across teams. That's a top-quartile profile. Can we get the base to CZK 3.3M and look at a larger option grant? That would get me to yes."

### Geographic discount pushback

> "I understand you index to Prague, and I'm not asking for a Geneva salary. I'm asking for the top of the band you publish, because the bottom of it doesn't work in Switzerland, and my experience fits the top of it anyway."

If they say the band is fixed and Q4 is reserved:

> "Understood. If the base can't move, can we close the gap elsewhere: a larger option grant, a sign-on, a ten-year exercise window, and a written review at twelve months against the top of the band?"

### Competing offer template (use only if real)

> "I want to be upfront: I have another process at the final stage, a Staff role with a base of around CHF [X]. Productboard is the role I'm most interested in, because of the growth and agent work. If we can get to [specific number and grant], I'd be ready to sign this week and close the other one."

Never invent a competing offer. If there isn't one, say there are other conversations in progress and leave it there.

## Before signing

- Employing entity, country of contract and which social security system applies, in writing
- Base in CZK or CHF? If CZK, who carries the currency risk? Ask for CHF or EUR if possible.
- Option grant: number, percentage, strike, vesting, exercise window, all in writing
- How much travel to Prague/Brno is expected, and who pays
- Notice period and whether there's a probation period (normally 3 months under Swiss law)
- Learning budget amount and the home office stipend
