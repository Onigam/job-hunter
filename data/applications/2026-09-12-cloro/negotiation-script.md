# Negotiation Script: Cloro, Founding Engineer

## Context

Cloro posted a base range of $80k-$150k USD, plus an ARR-based performance bonus (up to 100% of salary) with a 1.25x multiplier for individual performance. This is a US-anchored bootstrapped-startup range, not a Swiss one, and it sits well below what a Senior Staff Engineer with founder experience commands in Geneva. The gap is real and worth naming early, calmly, not as a complaint but as a data point they need to price the offer correctly.

## Switzerland market anchors

Geneva cost of living and local comp benchmarks put a Staff-level backend/infra engineer at roughly CHF 150k-190k base (about $170k-$215k at current rates), with founding or VP-adjacent hires often above that once equity is priced in. Even accounting for the fact that Cloro pays in USD and is optimizing for early burn, the top of their stated range ($150k) lands below the Swiss market floor for someone with an acquisition exit and hands-on Kubernetes/queue rebuild experience. Frame this as a fact about the market, not a demand: "For context, Staff-level infra roles in Geneva typically start around where your range tops out."

## Opening anchor

Aim for $150k-$165k base (top of or slightly above their stated range), justified by scope, not by need:

"Given the scope here, backend, scraping infra, and Kubernetes work as Employee #1, I'd expect to land at or above the top of your posted range. Somewhere around $155k-$160k base feels right, with the ARR bonus structure as you've described it. I'm also going to ask about equity, since founding-stage comp usually has three components, not two."

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

If the initial offer lands near the bottom of the range ($80k-$100k):

"I appreciate the offer. The number's below what I was expecting for this scope, and it's also below what similar founding-engineer infra roles pay right now, even accounting for early-stage constraints. I built and scaled a platform from zero through an acquisition, so I'm not looking to be priced as a mid-level hire. Could we get closer to $140k-$150k base? I'm flexible on how that's split between base and the bonus multiplier if cash is tight right now."

If they push back on budget constraints specifically:

"That's fair, and I get that bootstrapped means real constraints on cash. If base has a hard ceiling, I'd want to make up the difference through the individual performance multiplier or through equity. What room is there on either of those?"

## Equity negotiation

Cloro is bootstrapped and apparently already profitable (first million in ARR in under three months), which changes the equity conversation compared to a VC-backed startup: less dilution risk, but also potentially less urgency on their side to hand out meaningful equity since they're not burning investor cash to grow.

Points to raise:
- Ask for the actual percentage, not just "equity," and get it in writing before accepting.
- Standard vesting is 4 years with a 1-year cliff; push back if they propose anything longer.
- Ask directly whether there's an existing cap table and what an early hire's stake looks like relative to the founders', since "Employee #1" framing should come with real ownership, not a token grant.
- If it's structured as profit-sharing or phantom equity instead of real equity (common in bootstrapped companies), get the payout mechanics and any liquidity triggers spelled out clearly.

## Geographic discount pushback

If they try to anchor low because "you're not in the US":

"I get that the range was probably built around US costs, but Geneva isn't a discount market, it's one of the more expensive cities in Europe. I'd rather we set comp based on the role and my experience than on where I happen to be sitting."

## Competing offer template

Use only if there's an actual competing process in flight; don't bluff with specifics that could be checked:

"I'm currently in later-stage conversations with another team for a similar founding/infra role, and the comp there is tracking closer to $160k plus a stronger equity component. I'd genuinely rather join Cloro given the problem you're solving, so I wanted to give you the chance to get closer to that before I make a final call."

## Closing move

Whatever the final number, get the full package (base, bonus mechanics, equity percentage, vesting schedule, and the paid trial terms) in writing before accepting. The take-home test and paid 2-day trial mentioned in their process are good moments to ask, informally, how the bonus multiplier has actually played out for the team so far, real numbers beat the pitch-deck version.
