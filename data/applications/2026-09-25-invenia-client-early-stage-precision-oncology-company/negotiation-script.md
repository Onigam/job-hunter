# Negotiation Script: Lead Engineer, AI for Clinical Genomics (Invenia client)

**Context worth keeping in mind:** fully remote anywhere in Europe, early-stage and described as well-funded, "meaningful equity", and the offer goes through a recruiter (Invenia) with the client still unnamed. The company will probably benchmark pay against a European remote band, not a Geneva one. Everything below is about closing that gap without sounding rigid.

All figures are estimates from public compensation data and should be checked against the actual funding stage once the client is named.

---

## 1. Market reference points

| Benchmark | Base salary | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Geneva / Zurich, Lead or Staff engineer, funded startup | CHF 170k to 210k | Zurich tech tends to sit at the upper end |
| Geneva, Staff+ at scale-up or big tech | CHF 200k to 250k+ | Plus RSUs; your Benevity level sits here |
| Remote Europe, lead engineer, seed or Series A health-tech/AI | EUR 110k to 150k | Where the company will probably start |
| Remote Europe, founding or lead engineer, well-funded AI startup | EUR 140k to 175k | Well-funded AI companies often pay above the EU median |
| Equity, one of the first key engineering hires | 0.5% to 1.5% | Seed usually sits at the top of the range, Series A at the bottom |

**Your numbers:**
- **Target:** CHF 195k base plus about 0.8% equity
- **Opening anchor:** CHF 205k to 215k base
- **Walk-away:** CHF 165k base, unless equity is clearly above 1% on reasonable terms
- **Structure note:** if they hire through an EOR or as a contractor, ask for the gross employer cost figure and make sure Swiss social charges (AVS/AHV, LPP pension) are covered. A contractor day rate should be about 25% higher than the salary equivalent.

---

## 2. Talking to the recruiter first

Invenia's fee is usually a percentage of first-year base, so the recruiter gains from a higher number too. Use that, but don't give the first figure if you can avoid it.

> "I'd rather understand the full package before I name a number. Could you tell me the band the client has approved for this role, and roughly what stage and size of round they're at? That tells me how to weigh base against equity."

If they push:

> "For a lead role with full-stack and ML ownership, I'm looking at the CHF 200k to 215k range for base, since I'm in Geneva. I'm open on how it's split if the equity is serious. Can you check how that fits with what the client has in mind?"

---

## 3. Opening anchor with the company

> "Thanks, I've enjoyed the process and I'd like to make this work. Based on what I've seen for lead and staff engineers in Switzerland and at well-funded AI startups in Europe, I'm looking at around CHF 210k base. It reflects the scope here: owning the stack end to end, setting architecture for software that already runs in clinical labs, and building the team. I've done that before at Alaya, all the way to an acquisition."

Then stop talking.

---

## 4. If they come in low

**Offer around EUR 120k to 140k:**

> "I appreciate it, and I understand you're budgeting for a European remote band. The gap is real for me, though. My cost base is Geneva, and my last role was a Senior Staff position at a unicorn-scale company. I could get to about CHF 190k base if the equity moves to around 1% with a longer exercise window. Is there room to move on either?"

**If base is truly fixed:**

> "Understood, if base is capped, let's look at the rest. I'd want [X]% equity instead of [Y]%, a signing bonus of around CHF 20k to cover the difference in year one, and a written salary review at 12 months tied to the next round."

**If they call it the "top of the band":**

> "That makes sense for a band built around a senior engineer. This role is closer to first technical leader, since you're asking me to shape the architecture and the team culture. Could the band be reconsidered at that level?"

---

## 5. Pushing back on a geographic discount

> "I get why remote roles get benchmarked across Europe. The way I'd look at it is that you're paying for the scope and the risk you're taking off the table, not for my postcode. Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and a Swiss employee costs more to employ as well. I'm not asking for a Zurich big-tech package, only for something that doesn't mean taking a large step down from what I'd earn locally."

---

## 6. Equity: what to ask about

Questions to ask before you judge the number:

- Total fully diluted shares, so the % is real, and the last preferred price or valuation
- Option strike price, and the size of the option pool relative to the next round
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Exercise window:** push for 5 to 10 years after leaving instead of 90 days. This matters more than a few basis points.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination), at least 50%. You've been through an acquisition and know how integration goes.
- **Anti-dilution:** employees almost never get it, and asking for it can look naive. Ask instead for a refresh grant tied to the next priced round, or a written commitment to review the grant at Series A/B.
- **Tax:** Swiss tax on options depends on the plan type (taxed at grant or at exercise). Ask whether the plan has been set up for Swiss residents. If not, ask them to cover the advisory cost.

Script:

> "The equity matters to me, and I'd take a lower base for the right structure. For that to work I'd need to know the fully diluted count and last valuation, and I'd want a longer exercise window and double-trigger acceleration. If we agree on those, I'm flexible on base."

---

## 7. Competing offer template

Only say this if it's true, even if the other process is at an early stage. Never make up an offer.

> "Being open about it: I'm at the final stages with another company, a Staff+ role in Switzerland, and their range is above what's on the table here. I'd rather join you because of the domain and how early this is, so I'm telling you now instead of just going quiet. If you can get closer to [number], I can decide quickly."

If there's no real offer:

> "I'm talking to a few companies at the moment, mostly Staff and lead roles in Switzerland. This one is at the top of my list, so I want to get the numbers right rather than drag things out."

---

## 8. Other terms to cover before signing

- Budget for travel to company offsites (common for fully remote early teams)
- Equipment and home office allowance
- Learning budget, including a genomics or bioinformatics course in the first months
- Notice period and non-compete scope; keep any non-compete narrow to precision oncology
- Title: "Lead Engineer" is fine, but ask what the path to Head of Engineering or CTO looks like as the team grows
