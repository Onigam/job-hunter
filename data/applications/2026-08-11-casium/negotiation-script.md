# Negotiation Script - Casium, Founding AI Engineer

## Context

- Posted range: $90k - $170k base, 0.0% - 0.5% equity, Seattle, in-office with WFH flexibility.
- Posted requirement: 3 years of experience. Magino brings 17+, including a founding-engineer exit (Alaya to Benevity).
- Company stage: seed, ~10 people, $5M raised (Maverick Ventures, AI2 Incubator, GTMfund, Success Venture Partners), paying customers, zero churn reported.
- Relocation: Geneva to Seattle. Not a remote role, so relocation cost and timeline are part of the negotiation, not an afterthought.

The posted range reads like it is built around a 2-4 year engineer, not a founding-level hire who has already taken a company through acquisition. That gap is the main lever here.

## Market anchor (Seattle, seed-stage, founding-level AI engineer)

For a first or second AI engineering hire at a funded Seattle seed startup, with founder-level ownership expected, comparable total comp in the market runs roughly $170k-$230k base plus equity in the 0.5%-1.5% range, sometimes higher when the hire is expected to set technical direction rather than just execute. The posted $90k-$170k band is realistic for a strong mid-level IC; it is not calibrated for someone who already built and scaled a platform to acquisition. Anchor near the top of the posted range or above it, and let the experience gap do the talking rather than asserting it outright.

## Opening anchor script

"I looked at the posted range and I think it's a good fit for the role as scoped for someone earlier in their career. Given I've already taken a company from zero to acquisition as a founding engineer, and I'd be stepping into a similar level of ownership here, I'd want to talk about a base closer to $195k, plus equity toward the higher end of what you'd offer a first AI hire. I'm flexible on structure since I know you're early and capital is a real constraint, I just want the offer to reflect the level of the work."

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

"I hear that the seed budget is tight, and I want to be realistic about that. If base has to stay closer to your posted range, I'd want to make it up on equity, a faster vesting start, or a review checkpoint at six months tied to concrete milestones rather than waiting a full year to revisit comp. What I don't want is to lock in a number that reflects 3 years of experience when I'm bringing considerably more, so let's find the part of the package that has room."

## Equity negotiation

- Ask for the actual share count and current fully diluted share total, not just the percentage; percentages move with every future round.
- Standard seed-stage terms to expect: 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff. Push back if the cliff is longer than a year or vesting resets are non-standard.
- Ask whether there's a pro rata or anti-dilution provision for early technical hires participating in future rounds; at seed stage this is uncommon but worth a direct question.
- If equity lands at the low end (0.0%-0.2%), use that as leverage to push base higher rather than accepting both being low.

## Relocation

- This is not a remote role, so ask directly: does Casium cover relocation costs, temporary housing, or a visa/immigration process (notable irony given the company's own product) for a move from Geneva to Seattle?
- If there's no formal relocation budget, ask for a one-time relocation stipend, even $8k-$15k covers shipping, flights, and short-term housing while finding a place.
- Confirm whether the "WFH flexibility" mentioned in the posting means a hybrid schedule or fully in-office with occasional remote days; this affects the actual cost and disruption of the move and should shape the ask.

## Competing offer leverage template

"I want to be straightforward that I'm currently in later-stage conversations with another company at a similar stage, discussing a base in a similar range with a faster path to a comp review. I'd rather move forward with Casium given the problem you're solving, but I need the offer to be competitive enough that it's an easy decision rather than one I have to talk myself into."

Use this even without a live competing offer only if there genuinely is active interest elsewhere; do not fabricate a specific company or number if asked to name it.
