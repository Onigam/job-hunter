# Negotiation Script: Jobber, Principal Engineer (AI, Office of the CEO)

## Context that shapes everything

Jobber is a late-stage private company headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta, with about 1,000 employees. It's well past the startup phase, so this is a banded offer, not a founder-style negotiation. The role sits in a small team inside the Office of the CEO and reports to the VP of Customer Experience Innovation.

The posting publishes two bands (as of 2026-09-30):

| Posting | Minimum | Midpoint | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| US listing (LinkedIn) | USD 229,300 | USD 269,700 | USD 310,200 |
| Canadian listing (likely CAD) | 196,300 | 230,900 | 265,600 |

Bands include equity eligibility plus benefits (health and wellness stipends, retirement matching).

The first question isn't money, it's **where they can employ you**. The role is listed as remote, but the bands point to US and Canada. From Geneva, there are three realistic outcomes, and each changes the numbers:

1. They hire you in Switzerland through an employer of record (EOR) or a local entity.
2. They hire you as a contractor from Switzerland.
3. You relocate to Canada or the US (Jobber can sponsor or you already have status; you'd need to check).

Ask this in the first recruiter call, before any number comes up: "Which countries can you employ in for this role, and do you use an EOR for anyone in Europe?"

## Market reference points (estimates, verify before the call)

These are working estimates from public salary data. Check them against Levels.fyi, Glassdoor and current exchange rates before any number leaves your mouth. Exchange rates below are rough (USD to CHF around 0.80, CAD to CHF around 0.58).

| Reference | Base | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Geneva, Principal / Senior Staff at a scale-up | CHF 190k to 240k | Local market, what you'd expect staying put |
| Zurich, Staff+ at big tech | CHF 230k to 300k+ total comp | Includes RSUs |
| Jobber US band, converted | about CHF 185k to 250k | Midpoint around CHF 215k |
| Jobber Canadian band, converted | about CHF 115k to 155k | Well below Geneva market |

Takeaway: the US band lines up with Geneva market rates. The Canadian band, converted, is a large cut. If they hire you in Switzerland, the whole negotiation comes down to which band applies.

## Your numbers

**Walk-away floor:** decide this before the first call and write it down. As a starting point for thinking, something like CHF 200k base in Switzerland, or the US midpoint if relocating, is a reasonable floor for a Principal role, but it's your call.

**Target (Switzerland via EOR):** CHF 230k to 245k base, plus equity. Framed in USD, that's roughly the US band midpoint to upper-middle.

**Target (relocation to Canada):** upper third of the Canadian band, around 250k to 265k, plus a relocation package.

**Opening anchor:** upper quartile of whichever band applies. For a Swiss hire, anchor on the US band, not the Canadian one.

## Scripts

### When they ask for your expectations first

"I'd rather understand the structure first, mainly which country I'd be employed in, since that changes a lot. For context, I'm a Senior Staff Engineer in Geneva, where Principal-level base salaries sit around CHF 200k to 240k. Your US band is close to that; the Canadian one isn't."

If they push for a number:

"For a founding seat in the CEO's office, owning the architecture end to end, I'm thinking in the upper part of your US band, around USD 290k to 300k total cash, or the CHF equivalent if I'm employed in Switzerland."

### If they want to apply the Canadian band to a Swiss hire

"I get why that's the default, since Jobber is Canadian. The issue is that Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in the world, and the Canadian band converted comes out around CHF 115k to 155k. That's well under what this level pays locally, and below what I earn now. Your US band reflects the market I'm actually in. Can we use that as the reference?"

### If the base comes in low (lower third of the band)

"Thanks for the offer. I'm keen on the role, and I'd like to make it work. The base is lower than I expected for a Principal who's the founding engineer on a new team reporting into the CEO's office. I've done this exact job before, building from zero to acquisition, so the ramp-up risk is low. Could we get closer to the midpoint, or above it?"

### If the base can't move

"Understood. If the base is fixed, could we look at the other parts: a larger equity grant, a signing bonus, or a guaranteed compensation review at six months, tied to specific things I've shipped?"

### Remote from Geneva: time zones and travel

Jobber's core hours will be Mountain Time, which is eight hours behind Geneva. Settle this before you sign, not after: "I'd plan to overlap with Edmonton in my afternoons and evenings, and I'd expect to travel to Edmonton regularly, especially in the first quarter. Can travel be covered separately rather than out of comp?"

### Competing offer template (only use if it's real)

"I want to be open with you: I'm in late stages with another company, and their offer is [base] plus [equity]. Jobber is my first choice because of the mandate and the team, and I'd like to make this work. Is there room to close the gap on [base or equity]?"

Don't invent a competing offer. Recruiters at this level check, and a bluff that's called costs more than it gains.

## Equity notes (private late-stage company)

Jobber is private, so any equity will be options or RSUs in a company you can't sell shares of freely. Ask, and get the answers in writing:

1. **What instrument?** Options or RSUs. With options, ask for the strike price and the latest fair market value.
2. **Grant size in number of units and as a percentage** of fully diluted shares, or at least the latest valuation per share, so you can put a value on it.
3. **Vesting schedule.** Standard is four years with a one-year cliff. Ask whether there's a refresh grant policy.
4. **Exercise window after leaving** (options only). A 90-day window is common; longer windows (5 to 10 years) exist and are worth asking for.
5. **Liquidity.** Have there been secondary sales or tender offers for employees? Is an IPO being discussed?
6. **Swiss tax.** If employed in Switzerland, options and RSUs are taxed differently than in Canada or the US, often at vesting or exercise. Check with a Swiss tax adviser before accepting, especially if the grant is large.
7. **EOR compatibility.** Some EOR setups can't hold equity grants directly. Confirm that you'd actually receive the grant as a Swiss-based employee.

Treat the equity as upside, not salary. Negotiate the base as if the equity is worth zero, then push on the grant as a separate item.

## Checklist before the call

- Confirm the country of employment question is answered.
- Update exchange rates and Geneva salary data.
- Write your walk-away number down.
- Prepare one sentence on why you're the founding engineer they need (Alaya from zero to acquisition, 70% AI tooling adoption at Benevity).
- Have the equity questions ready.
