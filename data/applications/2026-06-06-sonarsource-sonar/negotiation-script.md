# Negotiation Script — SonarSource, Geneva

---

## Market Context

**Role:** AI Research Scientist / Engineer, Geneva (Hybrid, 3 days/week on-site)
**Company:** Sonar — well-funded scale-up, ~800 employees, reportedly $200M+ ARR, backed by General Catalyst. Profitable. Private company.

**Geneva AI/ML market rates (2026 CHF, gross annual):**
- Senior AI/ML Engineer: CHF 155,000 - 185,000
- AI Research Scientist: CHF 165,000 - 200,000
- Staff/Principal-level AI: CHF 190,000 - 230,000+

**Magino's positioning:** 17+ years, Staff-equivalent seniority, founding engineer background, production LLM systems, Geneva-based. Should anchor at the top of the Research Scientist band or into Principal territory.

**Target range:** CHF 185,000 - 210,000 base
**Opening anchor:** CHF 200,000
**Walk-away floor:** CHF 155,000 (below this, the role is mispriced for the seniority)

---

## Opening Anchor Script

Use when they ask about salary expectations — ideally after they make a first offer, but be ready to go first if pressed.

> "Based on the market for this level of AI/ML engineering in Geneva and my background, I'm targeting around CHF 200,000 gross annual. That's consistent with what Staff-level engineers with production LLM system experience are seeing in this market right now. I'm open to discussing the full package."

**Why CHF 200k:** It's high enough to create anchoring room without being implausible for a well-funded company with Geneva operations. Sonar can afford it; the question is whether they bracket this role at Research Scientist or Staff Engineer level.

---

## If They Come In Low (under CHF 155,000)

> "I appreciate the offer. I want to be straightforward: that range is below what I'd expect for this level of seniority and the market here in Geneva. I'm not saying no, but I'd need to understand whether there's room to move, either on base or total compensation. What's the flexibility?"

Follow-up if they say the range is fixed:
> "Understood. Is there any flexibility on a sign-on, accelerated review at six months, or equity? I want to find a way to make this work, but the base as it stands is a gap I'd need help bridging."

---

## Equity / Options Negotiation

SonarSource is private. Any equity would be options with an illiquidity discount and uncertain exit timeline.

**Starting position:**
> "For a private company, I'd want to understand the option pool structure, the strike price relative to the last 409A (or equivalent), vesting schedule, and what the cliff looks like. Options at a private company are worth something on paper until they're not. Can you walk me through the equity component?"

**Standard terms to push for:**
- 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff (standard; push back if cliff is longer)
- Acceleration clause on acquisition (at least partial single-trigger)
- Post-termination exercise window: ask for 2-5 years, standard is 90 days which is punitive for private company options

**If equity is minimal or token:**
> "If the equity component is limited, I'd want to make sure the base reflects that. At a public company with liquid RSUs, I'd accept a lower base in exchange for upside. With private options and an uncertain liquidity window, the base needs to do more of the work."

---

## Geographic Premium Pushback

If they reference lower salaries from non-Swiss candidates or try to anchor to a different market:

> "I'm based in Geneva, and Geneva is one of the more expensive cities in Europe. I'm not asking for a premium over the Swiss market; I'm asking for alignment with it. Remote candidates from lower-cost markets aren't the right comparison point here."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in hand (or can imply you're actively interviewing):

> "I'm in conversation with a couple of other companies at a similar stage, and one of them is moving fairly quickly. I'd prefer to be at Sonar, but I want to be honest with you about the timeline. Is there flexibility to move faster on your end?"

If no competing offer but you want to create urgency:
> "I'm being thoughtful about where I land, and I'm not going to accept something that doesn't fit. I'd rather close this conversation quickly if there's alignment. What's the fastest path to a decision on your side?"

---

## Benefits Checklist (Switzerland-specific)

Confirm before signing:
- [ ] LPP / 2nd pillar pension: confirm contribution split (employer vs. employee); some companies are generous here
- [ ] LAMal health insurance: does the company contribute? (Not legally required but some companies top up)
- [ ] Lunch / meal subsidy or on-site meals
- [ ] Remote work budget (standing desk, internet contribution)
- [ ] Conference and learning budget (important for an AI research role)
- [ ] Unpaid leave policy for side projects or advisory work

---

## Final Close Script

> "I think we're close. If you can get to CHF [X] on the base, I'm ready to sign. I'm not going to go back and forth on small increments. That's the number that makes sense to me, and I'm confident it's right for the role."

Keep it clean. One counter, then a decision. Prolonged negotiation signals uncertainty more than it extracts value.
