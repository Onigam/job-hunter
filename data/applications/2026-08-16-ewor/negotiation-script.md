# Negotiation Script - EWOR, AI Co-Founder / CTO

## Context

This isn't a standard salary negotiation. EWOR offers two structures: a salary while building the venture as a direct EWOR employee, or up to 500,000 euros in funding for the startup itself. The real negotiation is about which structure to pick and what EWOR takes in return, not about squeezing a base number. EWOR is backed by Paul Müller (Adjust) and Petter Made (SumUp), so the people setting terms have been on the founder side of this exact conversation before, which cuts both ways: they'll respect a sharp negotiation, and they'll also recognize a weak one immediately.

## Market anchor if choosing the salary path

For a Geneva-based technical co-founder with a prior exit, the closest comparable isn't a market-rate engineering salary, it's what an early-stage European accelerator pays a founder-in-residence or technical EIR while they're building: typically CHF 90,000 to 130,000 a year, well below what the same person would earn as a Staff or Principal engineer at a normal employer, because the upside is meant to come from the equity in the venture, not the salary. The opening ask should sit at the top of that band, CHF 120,000 to 130,000, benchmarked to Geneva cost of living rather than to wherever EWOR's default number was calculated for.

## Opening script for the salary path

"Before I pick between salary and funding, I want to understand how the salary track is structured day to day, since it changes the calculus. If I take the salary path, I'd expect something in the CHF 120,000 to 130,000 range, benchmarked to Geneva rather than a lower-cost hub, given this is a full-time commitment with no other income while the venture is being built. I'd also want clarity on how long that salary runs and what happens to it once the startup raises its own round."

## Funding path: what to ask before choosing it

Taking up to 500,000 euros in funding instead of a salary means EWOR is getting paid back through equity in the venture, not through a founder's time. Before agreeing to that path, get in writing: what percentage of the company EWOR takes in exchange for the funding and the program, whether that's a straight equity stake or a convertible instrument with a cap and discount, whether the 500,000 euros is disbursed as a lump sum or in tranches tied to milestones, and what happens to unvested EWOR equity if the founder leaves the program early. A number that sounds generous on a slide can be a very different deal once the cap table is in front of you.

## Counter-offer script if the funding terms come in aggressive

"I want to build something EWOR is proud to have backed, so I'd rather get the cap table right now than renegotiate it later with a lawyer in the room. If the standard stake is toward the high end of what similar programs take, I'd want to understand what justifies it relative to Y Combinator or a comparable seed program, and whether there's room to scale EWOR's stake down as the venture hits its early milestones rather than fixing it at day one."

## Equity and cap table notes

- Confirm whether EWOR's equity is priced at formation or after a defined milestone; taking a stake in a company with no product yet is a very different bet than taking one after product-market fit, and the percentage should reflect that.
- Ask about anti-dilution provisions on EWOR's stake in future rounds, since a founder-friendly program shouldn't need aggressive anti-dilution protection to feel confident in its own bet.
- Get clarity on vesting for any co-founder or early-hire equity pool the venture will need to offer later, and whether EWOR's terms constrain how that pool gets carved out.
- If multiple co-founders join through the program, confirm how equity splits between them are decided, since that's a common source of later conflict if it's left informal.

## Geographic pushback

"I'm building this from Geneva, and I'd want any salary component benchmarked there rather than converted from a lower cost-of-living market. Remote doesn't reduce the scope of building and running a company end to end, and I don't think it should reduce the number either."

## Competing options template

"I'm not running a formal process against EWOR right now, but I've had informal conversations with other founder programs, and the reason I'm prioritizing this one is the caliber of the operators behind it, not just the deal terms. I'd still want the terms to reflect that I have other paths into building a company, including bootstrapping this myself with a smaller initial raise."

Use only if genuinely true; don't invent a specific competing program or number if asked to name one.
