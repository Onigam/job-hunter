# Negotiation Script: Applied AI Engineer, DeepJudge

Context that shapes the math: DeepJudge closed a $41.2M Series A in late 2025 led by Felicis with Coatue returning, at a reported $300M valuation, on top of an earlier $10.7M seed. That's a well-capitalized Series A, not a scrappy pre-seed, so there's real room on cash comp even if the equity story is still early. Role is Zurich HQ, hybrid, not Geneva, so factor commuting or partial relocation into what "total comp" needs to cover. It's a hybrid customer-facing plus product engineering role rather than a pure research seat, which should be priced closer to senior/staff IC than to a junior ML engineer band.

## Market anchor

- Senior AI/ML engineer at a well-funded Zurich Series A: roughly CHF 130,000-160,000 base, with the range pushing toward CHF 170,000-190,000 for someone at Staff level with founder-grade production experience and direct customer ownership.
- Zurich cost of living runs close to Geneva's, so there's no meaningful geographic discount to concede versus a Geneva-based offer, unlike a Berlin or remote-EU role.
- Applied/customer-facing AI roles at this stage often carry a slightly wider band than pure backend or ML infra roles, since the company is paying for judgment and communication on top of the engineering, not just throughput.
- Opening anchor: CHF 165,000 base, framed as a Staff-level IC number given 17+ years of experience, a founder track record through to acquisition, and hands-on production LLM work, not a generic mid-level AI engineer rate.

## Opening anchor script

"Given the scope of this role and that DeepJudge just closed a $41 million Series A, I'd expect a base around CHF 160,000 to 175,000, plus a meaningful equity grant. I'm not anchored to a specific structure as long as the total package lands in that zone. Can you share where this role is banded within that range and what the equity component typically looks like at this stage?"

## If they come in low

"I hear the number. Is that priced against a general AI engineer band, or does it account for the customer-facing scope, essentially owning solutions end to end with law firm clients directly? That ownership is closer to what I did at Alaya and at Benevity than a typical implementation role, and I'd want the base to reflect that."

If they hold the line: "I can flex on base if the equity grant or a defined six-month review makes up the difference. What I'd rather not do is settle below CHF 150,000, that's under what this scope pays elsewhere in the Zurich AI market right now."

## Equity notes (Series A specifics)

- Ask for the strike price basis: is it set against the $300M post-money from the Series A, or an earlier 409A-equivalent valuation still in effect.
- Confirm standard 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff, and ask whether early exercise is allowed, useful at this stage if the strike price is still low relative to where the company might go.
- Ask about anti-dilution protection or refresh grant policy tied to future rounds, since a Series A company at this valuation is likely to raise again within 18-24 months and initial grants can get diluted fast without a refresh cycle.
- Get the total option pool size and how much has already been allocated, a small remaining pool is a signal to push harder on the cash side instead.

## Geographic and hybrid-cadence pushback

"The role is Zurich HQ with hybrid expectations. I'm Geneva-based, so I'd want clarity on what hybrid actually means in practice, days per week, and whether relocation or a commuting allowance is part of the package if I'm not moving full-time. Happy to talk through what a sustainable schedule looks like for both sides."

## Competing offer, if genuinely in play

"I'm further along in a couple of other conversations at comparable scope and comp. This role stands out because of the specific problem, getting AI search working against a firm's real document systems without a migration, but I want to be transparent about timeline so we can move at a pace that works for both of us."

## Close

A $41M Series A at a $300M valuation is a strong signal, not a reason to compress the offer, use it to justify a real base rather than leaning entirely on equity upside that's still unproven. Get base, equity terms including strike price and vesting, and the hybrid/relocation expectations in writing before accepting, since Series A comp structures tend to shift again once the next round or a formal leveling system lands.
