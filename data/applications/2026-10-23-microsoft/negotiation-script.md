# Negotiation Script: Microsoft, Principal Software Engineer (VS Code & GitHub Copilot), Zürich

## The shape of this offer

The LinkedIn listing places the role in Zürich, full-time, on the Visual Studio Code team (VS Code and GitHub Copilot). It's an IC5 posting, and Microsoft publishes the base range for Switzerland: **CHF 183,800 to 309,700 per year**. Work mode isn't stated. The page header has shown both "Des Moines, IA" and "Boydton, VA", which contradicts the body, so confirm with the recruiter that the role is really Zürich-based.

This is a big-company offer, not a startup one. There's no equity percentage to fight over, and no vesting cliff or anti-dilution to worry about. The package is built from five levers:

1. **Level.** The single biggest one. Principal usually spans two internal levels (67 and 68 in Microsoft's older numbering), and the posted CHF 184k to 310k range covers both. Getting placed at the upper level moves base, bonus target and stock all at once.
2. **Base salary**, inside the posted band.
3. **On-hire stock award (RSUs)**, granted in USD and vesting over several years.
4. **Sign-on cash bonus**, often split over the first one or two years with a clawback if you leave early.
5. **Annual bonus target and annual stock refreshers**, which are set by level and performance and are hard to change at offer time.

Three things to settle early:

1. **Level, before numbers.** Ask the recruiter which level the loop is calibrated for. Seventeen years of experience, a cofounder exit and cross-team technical leadership support the upper end. Push on this before comp talk starts, because it's much harder to move afterwards.
2. **Location and on-site rhythm.** Geneva to Zürich is roughly 2h45 each way by train. Ask how many on-site days the team expects and whether a regular remote pattern from Geneva is acceptable. If they expect three or more days a week, relocation support (or a permanent second base in Zürich) becomes part of the negotiation.
3. **Vesting schedule.** Microsoft has changed its stock vesting over the years. Ask exactly how the on-hire award vests (length, frequency, any front-loading) so you can compare year-one cash properly.

## Market anchors

These are approximations. **Check every figure against levels.fyi (Microsoft, Zürich, Principal / 67 and 68), Glassdoor CH and, if possible, one or two people at Microsoft Zürich or Google Zürich before any call.** Big tech in Zürich pays well above Swiss startups, and Geneva's cost of living is similar, so there's no reason to accept a "regional" discount.

The takeaway: base will probably land between CHF 240k and 290k, and total compensation in year one could reach CHF 350k to 450k depending on level, sign-on and stock. Most of the room to negotiate is in level, the on-hire stock award and the sign-on bonus, not the base.

Swiss specifics worth raising:

- **RSU taxation.** In Switzerland, RSUs are generally taxed as income when they vest, at the market value on that day. Geneva income tax applies if you stay resident in Geneva; Zürich rates apply if you move. Ask HR for the expected treatment and whether they withhold at source.
- **Pension (BVG/LPP).** Ask about the employer contribution rate and whether it goes above the legal minimum. At big employers it usually does, and it adds real value.
- **ESPP.** Microsoft runs an employee stock purchase plan at a discount. Small, but worth noting when you compare offers.
- **Notice and start date.** Swiss notice periods are often three months. Make sure the start date and any sign-on payment timing account for this.

## Scripts

### When they ask for expectations

> "Before I give a number, can we talk about level? I'd like to understand which Principal level the loop is calibrated for, because that shapes everything else. Given the cross-team leadership in the posting and my background building a platform through to acquisition, I'd expect to be considered at the upper end."

If they push for a number:

> "Looking at the posted Swiss band and what I see for Principal-level roles in Zürich, I'm thinking around CHF 295k base, with a strong on-hire stock award. I'm flexible on how base, stock and sign-on split, as long as the total reflects the level."

### If they come in low (for example lower level, CHF 240k base, modest stock, no sign-on)

> "Thanks, I'd like to make this work. The base is at the bottom of what I see for Principal in Zürich, and with no sign-on the first year is well below other options I'm looking at. Could we revisit the level? If the level is fixed, I'd need the base closer to CHF 270k and a sign-on of around CHF 50k to bridge the first year."

### If they won't move on base

> "I understand the band is the band. Where is there flexibility, the on-hire stock or the sign-on? Moving the stock grant up by USD 100k, or adding a CHF 50k sign-on, would close most of the gap for me."

### Pushing back on a location or "Geneva isn't Zürich" argument

> "Geneva's cost of living is the same as Zürich's, often higher on housing, so I wouldn't expect a location adjustment. If the team needs me on-site several days a week, I'd want to talk about relocation support or a travel budget, because that's a real cost on my side."

### Competing offer template (use only if true, or keep it general)

> "I'm being straight with you: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company for a Staff-level role in Switzerland, and their package is around CHF [X] total in year one. VS Code is my first choice because of the team and the product, and I'd rather not decide on money alone. Is there room to get closer to that number?"

If there's no specific competing offer, keep it general:

> "I'm talking to a few other companies, including big tech in Zürich. I'd like this to be the one, so I want to make sure the offer is competitive with that market."

## Things not to forget

- **Get the full offer in writing** before accepting: level, base, bonus target, stock amount in USD, vesting schedule, sign-on and any clawback terms.
- **Ask about the background check timeline.** The posting requires the Microsoft Cloud Background Check before starting, which can take a few weeks. Don't resign from anything until it's clear.
- **Fit with the profile.** Microsoft is the kind of large enterprise the profile usually avoids. The VS Code team works more like an open-source product team inside it, so the case for an exception is reasonable. Check that in the interviews by asking how decisions get made and how much the team ships in public.
