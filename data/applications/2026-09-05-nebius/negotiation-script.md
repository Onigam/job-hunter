# Negotiation Script: Manager, ML Solutions Architecture (Token Factory), Nebius

Context going in: this is a people-manager role over 8 Solutions Architects with growth planned, reporting to the Head of Solutions Architecture, at a Nasdaq-listed company (NBIS) with 1,500+ employees. The posting is explicit that it's remote from any EU country, not Switzerland-specific, which changes the negotiation math. Nebius will most likely benchmark against an EU-wide or Amsterdam-anchored band rather than a Geneva premium, so the first job is finding out which band applies before anchoring a number.

## Market context

- Manager-level roles over customer-facing technical teams (Solutions Architecture, Sales Engineering, Professional Services) at cloud and AI infrastructure companies typically land in the CHF 170,000-210,000 base equivalent when benchmarked to the Geneva-Zurich corridor, but the same scope benchmarked to an EU-wide remote band (Amsterdam, Berlin, Paris) often comes in 15-25% lower.
- The ML/LLM inference specialization here, vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM, quantization and routing internals, is a narrower and harder-to-source skill set than generic engineering management, which argues for the upper half of whatever band applies.
- As a Nasdaq-listed company, equity is likely RSUs with a standard four-year vest and a one-year cliff, not the phantom equity or private long-term incentive plans common at PE-backed or pre-IPO firms. That's a meaningfully cleaner instrument to value, since it has a real, tradeable market price.
- Bonus at manager level in this space usually runs 10-20% of base, sometimes tied to delivery metrics like time-to-first-optimized-endpoint given how the role is scoped here.

## Opening position

"I don't see a range listed, so I'd like to start there. Given the scope, managing and growing a team of 8 plus, owning delivery metrics, and reviewing technical work before it reaches customers, I'd be looking at a base in the CHF 175,000-190,000-equivalent range, with RSUs and a bonus structured on top. I'd rather anchor on a real number tied to the responsibilities than guess blind."

## If they come in low

"I'd want to understand which market this was benchmarked against, since a Geneva-based candidate managing an EU-wide team with this level of technical scope sits differently depending on whether the band assumes Amsterdam, a lower-cost EU hub, or Switzerland. If there's a banding constraint tied to my location, I'd rather hear that directly so we can look at where the actual flexibility is, whether that's base, the RSU grant, or bonus target."

Key move: don't accept a lowball as simply "the EU remote rate" without asking what location and level it assumes. The team has 8 Solutions Architects already and is growing, which is real management scope, not a first-time-manager band.

## Equity questions to ask before agreeing to anything

- Grant size in shares versus dollar value at time of offer, and which valuation date they're using, since NBIS trades publicly and the number can be pinned to a specific day.
- Standard four-year vest with a one-year cliff, or a different schedule.
- Whether there's an annual refresh grant built into the leveling, or if the initial grant is a one-time number that assumes attrition from the team.
- How refreshers are typically decided, tied to performance review cycles or a separate process.

## Geographic framing

"I'm in Geneva and open to the fully remote setup described in the posting. I'd want to understand whether the offer benchmarks to a Swiss cost basis or a broader EU-remote band, since that changes the number meaningfully, and I'd rather have that conversation upfront than find out after signing."

## Management scope framing

"I want to be upfront that formal performance-review management is newer ground for me than my founding and cross-team technical work. I don't think that should move the level down, given the hiring, onboarding, and cross-team leadership I've actually done, but I'd rather name it directly than have it surface as a surprise gap later. Where I'd want the comp conversation to focus is the delivery accountability and technical review scope, which maps closely to what I've done at Staff level already."

## Counter-offer script if the number lands mid-band

"That's closer, but managing a team of 8 with growth planned, plus technical review of benchmarking and configuration work before customer delivery, is closer to a senior manager scope than an entry-level one. If base is fixed at this stage, I'd want the difference made up on the RSU grant or bonus target, and I'd want a defined path to the next review point written into the offer rather than left informal."

## If they raise the management-experience gap first

"That's fair to flag, and I'd rather address it than talk around it. What I bring is real: hiring and onboarding a founding engineering team from scratch, and driving adoption of new practices across teams that didn't report to me, without a mandate, just evidence people could act on. I'd ask what specifically about the first 90 days would de-risk that for you, so we can build it into the plan rather than price it as an open question in the offer."
