# Negotiation Script: Staff+ Software Engineer, Platform Ecosystem (Anthropic)

Role is based in San Francisco or New York, hybrid with a 25% in-office minimum, visa sponsorship available. This is a US-based offer, so the primary comp conversation happens in USD against the posted band, but Switzerland market data is useful for deciding where in that band to anchor and for the relocation conversation.

---

## Market Context

**Posted range:** $405,000 - $485,000 USD total annual comp.

That is a roughly 20% spread, which usually signals Anthropic has some room to place a candidate based on scope and track record rather than a fixed level. For a Staff+ IC with founder-level ownership and 8+ years split between building a platform from scratch and operating inside a much larger org post-acquisition, the upper half of that range is the realistic target, not the midpoint.

**Switzerland anchors, for context:**

- Senior Staff IC comp at Benevity (Geneva, current): roughly CHF 180K-220K base, public-sector-adjacent by industry standard.
- Staff Engineer at Google or Meta Zurich: CHF 260K-380K total comp.
- Staff-level ML/platform roles at European AI labs: CHF 220K-320K base.

Converted at a rough CHF/USD parity, the US offer already represents close to a 2x jump over Geneva-market comp for an equivalent level. That is worth naming explicitly: it is not "I need X because Geneva is expensive," it is "the US range is already the stronger anchor, and here is where in that range the scope justifies landing."

**Realistic target:** $455,000-$485,000 total comp, weighted toward equity given Anthropic's growth stage, plus a relocation package covering the Geneva to SF/NYC move.

---

## Opening Anchor

**If asked directly about comp expectations:**

"Based on the posted range and what comparable Staff+ platform roles are paying right now, I'm targeting the upper end, around $470,000 to $485,000 in total comp. I'm flexible on how that splits between base and equity, especially given where Anthropic is in its growth curve."

Why this framing works: it names a number inside the posted band, so it doesn't read as fishing, and it opens the equity conversation without sounding like the base is the only lever.

---

## If They Come In Low ($405K-$435K range)

"I appreciate the offer. I want to be straightforward: that number is below what I was targeting, given the scope of this role and what the top of the posted range implies about it. I'm genuinely interested in the team and the split between the vertical work and the commercial infrastructure side, so I'd rather solve this than let comp be the blocker. Is there room to move closer to the top of the band, or to make up the gap with a sign-on grant?"

**If base is firm:**

"If the base is fixed, can we talk through the equity component in more detail? I'd want to understand the current common share valuation, the vesting schedule, and how refreshes work for people who are performing well at this level."

---

## Equity Notes

Anthropic is private; reported valuation moved sharply higher through 2025, with the most recent rounds putting the company in the neighborhood of $170-185 billion. That scale cuts both ways in a negotiation: the upside story is real, but so is dilution risk on any options tranche priced off an older, lower 409A.

Questions worth asking directly:

1. What's the most recent 409A valuation, and when was it set relative to the last funding round?
2. Is the equity RSUs or options? If options, what's the strike price and the exercise window after departure?
3. What's the standard vesting schedule, four years with a one-year cliff, and is there any flexibility on cliff length for someone leaving a stable role?
4. How does Anthropic handle refresh grants for continuing employees, and is that tied to a review cycle?
5. Is there any secondary liquidity program, or is this purely illiquid until an eventual exit?

**Script:**

"I have unvested equity at my current company that I'd be leaving on the table. A sign-on grant that partially offsets that would make the move a much easier call. What does a typical sign-on package look like at this level?"

---

## Relocation and Visa

This is the part of the deal that's easy to underweight. Moving from Geneva to SF or NYC involves a work visa, temporary housing, moving costs, and a spouse or partner's job search if applicable.

**Script:**

"Visa sponsorship being available is great to hear. I'd want to understand the timeline for that process, since it affects my start date, and I'd also want a relocation package that covers the move itself and a few months of temporary housing while I find a place. Is that something that's typically negotiated separately from the comp package, or bundled into the offer?"

If the timeline for visa processing is long, it's fair to ask whether a remote-first ramp-up period is possible before the in-office requirement kicks in.

---

## Using a Competing Offer

Even without a live competing process, market data does similar work:

"I've been in conversations with a couple of other platform-focused teams at this level, and the range I'm seeing for Staff+ platform and monetization roles is close to what I'm asking for here. Anthropic is where I'd rather land, but I want the comp to reflect that this is a competitive market for this kind of role right now."

If there is an actual competing offer, name the company and the number directly. Vague references to "other offers" without specifics tend to weaken the ask rather than strengthen it.

---

## What Not to Do

- Don't accept on the spot. "Let me sit with this overnight and get back to you tomorrow" is always a reasonable answer.
- Don't lead with current salary at Benevity as the anchor; anchor to the US market range and the scope of this specific role instead.
- Don't negotiate the equity number without first understanding vesting, strike price, and how the last 409A relates to the most recent funding round.
- Don't treat $405K as the real floor. That's where they'd land a strong hire with less breadth; the scope described in the posting supports asking from higher up the band.
