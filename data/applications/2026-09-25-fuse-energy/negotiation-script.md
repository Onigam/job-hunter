# Negotiation Script: Fuse Energy, Senior/Lead Backend Engineer (Remote)

The posting mentions a competitive salary, equity, a biannual bonus, private health insurance and expensed equipment, but no numbers. Fuse is London-headquartered and has raised over $200M, so expect them to think in UK bands. The ranges below are estimates from public Swiss and London comp data; check levels.fyi, Glassdoor and anyone you know who's interviewed there before the first comp call.

## 1. Market picture (2026 estimates)

| Reference point | Base | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| London scale-up, Senior backend | GBP 85k to 110k | Options common, bonus less so |
| London scale-up, Lead backend | GBP 105k to 135k | Top of range at well-funded companies |
| Swiss scale-up, Senior backend | CHF 130k to 160k | Small equity |
| Swiss scale-up, Lead / Staff | CHF 155k to 190k | |
| Zurich big tech, Senior / Staff | CHF 190k to 250k | Liquid RSUs on top |

At roughly 1.1 CHF to the pound, a strong London Lead offer lands around CHF 115k to 150k. That's below Swiss market for the same level, and the gap is the main thing you'll be negotiating.

**Your targets**
- Walk-away base: CHF 150k
- Target base: CHF 165k to 175k
- Opening anchor: CHF 185k base, leveled as Lead, plus equity

## 2. Level first, then money

The title is "Senior/Lead", which means the level is still open. Settle it before salary, because the band follows the level.

> "Given the scope I've had, building Alaya's platform from zero through to acquisition and five years running engineering there, I'd expect to come in at the Lead level. Can you tell me how you distinguish Senior from Lead in practice, and what the band looks like for Lead?"

## 3. Opening the conversation

If they ask for expectations first:

> "For a Lead backend role based in Switzerland, I'm looking at a base around CHF 185k, plus equity. I'm flexible on the split between base, bonus and options, and I'd like to see the full package before we settle anything."

If they push for one number, repeat 185k and don't offer a range. A range just gives them the bottom of it.

Justification to have ready: 17 years in backend and platform systems, five years as cofounder and technical lead through an acquisition by a $1B+ company, hiring and running the first engineering team, and recent Staff-level cross-team architecture work. You're being hired to own systems and lead, which is more than a pure senior IC scope.

## 4. If the offer comes in low

> "Thanks, I appreciate it and I want to make this work. The base is under what I'd expect for a Lead role in Switzerland. If you can get to CHF 170k, I'm ready to move forward."

If they say the band is fixed:

> "Understood. If base can't move, could we look at a larger option grant, a signing bonus to cover the first-year gap, or a guaranteed review at twelve months tied to what I've delivered?"

Don't accept on the call. "Let me look at the whole package and come back to you by Thursday" is always fine.

## 5. Location pushback (UK bands vs Swiss cost of living)

If they want to pay London rates:

> "I understand the bands are set in London. Living and working in Geneva costs noticeably more, from health insurance, which isn't covered by the state here, to housing and social charges. I'm not asking for Zurich big tech pay, but a London number converted at today's rate is a real cut for me. Somewhere in the middle is fair."

Also ask how they'd employ you. Through an employer of record, a Swiss entity or as a contractor? Contractor rates should be at least 20 to 25% higher than the equivalent salary, since you'd be covering AVS/AHV, pension (LPP/BVG) and accident insurance yourself. Check whether their private health insurance actually applies in Switzerland; if it doesn't, ask for a cash allowance instead.

## 6. Biannual bonus

- What's the target as a percentage of base, and what's the realistic payout history?
- Is it tied to company targets, individual targets or both?
- Is it paid if you join mid-cycle, pro-rated or not at all?

Don't let a bonus with no track record stand in for base salary. Count it at half its stated value when comparing offers.

## 7. Equity questions (private company, ask in writing)

- **Instrument for a Swiss resident.** UK EMI options generally only work for UK-taxed employees, so ask what you'd actually get (unapproved options, a growth share scheme, or something else) and get Swiss tax advice. Switzerland can tax options at grant or at exercise depending on how they're structured, and that matters a lot.
- **Percentage, not a headline value.** Ask for the number of options and the fully diluted share count. A grant quoted as "worth GBP X" means nothing without the strike price and the valuation it's based on.
- **Strike price** and the valuation it's set at. With $200M+ raised, find out when the last round priced and whether a new one is coming, since a strike set before a step-up is worth more.
- **Vesting.** Standard is four years with a one-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff and double-trigger acceleration on change of control.
- **Exercise window after leaving.** 90 days is common and painful; ask for several years.
- **Refresh grants.** Is there a policy, and when do they happen?
- **Liquidation preferences.** Energy is capital-heavy and large rounds often come with preference stacks. Ask whether any investors hold participating or greater-than-1x preferences, as that decides what common options are worth in a modest exit.

## 8. Competing offer template

Only use this if it's true, or once you actually have a live process further along.

> "I want to be open with you: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company and expect an offer shortly, at around CHF [X] base. Fuse is my first choice because of the problem, and I'd rather not let this turn into an auction. If you can get to CHF [Y], I'll close the other process."

## 9. Things worth asking for besides cash

- Travel budget for regular trips to London, paid by the company
- Home office budget in addition to the expensed equipment
- A clear first-year scope in writing (which systems you own, whether you'll lead people), since that's what justifies the Lead level at your next review
