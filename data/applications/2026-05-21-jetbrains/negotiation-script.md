# Negotiation Script — JetBrains Principal Engineer (Platform Architecture)

---

## Market Context

JetBrains is a Czech-headquartered private company (no public equity). The role is remote-eligible with office options in Amsterdam, Berlin, Munich, and Prague. Compensation benchmarks vary significantly by location anchor:

| Market anchor | Principal/Staff+ IC range |
|---|---|
| Geneva, Switzerland (your base) | CHF 185k–240k total comp |
| Amsterdam, Netherlands | EUR 160k–210k total comp |
| Berlin / Munich, Germany | EUR 140k–185k total comp |
| Prague, Czech Republic | EUR 90k–130k total comp |

JetBrains is known to pay above Czech market but below FAANG. For a remote-from-Geneva candidate they will likely anchor to the role's office location (Amsterdam/Munich) or to internal bands. Your goal is to anchor to Switzerland market rates, not Prague rates.

---

## Your Opening Anchor

When asked about expectations, lead with a number, not a range.

> "Based on the scope of this role and where I'm based, I'd be looking at CHF 200,000 base. I'm open to understanding how JetBrains structures comp for remote-from-Geneva candidates, since I know you have multiple office locations for this role."

Why CHF 200k: it's at the midpoint of the Geneva Principal Engineer market (Benevity, Temenos, EPFL-spinouts, large fintech). It's ambitious but grounded. It opens the negotiation above where they'll probably land, which gives you room.

---

## If They Come In Low (CHF 150k or below)

> "That's a meaningful gap from where I'd need to be. I want to understand the structure better before deciding if we have a path. Is there flexibility on the base, or is the band for this role fixed? And how does variable comp or a sign-on factor in?"

If they're firm on a lower base:

> "I appreciate the transparency. The range I mentioned is what the Geneva market looks like for this level. I'd want to think through whether there's a way to close that gap, whether through base, a sign-on that gets me through year one, or some other structure. Can you give me a week?"

Do not accept on the call. Always take 48–72 hours minimum.

---

## Equity (JetBrains is Private)

JetBrains does not have traditional startup equity (it's profitable and founder-owned). Do not expect stock options or RSUs. If they mention any profit-sharing or employee ownership program, ask specifically:

- What is the structure? (profit-share, phantom equity, other)
- What is the vesting or payout schedule?
- Is it discretionary or formula-based?
- What was the actual payout in the last two years?

If there's no equity component, treat the base as the total cash offer and anchor accordingly. A private, stable company with no equity should pay at the top of the base range for the level.

> "Since this is a private company without traditional equity, I'd expect the base to reflect the full comp picture. That's why I'm anchoring at CHF 200k rather than a lower base with upside."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

JetBrains may raise that you're remote from Geneva, not based in one of the role's office cities. Pre-empt this:

> "I'm aware Geneva is one of the more expensive places to be based in Europe. I'm not asking for a Geneva-specific premium, I'm asking for the going rate for a Principal Engineer in Western Europe. Amsterdam and Munich are the reference points I'm using, not Zurich banking comps."

If they push toward Prague rates:

> "I understand the internal bands may be structured by office location, but I'd be in a different tax and cost-of-living situation working from Geneva. I'd want to find a number that works for both of us, and that's going to be closer to the Amsterdam band than the Prague one."

---

## Competing Offer Template

If you have another offer (or are in late-stage with another company), use it:

> "I want to be straightforward with you: I'm at offer stage with another company at CHF [X]. It's a different type of role, and JetBrains is where I'd rather be for this particular work. But I need to know if you can get to CHF [your target] before I can make a decision."

If you don't have another offer, don't fabricate one. Instead:

> "I'm talking to a few companies at a similar level. I haven't set a deadline for myself, but I'm trying to make a decision within the next two to three weeks."

---

## Benefits to Negotiate If Base Is Stuck

If the base is genuinely fixed, negotiate the following (JetBrains has mentioned some of these in job postings):

- **Sign-on bonus**: Ask for CHF 20k–30k one-time to bridge year-one gap
- **Remote work allowance**: Home office setup, internet, equipment
- **Conference and training budget**: Named, not "we'll see"
- **International remote days**: They mention 30 days; confirm this in writing
- **Review cycle**: Request a 6-month salary review clause in writing if starting below target

---

## Closing the Conversation

Never accept on the call. Always:

1. Thank them for the offer
2. Ask for it in writing (email or document)
3. Give a clear timeline: "I'll get back to you by [specific date, 3–5 business days out]"
4. Use that time to evaluate and, if needed, come back with a counter

> "I appreciate the offer. Could you send it over in writing? I'll review it carefully and come back to you by [date]."
