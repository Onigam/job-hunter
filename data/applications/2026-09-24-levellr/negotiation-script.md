# Negotiation Script: Principal Applied AI Engineer @ Levellr (remote, UK hours)

## Context
- Levellr Ltd, London. CEO Tom Gayner, CTO Ben Barbersmith (ex-YouTube). Community intelligence for Discord and Reddit; customers include Epic Games, Krafton, Scopely, Google and YouTube. Processes 6B+ events a month.
- Funding: $1.75M (Nov 2024) and a $2.5M seed led by Fuel Ventures (Jan 2026), with games angels such as Mark Pincus (Workplay Ventures), Bing Gordon and Frank Gibeau. Roughly $4-5M raised in total, about 34 people across the US and Europe. **That's a lean cash position for a team that size**, so expect pressure on base and more room on equity. Ask about runway and Series A timing.
- Role: Principal IC, fully remote, "European Union" location, meaningful overlap with UK hours. Salary is "highly competitive" but only disclosed at first interview. 93 applicants on day one.
- **The key issue:** Switzerland is not in the EU. Before talking numbers, find out how they'd employ you: a UK contract, an Employer of Record (Deel, Remote, Oyster) in Switzerland, or a contractor agreement. This changes your net more than a £10k difference in base.

## Your target

- **Walk-away:** £130k base (or CHF equivalent via EOR), at least 0.3% of fully diluted in options, standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff, and an employment setup that gives you Swiss social security and pension coverage. If it's a contractor deal, the floor is ~£750/day, because you'd be paying all of AVS/AHV and pension yourself.
- **Target:** £145k to 150k base, 0.5% to 0.75% options, a written salary review at Series A, and a home office/co-working budget.
- **Opening anchor:** £160k base (about CHF 170k) and 1% options. Justify it with 17 years of experience, a platform built from zero to acquisition by a unicorn, hands-on Postgres at production scale, LLM integration work, and a Principal scope that sets AI architecture for the whole company.

## Script: when they ask for your expectations

"I'm coming from a Geneva senior staff package, so I'm pricing in CHF even if you pay in pounds. For a principal role that owns the AI and data architecture, I'm thinking around £160k base, or the CHF equivalent, with meaningful equity, around 1%. I know you're post-seed and I'm flexible on how cash and equity balance out. Could you share the band you've set, and how you'd structure employment for someone in Switzerland?"

Then stop and let them answer.

## Counter-offer script: if they come in low

"Thanks, I appreciate it. That number sits closer to a senior band in London, and the scope we've talked about is principal: agents, evals, anomaly detection and the Postgres and ClickHouse design underneath all of it. I understand cash is tight after a seed. If base stays at £[their number], I'd need the equity to carry more of it, something like [X]%, plus a written review when the Series A closes. Can we make that work?"

If base won't move: "Fair enough. Then let's look at the other levers: option percentage, the post-leaving exercise window, the review date and the employment setup. I'd rather find a package that works than haggle over one figure."

## Equity notes (UK startup, Swiss resident)

1. **EMI options won't apply to you.** EMI is the tax-efficient UK scheme, but it only covers UK employees. You'd get unapproved options or, possibly, a growth-shares or phantom arrangement. Ask which.
2. **Percentage of fully diluted capital** and the strike price. A number of options means nothing without the total share count.
3. **Vesting:** 4 years, 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for double-trigger acceleration on acquisition. Given that they're a likely acquisition target, this matters.
4. **Exercise window after leaving:** UK default is often 90 days. Push for 5 to 10 years, or at least 2. This is the clause that most often makes startup equity worthless.
5. **Leaver terms:** make sure vested options stay yours as a good leaver.
6. **Dilution:** ask about the pool size and whether a top-up is planned at Series A.
7. **Swiss tax:** employee options are generally taxed as income in Switzerland, and the timing (grant, vesting or exercise) depends on the plan and your canton. Geneva's tax administration can issue a ruling. Get a Swiss tax adviser to look at the plan before you sign.

## Employment setup (your biggest non-salary lever)

In order of preference:
1. **EOR in Switzerland** (Deel, Remote, Oyster): Swiss contract, AVS/AHV and LPP pension, Swiss holidays. Costs them ~15-20% on top of salary. If they push back on the cost, trade a little base for it rather than giving it up.
2. **UK employment with you as a remote worker in Switzerland:** usually messy for social security and tax. Avoid unless they already have a structure for it.
3. **Contractor via your own Swiss company or as self-employed:** workable, but price it properly. You carry social charges (~10% AVS as self-employed), pension, holidays and the gap between contracts. Day rate, not their salary divided by 220.

## Geographic pushback

They may say "it's remote, we pay the same across Europe" or propose an EU rate. Your answer:

"I get the logic of a single band. But I'm in Geneva, which is one of the most expensive places in Europe, and the band for EU remote roles is usually anchored on markets like Portugal or Poland. I'm not asking for a Zurich big-tech salary. I'm asking for a principal band at a London company, which is where you compete for talent anyway."

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

"I want to be upfront: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company for a staff/principal role at around CHF [X]. Levellr is my first choice because of the problem, turning community conversations into signals studios can act on, and the chance to set the AI architecture early. Is there flexibility to get closer to [target]? If so, I'm ready to commit."

Never invent a competing offer. If you don't have one, say instead: "I'm talking to a few other companies and expect to decide in the next couple of weeks."

## Questions to ask before signing

- What's the runway, and when do you expect to raise the Series A?
- How are LLM costs budgeted today, and who owns that number?
- What does success look like for this role after 6 and 12 months?
- Will I have direct reports, or is it a pure IC principal track?
- How much of the week overlaps with UK hours in practice, and are there in-person meetups in London?
