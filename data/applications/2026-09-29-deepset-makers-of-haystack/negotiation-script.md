# Negotiation Script: deepset, Product Engineering Tech Lead

Posted location: Berlin, remote-first with flexible hours. Candidate based in Geneva.
Posted comp: "competitive salary with stock options", no figure given. deepset was founded in 2018 and is VC-backed (GV, Balderton). Check the latest funding round and headcount before the call.

---

## 1. Read the situation first

**Where the contract sits.** deepset is a German company. Remote-first doesn't automatically mean they'll hire in Switzerland. Ask early whether they employ outside Germany, and if so how: a Swiss employer of record, a contractor agreement, or a German contract with you relocating. This decides whether the conversation happens in EUR against Berlin bands or in CHF against Swiss ones.

**Level.** The role is a management position with platform ownership, on-call and incident response, and hands-on work on critical problems. That's staff or lead scope. Anchor there, not to a senior engineer band.

**What "stock options" means at a German startup.** Usually a VSOP (virtual stock option plan), not real shares. Virtual options typically pay out only at an exit, and leaver terms decide whether you keep anything if you leave before then. Treat the equity as uncertain upside and negotiate base as if it were the whole package.

## 2. Market anchors (base, 2026, working estimates)

Re-check against Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, the German startup comp reports (e.g. Index Ventures' OptionPlan, Ravio) and recruiter feedback before the call.

| Market | Senior engineer | Tech lead / Staff | Eng lead at Series B/C AI startup |
|---|---|---|---|
| Berlin, EUR | 75k to 95k | 95k to 125k | 110k to 140k + VSOP |
| Geneva / Zürich, CHF | 140k to 170k | 170k to 210k | 175k to 215k + equity |

Berlin pays noticeably less than Munich, and far less than Geneva. A Swiss contract at Berlin rates would be a real-terms cut of roughly 35 to 40% for Geneva living costs.

**Targets**

- Swiss contract (EOR or contractor): walk-away CHF 170k, target CHF 185k to 195k, open at CHF 205k. On a contractor setup, add about 20% to cover social charges, pension and holiday.
- German contract with relocation to Berlin: walk-away EUR 115k, target EUR 125k to 135k, open at EUR 145k.
- On top of either: a meaningful VSOP grant with good leaver terms, plus an on-call allowance if the team runs a rotation.

## 3. Opening the conversation

If the recruiter asks for expectations early:

> "Before a number, can I check how you'd employ someone in Switzerland? I'm in Geneva, and a Swiss contract versus a German one changes the answer a lot. For a tech lead role that owns the execution platform, on-call included, I'm targeting a base around CHF 205k on a Swiss contract. If it's a German contract with me in Berlin, I'd be around EUR 145k. I'd also like to understand the option plan, since that affects how I weigh base against upside."

If they push for one number:

> "If you need a single figure, CHF 195k base on a Swiss contract, before equity. I'm flexible on how we get there if the rest of the package is strong."

## 4. If they come in low

**Offer on Berlin bands for a Swiss-based hire (e.g. EUR 115k equivalent):**

> "Thanks, I appreciate it. The trouble is that at Geneva costs, that's around a third below what the same role pays locally, so I'd be taking a real cut to do it. You're paying for someone who has built a platform from zero, run it through an acquisition and been responsible for what broke. Could we look at CHF 190k? If the base band can't move, I'd want to close the gap through a larger option grant and a signing bonus."

**Pushback on remote or location discount:**

> "I understand wanting consistency across the team. The posting says remote-first, and the work doesn't change with where I sit. I'm on the same timezone as Berlin and happy to be there regularly. Location pay makes sense when it follows the cost of the location, and in Geneva that points up, not down."

**If they say the band is fixed:**

> "Understood. Then let's look at the other levers: the level itself, the size of the grant, a signing bonus, the learning budget, and a salary review at six months tied to agreed platform milestones, say reliability targets for the execution layer."

## 5. Equity (VSOP) checklist

Ask for the plan document in writing before accepting. Things to check:

- **Grant size**: as a percentage of fully diluted shares, not just a number of options. For a lead hire at Series B/C, something in the range of 0.05% to 0.2% is a reasonable starting point; ask what the band is for this level.
- **Strike price and valuation**: which round valuation is the strike based on?
- **Vesting**: standard is four years with a one-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Leaver terms**: the most important clause. Good leaver should keep vested options. Watch for plans where vested virtual options lapse on resignation, or where "bad leaver" is defined broadly.
- **Payout events**: exit only, or also secondaries or tender offers? Is there a cap on payout?
- **Acceleration**: ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination).
- **Dilution**: VSOPs usually have no anti-dilution protection. Ask how the pool has changed over past rounds and whether there are top-up grants.
- **Tax**: for a Swiss resident, virtual option payouts are generally taxed as employment income at payout. Check with a Geneva tax advisor before comparing against a cash-heavy offer.

## 6. Competing offer template (use only if real)

> "I want to be upfront: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company, a role at a similar level with a base around [X]. deepset is my first choice because of the execution platform work and Haystack's open source base, and I'd rather make this work. Is there room to get closer to [target]?"

Don't invent an offer. If there isn't one, lean on market data and the Geneva cost argument instead.

## 7. Before signing

- Contract entity, currency and employment type confirmed in writing
- On-call rotation, compensation and expected frequency
- Travel budget for Berlin visits if you're remote
- Full VSOP plan document, not just the offer letter summary
- Notice period and non-compete scope (German non-competes require compensation to be enforceable)
- 30 days vacation as posted; confirm how that maps onto a Swiss contract
