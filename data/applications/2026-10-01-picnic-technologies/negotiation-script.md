# Negotiation Script: Picnic Technologies, Staff AI Engineer (Agentic Shopping)

## Context that shapes the numbers

The role is based in **Amsterdam**, not Switzerland. Picnic offers relocation (flight, first month's rent, moving costs up to €2,000, help with the Dutch 30% ruling), which strongly suggests on-site or hybrid in Amsterdam, not remote from Geneva. Ask about this in the first call, because it changes everything below.

Swiss anchors are included as requested, but they work as a reference point for "what I'm giving up," not as Picnic's band. Dutch tech salaries are well below Geneva's in gross terms. Rent and taxes are also different, so compare net and cost of living, not headline gross.

Things to confirm early:

- On-site, hybrid or remote? If remote from Geneva is on the table at all, it changes the frame completely (see the last section).
- Level. The title says Staff. Check where it sits in Picnic's ladder and whether there's anything above it on the IC track.
- Equity. Picnic is a private, heavily funded scale-up (large rounds in recent years from investors including the Gates Foundation's trust, Edeka and the EIB). Ask directly whether there's an employee share or option plan for this level, how it's structured (Dutch companies often use depositary receipts through a STAK foundation), and what the strike price and last valuation are. Don't assume equity is part of the offer.
- Bonus: is there one, and is it guaranteed or discretionary?

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough figures from public surveys and recruiter data. Check levels.fyi (filter Netherlands and Switzerland, and Picnic specifically), Glassdoor, the Hays and Michael Page guides and swissdevjobs.ch before the first conversation.

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Senior Software Engineer, Amsterdam scale-up | €80k to 100k |
| Staff Engineer, Amsterdam scale-up (Picnic, Adyen, Mollie tier) | €110k to 140k |
| Staff AI / ML Engineer with production LLM work, Amsterdam | €120k to 155k |
| Big tech Staff, Amsterdam (Booking, Uber), base only | €140k to 175k |
| Staff Engineer, Geneva | CHF 180k to 230k |
| Senior AI / ML Engineer with production LLM work, Switzerland | CHF 160k to 200k |
| Staff AI Engineer, Zurich, big tech base | CHF 200k to 250k |

**Target:** €140k to 150k base, plus equity if a plan exists.
**Opening anchor:** €160k base.
**Walk-away floor:** €125k base with the 30% ruling confirmed and a real equity grant. Below that, the move from Geneva doesn't make financial sense.

Why anchor at the top of the Amsterdam band: production agent experience is scarce, the role is explicitly staff-level and hands-on with architecture ownership, and Magino would be relocating from a market that pays roughly 40% more in gross terms.

## The 30% ruling

This matters as much as base salary. Under the Dutch expat ruling, part of gross pay can be paid tax-free for up to five years, which noticeably raises net pay. Magino almost certainly qualifies on distance, since Geneva is well over 150 km from the Dutch border. The rules are changing (the tax-free share is scheduled to drop from 30% to 27% in 2027, and there's a minimum salary threshold that rises each year), so check the current figures before the call.

Script: "Can you confirm Picnic will file for the 30% ruling and that the offer is structured so I clear the salary threshold with room to spare? I'd also like to know whether you quote gross with the ruling applied or before it."

## Leveling first

Bring this up in the recruiter screen, before any number:

"Before compensation, I'd like to understand the level. My last role was Senior Staff, and this one owns architecture and model strategy for the assistant and mentors engineers across the org. Where does Staff sit in your ladder, and what's above it on the IC side?"

## When they ask for expectations first

"I'm relocating from Geneva, where staff-level pay is in the CHF 180k to 230k range, so I'm aware Amsterdam is a different market. For this scope I'm looking at around €160k base, with the 30% ruling and equity on top. I'm flexible on the mix if the overall package is right."

## Counter-offer if they come in low

If the offer comes in around €115k to 125k:

"Thanks, I can tell there's real interest and the role is the one I want. The base is lower than I expected for staff scope in agentic AI, especially as I'd be leaving a market where the same level pays considerably more. Could we get the base to €145k? If the band really can't stretch, I'd look at a sign-on bonus to cover the move, a larger equity grant, or a defined review at six months tied to shipping the assistant."

If they say the band is fixed:

"Understood. Then let's work on the parts around it: a sign-on bonus of around €15k to 20k to bridge the first year, relocation support above the €2,000 cap since moving a household from Geneva costs more than that, and a written six-month review."

## Equity

Only relevant if a plan exists. Ask:

- What instrument (options, depositary receipts, phantom shares)?
- Vesting schedule and cliff. Four years with a one-year cliff is standard; push back on anything longer.
- Strike price and the valuation it's based on.
- What happens on a secondary sale, acquisition or IPO? Is there acceleration on a change of control?
- How long you have to exercise after leaving. Ninety days is common and harsh; ask for longer.
- Liquidation preferences on recent rounds, since they decide what common holders actually get in an exit. Picnic has raised a lot of capital, so this matters.
- Dutch tax treatment: when the taxable event happens (grant, vesting or exercise).

Script: "Equity matters to me here. I've been through an acquisition as a founder, so I know how much the terms matter next to the number of units. Could you send the plan documents so I can see vesting, exercise window and preferences?"

## Geographic pushback

Usually you'd push back on a geographic discount, but here the logic runs the other way: Magino would be moving to a cheaper, lower-paying market. So don't argue that Amsterdam should pay Geneva rates, because it won't land. Use the Swiss numbers to explain why the top of the Amsterdam band is reasonable:

"I'm not expecting Geneva pay in Amsterdam. I'm showing you where I'm coming from so the top of your band makes sense. Relocating a household is a real cost and a real commitment, and I'd want the package to reflect that."

If remote from Geneva is possible, flip it: "If I'm working from Geneva, I'd expect to be paid at Swiss rates, since that's where I live and pay tax. That would put base at around CHF 190k to 210k. Employing me in Switzerland would also mean a local entity or employer of record so pension (LPP/BVG) and social contributions are handled properly."

## Competing offer template

Use only if there's a real offer or an active late-stage process. Never invent one.

"I want to be open with you: I'm in final stages with another company for a staff role in AI agents, and their package is around [X]. Picnic is my first choice because of the problem; the grocery basket is a better test of agents than anything I've seen. If you can get closer to [Y], I'm ready to sign this week."

## Before any call

- Recheck Picnic on levels.fyi and Glassdoor for Amsterdam staff data.
- Check the current 30% ruling threshold and rate.
- Compare a net-pay calculation for Amsterdam (with the ruling) against current Geneva net, including rent.
- Decide the walk-away number in advance and don't move it during the call.
