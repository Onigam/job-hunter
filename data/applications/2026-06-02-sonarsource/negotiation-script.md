# Negotiation Script — Principal Platform Engineer, SonarSource

---

## Market Context

SonarSource is headquartered in Geneva, profitable, with reported ARR well above $300M and a mature product used by millions of developers. They're not a pre-revenue startup. Principal Platform Engineer is a senior IC role typically one level above Staff; in the Swiss market this sits at a higher band than standard "Senior" compensation.

**Geneva market range for this level (2026):**
- Senior Staff / Principal Engineer at a product company: CHF 185,000 to CHF 230,000 base
- SonarSource tends to be competitive but not at the absolute top of the Geneva market (they're not a FAANG equivalent)
- Target: CHF 205,000 to CHF 220,000 base
- Floor: CHF 190,000 (below this, push back hard or decline)

Benefits to factor in: SonarSource typically offers equity (RSUs or options), health insurance, learning budget, home office setup. Ask about all of these before finalizing any number.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask about expectations, lead with the top of range and justify it:

> "Based on what I've seen for Principal-level roles in the Geneva market, and given my background building and scaling platforms from zero through acquisition, I'm targeting around CHF 220,000 base. I'm open to the full package discussion once we've talked through scope."

Do not apologize for the number. Do not immediately offer flexibility. Let them respond first.

---

## If They Come In Low (below CHF 195,000)

> "I appreciate the offer. I want to be straightforward with you: that's below what I was targeting for this level, and below what comparable roles in Geneva are currently paying for Principal engineers. I'm genuinely interested in the role, so I'd like to understand if there's room to move on the base before we talk about the rest of the package."

Then be quiet. Let them talk. If they push back with a fixed budget:

> "If CHF [X] is the ceiling on base, I'd want to understand how the equity component is structured and what the total cash looks like with bonus. That might close the gap."

---

## Counter-Offer Script

If they offer CHF 195,000-205,000:

> "I'd want to come up to CHF 215,000. I've been in conversations with a couple of other companies and that range is consistent with what I'm seeing elsewhere for this level. SonarSource is my preference based on the scope of this role, but I want to make sure the number reflects that."

Use the competing-offer lever even if you only have exploratory conversations elsewhere. You don't need a signed offer to mention market interest.

---

## Geneva Cost of Living Pushback

If they try to justify a lower number with remote-work arguments or by comparing to non-Swiss markets:

> "The role is based in Geneva, which is one of the most expensive cities in Europe. CHF costs, CHF rent, CHF taxes. I wouldn't be comparing this to a Berlin or Paris base even if the role had some remote flexibility."

---

## Equity Notes

SonarSource has been exploring pre-IPO equity programs. Ask specifically:

- Are there RSUs or options for this level?
- What's the vesting schedule (standard is 4 years, 1-year cliff)?
- What's the current valuation or strike price basis for options?
- Is there any acceleration on acquisition or change of control?

If equity is offered, don't count it at face value. Treat it as upside, not base compensation. Negotiate the base independently.

If they offer meaningful equity (>CHF 50,000 in total value at current valuation), you can be slightly more flexible on base:

> "If the equity package is structured the way you're describing, I can work with CHF 205,000 on base. I'd want to see the grant details in writing before we finalize anything."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage Template

> "I've been talking to [company type, e.g., 'a developer tooling company based in Zurich' / 'a series B platform company'] and they're at a comparable level. SonarSource is the role I'm most interested in based on scope and the team, so I'd rather close this here than drag it out."

Use this once, credibly, and don't bluff about a specific number unless you have one.

---

## Non-Negotiables to Check Before Signing

- Notice period: What's expected? (Swiss standard is 1-3 months at this level)
- Trial period clause: Standard 3-6 months in Swiss contracts; negotiate if it's longer than 3 months for a Principal role
- Non-compete scope: Check for overly broad clauses, especially if they try to restrict future employment in developer tooling
- Learning and conference budget: Ask for CHF 3,000-5,000/year explicitly if it's not in the offer
- Home office stipend: CHF 1,000-2,000 one-time for setup is reasonable to ask for

---

## Closing Move

Once you're close on numbers:

> "I think we're aligned. I'd like to have the written offer so I can review the full details, and I'd plan to come back to you within [2-3 business days]. I'm not planning to shop this around."

That closes it without pressure and gives you time to read the contract properly.
