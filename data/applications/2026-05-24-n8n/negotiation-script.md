# Negotiation Script — n8n AI Engineering Manager

---

## Market Context

**Role level:** First-line Engineering Manager, AI-focused, Europe remote  
**Company stage:** Series B (~$55M raised as of 2023), Berlin HQ, ~200 employees  
**Currency note:** n8n is a German company, likely paying in EUR. Geneva cost-of-living is significantly higher than Berlin; push for CHF parity or explicit geographic premium.

**Switzerland / Geneva market anchors (2026):**
- Senior Software Engineer (Staff level): 140,000–180,000 CHF
- Engineering Manager (product teams): 160,000–210,000 CHF
- AI-focused EM or Tech Lead at Series B: 160,000–200,000 CHF
- Approximate EUR to CHF parity: 1 EUR ≈ 0.94 CHF (negligible gap, but Geneva cost-of-living is ~30% above Berlin)

**Target range:** 160,000–175,000 CHF total cash (base + bonus if applicable)  
**Minimum acceptable:** 140,000 CHF base, no exceptions below without significant equity offset  
**Walk-away floor:** Under 130,000 CHF base with no meaningful equity

---

## Opening Anchor

Use this when they ask for your range first:

> "For an Engineering Manager role in the Geneva market with my background, I'm targeting 160,000 to 175,000 CHF in base compensation. That's benchmarked against what comparable roles at Series B companies pay for someone with a founding-engineer background and five-plus years building AI-adjacent systems. I'm flexible on structure if there's meaningful equity involved."

**Why this works:** It's high but defensible. You anchor to Geneva market (not Berlin) and your specific background (founding engineer, not just a senior IC who got promoted).

---

## If They Come In Low (Under 140k CHF or equivalent)

**Script A — Push back with market data:**
> "I appreciate the offer. The base is lower than what I'm seeing for comparable roles in Switzerland, where Engineering Manager positions at funded startups are typically 150,000 CHF and above. I'd need to see that gap closed to move forward. Is there room to revisit the base, or should we talk about how equity could make up the difference?"

**Script B — If they cite "remote discount" or "Berlin rates":**
> "I understand the company benchmarks off Berlin, but my cost base is Geneva, which runs about 30% higher. I'm not asking you to pay Zurich consulting rates, but I do need the compensation to reflect where I actually live. The remote flexibility is valuable to me, but not at 40,000 CHF per year in lost purchasing power."

**Script C — If they're firm on base but offer flexibility elsewhere:**
> "If the base is a hard ceiling, let's talk about the equity tranche and vesting structure. A meaningful grant with a reasonable cliff would help bridge the gap. What's the current option pool, and what percentage are you offering at this level?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

**n8n is open source but commercially funded (Series B). Equity structure is likely options (EMI in the UK, or phantom shares / virtual options for EU employees).**

**What to ask for:**
- Target: 0.10% to 0.25% of the company on a fully diluted basis
- Vesting: 4 years, 1-year cliff (standard) — push back on anything longer than 4 years
- Acceleration: Ask for single-trigger acceleration on 25% if acquired (especially relevant given n8n's likely acquisition trajectory)
- Strike price: Ask for the most recent 409A or equivalent fair market value; confirm it's set at grant date, not exercise date

**Script:**
> "On equity: I'd want to understand the current strike price, the fully diluted cap table, and what percentage the grant represents. For a role at this level I'd expect something in the 0.1–0.25% range. I'd also want to discuss whether there's any acceleration clause on acquisition — given where n8n is in its trajectory, that's a real scenario worth planning for."

---

## Geographic Premium Pushback

If they ask why you should earn more than a Berlin-based EM:

> "Geneva has among the highest costs of living in Europe, above Zurich by some metrics. Health insurance alone runs 500–600 CHF per month for an individual here, compared to the German public system. I'm not asking for a hardship premium, I'm asking for the compensation to reflect the local market for this role level. The benchmark I'm using is Geneva-based EM roles at tech companies, not Berlin."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in play (or want to create urgency):

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations with one other company, and I'd expect to have something concrete from them within two weeks. I'm more interested in n8n for the product and the AI direction specifically, but I'd want to get to a number that lets me make a clear decision without feeling like I'm leaving significant money on the table. Is there anything you can do on the base or equity to close this out?"

**Note:** Only use this if true or if you have a realistic competing timeline. Don't fabricate an offer, but a competing interview process is enough to create honest urgency.

---

## Benefits to Negotiate Beyond Cash

- Home office setup budget (ask for 2,000–3,000 CHF one-time)
- Learning budget (5,000 CHF/year is common at this level)
- Pension contribution above legal minimum (BVG in Switzerland is mandatory, but top-up contributions are negotiable)
- Clarity on working hours vs. Swiss 42-hour norm
- Confirm that Swiss employment law applies (not German) for social security purposes

---

## Final Notes

n8n's likely target band for this role is 110,000–140,000 EUR based on Berlin market rates. Your job is to shift the framing to the Geneva market and your specific seniority. The founding engineer angle (Alaya acquisition) and the AI tooling track record are your strongest leverage points. Use them early.

Do not accept below 140,000 CHF base without a written equity offer that meaningfully closes the gap.
