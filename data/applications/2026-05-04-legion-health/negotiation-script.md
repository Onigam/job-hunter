# Negotiation Script — Legion Health, Founding Engineer

---

## Context

Legion Health is a YC-backed (S21) early-stage startup with $3.3M ARR and $7M raised. This is a founding engineer role. Compensation will likely include base salary plus meaningful equity. They are SF-based; the role may be remote-eligible.

Magino is Geneva-based. This creates two distinct scenarios to prepare for.

---

## Scenario A: Remote from Switzerland (preferred)

### Market anchor — Geneva/Switzerland

For a Senior Staff / Founding Engineer profile with 17+ years of experience in Geneva:

- **Market floor**: CHF 155,000 base
- **Market midpoint**: CHF 175,000-190,000 base
- **Strong anchor**: CHF 200,000 base + equity

At Benevity (Senior Staff, post-acquisition), Geneva-market comp sits at the CHF 170,000-185,000 range for a comparable IC level.

### Opening position

"For a founding engineer role with this scope, I'm anchoring at CHF 200,000 base. That's consistent with what Staff+ engineers with my background are earning in the Geneva market right now, and reflects the fact that I'd be the person building the system architecture from the ground up."

### If they push back on CHF and offer USD

If they quote USD for a remote-from-Switzerland role, push back on the geographic arbitrage framing:

"I live and pay taxes in Geneva, which is one of the more expensive cities in Europe. I'm not able to accept a discount on that basis. If the comp is USD, I'd need it to be in a range that accounts for local cost of living and Swiss tax rates, which means something north of $180,000 USD base."

---

## Scenario B: US-based remote or relocation

### Market anchor — US

For a founding engineer at a well-funded YC startup in the SF market:

- **Market floor**: $175,000 base
- **Market midpoint**: $190,000-210,000 base
- **Strong anchor**: $220,000-240,000 base + equity

Sources: Levels.fyi for Staff/Principal engineers at Series A startups, YC job market data, work-at-a-startup postings for comparable roles.

### Opening position

"For a founding engineer role where I'm owning the full backend architecture, I'm anchoring at $220,000 base. That's consistent with what engineers at this level and scope are earning at well-funded YC companies. I'm open to structuring the equity component more heavily if needed."

---

## Equity Negotiation

### What to expect at this stage

Legion Health: $7M raised, YC-backed, $3.3M ARR. A founding engineer at this point should expect meaningful early equity, not a standard employee grant.

**Target range**: 0.75% to 1.5% fully diluted at grant
**Minimum acceptable**: 0.5% fully diluted

Standard vesting: 4 years, 1-year cliff. Do not accept less favorable terms.

### Ask about these explicitly

- Current fully diluted share count (so you can calculate the dollar value of the grant)
- Last 409A valuation date and price per share
- Whether the company has a standard 90-day post-termination exercise window or an extended window (important: a short window can make options worthless if you ever leave)
- Acceleration provisions: double-trigger on acquisition? Single-trigger for involuntary termination?
- Anti-dilution protections in the option agreement

### Equity script

"Before we finalize, I want to understand the equity structure. What's the current fully diluted share count, and when was the last 409A? I want to make sure I understand what this represents in real terms. I'd also want to confirm the exercise window and whether there's any acceleration on acquisition."

### If the equity offer is low

"The equity is important to me in a founding engineer role, especially given that I'd be making architectural decisions that compound over time. If the base is at the lower end of my range, I'd want the equity to reflect the founding-level risk and contribution. Can we look at getting to 1% or higher?"

---

## Counter-Offer Script (Low First Offer)

If they come in materially below anchor:

"Thanks for putting that together. I want to be straightforward: the base is below where I'd need to land. I've been comparing this against what I know of the Geneva market and what comparable YC-stage companies are offering right now, and there's a meaningful gap. I'd need to get to [CHF 185,000 / $210,000] base to move forward. Is there room to work toward that, or should we talk about how the equity might compensate for a lower base?"

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or are in process), use it:

"I want to be transparent with you. I'm in late stages with another company and expect to have a decision by [date]. I'm genuinely more interested in what you're building at Legion, but I need the comp to be competitive enough that I can make that choice clearly. Can you confirm the offer by [date]?"

If you don't have a competing offer, do not fabricate one. Instead:

"I've had inbound interest from a few other companies at the staff-level, and I'm being selective about where I land. Legion is at the top of my list based on the problem and the stage, but I do need the compensation to be competitive."

---

## What Not to Do

- Don't give a number first if you can avoid it. If asked, say "I want to understand the full package before naming a number, including equity and benefits. What's the range you have budgeted?"
- Don't accept verbal offers. Get everything in writing before you stop negotiating.
- Don't negotiate against yourself. State your number, then be quiet.
- Don't apologize for negotiating. It's expected, especially at founding-engineer level.

---

## Notes on Healthcare / HIPAA Role Premium

You're being asked to build HIPAA-compliant infrastructure in a regulated domain. That's a premium skill set. If the role specifically requires healthcare compliance experience or clinical data handling, that's worth calling out:

"I've built GDPR-compliant multi-tenant data pipelines with strict isolation guarantees. That kind of compliance-first architecture design is directly applicable to what you need for HIPAA. It's a specialization, and I'd factor that into the comp discussion."
