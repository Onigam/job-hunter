# Negotiation Script: Pond AI, Head of Engineering (founding-level)

**Context.** Onsite in San Francisco, early stage, Tier-1 VC backed, no comp in the posting. Magino is Geneva-based, so this is a relocation conversation as much as a salary one. Figures below are market estimates from public comp data (Levels.fyi, Carta/Pave startup benchmarks, Swiss salary surveys), not verified quotes. Treat them as ranges to sanity-check before the call.

---

## 1. Market anchors

### Switzerland (the walk-away baseline)

| Role / setting | Base (CHF) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Senior Staff / Principal, Geneva or Zurich scale-up | 200k to 250k | Plus bonus, sometimes RSUs |
| Head of Engineering, Swiss Series A/B | 190k to 240k | Equity 0.3% to 1% |
| Big tech Staff+ in Zurich (Google, etc.) | 230k to 300k+ total | Hard ceiling on what a startup will match |

This is Magino's alternative if Pond doesn't work out, and it's what any relocation needs to beat after cost-of-living.

### San Francisco (where the offer will be)

| Role / stage | Base (USD) | Equity |
|---|---|---|
| Founding Head of Eng, seed | 190k to 240k | 1% to 3% |
| Founding Head of Eng, Series A | 220k to 280k | 0.75% to 2% |
| First engineer (not head) | 170k to 220k | 0.5% to 1.5% |

### Cost-of-living reality check

SF and Geneva are close on rent, but SF adds US health insurance, higher effective tax for a single earner in California, and the cost of a move. Rule of thumb: the SF package should be at least 15% to 20% above the Swiss baseline in cash once converted, before counting equity.

---

## 2. Opening anchor

Don't name a number first if it can be avoided. If they push:

> "For a founding Head of Engineering in SF at your stage, I'm looking at around $260k base and 2% to 2.5% equity. That reflects having taken a platform from zero to acquisition, and that I'm relocating from Geneva to do this. I'm flexible on how cash and equity balance out, and I'd rather get the equity right than squeeze the base."

Why this holds up: the Alaya to Benevity exit is the strongest card. Few candidates at this level have been the technical cofounder through an acquisition by a $1B+ company.

---

## 3. If they come in low

**Scenario: base around $180k, equity under 1%.**

> "Thanks, I appreciate you being upfront. The equity is where it feels light to me. I'd be the first engineering hire responsible for building the team and the platform that the 7,000 builders run on, which is closer to a cofounder-level scope than an early employee one. Could we get to 1.75% with the base where it is, or $230k with 1.25%?"

Giving them two options lets them say yes to one without losing face.

**Scenario: they say the cash budget is fixed.**

> "Understood. Then let's look at the other levers: a larger option grant, a signing bonus to cover the move, and a review of base once you close the next round."

---

## 4. Equity terms worth asking about

- **Type:** ISOs or RSAs. At this stage, ask whether early exercise is allowed so the 83(b) clock can start while the strike is low.
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask whether the cliff can be shortened to 6 months given the relocation risk.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination), at least 50%. As someone who went through an acquisition, Magino can speak to why this matters concretely.
- **Exercise window:** push for 5 to 10 years post-departure instead of 90 days.
- **Fully diluted count:** ask for the total share count and latest preferred price so the percentage means something. Ask about the size of the option pool and whether a refresh is planned at the next round.
- **Anti-dilution:** common holders rarely get this. Better to ask for a refresh grant tied to the next financing.

---

## 5. Relocation and visa (don't skip these)

> "Since the role is onsite, I'll need to talk through relocation and work authorization. Is the company set up to sponsor? For someone with a founder and acquisition track record, the O-1 tends to be the realistic route, and I'd want legal costs covered."

Ask for:
- Visa legal fees paid by the company (O-1 or H-1B)
- Relocation package, typically $15k to $30k, or a signing bonus to cover it
- A start date that accounts for visa processing, possibly with a remote period from Geneva in between

---

## 6. Geographic discount pushback

If they suggest a remote arrangement from Geneva at a lower rate:

> "Geneva isn't a discount market. It's one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and Swiss rates for this level are in the CHF 200k to 250k range. If remote works for you, I'd want that as the baseline, not a reduction from it."

---

## 7. Competing offer template

Use only if true at the time of the call.

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late stages with another company, a [Staff/Head of Engineering] role in [Geneva/Zurich] at around CHF [X] base plus equity. Pond is my first choice because of the problem you're working on, so I'd rather work something out with you. Is there room to get closer to [target]?"

---

## 8. Walk-away line

If the total package comes in below the Swiss baseline after cost-of-living, with no meaningful equity and no relocation support, it's not worth moving continents for. Say thanks, keep the door open, and move on.
