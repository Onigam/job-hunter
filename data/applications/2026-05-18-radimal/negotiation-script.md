# Negotiation Script — Radimal, Staff Platform Engineer

---

## Market Context

**Radimal's posted range:** $175K–$225K USD (US remote)

**Switzerland/Geneva market anchor:**
- Staff Platform Engineer in Geneva (IC, 15+ years): CHF 200K–240K gross base
- At current CHF/USD rate (~0.90), CHF 200K–240K = roughly $220K–$265K USD
- Bottom of Radimal's range ($175K) is meaningfully below Swiss market when converted

**Critical nuance — contractor vs. employee:**
Radimal is a 2–10 person US company. They will almost certainly engage Magino as an independent contractor (B2B), not a Swiss employee. This means:
- No employer-side AVS/social charges paid by them (~13% savings for them)
- Magino covers his own social contributions, pension (LPP), and benefits independently
- The loaded equivalent for a US employer hiring an employee would be $215K+ to deliver the same take-home as $175K contractor

**Target anchor:** $230K–$240K USD. Justification: Geneva cost of living, contractor overhead, and 17 years of hands-on platform work that maps directly to the role.

---

## Opening Anchor

When asked for expectations, state:

> "Based on the scope of the role and the Geneva market, I'm targeting $230,000. I know that's above the posted range, but as an independent contractor based in Switzerland, I'm covering social contributions and benefits on my own side — the equivalent for a US-based hire would be higher. Happy to discuss structure if there's flexibility."

If they push back immediately on the number being above range:

> "The $225K ceiling makes sense for a US-based employee. For a contractor in Geneva absorbing employer-side costs, the net is materially different. I'd be comfortable at $225K if there's meaningful equity attached — that's where I'd want the conversation to go."

---

## If They Come In Low (under $200K)

Do not accept on the first call. Use this:

> "I appreciate the offer. To be honest, $[X] doesn't quite work given the contractor economics on my end. I need to be at $220K minimum to make the numbers work after social contributions. Is there room to move, or is the range firm?"

If they claim the range is firm:

> "Understood. Then I'd want to look carefully at equity. At this stage, the upside on meaningful equity can offset a base that's lower than market. Can you walk me through the current cap table structure and what a realistic package looks like for this role?"

---

## Equity Discussion

Radimal is early-stage (2–10 people, founded 2020). This is the stage where equity can actually matter.

**Target ask:**
- 0.75% to 1.5% on a fully diluted basis, depending on funding stage
- Standard 4-year vest, 1-year cliff
- Extended post-termination exercise window (push for 5 years or "until liquidity event" — the standard 90 days is a trap for early employees)

**Questions to ask before accepting any equity offer:**
- What's the last 409A valuation, and when was it done?
- What's the current round structure and liquidation preferences?
- How many shares are outstanding (fully diluted)?
- Is this ISO or NSO? (ISOs are preferable for US tax treatment; less relevant for Swiss resident but worth knowing)
- What does the option pool look like post-round?

**Script for equity conversation:**

> "I'm interested in the equity component, especially at this stage. Can you share the fully diluted share count and the last 409A? I want to understand what the grant actually represents in percentage terms before reacting to a number of options."

If they're vague:

> "I don't need the full cap table, just the rough fully diluted count so I can calculate the percentage. That's standard for an offer conversation at a company this size."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

Some US companies will propose a discount for non-SF/NYC candidates. For Geneva, push back directly:

> "I hear that some companies apply a geographic adjustment. Geneva is in the top 5 most expensive cities globally by most indices, comparable to San Francisco and above New York for housing and cost of living. I wouldn't expect a discount relative to a US market rate, and I'd want to anchor to the top of the posted range or above for that reason."

---

## Currency Risk

If paid in USD as a contractor in Switzerland, FX volatility is real. The CHF/USD rate has moved 15%+ in both directions over recent years.

Raise this after base and equity are settled:

> "One practical consideration: I'll be receiving USD and converting to CHF for living expenses. If you're open to it, I'd appreciate either CHF payment or a quarterly review mechanism to address significant FX moves. Even a floor clause would help."

If they decline: accept it and model the risk yourself. At $230K USD, even a 10% FX move represents ~$23K. Price that into your minimum acceptable number.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in play (or expect one), use it:

> "I want to be transparent — I'm in final stages with another company and expect an offer this week. I'd prefer Radimal for the domain and the platform scope, so I wanted to give you first look. Is there a timeline on your end for moving to an offer?"

This creates urgency without pressure. Do not name the competing company unless asked directly and you're comfortable with the information being shared.

---

## Walk-Away Numbers

| Scenario | Minimum to accept |
|---|---|
| Base only, no equity | $230K USD |
| Base + meaningful equity (0.75%+ undiluted) | $210K USD |
| Base + token equity (under 0.5%) | $225K USD |
| Below $200K, any equity structure | Walk away |

---

## Final Checklist Before Signing

- [ ] Base rate confirmed in writing (USD or CHF)
- [ ] Option grant: number of shares, strike price, vesting schedule, cliff, exercise window
- [ ] 409A valuation date and value on record
- [ ] Contractor vs. employee classification confirmed (get it in writing)
- [ ] Payment terms: monthly or bi-weekly? Wire or ACH?
- [ ] Any non-compete or IP assignment clauses reviewed (Swiss law applies; be cautious about overly broad US-style NDAs)
