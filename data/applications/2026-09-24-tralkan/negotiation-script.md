# Negotiation Script: CTO & Technical Co-Founder @ Tralkan (Zürich)

## Context
- Tralkan, Zürich. It's building **Sentinel Command**, a command-and-coordination platform for autonomous aerial emergency response that coordinates drones, ground robots, sensors and human teams (fires, missing persons, infrastructure failures). Pre-seed, with a working software prototype that's been validated on operational use cases.
- Role: technical cofounder. Hybrid, Zürich area. Response on applications within 5 working days, then a 45-min founder call, a short case study, a final call and a written offer.
- **The terms are published, which changes the negotiation:**
  - **Phase 1 (pre-financing):** part-time, **no salary**, equity vests from the start date.
  - **Phase 2 (post-financing):** full-time, **CHF 125,000/year**, same vesting schedule continues.
  - **Equity:** 12 to 18% cofounder stake, 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff, double-trigger acceleration.
- **The key point:** CHF 125k is well below Zürich market rates for a CTO, and Phase 1 is unpaid with no end date. Everything you're giving up in cash has to come back as equity and protection. Treat the salary number as mostly fixed until the round closes. The real negotiation is over (1) the equity percentage, (2) what happens if financing never arrives, (3) share structure and leaver terms, and (4) how the salary moves after funding.

**Opportunity cost, spelled out:** at a Senior Staff level in Switzerland you'd expect around CHF 180k to 200k. At CHF 125k after funding, you're giving up roughly CHF 55k to 75k a year, plus whatever Phase 1 costs you in time. Over four years that's CHF 250k+ in foregone cash, before counting the unpaid months. Use that figure, calmly, to justify the top of the equity range.

## Your target

- **Walk-away:** 15% on a standard 4-year vest, shares rather than options, a time limit on the unpaid phase (or a fallback if it runs past that), and good-leaver terms that let you keep vested equity if you're pushed out without cause.
- **Target:** 18% or more, cliff credited or shortened for the unpaid months, CHF 140k base on financing with a written step-up to market at Series A, and a board seat or board observer seat.
- **Opening anchor:** 20% equity and CHF 150k on financing. Justification: technical cofounder who's already taken a Swiss platform from zero to an acquisition by a unicorn, taking on the architecture, standards, hiring and investor technical due diligence, and working unpaid until the round.

## Script: when they ask about expectations

"I've read the terms in the posting and I appreciate that you put them up front. The structure makes sense for where you are. Given that the first phase is unpaid and the salary afterwards sits well below what I'd earn at Staff level in Switzerland, I'd want the equity to reflect that. I'm thinking the top of your range or a bit above, around 20%, and CHF 150k once the round closes. I'm flexible on how that splits between cash and equity. Can you walk me through how you got to 12 to 18%, and what the cap table looks like today?"

Then stop talking and let them answer first.

## Counter-offer scripts

**If they say the salary is fixed at CHF 125k:**
"That's fine for the first period after the round; I understand runway matters more than my salary right now. What I'd like in writing is a review tied to the next milestone, say moving to market rate at Series A or when revenue passes an agreed level. And if cash is really fixed, I'd expect that to show up on the equity side."

**If they offer 12 to 14%:**
"I appreciate it, but I'd be the only technical cofounder, working unpaid until the round and then well below market for a few years. On top of that, I'd be the person investors look at during technical due diligence. Twelve percent is closer to what I'd expect for a key hire after the seed than for a cofounder in this position. Can we get to 18%? If there's a reason the top of the range isn't possible, I'd like to understand it, for example whether there's an investor or advisor allocation I'm not seeing."

**If they won't move above 15%:**
Trade the percentage for structure. Ask for: cliff credit for the unpaid months, founder shares at nominal value (not options), a top-up grant at the first round if the pool is refreshed, and a board seat. "If 15% is the ceiling, I can work with that, provided the terms protect the time I put in before the round."

**If they push back on "you haven't done drones":**
"Fair. Most of what's in the posting is backend reliability, integration interfaces, security, auditability and hiring, which is what I've done for 17 years. The autonomy and regulatory parts I'd learn with your partners and cover with the first hire. The equity I'm asking for reflects the platform and the team, not a claim to flight-control expertise."

## Equity terms to negotiate (the details matter more than the percentage)

1. **Shares, not options.** In Switzerland, founders usually hold real shares in the AG or GmbH, subscribed at nominal value. For a private individual, capital gains on shares are generally tax-free, while options and discounted employee shares can be taxed as income. Get this confirmed by a Swiss tax advisor before signing, and ask whether shares would be issued at nominal value now, before any round sets a higher valuation.
2. **Vesting as reverse vesting.** You receive the shares upfront and the company has a buy-back right on the unvested part. Ask whether **the existing founder(s) are on the same schedule.** If only the new cofounder vests, that's a red flag.
3. **The cliff and the unpaid phase.** A 1-year cliff while working unpaid part-time means you could put in 11 months for nothing. Ask for either a 6-month cliff or monthly vesting from day one during Phase 1.
4. **Time limit on Phase 1.** "What happens if the round hasn't closed by month 9 or 12?" Options: the company pays a reduced salary, your vesting accelerates, or both sides can part ways with vested equity kept. Get the trigger defined: what counts as "financing" (a minimum amount, and does a grant like Innosuisse or a convertible loan count?).
5. **Good leaver / bad leaver.** Good leaver (fired without cause, illness, company decision) keeps all vested shares at fair value. Bad leaver should be narrowly defined (fraud, serious breach), not "resigned voluntarily".
6. **Acceleration.** The posting says double-trigger "upon acquisition or founder departure". Clarify exactly: standard double-trigger means 100% of unvested shares accelerate if there's an acquisition **and** you're let go or your role is materially reduced within 12 months. Ask for it in writing, in the shareholders' agreement.
7. **Dilution and protection.** Full anti-dilution is unrealistic for a founder, but ask for pro-rata rights in future rounds, the size and timing of the option pool (created before or after your shares?), and what the next round is expected to dilute (typically 15 to 25% at seed).
8. **Governance.** A board seat, or at least observer rights, and a say on technical hires and architecture budget. Ask to see the current shareholders' agreement and cap table.
9. **IP and outside work during Phase 1.** Because Phase 1 is part-time and unpaid, check the IP assignment covers only Tralkan work, and that any non-compete doesn't stop you from paid work in parallel until financing.

## Geographic pushback (Geneva to Zürich)

The company is in Zürich and you're in Geneva. "Geneva is expensive" doesn't really work here, since Zürich is just as expensive. The argument that does work is commuting cost and time.

"I'm happy to be in Zürich regularly, but it's under three hours each way from Geneva. Could we agree on a fixed number of on-site days, say two a week, and have the company cover travel once the round is in, a GA travelcard or equivalent? A 2nd-class GA is roughly CHF 4k a year, so it's a small line item that makes the arrangement sustainable."

During Phase 1 there's no budget, so just agree on the on-site rhythm and put travel costs into the post-financing package.

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

"I want to be transparent: I'm also talking to [a Staff/Principal role at a Swiss scale-up / another early-stage company], where the cash is around CHF [X]k. Tralkan is the one I'm most interested in because of the problem and the cofounder role, but the gap in cash is real. If we can close the gap on equity and the Phase 1 terms, I'd be ready to commit."

Never make up an offer. If you don't have one, use the opportunity cost argument (market rates for Staff roles in Switzerland) instead. It's just as persuasive and it's true.

## Questions to ask before accepting

1. What's the cap table today, and who else holds equity or has been promised any (advisors, early investors, partners)?
2. How much are you raising, from whom, and what's the realistic timeline? Is anything already committed?
3. Are you, as the existing founder(s), on the same vesting schedule?
4. What happens to my equity if the round doesn't close within 12 months?
5. Who owns the existing Sentinel Command code and IP, and has it been assigned to the company?
6. Which operational partners are you validating with today, and who would I talk to first?
7. How are decisions split between us, and what does the board look like after the round?
8. Is there any grant funding (Innosuisse, armasuisse, civil protection programmes) in the pipeline, and does it count as "financing" for Phase 2?

## Final tip

This isn't a salary negotiation. It's a partnership agreement. Get the shareholders' agreement reviewed by a Swiss startup lawyer before signing anything, even if the founder is pleasant and the terms look standard. A clear, fair agreement now protects the relationship as much as it protects you.
