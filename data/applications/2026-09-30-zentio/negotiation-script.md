# Negotiation Script: Zentio, Founding Engineer (Berlin)

## Context that shapes everything

Zentio is pre-seed. They announced a €1.4M round in December 2025, led by HTGF with SI Ventures. Founded in 2025 in Berlin by Julian Rose (CEO), Immo Polewka (CCO) and Christophe Kafrouni (CTO). The posting gives no salary, equity or remote policy, and it's listed as Berlin, full-time. It was first posted in October 2025 and is still open, with 150+ applicants on LinkedIn.

Three things follow from that.

Cash is very tight. €1.4M funds maybe 12 to 18 months for three founders plus one or two engineers. Before any number, find out whether they've raised again since December 2025 or are raising now; a seed round in progress changes what they can pay. Ask directly: "Where are you on runway, and is a seed round in motion?"

Equity is the real lever. You'd be employee number one or two, next to three cofounders, with 17 years and an exit behind you. That's a strong position to ask for meaningful ownership, not a token grant.

Your reference point is Geneva, but the job is in Berlin, and Berlin pays less than Munich. A Berlin pre-seed offer will look very low next to Swiss numbers. Don't hide that. It's your main argument for more equity, and possibly for a remote or hybrid arrangement.

## Market reference points (estimates, verify before the call)

These are working estimates from public salary data and typical early-stage packages. Check them against current sources (Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Index Ventures' OptionPlan, the German Startup Association salary data, Kununu) before you say a number out loud.

| Reference | Base | Equity |
|---|---|---|
| Geneva, Senior Staff / Principal at a scale-up | CHF 190k to 240k | RSUs or options, varies |
| Zurich, Staff+ at big tech | CHF 230k to 300k+ total comp | RSUs |
| Swiss startup, founding engineer, seed stage | CHF 140k to 180k | ~0.5% to 2% |
| Berlin, senior/staff engineer at funded scale-up | €85k to 115k | small options pool (VSOP) |
| Berlin, founding engineer at pre-seed (employees 1 to 3) | €75k to 100k | ~0.5% to 2% |
| Berlin, founding engineer with architect/lead scope | €95k to 120k | ~1% to 3% |

The Zentio role owns the product stack end to end (UI, API, schema, integrations, monitoring). Pitch yourself in the bottom row, not the middle one.

A rough cost-of-living check: CHF 200k in Geneva and €110k in Berlin aren't equivalent after tax and rent, but the gap is smaller than the raw numbers suggest. Run your own numbers on net take-home, including German social contributions, before deciding your floor.

## Your numbers

**Walk-away floor:** decide this before the first call and write it down. Something like €95k base plus at least 1% is a reasonable place to start thinking, but it's your call, not a formula. If they can't reach your floor in cash, the question becomes how much extra equity closes the gap.

**Target:** €110k to 120k base, 1.5% to 2.5% equity, 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff.

**Opening anchor:** €125k base and 2.5%, or a lower base (around €100k) with 3%+ if cash is the constraint.

## Scripts

### When they ask for your expectations first

"I'd rather hear how you're thinking about the package first, since you know your runway better than I do. For context, I'm coming from a Senior Staff role in Geneva, so my current cash is well above what a pre-seed company can pay. I'm not expecting you to match it. I'm expecting the equity to make up a good part of the difference."

If they insist on a number:

"For a founding role where I own the stack end to end, I'm thinking around €125k base with 2.5% in equity. If cash is the constraint, I'm open to shifting that toward more equity. What matters to me is that the total package reflects taking this on as engineer number one."

### If they come in low (say €80k and 0.5%)

"Thanks, I appreciate you being straight about the numbers. I can see the constraint on cash, and I'm not going to push you into a base that shortens your runway. Where I'm stuck is the equity. At 0.5%, I'd be taking a big pay cut from Geneva with very little upside for being first in. Could we look at €100k and 2%, or keep the base closer to your number and go to 3%?"

Then stop talking. Let them answer.

### Equity: what to ask about

German startups usually offer virtual shares (VSOP) rather than real equity. Ask these explicitly, and get the answers in writing.

- **Type:** VSOP (virtual, cash payout on exit) or real shares/ESOP? Real equity has become more feasible since the 2024 German reforms to employee equity taxation (the Zukunftsfinanzierungsgesetz changes); ask whether they've considered it.
- **Percentage on a fully diluted basis:** ask for the percentage, not a number of units, and what the fully diluted share count is today.
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Good leaver / bad leaver:** in VSOPs these clauses decide everything. Make sure leaving on good terms (or being let go without cause) keeps your vested portion. Push back on any clause that strips vested shares on any departure.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination). Founding hires can often get this.
- **Dilution:** you can't really get anti-dilution protection as an employee, but you can ask for a top-up conversation at the seed round, written into the offer as a review point.
- **Exercise window (if real options):** ask for a long post-departure exercise window, ideally years, not 90 days.
- **Payout on exit (if VSOP):** check how the payout is taxed and whether it's triggered only on exit or also on secondary sales.

### Pushing back on location (Geneva vs Berlin)

The posting doesn't say remote. Don't assume either way; ask.

"I live in Geneva and I'm open to spending real time in Berlin, especially early on, when being in the room with Christophe and the pilot customers matters most. What I'd like to understand is whether you'd consider something like one week a month in Berlin plus remote the rest of the time. If full relocation is required, relocation support and a salary at the top of the range become part of the conversation."

If they want you in Berlin full time, factor in relocation support (a one-off sum, often €5k to 15k), and whether German employment is set up through a local contract or an Employer of Record if you stay Swiss-based.

### If you have (or might have) a competing offer

Only say this if it's true.

"I want to be transparent. I'm also in late-stage conversations with another early-stage company, and their package is around [X]. Zentio is where I'd rather be, because the problem is harder and more concrete. Is there room to close the gap, even if it's on the equity side?"

If nothing is live yet, a safe version:

"I'm talking to a few other teams at a similar stage, so I want to make sure we land on something that works. I'd rather decide on the problem and the people than on a small difference in cash."

### Before you sign, confirm

- Base salary, currency and payment country
- Equity type, percentage (fully diluted), vesting schedule, cliff, leaver terms
- Location and travel expectations in writing
- Title (Founding Engineer, and whether a lead title is on the table as the team grows)
- Holiday (30 days is normal in Germany)
- Equipment and any AI tooling budget
- Start date and notice period at Benevity

## What not to do

Don't open with your Geneva salary as a demand. Use it as context.
Don't accept on the call. "Thanks, let me think it over and come back to you by Friday" is always fine.
Don't negotiate on a number you haven't checked against current data.
