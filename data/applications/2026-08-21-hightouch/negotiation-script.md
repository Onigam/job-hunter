# Negotiation Script — Hightouch, Staff Engineer AI Productivity

## What's already public
The posting lists $180,000-$400,000 USD, explicitly location-independent, plus ISO equity with early exercise and a 10-year post-termination exercise window. That's a wide band, which usually means the actual Staff-level number clusters well above the floor and the floor exists for compliance reasons more than intent. Location-independent pay also removes the geographic-discount conversation before it starts, since Hightouch has already committed in writing to not adjusting for where I sit.

## Market anchor (Switzerland reference, for context only)
Staff/Principal engineers in Geneva and Zurich at well-funded scale-ups typically land CHF 190,000-240,000 base, often with equity or bonus on top. That's roughly $215,000-$270,000 depending on exchange rate. Given this role is explicitly US-band and location-independent, I'd anchor to the US Staff range rather than the Swiss one, but it's useful as a floor: nothing below the Swiss-equivalent number makes sense to accept even if the US band nominally allows it.

## Opening anchor
When asked for a number, name a figure in the upper third of the posted band rather than the midpoint: around $340,000-$360,000 total target, split between base and equity depending on how Hightouch structures it. Frame it as informed by the AI tooling and platform work described in the role plus the acquisition track record at Alaya, not as an arbitrary ask. Something like: "Given the band you've published and the scope here, I'd want to be talking in the $340K-$360K range for total comp, with the mix open to discussion."

## If they come in low
If an offer lands in the lower half of the band, don't accept or reject on the spot. Ask what specifically in the leveling or the interview loop put the number there, since that tells you whether it's a leveling disagreement (fixable with more information) or a budget constraint (fixable with mix, not base). Then counter with something like: "I was expecting to land closer to the top third of the range you posted, based on the scope of the role and my background building and scaling platforms through an acquisition. Is there room to move the base, or should we look at the equity split instead?"

## Equity notes
ISOs with early exercise and a 10-year post-termination window are genuinely good terms, most companies default to 90 days, so that part of the offer is worth acknowledging rather than fighting. Focus equity questions on: current valuation and last round terms, total shares outstanding for dilution context, standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff (confirm it's standard, don't assume), and whether early exercise comes with an 83(b) election window that needs immediate action if exercised early. Ask for the strike price and 409A valuation in writing before agreeing to any equity-heavy mix.

## Geographic angle
No pushback needed here since the band is location-independent by design, but it's worth confirming explicitly in writing during the offer stage that Geneva residency doesn't trigger a later adjustment, some companies apply location bands quietly after initial offers.

## Competing offer leverage
If another process is live, use it plainly rather than vaguely: "I'm further along with another company at a similar level, and I'd rather make a decision on the merits than let timing force it. Where can you move to make this the clear choice?" Even without a live competing offer, timeline pressure works honestly: ask for a decision date and use the gap to negotiate rather than bluffing a number that isn't real.

## Walk-away point
Given the posted floor and the fact that pay is explicitly not geo-adjusted, treat anything under $260,000 total comp as a signal the leveling is off rather than a number worth negotiating up from. At that point the better move is asking directly whether the role was scoped as Staff or something more junior, not continuing to negotiate against a mismatched level.
