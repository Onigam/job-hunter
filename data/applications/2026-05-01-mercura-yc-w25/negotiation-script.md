# Negotiation Script — Mercura AI Engineering Lead

## Context

- **Company**: Mercura (YC W25), Munich, Germany
- **Stage**: Early-stage, ~$2M ARR, targeting $10M ARR by end of 2026
- **Role**: AI Engineering Lead (hands-on + leadership)
- **Posted comp**: €80K-€130K cash (RemoteNomad) / €100K-€300K total comp including bonus and equity (YC listing)
- **Your base**: Geneva, Switzerland — relocation to Munich required

---

## Market Anchors

**Munich market for AI Engineering Lead / Staff Engineer (2026):**
- 8-12 years experience, IC with team leadership: €100K-€140K base
- 15+ years with founding-engineer track record: €130K-€160K base
- Equity at this stage: typically 0.1-0.5% for a senior hire, 4-year vest, 1-year cliff

**Your Geneva anchor:**
- Geneva Senior Staff Engineer rate: CHF 160-200K total comp (roughly €175K-€220K)
- Munich is cheaper than Geneva but not dramatically so: Munich salaries track at roughly 80-90% of Zurich/Geneva rates
- A relocation from Switzerland to Germany represents a real cost-of-living and currency adjustment

**Reasonable target for this role:**
- Base: €130K-€150K
- Equity: 0.25-0.5% (4-year vest, 1-year cliff)
- Relocation: €5K-€10K one-time support, or 3 months temporary housing

---

## Opening Anchor

If asked "what are your salary expectations?" before they make an offer:

> "I'm looking at €140K base as a starting point, reflecting both the Munich market for this level and the fact that I'm relocating from Geneva where the benchmark is higher. I'd want to understand the equity package alongside that, since the total comp picture matters as much as the base at an early-stage company."

If pushed to give a range:

> "I'd say €130K-€150K on the base, with equity that reflects the founding-engineer type of contribution you're describing. I'm flexible on the mix between cash and equity if the upside story is compelling."

---

## If the First Offer Comes in Low (below €100K base)

Stay calm. Don't reject immediately.

Script:

> "Thanks for the offer. The role is genuinely interesting and I've been impressed by the team. I do want to be transparent: the base is meaningfully below what I was targeting. Your own YC listing puts the total comp at €100K-€300K, and for someone at my level relocating from Geneva, I'd need the base alone to be in the €130K range to make that math work. Is there room to move on the cash side, or alternatively, is there flexibility on the equity to offset a lower base?"

Then stop talking. Let them respond.

---

## Equity Negotiation

Before accepting any equity number, get clarity on:

1. **What percentage of the company does the grant represent?** (not just the number of shares)
2. **What was the last round's post-money valuation?** (to estimate what 0.25% is worth today)
3. **What's the vesting schedule?** Standard is 4-year vest, 1-year cliff. Anything shorter on the cliff is a red flag.
4. **Is there a refresh grant after year 2?**
5. **What's the exercise window if you leave?** (push for 5-10 years, not 90 days)

Script for equity counter:

> "I'd like to understand the equity a bit more before I can assess the total picture. Could you walk me through the cap table impact: what percentage does [X shares] represent at the last round's post-money? And what's the exercise window if I leave after the cliff?"

If they offer below 0.2% for this role at this stage:

> "Given that this is framed as a founding-engineer type role, I'd expect the equity to reflect that. At a pre-Series A YC company where I'd be the senior-most engineering hire, I'd be targeting 0.3-0.5%. Is there room to move on that, or is the option pool constrained right now?"

---

## Relocation Negotiation

Don't accept zero relocation support if you're moving from Geneva to Munich.

Script:

> "One thing I'd want to include in the offer is some relocation support. Moving from Geneva to Munich is a real undertaking: shipping, deposits, temporary housing while I find a place. A one-time €8K-€10K relocation allowance is standard for senior hires asked to relocate. Is that something you can include?"

If they say no cash but offer other support:

> "I understand. Could we at least work in 2-3 months of temporary housing stipend while I get settled? That would make the transition significantly easier."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you receive another offer (or want to create urgency):

> "I want to be straightforward with you: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company. The offer there is at €X base with Y equity. I'd prefer Mercura because of [specific reason], but I need to make a decision by [date]. Is there anything you can do on the offer to help me make that call?"

Even without a competing offer, you can use market data:

> "I've been doing research on market rates for this level in Munich and Geneva, and the comps I'm seeing from other AI-first startups at a similar stage are in the €130K-€150K range. I want to make this work, but I need the comp to reflect that."

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

- Equity grant described only in share count, not percentage: push back until you get the percentage
- 90-day exercise window on departure: this is a serious downside, negotiate for longer
- No relocation support at all for a cross-country move: at minimum negotiate temporary housing
- Base significantly below €100K with vague promises about equity upside: the math rarely works out
- "We'll revisit comp after 6 months": get any comp adjustment in writing, with specific criteria, before signing

---

## Walk-Away Point

If base offer stays below €110K with no meaningful equity upside and no relocation support, it's not the right deal. Munich with a relocation from Geneva at that comp level doesn't pencil out compared to remote or Geneva-local alternatives.
