# Negotiation Script: Glass Health, Founding Product Engineer (Remote)

## Context
- Posted range: $120K-$210K/year (broad, likely spans levels across Product/Full-Stack/Backend/AI roles)
- Stage: seed, $6.5M raised (incl. $5M seed) from YC and Initialized Capital, targeting Series A
- Leverage: 17+ years experience, founding engineer with an actual exit (Alaya to Benevity, $1B+ acquirer), directly relevant LLM/gen-AI production experience, full-stack across their exact posted stack (TypeScript/React, Python)
- Counter-leverage they'll have: seed-stage cash discipline, small team, likely limited room on cash comp until Series A closes

## Switzerland market anchor
Geneva-based Staff/Principal-level engineers with founding or exit experience typically see CHF 150,000-190,000+ base in the local market, and Geneva consistently ranks among the most expensive cities globally for cost of living. Converted at a rough 0.90-1.00 USD/CHF rate, that's roughly $165,000-$210,000, which lands at or above the top of Glass Health's posted band. That number is the anchor, not the ask: it justifies sitting at the top of their range rather than the middle, even though the role is paid on a US band as a remote hire.

## Opening anchor
"Thanks for sending the range. Given the founding-engineer scope here and that I've been through this exact stage before, building Alaya from zero to an acquisition by Benevity, I'd want to talk about the top of your posted band, around $200K-$210K base, plus a founding-level equity grant. I know seed-stage cash is tight, so I'm open to structuring the gap between cash and equity if that helps on your end."

## If they come in low (say $150K-$160K)
"I hear you on where the budget sits post-seed. A couple of options that might work: we true up base as part of the Series A comp review with a written commitment to revisit in writing, or we shift more of the difference into equity now, while the strike price is still low. What I'd rather not do is land meaningfully under band for a role with this much ownership attached, since the scope here is closer to a technical co-founder than a typical senior hire."

## Equity negotiation
- Ask for the target equity range explicitly in percentage, not just option count. At seed with a small cap table, a founding-level senior hire should expect somewhere in the 0.5%-1.5% range depending on how early they consider this seat.
- Confirm standard 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff, and ask whether there's a double-trigger acceleration clause in case of an acquisition, given Glass Health's stated trajectory toward Series A and eventually an exit.
- Push for a 10-year post-termination exercise window instead of the standard 90 days. This is increasingly common and costs the company nothing at signing.
- Ask whether there's a refresh grant tied to the Series A close, since the option pool will get diluted at that raise and early hires often get left out of the conversation unless they ask upfront.

## Geographic discount pushback
If they try to peg comp lower because Geneva "isn't a US tech hub": "Geneva is consistently one of the most expensive cities in the world to live in, ahead of most US metros including the Bay Area on several cost-of-living indices. I'm not asking for a US-hub premium, I'm asking that the offer reflect where I actually live and what a comparable local role pays, which is the same top-of-band number we already discussed."

## Competing offer leverage template
Use only if genuinely in play with another process: "I want to be transparent that I'm further along with another company at a similar stage, and their initial number is close to what I've proposed here. I'd rather join Glass Health if we can get aligned, the product problem is more interesting to me, but I do need to move on this within the next week or two."

## Close
"I want this to work, the product problem here is one I'd genuinely enjoy solving. Let's get the base and equity numbers aligned this week so I can give you a real answer instead of a maybe."
