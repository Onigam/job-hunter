# Negotiation Script: Proton, Senior Foundation Engineer (Drive)

## Context that shapes the numbers

Proton is headquartered in Geneva and the role is listed there, with offices in Zürich, Barcelona and London. It's a profitable, privately held company of several hundred people, and the Proton Foundation, a Swiss non-profit, has been its majority shareholder since 2024. The posting mentions stock options and doesn't give a salary.

What that means for the negotiation:

- **This is a "Senior" title, not Staff.** Proton will slot you into a senior band. Your 17 years and founder background argue for the top of that band, or for a conversation about level, but the missing Rust experience is the weak point they'll press on. Don't negotiate as though you meet every requirement.
- **Geneva is the HQ, so there's no geographic discount to fight off.** The risk is the opposite: Proton hires heavily in Barcelona and may quietly benchmark against cheaper offices. Keep the conversation on Geneva rates.
- **Proton pays below Zürich big tech.** It's a mission-driven company and it knows candidates take a discount to work on privacy. Expect a solid Geneva salary, not Google Zürich money.
- **Equity is illiquid.** With a foundation as majority owner and no IPO or sale on the table, options are worth little until there's a liquidity event or a buyback programme. Treat them as a bonus, not as compensation, and push on base instead.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough figures from public surveys and recruiter data. Re-check on levels.fyi, Glassdoor (Proton, Geneva), swissdevjobs.ch, and the Michael Page and Hays Switzerland salary guides.

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Senior Software Engineer, Geneva, product company | CHF 130k to 165k |
| Senior Software Engineer, Proton (reported) | CHF 120k to 155k |
| Staff Engineer, Geneva | CHF 165k to 210k |
| Senior / Staff Engineer, Zürich big tech (base only) | CHF 170k to 230k |

Swiss packages usually include a 13th month, either explicitly or folded into the annual figure. Always confirm which one you're being quoted. Also ask about the LPP pension split (anything above the legal minimum employer contribution is real money) and holidays (25 days is standard; more is a lever).

**Target:** CHF 160k base (annual total, 13th month included).
**Opening anchor:** CHF 172k.
**Walk-away floor:** CHF 145k, unless the level is reconsidered or there's a written review at six months.

Why anchor above the senior band: you've designed shared platform code used by several teams for most of 17 years, you've done cross-team architecture as a Senior Staff engineer, and you've owned a platform through acquisition. The Rust gap is real, so the argument isn't "I'm a Rust expert", it's "you're getting someone who's done the hard part of a Foundation role before, the API design, versioning and cross-team adoption, and will close the language gap fast."

## Opening anchor script

"For a senior foundation role in Geneva, and given I've spent the last five years at Senior Staff level doing cross-team architecture, I'm looking at around CHF 172k base. I know I'm coming in without production Rust, and I've priced that in; without that gap I'd be asking for more. Where does that sit against your band for this role?"

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low (for example CHF 135k to 145k)

"Thanks, I appreciate the offer and I do want to make this work. The number is below where I'd expect for Geneva at this level of experience. Senior engineers at Geneva product companies are generally in the 140s to 160s, and my background is closer to the Staff end. Is there room to get to 160?"

If they cite the Rust gap:

"That's fair, and I'd rather it was reflected in a review than in the starting number. Could we agree on CHF 152k now with a formal compensation review at six months, tied to specific outcomes, such as me owning a piece of the SDK core in Rust?"

If base is truly fixed, trade for things that cost them less:

- A higher level or title (Staff), even with the same base, because it sets the next raise
- A signing bonus of CHF 10k to 15k
- An extra week of holiday
- A written six-month review date with a defined salary range
- A larger option grant (low cash cost for them, though see the equity notes below)

## If they try to benchmark against Barcelona or another office

"I understand Proton has teams across several offices, but the role is in Geneva and so am I. My costs are Geneva costs. I'd like the offer benchmarked against Geneva, not a company-wide average."

## Equity notes

Ask these before you put any value on the options:

1. How many options, and what percentage of fully diluted shares is that?
2. What's the strike price, and how was the last valuation set?
3. Vesting schedule and cliff. Four years with a one-year cliff is standard; push back on anything longer.
4. What happens to vested options if you leave? A 90-day exercise window on illiquid stock is close to worthless. Ask for an extended window (several years) or confirm there's a buyback mechanism.
5. Has Proton ever run an employee buyback or secondary sale, and is one planned?
6. Swiss tax treatment: when is the grant or exercise taxed, and does Proton help with the paperwork?

Script:

"I'm glad options are part of the package. Given Proton's ownership structure, I'm valuing them conservatively, so I'd like to understand the liquidity path before we talk about the grant size. Has there been a buyback or secondary, and what's the post-departure exercise window?"

If the answers are vague, put the weight back on base salary.

## Competing offer template (use only if real)

"I want to be upfront. I'm in late stages with another company and expect an offer around CHF [X]. Proton is my first choice because of the work on the Drive SDK and because I'd be in Geneva, so I'd rather close here. Is there anything you can do on [base / level] to make this an easy decision?"

Never name a number you can't back up. If there's no competing offer, say you're "in conversations with a few other companies" and leave it there.

## Closing

Once you agree, ask for the offer in writing, including base, 13th month treatment, pension split, holidays, level, option count and full option terms, before you resign or decline anything else. Read the option plan document itself, not just the summary in the offer letter.
