# Negotiation Script: CTO, Connectome

Context that shapes the math: Connectome is a one-to-ten person company building a consumer brain health app on top of scan, wearable, and behavioral data, with a posted $60K-$120K salary band plus 0-10% equity for a CTO based in London, Zurich, or remote. A band that wide, and that low for a CTO title, almost always means the posting is written for a range of markets and experience levels at once rather than benchmarked against Zurich specifically. The role covers full-stack ownership, the data pipeline, the AI/LLM layer, and hiring, which is closer to a founding-CTO mandate than a senior IC role, and the equity band should move accordingly.

## Market anchor

- CTO at a pre-seed to seed-stage Swiss or Zurich-adjacent startup, cash-constrained: realistic base is CHF 140,000-180,000, with equity in the 5-8% range for someone joining after the founders but before a real engineering team exists.
- The posted $60K-$120K converts to roughly CHF 53,000-106,000, well under a livable Zurich or Geneva CTO number on cash alone, so the case for this role has to be made mostly on equity plus a base that at minimum covers Swiss cost of living.
- Health data, scan imagery, wearable feeds, behavioral logs, carries real regulatory weight, GDPR at minimum and likely medical-device-adjacent scrutiny depending on the claims the app makes. That's a risk premium worth naming explicitly rather than assuming it's priced in.
- Opening anchor: CHF 150,000 base (or the equivalent in the currency they pay), plus equity toward the top of the posted 10% band, framed around full ownership of architecture, the AI layer, and early hiring rather than a generic senior engineer rate.

## Opening anchor script

"Given the scope, full-stack architecture, the data pipeline for scan and wearable inputs, the AI layer, and building the team from close to zero, I'd expect a base around CHF 150,000 to 170,000, or the equivalent depending on how you're structuring pay, plus equity toward the upper end of the range you've posted. I know early-stage comp is a trade between cash and ownership, so I'm open on the split as long as the total reflects a CTO mandate rather than an early engineering hire. Where does this role currently sit within your posted band, and how much of the equity pool has already been allocated to the founders?"

## If they come in low

"I hear the number. Is that benchmarked against a UK or remote-global engineer rate, or does it account for someone owning the entire technical function, architecture, hiring, and the AI roadmap, from day one? That's a different scope than the title alone suggests, and I'd want the offer to reflect it."

If they hold the line on cash: "I can work with a lower base if the equity grant moves toward the top of your stated range and vesting starts from an early date. What I wouldn't want is to land at the bottom of both the cash band and the equity band at once, since that combination doesn't really price in the risk of joining as employee two or three at a company handling health data."

## Equity notes (CTO-level specifics)

- Ask for the current fully diluted share count and how much of the option pool the founders have already carved out; a 5-10% CTO grant means very different things depending on how the cap table looks before a first priced round.
- Confirm standard 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff, and ask whether early exercise is available given the tax treatment differs meaningfully between the UK, Switzerland, and wherever the entity is incorporated.
- Ask directly about anti-dilution protection or a refresh policy tied to future rounds, since a CTO joining pre-seed should not be diluted the same way a later hire would be.
- Given the title, ask whether the role comes with a board seat or observer rights, that's a normal ask at this level and worth raising even if the answer is no.
- Ask about single versus double-trigger acceleration in an acquisition scenario; worth raising explicitly rather than assuming, especially for a health-data company that's an obvious acquisition target for a larger platform.

## Geographic and regulatory framing

"I'm Geneva-based and can work the 9 to 6 UTC hours comfortably either fully remote or with time in Zurich, so there shouldn't be a London-cost-of-living discount applied to my number. Given the health-data angle, I'd also want to understand who currently owns GDPR and clinical-adjacent compliance, since that's typically part of what a CTO is expected to build out, not something handled elsewhere."

## Competing offer, if genuinely in play

"I'm in parallel conversations for platform and technical leadership roles at comparable scope. What makes this one stand out is the brain and wearable data problem specifically, but I want to be upfront about timeline so we can move at a pace that works for both sides."

## Close

A CTO title on a $60K-$120K band is a signal the company hasn't yet priced the role against Swiss or London market reality, not a ceiling to accept as given. Anchor on scope, not the posted number, and get base, the equity percentage, vesting start date, dilution protection, and board rights in writing before accepting. At a ten-person company building on health data, the equity and governance terms are the part most likely to matter five years out, more than the first year's base salary.
