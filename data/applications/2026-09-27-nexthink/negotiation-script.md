# Negotiation Script: Nexthink, Senior AI Engineer (Lausanne, hybrid)

## Context before talking numbers

Nexthink is a Lausanne-founded DEX (digital employee experience) company with a large enterprise customer base. Vista Equity Partners took a majority stake in 2024 at a reported valuation of around $3B, so this is a PE-backed scale-up, not an early-stage startup. Spark, their autonomous IT agent, launched in January 2026 and is clearly a strategic bet, which is where this role sits.

What that means for the negotiation:

1. **Equity won't look like startup equity.** PE-owned companies usually offer management incentive units or phantom/cash LTI plans to a limited group, if anything, and rarely standard ISO-style options for individual contributors. Ask what exists before treating it as part of the package.
2. **Base and bonus carry most of the value.** Push hardest on base, then the variable component, then a sign-on.
3. **Level is the real lever.** The posting is "Senior AI Engineer" and asks for 5+ years. I have 17+ years, a cofounder exit and Senior Staff experience. Whether they level me at Senior, Staff or Principal will move the band more than any line-by-line negotiation.
4. **Posted perks** (unlimited PTO plus 25 days, relocation, fitness, transport) are standard. Don't trade base for them.

Questions for the recruiter in the first call:
- "What's the band for this role, and is there a Staff or Principal level in the AI team it could map to?"
- "How is variable pay structured: target bonus percentage, and what share is company versus individual?"
- "Is there any long-term incentive for engineers under Vista, and how does it vest or pay out?"
- "What does hybrid mean in practice: how many days a week in Lausanne?"

## Market reference (estimates, verify before the call)

Ranges from general market knowledge for 2025-2026. Check levels.fyi (Nexthink, Logitech, Google Zürich), Glassdoor for Nexthink Lausanne, the swissdevjobs.ch salary report, and ideally someone who's worked there.

| Reference | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Senior Software/AI Engineer, Lausanne scale-up | CHF 140k-175k |
| Staff/Lead AI Engineer, Romandie scale-up | CHF 170k-205k |
| Staff/Senior Staff, Geneva or Zürich scale-up | CHF 180k-230k |
| Senior/Staff ML engineer, Zürich big tech (base only) | CHF 190k-260k |

Typical variable at a company like Nexthink: 10-15% target bonus. Treat that as an assumption until the recruiter confirms it.

**My numbers**

- **Opening anchor:** CHF 195k base plus bonus, justified by pitching at Staff scope.
- **Target:** CHF 180k-190k base, 15% target bonus, and a sign-on of CHF 15k-20k if there's no LTI.
- **Walk-away:** below about CHF 165k base. Below that it's a step down from Senior Staff and doesn't pay for the Geneva-Lausanne commute.

## Opening anchor script

When they ask for expectations, try to get their band first:

> "I'd rather understand the band before giving a number. What range have you set for this role?"

If they push back:

> "Based on what I'm seeing for Staff-level AI engineers in Romandie and Zürich, and given I've been operating at Senior Staff for the past five years, I'm looking at around CHF 195k base plus the standard bonus. I'm flexible on how the package is structured, but that's the level of responsibility I'd be taking on."

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low

If the offer is in the CHF 150k-165k range:

> "Thanks, I appreciate it and I'm keen on the role. The base is below where I expected, though. Right now it's pitched at Senior level, and what I'd be doing on Spark (setting eval standards, mentoring, owning architecture calls on agent workflows) is Staff scope. Could we look at leveling it there? At Staff I'd expect the base to land around CHF 185k-190k."

If they can't move the level:

> "Understood. If the band is fixed, can we close the gap another way: a sign-on bonus of around CHF 20k, a higher bonus target, and a written review of level and comp at six months against agreed goals?"

If they say base is final, ask about:
- sign-on bonus (one-off, easier for them to approve)
- guaranteed first-year bonus at target
- six-month level review in writing
- extra remote days (worth real money and time, see below)
- training/conference budget, useful given the fine-tuning gap

## Equity and long-term incentives

Nexthink is majority owned by Vista, so:

- Ask directly whether engineers get **management incentive units, phantom equity or cash LTI**. If units: what share class, vesting (time-based and/or tied to exit), what happens on leaving (good leaver/bad leaver terms), and what triggers payout (usually a sale or IPO).
- Anything paid only at exit, with no sense of timing, should be counted at close to zero when comparing offers.
- If there's nothing on offer, use that to justify a higher base or sign-on: "Since there's no equity component, I'd need the cash side to carry more of the package."
- Swiss tax treatment of units or options depends on the structure. Get the plan documents and check with a tax advisor before accepting anything material.

## Location and commute pushback

The role is in Lausanne and I live in Geneva. That's not a discount argument for them, it's a cost for me.

> "Geneva to Lausanne is about 40 minutes by train each way, which is fine a few days a week. I'd like to confirm hybrid means two to three days on site. If it's more, I'd want the package to reflect the commute: a GA or half-fare travel card covered, and base at the top of the range."

If they suggest Lausanne pays less than Geneva: Nexthink hires across Europe and the US for AI talent, and the relevant comparison is Swiss AI engineering pay, not local cost of living. Both cities sit in the same Swiss pay bracket for senior tech roles.

## Competing offer template

Use only if it's true, or as a general market statement without naming anything invented:

> "I'm in conversations with a couple of other companies for Staff-level roles, and one is moving toward an offer in the CHF 190k+ range. Nexthink is my first choice because of what you're doing with Spark, and I'd like to make this work. Is there room to get closer to that number?"

If there's no real competing offer, drop the number and use market data instead:

> "Staff-level AI roles in Switzerland are landing around CHF 185k-200k base at the moment. I'd like us to be in that range."

## Final check before accepting

- Level and title in writing
- Base, bonus target and how bonus is calculated
- Sign-on and any clawback terms
- LTI plan documents, if any
- Days on site per week
- Notice period and start date
- Pension (LPP/BVG) plan: employer contribution above the legal minimum is worth asking about, since it varies a lot between Swiss employers
