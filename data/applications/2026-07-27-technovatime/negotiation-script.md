# Negotiation Script: Technovatime, Founding Engineer

Context: early-stage, Techstars-backed and SBIR-funded, three-person founding team out of MIT and Amazon with a DoD automation background. Four customers signed in 2026, one a major health system, which is real traction for this stage but the company is still small enough that comp is likely closer to seed-plus than a funded Series A band. Role is based in New York with remote available, and this is a true first-engineer hire, not employee number twenty, so the equity conversation matters as much as base here, maybe more.

## Market anchors

Founding engineer at a Techstars/SBIR-stage startup in the US typically lands base in the USD 140,000 to 180,000 range, well below what the same skill set commands at a funded Series A or later company, because the early equity grant is meant to make up the difference. Geneva-equivalent for a senior IC of this level, someone who's already run a platform end to end, runs CHF 150,000 to 190,000, which is a useful anchor if the conversation drifts toward "what would keep you whole" rather than "what does the role pay." Since the posting doesn't list a number, the opening move should be to get their range first rather than naming one blind, a first-engineer offer with no stated band usually means there's real room to shape it.

## Opening anchor

"Before I put a number out, what range are you working with for this hire? Given the scope, I'd expect it to land somewhere between a strong senior engineer and a technical co-founder-adjacent hire, since one person owning orchestration, computer-use, infra, and deployment is closer to the second than the first. If it's helpful, I'm looking at something in the USD 160,000 to 180,000 base range for a remote setup, with the rest of the value coming from equity that reflects being employee one on the technical side."

## If they come in low

"I get that SBIR and Techstars-stage budgets are tight, and I'm not trying to price myself out of a role I actually want. Two things I'd ask for instead of just pushing base higher: first, can we look at equity as the lever that closes the gap, since that's where a founding hire should see the upside if the company does well. Second, is there a compensation review built in at month six or nine tied to the next funding milestone, rather than waiting a full year to revisit this."

## Equity negotiation

This is the part that matters more than base at this stage.
- A true founding engineer joining pre- or early-Series A should expect somewhere in the 1% to 3% range on a fully diluted basis, depending on how much has already been granted to the three founders and how far along the SBIR/Techstars funding actually is
- Ask directly what's already been allocated to the founding team and how much of the option pool is left before agreeing to a percentage
- Standard four-year vest with a one-year cliff, resist anything longer
- Ask for the strike price and 409A valuation in writing before agreeing to a number, and confirm whether early exercise is available, since that matters a lot at this valuation stage
- If the percentage feels light given the traction already shown, four paying customers including a major health system, that's a fair thing to raise directly rather than assume the number is fixed

## Geographic and remote pushback

If Technovatime anchors to New York cost of living and tries to discount a Geneva-based remote hire: "Geneva isn't a lower cost-of-living market than New York, it's comparable or higher for senior technical talent, so I wouldn't expect a location-based discount here. If overlap hours are the actual concern rather than cost, I'm glad to talk through how I'd structure my day against Eastern time."

## Competing offer template

Use only if there's an actual parallel process running; don't invent one.

"I want to be upfront that I'm also further along with another company, and the total comp there is in a similar range to what we've discussed. I'd rather build this with Technovatime given the product and the stage of traction, so if there's flexibility on the equity side to make the full package comparable, I'd rather spend the next few days finalizing details than comparing offers."

## Close

Don't accept verbally on the call. Ask for the written offer and take at least 48 hours. Before responding, confirm base, the exact equity percentage or share count with strike price, vesting schedule, cliff, what happens to unvested equity on an early acquisition, and remote work terms are all spelled out in writing.
