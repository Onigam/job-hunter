# Negotiation Script — Clubb, Founding Software Engineer

---

## Market Context

**Role:** Founding Software Engineer (first engineering hire)
**Stated range:** £90,000-£110,000 + performance bonus
**Location:** London (Peckham), 4 days/week in-office
**Equity:** Not mentioned in the posting

London market for Senior/Staff engineers with 10+ years: £110k-£145k in 2026.
For a founding engineer (first hire, architectural ownership, direct founder collaboration): £115k-£130k is defensible.

Clubb's position: bootstrapped and profitable, no VC dilution to worry about. This is actually good for equity negotiation since founder shares are real, not hypothetical post-dilution scraps.

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for your number first, or after an offer comes in below target:

> "Based on my background as a founding engineer who's done exactly this before, and London market data for senior engineers at this level, I was thinking in the range of £118,000-£120,000 base. On the equity side, I'd also expect to have a conversation there, given this is a founding role, even if it wasn't in the original posting."

Key principles:
- Name a range, not a single number. The bottom of your range is where they'll anchor to.
- Introduce equity unprompted. If they didn't offer it, ask. A bootstrapped founder who gives nothing to the first engineering hire will tell you a lot.
- Don't justify the ask with need ("I need X to cover rent"). Justify with market data and value ("engineers with this background in London are at X").

---

## If They Come In Low (at or below £95k)

> "I appreciate the offer. The role is interesting enough that I want to make this work, but I'd need to get to something closer to £112k-£115k for the base to feel right given what I'm bringing and the London cost of living. Is that a conversation you can have, or is the range fixed?"

If they say the range is fixed:
> "Understood. In that case, could we revisit on the equity side? If the cash is capped, I'd want to make sure the founding engineer stake reflects the scope of what I'd be owning."

---

## Equity Negotiation

Clubb is bootstrapped. This means any equity is real founder dilution, not options in a company that's raised 5 rounds. They'll be more protective of it. Frame accordingly.

**Opening ask:**
> "For a founding engineer role where I'm effectively the technical co-owner of the product from day one, I'd expect something in the 0.5% range. I know that's real dilution for a bootstrapped company, which is exactly why I think it means something."

**If they push back:**
> "I'm flexible on the number, but not on having something. A founding engineer who has no equity is just a very senior hire, and that changes the relationship and the commitment. What's the range you've been thinking about?"

**Vesting terms to negotiate:**
- Standard: 4 years, 1-year cliff
- Push back on any cliff longer than 1 year
- Ask about acceleration on acquisition (you know how this goes from Alaya)

> "Given that I've been through an acquisition before, I'd also want to talk about what happens to unvested equity in a change of control. Single-trigger acceleration, even partial, matters."

---

## Relocation Support (Geneva to London)

> "One practical thing: I'm relocating from Geneva, which involves some setup costs. A one-time relocation allowance of £4,000-£5,000 would make the transition easier. Is that something Clubb does for senior hires?"

If no: acceptable, but log it as a data point about the company.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

You may not have one right now, but if you receive another offer during this process, use it:

> "I want to be upfront: I have another process moving along and expect to have a decision to make in the next week or two. Clubb is genuinely my preference for the founding role, but I'd need the package to be close to what's on the table elsewhere to make that call easy. Can we accelerate the timeline on your end?"

---

## What to Accept vs. Walk Away From

**Accept if:** £108k+ base, some equity (even 0.25% is a starting point), standard vesting terms.

**Walk away if:** Base below £105k with no equity path whatsoever, or a 2-year cliff, or any language suggesting the role is "senior engineer" rather than genuine founding engineer ownership.

**Red flag to probe:** The posting says "minimum 3 years at a strong, product-driven company." With 17 years and a co-founder exit, you're significantly above that bar. If the offer reflects a mid-level rate rather than the founding engineer premium, push back directly.

> "I want to make sure we're aligned on the level here. The posting says 3 years minimum, but my background is closer to what a very early founding engineer looks like, including having done this exact thing before with Alaya. I'd expect the offer to reflect that."
