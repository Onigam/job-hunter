# Negotiation Script: Gusto, Staff Software Engineer, AI Developer Tools

## Context to hold in mind

Gusto posted a band, which is a rare gift, use it. Denver and "most remote" sits at $180k-$200k base, San Francisco/New York/Seattle sits at $220k-$245k, plus RSUs on top of both. The role is hybrid, 2-3 days a week in Denver, SF, or NY, and Magino is based in Geneva. This is not a remote-from-Switzerland role as posted, so the real first conversation is relocation, not comp. If Gusto is open to a Swiss-based or fully remote exception, comp needs a different anchor entirely, because a US band applied to a Geneva cost of living, and a Geneva-based professional's market rate, are two very different numbers. Treat the negotiation as two separate branches depending on how that resolves.

## Branch A: Role stays US-based, Magino relocates

### Opening anchor
Anchor at the top of the disclosed band for whichever location applies, and treat "Staff" level plus 17+ years and a founding-engineer/acquisition track record as justification to push above it rather than settle inside it.

- If Denver or remote-eligible: open at $210k base, citing the top of band plus the AI-specific track record (production LLM tooling, adoption at scale, not lab experimentation).
- If SF/NY/Seattle: open at $255k base, same logic, band top plus a premium for direct founder-level ownership experience.
- RSU ask: request the equity grant be sized at the high end of whatever's typical for Staff, and ask for the vesting schedule and refresh policy in writing before verbally agreeing to any number. A grant with no refresh policy disclosed is a grant that quietly shrinks in year three.

### Counter-offer script if they come in at the bottom of the band
"I appreciate the offer. Given the scope here, standing up an internal LLM platform with eval and safety infrastructure from a fairly early stage, and given that this maps closely to what I was doing at Benevity and building from zero at Alaya, I'd expect to land closer to the top of the band you've published, around [$210k / $255k depending on location]. Is there room to move there, or is the equity component where there's more flexibility?"

If they hold the line on base: pivot explicitly to equity size, sign-on bonus (to offset relocation costs, which are real and specific: visa/work permit process, moving costs, temporary housing, loss of Swiss health coverage during transition), and a guaranteed first-year review date rather than the standard annual cycle.

### Relocation-specific leverage
Relocating from Switzerland to the US is not a lateral move, it's a full life and tax-residency change. That's legitimate grounds to ask for:
- A relocation package covering moving costs, temporary housing (60-90 days), and visa/immigration legal fees, itemized rather than a flat lump sum that undersells the real cost.
- A start date with real runway, not "start in three weeks", since visa processing alone can take months depending on the sponsorship route.
- Written confirmation of visa sponsorship terms before resigning from anything or signing a lease.

### Geographic discount pushback
Not applicable here in the usual direction, Geneva is expensive but the offer is US-anchored, so the risk is the opposite: being lowballed because "Geneva rates are high, so this US offer is generous by comparison." Counter that framing directly: "I'm not comparing this to a Geneva salary, the relevant comparison is US Staff-level comp for equivalent scope, and I'd like to be evaluated on that basis."

## Branch B: Gusto is open to remote-from-Switzerland

If this comes up in conversation, this becomes the primary branch and the whole calculus changes.

### Swiss market anchors for reference (Staff/Principal-equivalent, Geneva)
- Local Swiss market for Staff/Principal engineers in Geneva or Zurich: roughly CHF 160,000-CHF 210,000 base, with total comp (bonus, benefits) often pushing toward CHF 230,000 at strong tech employers.
- US remote comp paid to a Switzerland-based employee, when companies do offer it, is typically negotiated as a blend: not the full US band (cost-of-labor adjustments are common), but meaningfully above local Swiss-only benchmarks given the specialization.
- Realistic opening ask in this branch: CHF 195,000-CHF 220,000 base equivalent (roughly $220k-$250k at current exchange rates), framed as "US band, cost-of-labor-adjusted for Geneva, not Geneva-market-rate."

### Script for this branch
"If remote-from-Switzerland is genuinely on the table, I'd want to align on how compensation gets calculated for that setup before we go further, since a straight US-band number and a cost-of-labor-adjusted number can differ meaningfully. My starting point would be [CHF equivalent], which reflects the seniority of the role rather than a full geographic discount."

### Equity and benefits notes for this branch
- Ask explicitly how RSUs are administered for a non-US employee, there are real tax and brokerage complications for Swiss residents holding US equity, and getting this in writing avoids an unpleasant surprise at vesting.
- Confirm how health insurance and pension contributions work outside the US benefits framework; Swiss LPP/pension obligations don't map cleanly onto a US benefits package, and this needs its own line item, not an assumption that "benefits are benefits everywhere."

## Competing offer leverage template (use only if genuinely applicable)
"I'm currently weighing this against another process that's further along on comp discussions. I'd rather make a decision based on Gusto's best offer than use a competing number as a lever, so if there's flexibility on your end, now's the time to show it."

## General reminders
- Get every verbal number in writing before responding with agreement.
- Don't accept "we don't have room" as final on the first pass; ask what would create room (a different level, a different equity mix, a later start date).
- If relocation is required and unresolved, don't let comp negotiation finish before that's settled, the two are linked and negotiating comp first can lock in numbers that don't account for relocation costs.
