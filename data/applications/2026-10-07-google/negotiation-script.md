# Negotiation Script: Google, Senior Staff Forward Deployed Engineer, GenAI, Google Cloud (Zurich)

## The shape of this offer

The role is based in Zurich, full time, and the LinkedIn posting gives no salary. "Senior Staff" at Google normally maps to **L7** (Staff is L6, Principal is L8). Google pays in three parts: base, an annual bonus with a target percentage, and RSUs (GOOG stock) granted at hire and topped up with yearly refreshers. There's no startup equity to negotiate, so the usual VSOP, cliff and anti-dilution questions don't apply. What matters instead is the level, the size of the initial RSU grant and the sign-on bonus.

Three things to settle early:

1. **Level first, money second.** The biggest risk is being slotted at L6 instead of L7. The minimum bar asks for 8 years of production AI and cloud customer-facing work, and the hiring committee may argue Magino's 17 years are mostly platform and product, not GenAI field work. Losing a level costs more than any amount of haggling recovers, so the strongest argument (founding engineer, enterprise rollouts, acquisition, Senior Staff title at Benevity) has to land during the interviews and the recruiter debrief, not at the offer.
2. **Location and on-site days.** Magino lives in Geneva. Ask how many days a week the Zurich office expects, and whether customer travel counts. Geneva to Zurich is about 2h45 by train, so a 3-day-a-week office requirement means either relocating or a weekly commute. A Swiss contract stays a Swiss contract either way, so there are no cross-border payroll issues.
3. **Fit check.** The profile says to avoid large legacy enterprises and prefers startups. Google Cloud's FDE team is small and new and runs more like a startup, but it's still Google. Be honest with yourself about this before investing in a long loop.

## Market anchors

Rough ranges for Google Zurich and comparable Swiss big tech roles. **Verify every figure on levels.fyi (Google, Zurich, L6/L7), Glassdoor CH and with the recruiter before any call.** Treat these as approximations.

| Level / market | Base (CHF) | Bonus target | RSUs per year (at grant) | Approx. total comp per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google L6 Staff, Zurich | 200k to 235k | ~20% | 90k to 160k | 320k to 430k |
| Google L7 Senior Staff, Zurich | 235k to 280k | ~20 to 25% | 160k to 280k | 450k to 600k |
| Other big tech Zurich, Staff/Senior Staff (Microsoft, Meta, Apple) | 210k to 270k | 15 to 25% | 100k to 250k | 350k to 550k |
| Geneva, Staff/Principal outside big tech | 180k to 230k | 10 to 15% | little or none | 200k to 260k |

What to take from this: a Google L7 offer will likely be well above anything Geneva offers locally. The negotiating room is mostly in RSUs and sign-on, not base, because Google's base bands are fairly tight per level and location.

Swiss specifics worth asking about: Google's pension (BVG/LPP) contributions above the legal minimum, and whether RSU vesting is taxed as income at vest (it is in Switzerland, at the cantonal rate where you live, so Geneva rates apply if Magino stays put).

If the offer comes in at L6: target the top of the L6 band (base around CHF 235k, RSUs at least CHF 600k over four years) and ask for a written commitment to a promotion review at 12 months.

## Vesting and refreshers

Google's newer grants have been front-loaded (historically around 33% / 33% / 22% / 12% over four years) and vest frequently rather than after a one-year cliff. **Confirm the current schedule with the recruiter**, because it changes how a sign-on bonus should be sized. If the grant is back-loaded or has a cliff, ask for a bigger sign-on to cover year one. Ask what a typical L7 refresher looks like after the first year, since refreshers are what keep total comp level in years three and four.

## Scripts

### When the recruiter asks for expectations

> "I'd rather not give a number before we agree on level. Based on the scope in the posting, embedded customer delivery plus turning field patterns into product, and my background as a founding engineer and Senior Staff, I'm interviewing at L7. Can you tell me the L7 band for Zurich?"

If they insist on a number:

> "For L7 in Zurich I'm looking at total compensation around CHF 550k a year, with the bulk in equity. I'm flexible on the split between base, stock and sign-on."

### If they come in low (e.g. L7 at CHF 250k base, CHF 650k RSUs, no sign-on)

> "Thanks, I'm keen on this role and I want to make it work. The base is close. The equity is where I see the gap: from what I can see, L7 grants in Zurich are typically in the CHF 800k to 1M range over four years. Could you take the grant to CHF 900k and add a sign-on to cover the first year? That would get me to yes."

### If they offer L6

> "I appreciate the offer. My concern is the level, not the money. I've been operating at Senior Staff for the last five years and built a company's whole platform before that. What would the committee need to see to reconsider L7? If L6 is final, I'd want the top of the band and a defined promotion review at twelve months, in writing."

### Geneva versus Zurich

There's no geographic discount to fight here. Zurich bands are among the highest in Europe and a Geneva address won't lower them. If anything, use the commute:

> "I'm based in Geneva, so the office days have a real cost for me. I'm not asking for a different band, but I'd like either a relocation package or travel support built into the offer."

### Competing offer template

Only use this if there really is another process in progress. Google recruiters check, and bluffing can backfire with a committee-based process.

> "I want to be transparent: I'm at the offer stage with [company] for a [Principal/Staff] role, with total compensation of about CHF [X]. Google is my preference because of the FDE mandate and the product feedback loop. If you can get the equity closer to CHF [Y], I'm ready to sign this week."

Credible comparison sets for Zurich: other big tech Zurich offices, AI labs with Zurich offices, and well-funded AI startups hiring founding or principal engineers in Europe.

## Before signing

Get the full breakdown in writing (base, bonus target, RSU grant in CHF and in shares, vesting schedule, sign-on and any clawback terms), the confirmed level, the expected office days and the customer travel percentage. Forward deployed roles can mean a lot of travel, and that's worth knowing before saying yes.
