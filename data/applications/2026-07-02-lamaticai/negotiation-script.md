# Negotiation Script: Lamatic.ai, Founding Engineer

## Context to anchor on

Lamatic has raised roughly $955K total across a pre-seed and a seed-adjacent round, closed a follow-on round in November 2025, and says it reached breakeven in July 2025. That combination, profitable on under a million dollars raised, is unusual: it means less desperation for runway than a typical burning-fast seed startup, but also a genuinely small cash pool for a founding-level hire, since the team hasn't been forced to raise a large round that would normally come with bigger comp bands. The posting lists the role as United States, remote. I'm in Geneva, so the geography question needs to be resolved before comp does, since it changes whether we're negotiating a US salary, a Swiss-anchored remote package, or something contractor-shaped.

Switzerland market context to hold in reserve: a Senior Staff or founding-level engineer in Geneva or Zurich typically anchors between CHF 150,000 and CHF 190,000 base for a comparable scope, before equity. That's the number I'd bring up if the conversation shifts toward a Swiss-based remote arrangement rather than US payroll. If they insist on US-only remote, the useful comparison becomes seed-stage founding engineer bands in the US, generally $140K to $190K with 1% to 3% equity for a team this size, adjusted down somewhat given the capital efficiency story they're telling.

## Opening anchor

"Given the founding-engineer scope and what a team at your stage usually needs from a first senior hire, I'd want to start near the top of whatever band you're working with, in the $170K to $190K range if this runs through US payroll, or CHF 175,000 if we structure it as a Swiss-based remote role. On equity, I'd want to be in the 1.5% to 2.5% range given how early this still is and how much of the platform is still unbuilt."

## If they come in low on salary

Given the breakeven story, they may lead with a number that reflects genuine cash discipline rather than distress, which is a different conversation than a startup burning fast.

"I hear that staying capital efficient is part of how you've operated, and I respect that, but if the salary needs to sit closer to $140K, I'd want the equity to move up to reflect that, closer to 3%, along with a clear conversation about when comp gets revisited, ideally tied to a specific revenue or funding milestone rather than left open-ended."

## Equity negotiation

With only two small rounds closed and breakeven already reached, the cap table is probably still fairly clean, which is worth confirming rather than assuming.

"Before we land on a number, I'd like to understand how much of the option pool is already allocated between the two co-founders and the four founding engineers, what the strike price looks like given you're already profitable, and whether there's a plan for a future priced round that would reset the valuation basis. Standard 4 year vesting with a 1 year cliff works for me. What I'd want to avoid is equity priced off a valuation that's stale relative to where the business actually is now."

## Geographic and remote angle

The listing frames this as a US role, and I'm not a US resident, so this has to be raised early rather than discovered during onboarding.

"I want to flag upfront that I'm based in Geneva, not the US, so I'd need to understand whether this can run as an international remote contract, whether there's an entity or EOR that can handle Swiss payroll, or whether the US framing is a hard requirement. If it has to be US-based, I'd rather know that now than after we've gone deep on comp."

## Competing offer template

Use only if there's an actual competing process in motion.

"I'm in later-stage conversations with a couple of other early-stage teams right now, in a similar comp range. I'd rather decide based on the problem and the team than run a bidding process, but if there's room to move once you're ready to make something formal, I'd want to know before I close things out elsewhere."

## Closing move

"None of this is a blocker on my end. If the structure reflects the stage you're at, being early, lean, and already breakeven, and the geography question has a real answer, I'm ready to move quickly."
