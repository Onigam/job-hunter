# Negotiation Script — Nexthink / Principal Staff Software Engineer

---

## Market Context

Nexthink is a publicly traded company (NASDAQ: NXTP), headquartered in Lausanne, ~1,000+ employees, enterprise SaaS. Staff/Principal-level engineers at companies of this profile in the Lausanne/Geneva corridor command:

- **Base salary**: CHF 170,000 to CHF 210,000 (Staff), CHF 200,000 to CHF 230,000 (Principal)
- **RSUs / equity**: Public company, so stock-based compensation is RSUs, typically 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff. Expect CHF 40,000-80,000/year in RSU value at grant, depending on the role level.
- **Total compensation**: CHF 220,000 to CHF 310,000 is a realistic range for a Principal-level hire with 17+ years and a founding/staff background.
- **Bonus**: Some enterprise software companies add a 10-15% performance bonus on top; worth clarifying if it exists and whether it's discretionary or target-based.

Note: Lausanne is slightly cheaper than Geneva but both are tier-1 Swiss cities. The cost of living argument works both ways; don't volunteer it.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for your expectations, go to the top of the range with a brief justification:

> "Based on what I know about Principal-level compensation in Lausanne for someone with this background, I'd expect something in the CHF 200,000-220,000 base range, with equity on top. That's consistent with what I've seen at comparable enterprise software companies in Switzerland. Happy to discuss the full package structure."

Why this works: you anchor high without being aggressive, you signal you've done market research, and you invite them to share their structure rather than negotiating blind.

---

## If They Come in Low (base under CHF 170,000)

Don't reject outright. Counter with specifics:

> "I appreciate the offer. The base is lower than what I had in mind given my background and the scope of the role. I'm targeting CHF 195,000-205,000 for base. Is there flexibility there, or can we offset the gap through equity or a sign-on?"

If they cite budget constraints:

> "I understand there are constraints. One option is a sign-on that bridges year one while we align on base. Another is accelerating the first RSU vest. What levers do you have to work with?"

---

## RSU / Equity Negotiation (Public Company)

Since Nexthink is publicly traded, equity is RSUs, not options. Key asks:

- **Grant size**: Negotiate the initial grant size. A typical Staff grant might be CHF 150,000-200,000 total (4-year vest). Principal could be CHF 200,000-300,000. Ask for the total grant value and the vesting schedule upfront.
- **Cliff**: Standard is 1-year cliff. Acceptable. Don't fight this unless they ask for a 2-year cliff, which is unusual and worth pushing back on.
- **Refresh grants**: Ask when and how performance-based refresh grants work. At a public company this matters more than the initial grant over time.
- **Vesting acceleration**: Ask if there's single-trigger or double-trigger acceleration on change of control. For a company that could be acquired, this matters.

Script for RSU conversation:

> "What does the equity package look like? I'd want to understand the initial grant value, the vesting structure, and how refreshes work for performance."

---

## Sign-On

If base is below target and they have no room to move, ask for a sign-on:

> "If the base is fixed, a one-time sign-on could bridge the gap. I'm thinking CHF 20,000-30,000 to get to the equivalent of the first year of what I'd expect. That's a one-time cost for you versus a recurring delta."

---

## Geneva Premium / Commute Factor

Magino is in Geneva and Nexthink is in Lausanne. This is a 45-minute commute. If they offer relocation assistance or a remote-friendly arrangement, that changes the calculus. If the role is hybrid and they expect 3+ days in Lausanne, this adds real friction.

Talking point if hybrid expectations feel heavy:

> "I'm comfortable with a hybrid arrangement, though I'd want to align on what that looks like in practice. I'm in Geneva, so the commute to Lausanne is real. Two to three days a week in Lausanne is workable; more than that starts to affect the economics of the role for me."

This is also a mild negotiation lever: if they want more in-office, that's worth something.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in play (or want to create urgency):

> "I'm currently at the final stage with another company, and I'd like to move forward with whichever offer makes more sense by end of week. I don't want to play games — I'd genuinely prefer Nexthink if the numbers work. Can we close this out by Thursday?"

If the offer is hypothetical (no competing offer), don't lie. Instead use timeline pressure:

> "I'm actively in several processes and things are moving quickly. I want to make a decision in the next two weeks. Is there a way to accelerate the offer on your side?"

---

## Things to Clarify Before Accepting

- Exact role level (Principal vs. Staff has different comp bands)
- Bonus structure: discretionary or target-based, and at what percentage
- RSU grant value, vesting schedule, cliff, refresh policy
- Remote vs. hybrid expectations in days per week
- Health insurance contribution (LaMal is employee-paid in Switzerland; some employers contribute)
- Pension fund (2nd pillar): employer contribution percentage matters, some contribute more than the legal minimum
- Relocation assistance (not relevant here, but confirms they've thought about onboarding)
- Probation period: standard in Switzerland is 3 months; confirm it's not longer
