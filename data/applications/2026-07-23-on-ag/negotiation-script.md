# Negotiation Script: On AG, Principal Engineer (AI & Cloud Infrastructure)

## Context to keep in mind
On is a publicly traded company (NYSE: ONON), headquartered in Zurich, not an early-stage startup. That changes the equity conversation: expect RSUs on a standard vest, not founder-style options with a strangler cliff structure. The listing seen on SportsTechJobs quoted $155,000 to $200,000 as an "AI-calculated estimate," which reads low for a Principal-level infrastructure role in Zurich. Treat that number as a floor to anchor against, not a target.

## Swiss market anchor
Principal and Staff-level engineers at established tech and product companies in Zurich typically land base salary in the CHF 180,000 to CHF 230,000 range, before bonus and equity. Public companies with a real engineering brand (not banks or insurers, where comp structures differ) tend to sit toward the upper half of that band for infrastructure and AI platform roles, given how competitive Zurich hiring is for that skill set. Given 17+ years of experience, a founder background, and direct ownership of a platform through an acquisition, the opening ask should sit at CHF 220,000 to CHF 240,000 base, plus an annual bonus target of 15 to 20%, plus an initial RSU grant.

## Opening anchor script
"Based on what I'm seeing for Principal-level infrastructure and AI platform roles in Zurich, and factoring in the founder experience and the scope this role covers, I'd be looking at a base in the CHF 220,000 to 240,000 range, plus bonus and equity. Does that line up with the band you have for this level?"

## If they come in low
If the initial offer lands meaningfully under CHF 200,000 base: "I appreciate the offer. The scope here, owning AI infrastructure across serving, retrieval, and MLOps for the whole engineering org, is closer to what I'd expect to see priced at CHF 215,000 to 230,000 in this market. Is there room to move the base closer to that, or make it up through the RSU grant and sign-on?" If they hold firm on base, shift the ask to the equity grant size and a guaranteed first-year refresher rather than pushing base further; public companies often have more flexibility in equity than in published salary bands.

## Equity notes
RSUs at a public company vest on a schedule, usually four years with a one-year cliff, sometimes quarterly vesting after that cliff rather than a cliff plus monthly. Ask specifically: vesting schedule, whether the grant is annual or a one-time new-hire grant, and whether there's a refresher grant policy tied to performance reviews. Since ONON stock has moved meaningfully with the company's growth, ask for the grant to be described in dollar or CHF value at grant date, and get the vesting start date confirmed in writing, since a delayed start date can quietly cost a quarter of vesting.

## Geographic and relocation notes
Zurich cost of living is comparable to Geneva, so there's no discount to push back on the way there might be for a remote-from-elsewhere offer. If relocation is discussed, ask for a relocation allowance and, if not immediately overlapping, a temporary hybrid arrangement while the move is sorted out, since Geneva to Zurich is not a normal daily commute.

## Competing offer leverage template
"I'm currently in later-stage conversations with another company for a similar scope of work, and I want to be upfront that comp is one of the factors I'm weighing. I'd rather find the right number with On directly than let this turn into a bidding situation, so if there's flexibility on base, bonus, or the equity grant, now's the time to use it."
