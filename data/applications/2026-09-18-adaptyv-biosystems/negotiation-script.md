# Negotiation Script: Head of Software Engineering @ Adaptyv Biosystems

Context: Y Combinator-backed, early-stage (roughly 11-50 people), Lausanne-based biotech/techbio startup. Role is C-level/Director, player-coach, hands-on architecture plus team-building. No comp disclosed in the posting.

## Market anchors (Switzerland, Head of Engineering / Director level)

- Established Swiss tech companies (Logitech-scale, banks' tech arms, larger scale-ups) pay Director/VP Engineering roughly CHF 190k-240k base, sometimes higher in Zurich.
- Early-stage, YC-backed startups in Switzerland generally pay below that band on base, more like CHF 150k-190k, and make up the gap with equity and speed of vesting.
- Lausanne/Geneva-area cost of living justifies staying near the top of whatever band the stage supports; this isn't Berlin or Lisbon pricing.
- Biotech/techbio companies with hardware in the loop (lab automation, instrument control) tend to benchmark engineering leadership against general tech, not against life-sciences-specific pay scales, since the software problem is the same shape as any infra-heavy startup.
- A reasonable opening target for this role: CHF 195k-210k base, plus a meaningful equity grant given the stage and the C-level scope.

## Opening anchor

"Based on what I'm seeing for Head of Engineering / Director-level roles at Series A-to-B startups in Switzerland, and factoring in that this is a hands-on, architecture-owning seat rather than a purely managerial one, I'm targeting a base around CHF 205k, plus equity that reflects joining at this stage. I'm flexible on the mix if the equity or scope changes the picture."

Deliver this after they've shared a number or a range, not before. If asked to name a number first, give the range (195k-210k) rather than a single figure, and let equity be the variable that moves.

## If they come in low

"I hear you on the stage you're at, and I'm not trying to price this like a scaled company. But the base you're describing is closer to what I'd expect for a senior IC role, not one that owns the roadmap and the architecture across hardware and software. Is there room to close some of that gap through equity or a faster vesting start, if the cash isn't there yet?"

Fallback sequence if they hold firm on base:
1. Ask for equity percentage to move up instead of cash.
2. Ask about a 6-month or 12-month performance-based comp review built into the offer letter, not just implied verbally.
3. Ask about a signing bonus to bridge the first year if cash is genuinely constrained at seed/Series A.

## Equity negotiation

- At this stage, expect a range roughly 0.5%-2% for a Head of Engineering hire, depending on how early and how much of the option pool is already allocated. Ask directly what percentage of the fully diluted cap table the offer represents, not just a share count.
- Standard is a 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff; push back if they propose anything longer on the cliff.
- Ask about anti-dilution protection and whether there's a plan for a future option refresh as the company raises more rounds, since a Head of Engineering hire's equity gets diluted the same as everyone else's without one.
- Ask for the strike price and the most recent 409A or equivalent valuation used to set it, so the number means something concrete rather than a percentage on a slide.

## Geographic discount pushback

If they suggest a discount because Lausanne is "not as expensive as Zurich" or try to anchor to a lower-cost-of-living region: "Lausanne and Geneva track close to Zurich on housing and general cost of living, they're not a discount market. I'd want the offer benchmarked against Swiss rates generally, not against a remote or lower-cost location."

## Competing offer template (use only if genuinely applicable)

"I'm currently in later-stage conversations with another company at a similar level, and the comp there is in the CHF [X]k range plus [equity/other terms]. Adaptyv is my first choice because of the player-coach scope and the hardware problem, but I do need the offer to be competitive with what's on the table elsewhere before I can commit."

Only use this if there's an actual competing process; a fabricated offer is easy to see through and not worth the risk to the relationship.

## Close

Once the number lands somewhere workable, confirm the full package in writing before giving a verbal yes: base, equity percentage and vesting schedule, any signing bonus, start date, and title. Ask specifically whether "Head of Software Engineering" carries a formal C-level designation (CTO-equivalent) or sits a level below, since that affects both the negotiation and how the role reads externally later.
