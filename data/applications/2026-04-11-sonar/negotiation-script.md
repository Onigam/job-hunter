# Negotiation Script — Engineering Manager, Sonar
**Geneva, Switzerland · April 2026**

---

## Market Context

**Sonar company profile:**
- ~1,000 employees, global HQ Geneva, offices in Austin, Bochum, Singapore
- Backed by Insight Partners and General Catalyst (late-stage, profitable product company — not a startup burn-rate situation)
- SonarQube is a market-leading developer tools product with millions of daily users
- Revenue estimated CHF 150M+ ARR (2024 reports); not pre-revenue, not equity-dependent

**EM comp range in Geneva (2025-2026), IC-to-EM or experienced EM level:**
- Bottom of range (junior EM, first management role): CHF 140,000 – 155,000 base
- Market (solid EM, 3-5 years experience): CHF 165,000 – 185,000 base
- Strong (senior EM, 5+ years or significant IC depth): CHF 190,000 – 215,000 base
- Your target band: **CHF 195,000 – 215,000 base**

**Sonar's published benefits (from JD):**
- 13.5–15.5% base salary in pension savings (employer + employee combined; this is your Swiss BVG/LPP)
- 60% employer pension contribution within that
- Discretionary annual Company Growth Bonus (typically 10–15% for EM level)
- 27 PTO days (Geneva) + seniority additions
- 60% transportation refund
- Annual company kickoff

**Total package target:** CHF 215,000–235,000 base + 10–15% bonus + benefits
(Effective package value at CHF 210k base + 12% bonus + 15% benefits-in-kind ≈ CHF 255k–265k total)

---

## Your Leverage

1. **Dual-mode value (IC + EM):** You can still code. For a technical squad like Platform Team, this is rare and worth a premium. You're not a process manager who needs the team to carry the technical load.
2. **Zero ramp cost on platform thinking:** You've built and architected a platform from zero. You understand the EM role conceptually and technically. Day 30 is probably their Day 90 for a typical EM hire.
3. **Geneva-based, immediately available:** No relocation, no notice period negotiation, no timezone risk.
4. **Acquisition pedigree:** The platform you built survived a $1B+ M&A due diligence. That's a signal Sonar can verify.
5. **This is a product company, not a startup:** No equity pressure, which means cash negotiation is cleaner and less complex.

---

## Opening Anchor

When asked "What are your compensation expectations?":

> "Based on my research into the Geneva market for senior engineering managers, and given the combination of platform technical depth and team-building experience I bring, I'm targeting base compensation in the range of **CHF 210,000 to 220,000**. I'm open to discussing how the full package including bonus and benefits structures around that. What does the band look like for this role?"

**Why this works:**
- Anchors at the top of realistic range without being delusional
- Ends with a question — puts the ball back in their court
- Signals you've done research (not naive)

---

## If They Come Back Low (CHF 160–175k range)

> "I appreciate the transparency. That's below what I was expecting based on Geneva market data for this experience level. I want to make this work — can you help me understand what flexibility exists, either in base or in the annual bonus structure? I'd also want to understand the growth trajectory: if I hit the objectives in Year 1, what does a review cycle look like?"

**Then hold ground by returning to value:**
> "To be direct: the combination of having built and led a platform from zero, managed the engineering team through acquisition, and now being immediately available in Geneva — that's not a common profile. I want to be fair to both of us."

---

## If They Push Back on EM Experience (You Were IC, Not a Manager)

> "That's a fair question. I didn't have the title, but I had the full scope: I hired engineers, set the hiring bar, ran 1:1s and growth conversations, made scope and prioritization calls, and owned team delivery. I did it as a cofounder, which is harder — no HR playbook, no existing team, no org structure to fall back on. At Benevity I led cross-team initiatives with 15+ engineers across two teams who didn't report to me. I'd argue that's tougher than formal management, not easier."

---

## Equity / Bonus Negotiation

Sonar is **not** a startup — no options or warrants are likely. Focus on:

1. **Signing bonus:** If base is stuck below CHF 195k, push for a CHF 15,000–25,000 signing bonus to bridge the gap year one.
   > "If there's less flexibility on base, would a one-time joining bonus be possible to close the gap while I ramp?"

2. **Annual bonus:** Ask for the bonus to be formula-based rather than fully discretionary.
   > "Is the annual bonus tied to specific objectives, or is it fully at management discretion? I prefer clarity on what 'achieving objectives' looks like."

3. **Review timing:** Negotiate for a 6-month check-in rather than waiting 12 months.
   > "Given I'm coming in with significant experience, would you be open to a 6-month compensation review milestone tied to delivery targets? That gives both of us a clear moment to recalibrate."

## Geneva Cost of Living Context (Use If Needed)

> "Geneva is consistently ranked as one of the two most expensive cities in the world to live in. I've been here for 10 years — I have a concrete sense of what compensation makes sense for a stable, senior hire who's planning to stay."

Geneva cost benchmarks (2026):
- 3-bedroom apartment: CHF 3,500–5,000/month
- Full family health insurance: CHF 800–1,400/month (employer doesn't cover health in CH)
- Transport (annual zone pass): CHF 700–1,200
- Childcare (if applicable): CHF 2,500–4,000/month

Point: at CHF 160k gross, take-home in Geneva after taxes is approximately CHF 105k–115k/year. That's CHF 8,700–9,500/month. Livable, not comfortable for a senior hire with family costs.

## Competing Offer Leverage (If You Have One or Want to Create Urgency)

If you have another offer in process:
> "I want to be direct — I'm at a late stage with one other company and expect to have a decision in the next 2 weeks. I'm prioritizing Sonar because of the product, the team, and the Geneva location. But I want to be fair to both sides and not let this drag. If we can align on terms, I'd like to commit by end of next week."

If you don't have another offer, don't fabricate one. Use scarcity differently:
> "I'm being selective — I've turned down conversations with companies where the role or product wasn't the right fit. Sonar is one of the two or three companies I'm actively prioritizing. I'd like to move quickly."

---

## What Not to Do

- Don't give a single number. Give a range with your floor above your actual floor.
- Don't accept verbally in the meeting. Take 24–48 hours.
- Don't negotiate health insurance — it's not employer-provided in Switzerland. Don't waste political capital there.
- Don't let them anchor you to your current (or prior) salary at Benevity. The question "What are you making now?" can be redirected: "I'd rather talk about the value I bring to this role and what makes sense in the Geneva market for this scope."
