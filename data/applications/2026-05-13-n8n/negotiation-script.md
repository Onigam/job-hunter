# Negotiation Script — n8n Senior AI Engineer

_Geneva / Switzerland market. n8n is a German company offering Europe remote with local-norm compensation._

---

## Market Context

**n8n company stage:** Series C+, $2.5B valuation (2024), ~220 employees, well-funded. They're in growth mode, not survival mode. Budget exists.

**Role level:** Senior AI Engineer, IC. This maps to a Senior/Staff IC role in the Swiss market. n8n is hiring across Europe remote, which typically means they adapt to local cost-of-living. Verify this in early screening.

**Switzerland market rates for Senior AI Engineer (Geneva), 2026:**
- P50 (solid senior): CHF 150,000-165,000 base
- P75 (strong senior with specialization): CHF 165,000-185,000 base
- P90 (staff-level AI specialist, rare profile): CHF 185,000-210,000 base

Magino's profile (17 years, cofounder, platform-to-acquisition, AI tooling at enterprise scale) justifies a P75-P80 anchor.

**Opening anchor: CHF 175,000 base + equity**

Rationale for the anchor:
- Geneva is the most expensive labor market in Switzerland (and one of the most expensive in Europe)
- 17 years of experience with founding-engineer depth
- Production LLM/AI feature delivery is genuinely rare; most "AI engineers" in 2024-2026 are prompt wrappers, not system builders
- n8n's product depends on AI reliability; this isn't a nice-to-have skill set for them

---

## Opening Conversation Script

When they ask for expectations early in the process (HR screen or recruiter):

> "I'm targeting around CHF 175,000 base for a Geneva-based senior role. I know you adapt to local markets, so I want to make sure we're calibrated early. That's aligned with what the Geneva market looks like for senior AI engineers with a founding background. I'm also interested in how equity is structured, since for a company at n8n's stage that's a meaningful part of the package."

If they push back immediately on the number:

> "That's the Geneva market rate for this seniority level. If the budget is structured around a different geography's rates, I'd want to understand the logic, because my cost of living doesn't adjust to where your headquarters is."

---

## Counter-Offer Scripts

**Scenario A: They come in at CHF 130,000-145,000 (German or UK-adjusted)**

> "That's a meaningful gap from the number I shared. CHF 130k is a reasonable rate for a senior engineer in Berlin or London, but Geneva is structurally different. Rent alone for a two-bedroom in central Geneva runs CHF 3,000-3,500/month. To make this work, I'd need to get to CHF 165,000 at minimum, plus meaningful equity. Is there flexibility in how you structure local-market adjustments?"

**Scenario B: They come in at CHF 150,000-160,000**

> "That's closer. I'd like to work with CHF 170,000, and I want to understand the equity portion more precisely. At n8n's current stage, the options could be genuinely significant, and that changes how I think about the total package. Can you walk me through the current strike price, the vesting schedule, and approximately what percentage this grant represents?"

**Scenario C: They offer exactly what you anchored (CHF 175,000)**

Don't accept immediately. Take 24 hours. Then:

> "I'm comfortable with the base. I'd like to push on the equity side before I sign. Given the company's current trajectory, I'd like to see if there's room to add 20-30% to the initial grant. That's the negotiation I'd rather have now than in two years."

---

## Equity Negotiation

n8n is pre-IPO with $2.5B valuation. Options at this stage are meaningful but uncertain. Key questions to ask:

1. "What's the current 409A/strike price for new grants?"
2. "What's the vesting schedule, and is there a standard cliff?" (Standard: 4-year vest, 1-year cliff)
3. "What percentage of fully-diluted shares does a typical senior IC grant represent?"
4. "Is there a liquidity event planned in the next 24 months, or are employees expected to hold long-term?"
5. "What happens to unvested options if the company is acquired?" (double-trigger acceleration is what you want)

**Anchor for equity at Senior IC level:**
- 0.05-0.15% of fully diluted shares is reasonable for a senior IC at a Series C company
- n8n is well beyond early-stage, so don't expect co-founder-level grants, but 0.1% would be a reasonable ask if the base is on the lower end of range

**If they can't move on cash, ask for more equity:**

> "If CHF 165,000 is the ceiling on base, I'd like to offset that with a larger equity grant. A 25% increase in the option count would bring the total expected value closer to where I need it to be. Is that something you can run by the comp committee?"

---

## Geographic Premium Pushback

n8n may try to apply German market rates. This is common with European remote companies. Script:

> "I understand your base comp bands may be anchored to Berlin. I want to be transparent: I'm in Geneva and I've been offered roles at Swiss market rates by other companies in my pipeline. I'm genuinely interested in n8n specifically, which is why I'm having this conversation rather than just accepting another offer. But I can't take a meaningful discount to German rates just to work remote."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage (if applicable)

If you have a real competing offer, use it directly:

> "I want to be straight with you: I have an offer at CHF [X] from [Company type: 'a Series B developer tools company']. I'd rather be at n8n because [specific reason]. If you can match or come within 10% of that number, I'm ready to commit. If not, I'll have to go with what's in hand."

If you don't have a competing offer, don't fabricate one. Instead:

> "I'm actively in process with two other companies that are further along in the timeline. I'm not using that as pressure, but I do need to make a decision within two weeks. Being direct about the number would help me prioritize this properly."

---

## Benefits to Push On

n8n's posted benefits are decent but negotiable at the margin:

- **Start date**: ask for a 4-week notice period if your current role needs it
- **Equipment budget**: standard CHF 1,500-2,500 for home office setup; ask if it's not listed
- **Development budget**: they offer 1,000 EUR/year; ask if this can be increased for conference travel or certifications
- **First-year review**: ask for a 9-month performance review with a salary adjustment conversation baked in, rather than waiting 12 months

---

## Red Lines

Walk away if:
- Base is below CHF 145,000 with no equity upside justifying the gap
- No double-trigger acceleration on options in an acquisition scenario
- They refuse to disclose strike price or fully-diluted share count before signing
- Probation period longer than 3 months with termination rights that would leave you unprotected
