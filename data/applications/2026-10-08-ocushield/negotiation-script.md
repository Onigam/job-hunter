# Negotiation Script: Senior Founding Software Engineer, Ocushield

## Read this first: two things decide whether this is worth negotiating

**1. Eligibility.** The posting calls this a hard requirement: candidates must be based in the EU, EEA or UK with the right to work there. Switzerland isn't in the EU or EEA. Holding an EU passport doesn't change where you live, and a Geneva resident working full-time for a UK company normally falls under Swiss social security, which means Ocushield would need an employer of record (EOR) in Switzerland or a contractor setup. Raise this in the first call, before any numbers. If the answer is "EU/UK residents only, no exceptions", stop there.

**2. The pay gap.** The posted base is **€55,000 to €85,000**, "benchmarked to the candidate's location". That's roughly CHF 51k to 80k at current rates (check the rate on the day). A senior engineer in Geneva usually earns about twice that. The posting is upfront about it: no FAANG pay, bootstrapped, no venture-style trajectory. So the real question is whether the equity, the scope (first engineer, path to VP Eng/CTO) and fully remote work are worth a large cash cut. Decide your floor before the first call, not during it.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough ranges from public comp data. Re-check on swissdevjobs.ch, levels.fyi, Glassdoor and the Swiss salary calculator (Salarium) before quoting any of them.

| Benchmark | Base (approx.) | Equity |
|---|---|---|
| Ocushield posted band (EU/UK remote) | €55k to €85k | unspecified |
| Senior / lead engineer, Geneva, scale-up | CHF 140k to 175k | small or none |
| Founding engineer, Swiss seed/Series A startup | CHF 130k to 170k | ~0.5% to 2% |
| Staff engineer, Geneva or Lausanne | CHF 170k to 210k | + equity |
| Senior engineer, fully remote for a UK startup | £75k to £100k | options |

Geneva costs are why the numbers sit where they do: rent, mandatory health insurance you pay yourself (LAMal), and a salary-linked pension (2nd pillar) that an EOR may or may not fund properly.

**Suggested positions (adjust to your own floor):**
- **Opening anchor:** €120k base (about CHF 112k). Still well below Geneva rates, so it's easy to justify.
- **Target:** €100k to €110k base plus a meaningful, clearly documented equity grant.
- **Walk-away floor:** your call, but anything under about €95k only makes sense if the equity is substantial and the terms are written down.
- **If hired via EOR or as a contractor:** add 15 to 20% to cover employer social charges, pension and holidays you'd carry yourself, and confirm who pays the EOR fee. As a contractor, convert to a day rate: €110k ÷ ~210 working days ≈ €525/day before those extras, so aim for €600 to €650/day.

## Step 1: eligibility and band (first call with Dhruvin)

"Before we get into the role, one practical thing. I live in Geneva, which isn't in the EU or EEA. Is that a hard stop, or have you hired outside the EU/UK through an employer of record or a contractor agreement? I'd rather we know now than three interviews in."

If they're open to it, follow with:

"The posting says salary is benchmarked to location. For Geneva that benchmark is a lot higher than the €85k top of the range, so I'd like to understand how flexible the band is before we go further."

If they ask for your number first:

"For a founding engineer owning the whole function from Geneva, I'm at around €120k base, plus equity. I know that's above the posted range. It's still a fair bit below what this scope pays in Switzerland, and I'm open to talking about how cash and equity balance out."

## Step 2: if they come in low

"Thanks for being straight with me. €[their number] works for a senior IC elsewhere in Europe. This role is different: I'd be taking over the platform from the external team, running both platforms in parallel during cutover, owning SSO, partner APIs and GDPR, and hiring engineer #2. That's a tech-lead and future-CTO scope. Could we get to €110k? If cash really can't move, I'd want the difference reflected in the equity."

If they hold firm on cash:

"Understood, you're bootstrapped and I respect running the business on profit. Then let's be specific about equity: what percentage, on what terms, and what does the 'credible path to payout' in the posting actually look like?"

Other levers if base is capped:
- A salary review at 6 months tied to the cutover being finished, with the number agreed now.
- A step-up when engineer #2 is hired or when the title moves to Head of Engineering.
- Profit share or an annual bonus, which a profitable company can actually pay.
- Budget for home office, coworking and the travel to meet-ups (the posting already covers travel).

## Step 3: pushing back on the geographic discount

If they say they pay EU rates regardless of location:

"I get why you'd want one band. But the posting benchmarks to location, and for Geneva the benchmark is around CHF 140k to 175k for a senior engineer, before any founding scope. I'm not asking for Swiss rates; I'm asking to meet in the middle, given I'd carry the cost of living here."

If they say a candidate in Portugal or Poland would be cheaper:

"That's true, and you should weigh it. What you'd get from me is someone who's already done this exact path: built a B2B platform from zero, sold to enterprise buyers, hired the first engineers and taken it through an acquisition. That's the risk you're paying to remove."

## Step 4: equity notes (bootstrapped and profitable, which changes the usual playbook)

There's no VC on the cap table, so the usual startup worries (liquidation preference stacks, down rounds) matter less. The bigger risk is the opposite: no exit is planned, so options could sit worthless for years. Ask these questions and get the answers in writing.

- **What, exactly.** A percentage of fully diluted shares, not just a number of options. For a first engineer at a small profitable company, ~1% to 3% is a reasonable ask given the pay cut; trade cash for a point at the higher end.
- **Instrument.** EMI options are the UK default, but their tax benefits generally require being a UK employee. If you're hired via EOR or contract, ask about unapproved options, growth shares or phantom equity, and get Swiss tax advice, because Switzerland taxes options in its own way (often at exercise).
- **How it pays out without an exit.** Since the company is profitable, ask whether equity holders share in dividends, whether there's a buyback policy, or whether phantom equity pays out on profit. "A credible path to meaningful equity payout" should come with a concrete answer.
- **Vesting.** Four years with a one-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Acceleration.** Double-trigger acceleration (sale of the company plus losing the role) is a fair ask for a founding hire.
- **Leaver terms.** Good leaver should keep vested shares. Watch for bad-leaver clauses that are broad enough to cover a normal resignation.
- **Exercise window.** Push for 5 to 10 years after leaving rather than 90 days, or at least 12 months.
- **Dilution.** Employees rarely get anti-dilution protection. Instead ask for information rights: the cap table now, and notice of any new share issues. If they bring in investors later, ask for a top-up grant review.

## Step 5: using a competing offer (template)

Only use this if it's real. If it isn't, use the market data instead.

"I want to be transparent. I have another offer at CHF [X] base for a [Staff/founding] role in Switzerland. I'd prefer Ocushield; the scope and the chance to build the engineering function from the start are why I'm here. But the gap is large. If we can get to €[target] with [equity terms], I'm ready to commit this week."

Without a competing offer:

"I'm in conversations with a few Swiss companies where the base is meaningfully higher. I'm not using that as a threat; I'd just like the offer to be close enough that the choice is about the role, not the money."

## Before you sign

- Confirm the employing entity (UK company, EOR, or contract) and who handles Swiss social security and pension.
- Get the equity terms in the offer letter or a signed term sheet, not a promise to sort it later.
- Check the notice period and any non-compete, especially if you'd also do other work.
- Confirm the title (and the path to VP Engineering/CTO), the reporting line to Dhruvin, and the budget and timing for engineer #2.
