# Negotiation Script: JetBrains, Founding ML Engineer (Spectrum)

No salary is disclosed. The posting says "competitive salary and JetBrains benefits" and lists the role in several EU hubs (Munich, Madrid, Belgrade, Cyprus, Netherlands remote). JetBrains doesn't advertise a Swiss office, so the first thing to settle is where you'd be employed, because that decides which pay band you land in. The figures below are estimates from public comp data; check levels.fyi, Glassdoor and anyone you know at JetBrains before the first comp call.

## 1. Settle the location question first

Three likely setups, from best to worst for you:

1. **Employed in Switzerland** (Swiss entity or employer of record). Swiss band, Swiss social security, you stay in Geneva.
2. **Employed in Munich, working partly remote from Geneva.** German band, relocation or a commuting arrangement.
3. **Contractor from Switzerland.** Watch this one: you'd carry your own social charges and lose benefits, so the day rate needs to be about 25-30% above the salaried equivalent just to break even.

Ask early: "Can JetBrains employ me in Switzerland, or would this be a Munich contract?"

## 2. Market picture (2026 estimates)

| Reference point | Base | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Munich, senior ML / AI engineer at a product company | EUR 90k to 120k | JetBrains typically adds an annual bonus |
| Munich, ML lead / founding-level role | EUR 120k to 160k | This posting is marked management level |
| Zurich/Geneva, senior ML engineer at a scale-up | CHF 160k to 200k | |
| Zurich, ML lead or Staff ML at large tech | CHF 210k to 270k | Liquid RSUs push total comp well above base |

JetBrains is privately held and doesn't hand out public-company RSUs, so cash and bonus carry more weight here than at big tech.

## 3. Your targets

If employed in Switzerland:
- Walk-away base: CHF 185k
- Target base: CHF 210k to 225k
- Opening anchor: CHF 235k

If employed in Munich:
- Walk-away base: EUR 125k
- Target base: EUR 140k to 150k
- Opening anchor: EUR 160k

Keep in mind that your ML depth is the soft spot they could use to pull the level down. Your justification rests on the founding side: 17 years in platform systems, five years running engineering as cofounder through to an acquisition by a $1B+ company, hiring the first engineers, and recent hands-on LLM, embeddings and multi-agent work. You'd be building the stack and the team, which is more than an IC ML role.

## 4. Opening the conversation

If they ask for expectations first:

> "For a founding role where I'd own the ML stack and build the team, and based in Switzerland, I'm looking at a base around CHF 235k plus the standard bonus. I'm open on how the package is structured, and I'd want to understand the incubator side before we settle anything."

If they insist on a single number, repeat it. Don't offer a range, since they'll anchor on its bottom.

## 5. If the offer comes in low

> "Thanks, I appreciate it and I want to make this work. The base is below what founding-level roles pay in Switzerland, and this one covers both the ML architecture and building the team. If you can get to CHF 220k, I'm ready to move forward."

If they say the band is fixed:

> "Understood. If base can't move, could we look at a signing bonus for the first year, a higher bonus target, or a guaranteed compensation review after twelve months tied to Spectrum's milestones?"

Never accept on the call. "Let me look at the full package and come back to you by Thursday" is always fine.

## 6. Pushing back on a geographic discount

If they benchmark a Geneva-based hire against Munich or Belgrade pay:

> "I understand the bands differ by country. Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, though, and Swiss employer costs are part of hiring here. I'm not asking for a premium over the Swiss market, just to be paid at it. If the Swiss number doesn't work, I'd rather talk about a Munich contract with a sensible hybrid arrangement than a Swiss one at German pay."

## 7. Equity and incubator upside

Spectrum is a JetBrains incubator project, so ordinary startup equity may not exist. Ask these directly rather than assuming:

- Is there any ownership stake if Spectrum spins out or raises outside money? Phantom shares, a spin-out option pool, or a pre-agreed founder allocation?
- If it's option-style, what's the vesting schedule and cliff (4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard), and what happens on a spin-out, acceleration on a change of control, or dilution in outside rounds?
- If Spectrum is shut down or folded back into JetBrains, is there a guaranteed role elsewhere in the company, and does the bonus survive?
- Are there milestone bonuses tied to product or revenue targets, given that "founding" usually means taking on startup risk?

If there's no equity at all, say so out loud and ask for it back in cash:

> "Founding roles usually come with upside that makes up for the risk. If there's no ownership component here, I'd expect that reflected in base or bonus."

## 8. Competing offer template

Use only with a real process in play, or phrase it as market context rather than inventing an offer.

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations for a Staff platform role in Switzerland at around CHF [X] base. Spectrum is my first choice because of the problem and the founding scope, and I'd like to make it work. Is there room to close the gap on base or bonus?"

## 9. Non-cash items worth asking for

- Clarity on remote days if you're employed in Munich
- Relocation support or a travel budget for regular Munich visits
- Conference and research budget, given the collaboration with JetBrains Research
- Hardware and GPU/API budget for experiments
- Headcount plan: how many ML hires you can make in the first year, and whether you'll lead them
