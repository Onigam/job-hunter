# Negotiation Script — Principal AI/ML Engineer (Undisclosed AI Startup via Pragmatike)

## Role Context

This is a US-based company (Cambridge, MA), founded by MIT CSAIL researchers, recognized as a Top 10 GenAI company by GTM Capital, serving Fortune 500 clients. This is a Principal-level IC role — not entry ML or mid-level. The comp structure will be base + equity + likely a sign-on (mentioned in the posting).

Remote appears to be an option. Whether they expect US-based remote or are open to Switzerland-based remote will shape the negotiation significantly.

---

## Questions to Ask Before Naming a Number

1. Is this role open to fully remote, or is there an expectation of on-site presence in Cambridge?
2. What is the company's current funding stage and total raised to date?
3. How large is the current engineering team, and how many ML engineers specifically?
4. What does the equity structure look like for Principal-level hires (option pool, typical grant size, vesting)?
5. Is there a timeline on their next funding round, and how does that affect equity pricing?

These are standard due-diligence questions. If they're evasive, that's information.

---

## Market Anchors

**US market — Principal AI/ML Engineer, Cambridge MA / remote (2025-2026):**
- Base: $220,000 - $270,000
- Total comp (base + equity + bonus): $280,000 - $380,000+
- Sign-on bonus: typically $20,000 - $50,000 at this level
- Equity at a Series A-B startup: 0.25% - 0.75% fully diluted for a Principal hire

**Switzerland market — equivalent level, for context:**
- CHF 180,000 - 230,000 base
- AI/ML specialist premium: +10-15% above equivalent non-AI roles
- Geneva is one of the most expensive cities globally; do not accept a "European remote discount" framing

**Key anchor:** If they plan to pay on a Swiss/European scale despite this being a US company, push back. A Principal AI/ML role at a US-funded, Fortune-500-facing startup should comp on US rates regardless of where the engineer is located, especially at this seniority level.

---

## Opening Anchor

**When they ask your expectations:**

"For a Principal AI/ML role at this level and at a company with Fortune 500 deployments, I'm thinking in the range of $240,000-$260,000 base, with equity commensurate with a Principal IC hire. I'd also expect the sign-on you mentioned in the posting to be part of the conversation. That said, I'd like to understand the full package and the equity terms before landing on a specific number — the options structure matters as much to me as the base."

**Why this works:** It anchors at the high end of realistic without being absurd, signals you've done your research, and pivots to total comp rather than just salary.

---

## If They Come In Low on Base (under $200,000)

"I appreciate the offer. The base is below where I'd expect to land for a Principal AI/ML role at a company of this profile. Given my background — building production AI systems, leading ML adoption at scale, and 17+ years at Senior Staff and founding-engineer level — I'd need to be closer to $230,000-$240,000. Is there flexibility there? If base is constrained, I'd want to offset that with a larger equity grant and a stronger sign-on."

Hold the line: do not accept a below-market base with a "we'll revisit at your next review" promise.

---

## If They Try to Apply a Geographic Discount (Geneva = lower pay)

"I understand remote comp varies by company policy. For reference, Geneva is consistently ranked among the most expensive cities globally, comparable to Zurich, and well above London or Berlin. I'm also operating in the Swiss tax environment. I wouldn't expect to take a geographic haircut for a Principal-level role — if anything, that context reinforces why market rate matters. I'd benchmark this against comparable US remote offers at this level, not against European averages."

If they persist: reference that AI/ML Principal roles in Switzerland from US companies pay in the CHF 190,000-230,000 range, which converts to roughly $210,000-$255,000 at current rates — consistent with the US range.

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Target terms for a Principal-level AI/ML hire:**
- Grant size: 0.3% - 0.6% fully diluted (higher if early-stage or if this is effectively a founding ML role)
- Vesting: 4-year with 1-year cliff (standard; don't accept anything shorter on the cliff)
- Acceleration: double-trigger acceleration on acquisition — this is standard and worth asking for explicitly
- Strike price: at the current 409A valuation, confirmed in writing
- Refresh cadence: ask whether there's a policy for refresh grants tied to performance reviews

**On dilution:** You won't get anti-dilution protection as an employee. What matters is that your initial grant is large enough that post-Series A/B dilution still leaves something meaningful. If they grant 0.3% and expect two more funding rounds, model out what that looks like at 10-15% dilution per round before agreeing.

---

## Sign-On Bonus

They listed it in the posting. That means it exists. Treat it as a negotiable line item.

"What's the sign-on bonus range you're working with? Since relocation logistics and tax timing are factors for a Geneva-based hire, I'd want to make sure we structure that appropriately."

Target: $25,000 - $40,000. This is normal for Principal-level at a funded startup with Fortune 500 revenue.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have other active processes:

"I'm in late stages with another company in a comparable profile. I'd prefer to move forward here if we can align on terms — I'd rather not drag this out. What's your timeline to finalize the offer?"

If you're in early stages elsewhere:

"I'm currently speaking with two other companies at a similar level. I expect their offers will come in around the $240,000 range. I'm not trying to play games, but I want to be transparent so we can move efficiently if there's alignment."

---

## Walk-Away Numbers

**Base floor:** $210,000 if equity is strong (0.4%+ fully diluted with clean terms)
**Equity floor:** 0.25% fully diluted with standard vesting, if base is $240,000+
**Sign-on floor:** $20,000 (don't leave it on the table — it was in the posting)

Do not accept: below-market base + small equity grant + "we'll revisit at the next round." That is three uncertainties stacked on top of each other.

---

## Final Closing Move

"If you can get me to $245,000 base, 0.4% equity on a 4-year/1-year cliff with double-trigger acceleration, and a $30,000 sign-on, I'm ready to commit. Those are terms I can plan around long-term."

Give them one clear number. Ambiguity at the closing stage costs time on both sides.
