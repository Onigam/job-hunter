# Negotiation Script — Quin (Founding Engineer / CTO)

---

## Context

Quin is pre-scale, founder-led (Neil, CEO), with a CTO (Jeff) already in place. They've validated GTM and are raising. The job posting says "salary post-raise + meaningful equity," which means the salary will improve after a raise closes but isn't set yet, and they're offering equity as a main component of the package.

This is a founding engineer role with an explicit CTO ceiling. The comp negotiation should be anchored to that ceiling, not to a mid-level engineering hire.

---

## Market Anchors

**Switzerland / Geneva market (what Magino currently works in):**
- Senior Staff Engineer at scale-up: CHF 160,000-190,000
- Founding Engineer / Head of Engineering at startup: CHF 170,000-210,000
- CTO at early-stage startup (Series A): CHF 200,000-250,000

**US market (where the role is anchored, Bay Area / NYC):**
- Founding Engineer at seed-stage startup: $160,000-200,000 base
- CTO at seed-stage: $180,000-230,000 base (often lower base, compensated by equity)
- Geneva COL is in the same tier as San Francisco, so no geographic discount is appropriate

**Equity benchmarks for founding engineer:**
- True founding engineer (first hire): 0.5%-2.0% depending on company stage
- Post-seed, first engineering hire: 0.5%-1.0% is common, 1.0%-1.5% is strong but achievable with good negotiation
- Standard vesting: 4-year vest, 1-year cliff
- Ask about accelerated vesting on acquisition (double trigger preferred)

---

## Opening Anchor

When they make an offer, don't respond immediately. Say you'd like a day to review.

Then come back with:

> "Based on the founding engineer scope and the CTO pathway you described, I'd be looking at a base in the $190,000-210,000 range once the raise closes, with 1.0%-1.5% equity on a 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff. I know early stage means flexibility on both sides, and I'm genuinely more focused on the equity and the scope than on squeezing the base. But I want to be transparent about where my expectations sit so we don't waste each other's time."

---

## If They Come In Low on Base

If the initial offer is below $150,000 (or CHF equivalent):

> "I appreciate the offer. I want to make this work, but the base is meaningfully below what I'd need to make a move at this stage. I'm not trying to hold the role hostage over base, but I'm comparing this against my current salary at Benevity and what the Switzerland market looks like for someone at my level. Can we talk about whether there's room to get closer to $175,000-190,000, even if some of that comes post-raise? Or alternatively, if the equity number can compensate, I'd be open to that conversation."

Key move: always offer an alternative path (more equity) rather than making it binary.

---

## If They Come In Low on Equity

If the equity offer is below 0.5%:

> "I want to flag the equity number. You described this as a founding engineer hire, and the CTO pathway was part of what made this interesting. For someone who's going to build the engineering foundation from scratch, 0.5% or below is more consistent with a mid-stage hire than a founding engineer. Is there room to get above 0.75%, ideally to 1.0%? I'd rather take a slightly lower base and have equity that genuinely reflects the risk and scope of what I'm walking into."

---

## Equity Questions to Ask

1. What's the current cap table structure, and what's the total option pool size?
2. Has the pool already been allocated to advisors or early team members?
3. What's the preference on double-trigger acceleration in an acquisition scenario?
4. If I grow into the CTO role, does the equity refresh or is this a one-time grant?
5. What's the current valuation and the post-raise valuation target?

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they mention that Geneva-based candidates expect a discount vs US rates:

> "Geneva is consistently ranked alongside San Francisco and Zurich as one of the most expensive cities in the world. The COL comparison doesn't support a discount. The parallel is closer to hiring someone in New York than hiring someone in Lisbon. I'd push back on the framing that Swiss-based means lower cost to the company."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have other processes running in parallel, reference them without fabricating specifics:

> "I want to be straightforward with you: I'm at final stages with another company in the AI infrastructure space, and the package they're putting together is in the range I mentioned. I prefer Quin because of the scope and the founding engineer angle, and I'd rather figure out how to make this work than default to the other offer. But I do need the numbers to be in range."

If you don't have a competing offer yet, use a version like:

> "I'm being careful about the move because my current situation at Benevity is stable, and I'm only open to leaving for the right founding engineer opportunity. That means the package needs to reflect the step-up in scope and risk I'm taking."

---

## Timeline

- Don't accept on the spot. Always ask for 48-72 hours.
- Once you have an offer in writing, treat the clock as real, but you can push it 24 hours once without damaging the relationship.
- Get everything in writing (base, equity percentage, vesting schedule, cliff, acceleration clauses) before signing.
