# Negotiation Script: Nexthink, Principal/Staff Software Engineer (Lausanne)

## Market anchor

The posting carries a dual title, Principal/Staff, which usually means Nexthink hasn't fully decided the level and will calibrate based on the interview loop. That's useful for you: don't self-select into the lower band before they tell you where they've landed. Generic "software engineer" salary aggregators for Lausanne quote roughly CHF 95k-125k, but those numbers describe mid-level IC roles at companies without a mature platform, not a Principal/Staff engineer expected to set technical direction on an AI system serving 1,300+ customers and around 18 million employees. With 17+ years of experience, a founding-engineer track record, and an acquisition by a unicorn as a credibility marker, anchor toward the Principal end of the range:

- **Target range:** CHF 175,000-205,000 base
- **Floor (walk-away consideration point):** CHF 165,000 base
- **Stretch anchor (opening ask):** CHF 210,000 base

Total comp with bonus should land CHF 20k-35k above base. Nexthink is a profitable, ~1,200-person company post-unicorn round, not an early-stage startup, so the number to fight for is base and bonus target, with equity treated as a smaller add-on rather than the main event.

## Opening anchor

If asked for a number first, don't just state a figure, tie it to scope:

"Given the Principal/Staff scope here, setting technical direction on the AI chat platform rather than just implementing against a spec, and given the Lausanne market for that level, I'm looking at a base in the CHF 205,000-210,000 range plus the standard bonus target. That reflects 17-plus years of experience, a founding-engineer background that included an acquisition, and the kind of cross-team architecture influence this role is asking for."

If pushed for a single number, give CHF 205,000, the top of the target range, not the midpoint. It's much easier to negotiate down from a high anchor than to claw your way back up from a low one.

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

If the offer lands below CHF 170,000 base:

"I appreciate the offer, but it reads closer to a Staff-level number than the Principal/Staff scope described in the role, setting direction on a platform meant to serve millions of users usually moves compensation up, not flat. Based on what I'm seeing for comparable Principal roles in the Lausanne and Geneva area, I was anchored closer to CHF 195,000-205,000 base. Is there room to move toward that, either on base or through the bonus structure?"

If base is fixed but there's flexibility elsewhere:

"If base can't move right now, I'd want to look at a sign-on bonus or a higher annual bonus target to close the gap. A one-time sign-on around CHF 12,000-15,000 would make the package work for me this year."

## Equity notes

Nexthink is a mature, later-stage private company that already hit unicorn valuation in 2021, so any equity offer will likely be a modest RSU or phantom-share grant rather than founder-style options with real multiple potential. Treat it as a bonus, not a substitute for base:

- Ask directly whether the grant is RSUs, options, or phantom shares. Swiss tax treatment differs meaningfully between them, and that changes what the number is actually worth.
- Ask for the vesting schedule, standard is four years with a one-year cliff, and whether any acceleration applies on a future liquidity event.
- If it's options, ask for the strike price and the most recent valuation reference so the grant means something concrete rather than a headline number.
- Don't let an equity grant justify accepting a below-market base. Base is what compounds into pension contributions here in Switzerland, and it's the number that's actually guaranteed.

## Geographic discount pushback

Some companies try to apply a Geneva or remote-adjacent discount to a role that's really based in Lausanne. Push back directly if that comes up:

"This role is based in Lausanne, so I'd expect Lausanne market rates to apply. I'm coming from Geneva, which is a similarly high cost-of-living area, so there's no case for a discounted band here just because I'm not relocating from somewhere cheaper."

## Competing offer template

Only use this if there's an actual second process running. Experienced recruiters tend to spot a vague or invented competing offer quickly:

"I'm further along in another process at a similar level right now, so I've got a timeline I'm working against. I'd like to keep this moving on both sides, and if Nexthink can get closer to the range we discussed, I'd be glad to prioritize this one over the other."

## Practical notes

- Negotiate total comp as one package, base, bonus target, sign-on, equity, rather than line by line. It leaves more room to trade pieces against each other.
- Confirm early whether the role settles at Staff or Principal before the offer stage; that answer should drive which end of the range you push for.
- Ask how the hybrid or in-office expectation for Lausanne actually works day to day, and whether any commuting support exists for people based in Geneva.
- Get any verbal commitment in writing before signing, especially the level, sign-on bonus, and start date.
