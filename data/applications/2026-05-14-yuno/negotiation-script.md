# Negotiation Script — Senior Platform Engineer, AI Agent Infrastructure at Yuno

---

## Market Context

**Role:** Senior Platform Engineer, AI Agent Infrastructure (senior IC, owns agent platform infra and observability)
**Company:** Yuno — VC-backed payments orchestration startup (KASZEK-backed, estimated Series B)
**Location:** Fully remote (Geneva-based candidate)
**Currency:** CHF preferred; Yuno likely quotes in USD given Latin American operations — push for CHF or USD equivalent at current exchange rate

**Switzerland market rates for Senior/Staff Platform Engineer at VC-backed startup (2026):**
- P50 range: CHF 140,000-165,000 base
- P75 range: CHF 165,000-195,000 base
- AI infrastructure specialization premium: +10-15% above generic platform engineering
- Geneva cost of living: among the highest in Europe; no geographic discount applies

**Target opening anchor:** CHF 185,000-195,000 base + equity
**Acceptable floor:** CHF 155,000 base with meaningful equity (0.10%+ on 4-year vest)
**Walk-away:** Below CHF 140,000 base with weak or no equity

---

## Opening Anchor Script

Use this when they ask "what are your salary expectations?" or "what range are you targeting?"

> "For a senior platform engineering role with this scope — production AI agent infrastructure, owned architecture, observability from scratch — I'm targeting CHF 185,000-195,000 base. That's consistent with what senior engineers with AI infrastructure specialization are commanding in the Swiss market. I'm also assuming there's equity in the package; what does that look like at Yuno?"

**Why the anchor works:**
- Grounded in Swiss market rates, not inflated
- Frames AI infra as a specialization premium, which it is
- Opens the equity conversation before they anchor it

---

## If They Quote in USD

Yuno may quote USD given their LATAM and US-crossover investor base. Don't accept a USD number as if it were CHF.

> "I want to make sure we're comparing the right numbers. I'm based in Geneva, so my costs are in CHF. At current exchange rates CHF 185,000 is roughly USD [X]. If you're quoting in USD I'd want the equivalent to land in that range, because a USD salary that looks similar on paper has different purchasing power here."

---

## If They Come In Below CHF 160,000 Base

Do not accept or counter immediately. Pause, then:

> "I appreciate the transparency, but that's below where I need to be given the scope and Geneva cost of living. I'm not saying it can't work, but I'd want to understand the full picture first: equity terms, vesting, and whether there's a structured review cycle. Can we walk through those?"

Then listen. If they offer meaningful equity:

> "Okay. If the base is at [their number], the equity would need to close some of that gap in expected value terms. Can you share the current 409A valuation, total fully diluted share count, and the option pool for this role? I want to work from real numbers rather than percentages."

---

## Counter-Offer Script (Standard)

If they offer CHF 165,000 and you want CHF 185,000:

> "I think we're close. My number was CHF 185,000, and here's the reasoning: this role is building the reliability and observability layer for AI agent infrastructure in production, which is a genuine specialization — it's not generic backend work. The combination of platform engineering depth and hands-on LLM integration experience narrows the candidate pool significantly, and that has market value. If the base is hard to move, I'm open to discussing a signing bonus or accelerated cliff on the first equity tranche."

---

## Equity Negotiation

**For a Series B startup (estimated 100-300 employees):**
- Target: 0.08%-0.15% of fully diluted shares (ask for share count AND percentage; percentages shift with future rounds)
- Standard vesting: 4 years, 1-year cliff
- Ask for: double-trigger acceleration on change of control (if the company is acquired while you're employed and your role is eliminated or materially changed within 12 months, 100% of unvested shares accelerate)

**Script:**

> "On equity: can you share the total fully diluted share count and the latest 409A valuation? I want to work from real numbers. For a senior IC at this stage, I'd expect something in the 0.10%-0.15% range on a 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff. I'd also want double-trigger acceleration written into the grant — you're in payments, which is a natural acquisition target, and that clause is standard at this level."

---

## Remote / Geographic Discount Pushback

Some companies apply a remote discount assuming the candidate is in a low-cost market. Push back directly:

> "I want to address this upfront. I'm in Geneva, which is one of the most expensive cities in Europe. A geographic discount makes sense for candidates in lower-cost markets; it runs the other way here. The number I gave you reflects Swiss market rates for this role level, not a premium. I'd rather we anchor to the role scope rather than try to apply a location adjustment that actually goes in the wrong direction."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in process:

> "I should be transparent: I'm in late stages with another company, and their package is in the CHF 180,000-190,000 range with equity. I prefer Yuno's technical scope and the payments domain, which is why I want to make this work. But I'd need the total package to be comparable to make a clear decision."

If you're in early conversations elsewhere but don't have a firm offer:

> "I'm actively in conversations with a few companies at this level, and the packages I'm seeing are in the CHF 175,000-195,000 range. I'd rather close this quickly if the numbers work, but I'll need them to be in that range."

---

## Yuno-Specific Notes

- **Funding reality:** KASZEK-backed with strong LATAM VC presence and US crossover. Funding appears real; runway is likely 2-3 years at current burn. Exit timeline probably 3-5 years.
- **AI differentiation:** NOVA AI and Agentic Commerce are strategic bets in a crowded payments market. They increase execution risk but also equity upside if they land.
- **Infrastructure budget:** Ask explicitly what the LLM API and GPU budget looks like for the agent platform. At scale this becomes a significant cost center. You want to know if the platform engineer owns that budget or is gated through central IT.
- **Stack gap on Go:** The job description lists Go as the primary language. Magino's stack is TypeScript/Node.js/Python. Address this proactively if it comes up: "I've shipped production platforms in TypeScript and Node.js at scale, and Go's concurrency model and type system aren't foreign territory. I'd expect a ramp period, not a wall."
- **Terraform/Pulumi:** Not explicitly in Magino's profile. If asked, ground the answer in infrastructure-as-code familiarity broadly (CI/CD pipelines, containerized deployments, AWS configuration) and position Terraform as a tool, not a discipline.

---

## Timeline and Closing

If they push for a fast decision:

> "I can move quickly. I'd need 3-5 business days to review the written offer and work through the equity structure carefully. That's reasonable for a role of this scope on both sides."

If they ask what would make you sign today:

> "A written offer at CHF [X] base, [Y] options on a 4-year vest with 1-year cliff and double-trigger acceleration, and a clear sense of the first 90-day scope. That's it. I don't need weeks to decide — I need the document."
