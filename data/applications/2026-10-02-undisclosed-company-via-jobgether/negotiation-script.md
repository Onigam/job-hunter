# Negotiation Script: Engineering Manager, Foundations & Enablement (via Jobgether)

## Context that shapes the numbers

The employer isn't named, but the posting matches Storyblok's listing for the same role word for word. Storyblok is a headless CMS company headquartered in Linz, Austria, founded in 2017, Series C ($80M led by Brighton Park Capital, with HV Capital, Mubadala Capital, 3VC and firstminute; ~$138M raised in total). Roughly 300 people, fully remote across 45+ countries. Confirm the employer with Jobgether before talking money.

What that means:

- **Austrian HQ, remote-first, hiring in Switzerland.** They'll most likely employ you through a Swiss entity or an employer of record (Deel, Remote and similar). Ask early which one. It affects pension (BVG/LPP), contract law and whether they treat you as a "Swiss" hire or apply a company-wide band.
- **Remote-first companies often run location-based pay.** Expect them to have a band per country or region. Your job is to make sure you're placed in the Switzerland band, not a blended European one.
- **Series C at ~300 people.** Cash salaries are structured and bands exist. Base can move within the band; it rarely jumps outside it. Equity (Storyblok advertises a VSOP) will be a standard grant by level, with less room than at seed stage.
- **This is a people-manager role, not Staff IC.** They'll benchmark against Engineering Manager bands. Your 17+ years and co-founder background argue for the top of that band, or for a Senior EM level.

## Placing yourself in the Swiss band

Raise this with the recruiter before the first hiring manager call:

"Before we go further, could you share the salary band for this role for someone based in Switzerland? I'm in Geneva, and I want to make sure we're working from the Swiss range rather than a European average, so neither of us wastes time."

If they say the band is company-wide or EU-based:

"I understand having one band keeps things simple. Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, though, and the roles I'm comparing against here are Swiss-paid. A Swiss employee also costs you less in employer social charges than an Austrian one at the same gross, roughly 12 to 15% against about 20% or more, so part of the gap pays for itself. Is there flexibility to apply a Swiss adjustment?"

## Opening anchor script

"Based on what engineering managers at Swiss scale-ups are earning, and given I've built and run engineering foundations from zero through an acquisition, I'm looking at around CHF 210k base, plus the VSOP. I know that's at the upper end. It reflects the scope of this role, which touches every team in the org. How does that compare to your range?"

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low

**Script A, base well below range (for example CHF 150k to 165k):**
"Thanks for being straight with me. That number looks closer to a senior IC salary in Geneva than to someone owning standards, security practices and hiring across the whole engineering org. I'd need the base closer to CHF 195k for this to work. Where's the flexibility in the band?"

**Script B, the band is fixed:**
"Understood. Then let's look at what can move: the level (Senior EM rather than EM), a larger VSOP grant, a sign-on bonus, and a compensation review after six months tied to agreed outcomes, for example CI times, onboarding time for new engineers, or a security baseline rolled out across teams."

**Script C, they push a remote or location discount:**
"I'd understand that if I were asking for a Geneva cost of living on a lower-cost location's job. But I'm hired in Switzerland, paid in CHF, and the comparison set is Swiss roles. The Swiss band is the fair reference."

**Script D, level pushback ("you haven't been a line manager recently"):**
"Fair point, and I won't pretend otherwise. My line management was at Alaya, where I hired and led the first engineers, and the last five years were Staff-level leadership across teams without formal reports. What you'd get is someone who's driven cross-team standards without authority, which is most of this job. I'm open to starting at EM if we agree a level review at six months with clear criteria."

## Equity (VSOP): what to ask

Austrian VSOPs are virtual shares that pay out in cash on an exit or liquidity event. Get answers in writing:

1. **Grant size as a percentage** of fully diluted capital, plus the total number of virtual shares outstanding. A unit count alone means nothing.
2. **Base or strike price.** Usually the last round's share price. Your payout is exit value minus that base, so a high base after Series C limits upside.
3. **Vesting and cliff.** Four years with a one-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
4. **Leaver clauses.** The most important part. Push for vested units to be kept as a good leaver (dismissal without cause, or resignation after the cliff), bad leaver limited to real cause, and no clause that wipes vested units just because you resign.
5. **Change of control.** Ask for double-trigger acceleration: if the company is acquired and your role is cut or materially reduced within 12 months, unvested units vest. You've been through an acquisition, so you can explain why from experience.
6. **Refresh grants.** Anti-dilution protection isn't realistic for employees. Ask instead whether refresh grants exist after promotions or new rounds.
7. **Swiss tax.** VSOP payouts are taxed as employment income in Switzerland when paid. If the program is administered from Austria, check how withholding and social contributions work for a Swiss resident. A short opinion from a Geneva tax adviser is worth it before signing.

Script if the grant is small:
"I know the VSOP at this stage is a top-up rather than the main event. Since base is constrained by the band, I'd like the grant at the top of what you'd give this level, and good leaver terms that protect vested units if I resign after the cliff."

## Competing offer template (use only if real)

"To be transparent, I'm in late-stage conversations for a [Staff / Head of Engineering] role in Switzerland, with a base around CHF [X]. This role is my preference because of the scope across the whole engineering org, and I'd rather make it work. Is there room to close the gap?"

Don't invent a competing offer. If there isn't one, point to the Swiss market data instead: "the roles I'm otherwise looking at in Geneva and Zurich pay CHF 190k-plus."

## Other things worth negotiating

- **Level and title**: Senior Engineering Manager if possible, or a written six-month level review.
- **Pension (LPP/BVG)**: check whether the employer pays above the legal minimum. This can be worth several thousand CHF a year.
- **Team-building and travel**: the posting mentions an annual international trip. Confirm any other travel to Linz is covered and how often it's expected.
- **Learning budget**: the posting mentions a personal development fund. Ask the amount.
- **Vacation**: posting says 25 days. Swiss norms for senior roles are 25; ask for 27 or 30 if base can't move.
- **Remote stipend and equipment**: already offered; confirm the amounts.
- **Sign-on bonus** if you're leaving a bonus or unvested equity behind.
