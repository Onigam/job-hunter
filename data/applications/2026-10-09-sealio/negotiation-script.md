# Negotiation Script: Senior AI Product Engineer (Sealio)

## Context that shapes the numbers

- **Pre-seed, with no salary listed.** The ad offers "base salary plus early-employee equity". At this stage cash is tight and equity is the main lever. Your aim is a fair Geneva base you can live on, plus a grant that matches being one of the first engineers, not a later hire.
- **The title undersells the scope.** "Senior" is a mid-level title, but the job covers full-stack ownership, RAG pipelines, enterprise security, hiring, standards, and a stated path to Engineering Lead. With 17+ years, a platform built from zero through an acquisition, and Senior Staff at Benevity, negotiate on the scope, and get the Lead path written down.
- **Second-time founders.** They've raised and exited before, so they know the market and won't be thrown by a direct conversation about equity terms. They'll respect precise questions.
- **Check the employment basics first.** The LinkedIn page header showed "Des Moines, IA", which looks like an error. Confirm it's a Swiss employment contract with a Geneva entity, 3 days on-site.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough ranges from public comp data. Re-check on swissdevjobs.ch, levels.fyi (Switzerland), Glassdoor and the Swiss startup salary surveys before quoting any of them.

| Benchmark | Base (approx.) | Equity (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Senior engineer, Geneva, seed/pre-seed startup | CHF 120k to 150k | 0.2% to 0.8% |
| First engineers / founding engineer, Swiss pre-seed | CHF 130k to 160k | 0.5% to 2% |
| Engineering lead / Head of Engineering, Swiss seed to Series A | CHF 160k to 200k | 1% to 3% |
| Staff/Principal engineer, Geneva, scale-up | CHF 170k to 220k | small, later-stage grants |

Geneva rent, health insurance you pay yourself (LAMal) and the salary-linked pension (LPP) are why Swiss bands sit well above EU ones. A pre-seed company paying well below CHF 130k in Geneva is asking you to fund the gap in equity, so price that in.

**Target:** CHF 160k base + 1.25% (fully diluted), with the Engineering Lead title and comp review agreed at a defined milestone.
**Opening anchor:** CHF 175k base + 1.5%.
**Walk-away floor:** CHF 140k base + 1%. Below CHF 140k, the equity needs to be clearly above 1.5% to be worth it, and you should only accept that if you believe in the company's odds.

## Step 1: if they ask for your expectation first

"For one of the first engineering seats, with a path to leading the team, I'm looking at around CHF 175k base, plus early-employee equity in the 1 to 2% range. That reflects 17 years of engineering, a platform I built from zero through an acquisition, and the fact that I'd be setting the standards and hiring the next engineers. I know cash is tight at pre-seed, so I'm open to balancing base and equity if the structure is right."

If they push for a single number: say CHF 175k and stop talking.

## Step 2: if they come in low

"Thanks for being upfront. CHF [their number] is a fair number for a senior engineer, but the scope here is closer to a founding engineer and future lead: the RAG and extraction stack, tenant isolation and SSO, CI/CD, and hiring. Could we get to CHF 160k? If base is really capped by the runway, I'd want to make up the difference in equity."

If base truly can't move:

"Understood, runway comes first at this stage. Then let's look at the grant. At CHF [their number], I'd need [their % + 0.5 points] to make it work, along with a written salary review once you close the seed round."

**A seed-round step-up is the most useful ask here.** Something like: "Base goes to CHF [target] when the next round closes, or in 12 months, whichever comes first." It costs them nothing now and protects you from a long pre-seed stretch.

## Step 3: pushing back on a "startup discount" or remote parity

"I get that pre-seed salaries are lower, and I've been on the founder side of that conversation at Alaya. But I'd be paying Geneva rent and Swiss health insurance on day one, and the role is on-site three days a week. If the base sits well under Geneva market, the equity needs to carry that, and I'd want to see the terms that make it real."

## Step 4: equity questions (ask all of these, get answers in writing)

- **Percentage of what?** Ask for the grant as a percentage of the fully diluted share count, including the unallocated ESOP pool. A number of options means nothing without it.
- **Instrument.** Swiss startups use options, shares bought at nominal value, or phantom/virtual shares. Ask which one. Phantom shares pay out only on an exit and give no shareholder rights, so they're worth less than real options at the same percentage.
- **Strike price and valuation.** What's the strike (or purchase price), and what was the pre-seed post-money valuation? A low strike now is the main value of joining early.
- **Vesting.** The usual setup is four years with a one-year cliff. As one of the first engineers, ask for no cliff, or a six-month cliff, or a portion vested on start.
- **Acceleration.** Ask for double-trigger acceleration: if the company is acquired and your role is cut or significantly changed within 12 months, unvested equity vests. You've been through an acquisition, so you can say why it matters.
- **Leaver terms.** Good leaver and bad leaver clauses are common in Swiss ESOPs. Make sure "good leaver" covers leaving after the cliff, not just death or disability, and ask how long you have to exercise after you leave (push for longer than 90 days).
- **Liquidation preferences.** Ask what preferences the pre-seed investors hold. 1x non-participating is standard; anything more eats into what your percentage is worth.
- **Anti-dilution.** Employees almost never get anti-dilution protection, so don't spend capital on it. Instead, ask for a refresh grant tied to the Engineering Lead promotion, which handles dilution from the seed round in practice.
- **Swiss tax.** Unlisted options are generally taxed as income at exercise in Switzerland; shares bought at nominal value can be taxed differently. Ask whether they've got a tax ruling from the Geneva tax office for their plan. Founders who've done this before often have one.

## Step 5: competing offer template (use only if true)

"I want to be open with you. I'm in late-stage conversations with [company] for a [Staff/Lead] role, around CHF [X] base plus [equity]. Sealio is where I'd rather be, because extraction and retrieval on real tender documents is a problem I want to own end to end, in Geneva. If we can get to CHF [target] base and [target %], I'm ready to sign this week."

If you don't have a competing offer, don't invent one. Say this instead:

"I'm talking with a few other teams, and I'd like to decide within the next two weeks. Sealio is the one I want if we can land the package."

## Step 6: non-salary items worth asking for

- The Engineering Lead path in writing: what milestone triggers it (team size, round closed, or a date) and what changes in comp when it happens.
- A full AI developer tools budget (already offered in the ad, so confirm the amount and that it covers Claude Code at the usage you need).
- Remote days: the ad says three days on-site. Confirm which days are fixed, and that the other two are actually flexible.
- A learning or conference budget, especially for the LLM eval and security side where you'd be ramping up.
- A start date that leaves at least two weeks between roles.
