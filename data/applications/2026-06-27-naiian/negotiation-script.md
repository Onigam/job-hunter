# Negotiation Script — naiian Founding Principal Backend Engineer

## Situation Overview

**Posted range:** €85,000–€95,000 base (Madrid, in-office, full-time permanent)
**Equity:** Founding-stage package — specifics unknown until asked
**Location:** Madrid, Spain — 100% in-office, at least during foundational phase
**Magino's current market:** Geneva, Switzerland — Staff+ rate is CHF 160,000–195,000

The posted range is reasonable for Madrid senior/staff backend engineers but below what a true founding engineer with a unicorn-exit track record should command anywhere. This role isn't just a backend hire; it's the person who designs the multi-tenant architecture, the API contracts, the async job semantics, and the audit trail — decisions that will cost multiples more to revisit at scale. That's the argument.

**Key gaps to be aware of:** The job lists Python and/or Go as the production backend language. Magino's primary production experience is Node.js/TypeScript, with Python as a secondary skill. Frame this honestly: strong Python familiarity, Go learnable, but this may be raised. Also: the job description mentions "operational Spanish." Address this proactively if it comes up.

---

## Opening Move: Framing Before Numbers

When asked about compensation expectations on a screening call:

> "I've seen the posted range. Before we talk numbers I want to understand the full picture: the equity structure, the vesting schedule, and what 'founding engineer' means in terms of actual ownership stake. That said, my base expectations are above the top of the posted range given my background and the relocation from Geneva involved. I'm confident we can find a number that works once we establish the fit."

This defers the number game without sounding evasive, flags the relocation factor early, and signals you've looked at the range critically.

---

## Salary Anchor

If pressed for a concrete number:

> "Given the founding nature of the role, the relocation from Switzerland involved, and my track record designing and scaling a platform that was acquired by a $1B unicorn, I'm targeting €105,000–€115,000 base. I know that's above your posted range, but the first technical hire at a company like naiian isn't the same profile as a senior backend engineer joining a 50-person team. The decisions this person makes in the first six months are the ones everyone else inherits."

**If they push back that the band is fixed:**

> "I understand the band exists. What I'd ask is whether there's flexibility on equity to make the total package competitive at €95K base. If the base stays there, what does a meaningful founding-engineer grant actually look like in terms of percentage?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

Ask for specifics before evaluating:

> "Can you walk me through the equity structure: the vesting schedule, the cliff, and roughly what percentage of the company the founding engineer grant represents? I want to understand what 'founding equity' means in practice before comparing it to other factors in the package."

**Target ranges for a well-funded seed/early-stage company:**
- 0.5%–1.0% is the appropriate founding engineer range at this stage
- 4-year vest, 1-year cliff is standard — anything with a longer cliff is a red flag
- Ask whether there's an acceleration clause on change of control

**If the equity seems thin:**

> "The equity is meaningful to me here, precisely because this is a founding role. The person who designs the multi-tenant layer, the async job architecture, and the audit logging from day one is taking on a different kind of risk than someone joining at Series B. If the base is constrained by the band, I'd like to have a real conversation about whether the grant size can reflect that."

---

## Relocation Factor

If they assume Magino covers relocation costs:

> "I'd need to factor in relocation from Geneva — flights, moving costs, initial setup in Madrid. I wouldn't expect to absorb that fully when I'm relocating specifically for this role. Is there a relocation budget or a one-time signing bonus that would help bridge that gap?"

**Aim for:** €5,000–€10,000 one-time relocation support or equivalent signing bonus.

---

## Remote/Hybrid Exploration

Probe this early, especially given the in-office framing:

> "I noticed the role is 100% in-office during the foundational phase. Is there a longer-term expectation there, or is hybrid a possibility once working rhythms are established? I'm asking because it directly affects how I think about the relocation commitment."

If they signal any future flexibility, document it. Founding-phase in-office requirements sometimes soften once the team is stable.

---

## If They Come In Below the Posted Range

Pause before countering:

> "That's below the lower bound of what I saw posted, and below what I'd expect given the seniority and founding nature of the role. Can you help me understand how you arrived at that number? I want to make sure we're aligned on what this position actually is."

Never counter a below-range offer in the same breath. Get the explanation first.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in process:

> "I'm currently in late stages with another opportunity at [X]. I find the naiian role more compelling for the technical reasons we've discussed, but I'd need the total package to be comparable to move forward. Is there room to get closer to [X] on base, equity, or both?"

If the competing offer is a Geneva-market role (CHF-denominated), make clear you're not inflating — convert at the current rate and state it plainly.

---

## Red Flags Worth Watching

- Equity below 0.25% at seed/early stage for a self-described "founding engineer" role — the title is doing more work than the grant
- Cliff longer than 12 months
- "Founding engineer" label applied to a team that already has 5+ engineers — ask who the other technical founders are and when they joined
- In-office policy with no written or verbal commitment to revisiting hybrid over time
- A base at or below €95K with equity that doesn't compensate for it
- Evasiveness about total headcount or runway when asked directly
