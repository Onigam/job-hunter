# Negotiation Script — Alpaca, Senior AI Platform Engineer

## Context

- **Company:** Alpaca, US-based broker-dealer, Series D ($320M raised)
- **Role:** Senior AI Platform Engineer (Remote — North America EST posted)
- **Location note:** Magino is Geneva-based. The posting targets North America EST. Clarify early whether they're open to a European-based remote hire. If yes, anchor on US market rates — Geneva LCOL is irrelevant for a USD role. If they apply a geo-modifier, push back (see below).

---

## Market Rate Anchors (USD, US Remote)

| Level | Base Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Senior AI Platform Engineer (8-10y) | $185K–$220K | Market baseline |
| Staff-equivalent with 17y exp + AI specialization | $220K–$270K | More accurate for Magino's profile |
| Series D equity typical | 0.05%–0.20% | Depends on grant type and strike price |

**Opening anchor:** $240K base. Justification: 17 years of platform engineering, AI platform work already in production, founding engineer background (startup + acquisition), and a direct match to agentic governance requirements. This is the top of Senior and the bottom of Staff — reasonable given the role scope.

---

## Offer Evaluation Framework

When an offer comes in, check these four levers before responding:

**1. Base salary** — target $230K+. If they come in below $200K, treat it as room to negotiate, not a final number.

**2. Equity** — Series D means the cap table is meaningful but not early-stage. Ask for:
- Total shares granted and current total shares outstanding (to get %)
- Last 409A valuation (to estimate current strike price)
- Vesting schedule (standard is 4 years / 1-year cliff — don't accept less)
- Any acceleration on change of control (single or double trigger)

**3. Benefits** — $500 home office + $150/month Brex stipend is modest. Not a dealbreaker but note it relative to European SaaS norms (often higher hardware allowances).

**4. Location flexibility** — if they require EST timezone overlap, clarify how many hours per day. Geneva is UTC+2/+1, so 9am EST = 3pm Geneva. Morning overlap is workable; full EST workday overlap is not.

## Opening Counter Script

Use this after receiving a written offer.

> "Thanks for sending this over — I've had a chance to review it. I'm genuinely interested in what you're building at Alpaca, and I think this role is a strong fit given the work I've done on agentic platform infrastructure.
>
> On base, I was expecting something in the $235K–$245K range based on market data for this level and the AI platform specialization. Can we get to $235K?
>
> On equity, I'd want to understand the current cap table a bit better before treating the options as a meaningful part of the comp package. Could you share the total shares outstanding and the last 409A? That'll help me evaluate the grant properly."

---

## If They Push Back on Salary

Anchor on scope and specialization:

> "The role is effectively Staff-level in scope: you're establishing the AI platform from scratch, defining governance standards, and driving adoption across the engineering org. Market rate for that scope with 17 years of platform experience, plus the AI-specific work, puts the range I mentioned squarely in line. I'm not asking for a VP number, I'm asking for the right Senior/Staff range for what the role actually requires."

---

## If They Apply a Geo-Modifier (Geneva-based)

> "I understand some companies apply geographic adjustments, but I'd push back here for a couple of reasons. First, Geneva has one of the highest costs of living in Europe, comparable to San Francisco. Second, the value I bring to this role isn't reduced by my location — I'm operating at US Senior/Staff market level and the work product is identical. If the adjustment is significant, I'd want to understand whether the role truly works as fully remote, or whether proximity is implicitly expected."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in hand (or expect one soon), you can use this:

> "I want to be straightforward — I'm in final stages with one other company. I'd prefer Alpaca given the problem space, but I'd need the comp to be competitive to make that decision easy. If you can get to $235K base, I'd be ready to move quickly."

If no competing offer, don't fabricate one. Instead use urgency differently:

> "I've been actively interviewing and have a few conversations at the final stage. I'd like to make a decision within the next two weeks. Getting to $235K would make this the clear choice."

---

## Equity Negotiation

At Series D, equity is real but not early-stage. Key questions to ask:

- "What's the current share count outstanding?" — divide your grant by this to get percentage
- "What was the last 409A valuation?" — divide by shares to get per-share value, multiply by your grant to estimate value at current strike
- "Is there a liquidity preference on the preferred shares?" — matters for downside protection
- "Does the company have a history of secondary sales or tender offers for employees?" — Series D companies sometimes run these
- "Is there any acceleration clause in the event of acquisition?" — ask for at minimum single-trigger acceleration on your unvested shares

**Minimum acceptable equity:** For a founding-grade role on an internal platform, target $200K–$400K in equity value at current 409A. For a company at $320M raised, Series D, that's a reasonable ask.

---

## Walk-Away Threshold

Below these numbers, the offer doesn't match the market or the role scope:

- Base below $190K with no equity acceleration
- Equity grant below 0.05% with 4-year vesting and no acceleration
- Full EST timezone requirement with no flexibility (not compatible with Geneva)

---

## Closing

At the end of negotiation, confirm in writing:

> "To confirm what we've agreed: $[base] base, [shares] options at $[strike] strike with 4-year vesting and 1-year cliff, and [any other terms]. Happy to sign on receipt of the formal offer letter reflecting these terms."
