# Negotiation Script: Glacis, Founding Software Engineer, Agentic AI

## What's on the table

The posting doesn't disclose a salary or equity range, which is common for a nine-person, San Francisco-based seed-stage company still figuring out its own comp bands. That means the first real number in this process will likely come from them, and the job is to anchor before that number gets said out loud, not react to it after. Glacis is backed by investors behind Flexport, Databricks, and Perplexity, has enterprise logos like Tesla and Coca-Cola already signed, and is asking for a "founding" engineer with 3+ years experience, which suggests a team trying to punch above a typical seed-stage budget on responsibility while possibly still anchoring comp to a junior-to-mid band. Worth confirming early which one it actually is.

## Market anchor

US seed-stage founding engineer roles with this scope, full-stack ownership plus AI agent design plus direct enterprise customer contact, typically land base salary in the $150K to $190K range with equity between 0.50% and 1.5%, when the company has already closed a real seed or Series A round and named customers. Geneva cost of living doesn't apply here since this isn't a Swiss-anchored role; the relevant comparison is US remote-founding-engineer bands, adjusted upward for the fact that a single hire is covering what's usually two or three roles, full-stack development, AI/agent architecture, and customer-facing integration work.

Opening line, once they name a structure: "Given the scope, full-stack plus agent design plus direct customer integration work, I'd want to be talking about the upper end of whatever range you have in mind, both on base and on equity. What's the band you're working with for this hire?" Asking the question directly, rather than naming a number first, is worth trying since the range wasn't published; whoever names a number first gives up information.

## If they come in low

If the initial number lands noticeably below $150K base with equity under 0.5%, the response should focus on scope, not personal need: "The responsibilities here, agent design, ERP and TMS integration, direct customer work, are usually split across a couple of hires once a company has your traction. I'm glad to be the one covering all of it, but that's exactly why I'd ask you to move the number rather than the scope." If cash is genuinely tight at this stage, that's the moment to shift the conversation toward equity and title rather than holding firm on base: trade some cash for a meaningfully larger equity grant or a co-founder-adjacent title, since both cost the company less today than they cost tomorrow.

## Remote and time zone reality

Glacis is a San Francisco company hiring remote, and Geneva sits nine hours ahead of Pacific time. That's a real constraint worth naming rather than hiding: "I'm in Geneva, which means limited but real overlap with a Pacific-hours team, mornings for me are your evenings before. I'm used to async work and can shift my schedule to cover more of your afternoon, but it's worth confirming upfront whether the customer-facing part of this role assumes US business-hours availability, since that's where the time zone gap actually bites." If they need someone covering live customer calls during Pacific mornings, that's a legitimate reason to discuss a schedule shift rather than a reason to walk away from the conversation.

## Equity notes

Ask for the fully diluted share count before evaluating any percentage, a headline equity number is close to meaningless without knowing what it's a percentage of. Confirm what round the company last closed and at what valuation, since "founding engineer" equity at a nine-person company with named enterprise customers should reflect a real seed or Series A valuation, not a pre-seed placeholder. Standard four-year vesting with a one-year cliff is fine; push for monthly vesting after the cliff instead of a second annual cliff. Ask directly whether there's a plan for an option pool refresh in the next 12 to 18 months, since early hires often get diluted by pool top-ups nobody flagged at signing. If there's any secondary or accelerated vesting language tied to an acquisition, given Glacis's investor pedigree an exit is plausible, get it in writing rather than relying on a verbal assurance.

## Competing offer as pressure

Even without a live competing offer in hand, the honest version of this still works: "I'm in parallel conversations with a couple of other early-stage platform and agent-infrastructure teams, so I'd like to move on both the comp structure and the time zone question within the next week or two." Use it to compress the timeline and force a real number sooner, not to bluff a figure that doesn't exist; a fabricated competing offer is easy to unravel in a follow-up question and costs more in trust than it's worth.
