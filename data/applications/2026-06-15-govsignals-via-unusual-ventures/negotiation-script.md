# Negotiation Script — GovSignals Platform Engineer

## Market Anchors

**US market (remote-friendly Platform Engineer, senior/staff level, Series A startup):**
- Base salary: $170k-$215k
- Total comp including equity (paper value at Series A): $220k-$300k+
- Equity: 0.25-0.75% for a senior founding-team-adjacent hire

**Geneva equivalent (CHF, for cost-of-living reference):**
- Senior Staff Platform Engineer: CHF 160k-200k base
- Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in the world. A geographic discount for remote work is not legitimate — the output is identical.

**GovSignals context:**
- Unusual Ventures backed, early stage (pre-Series A or Series A)
- Working directly with the CTO — this is a high-trust, high-scope role
- At this stage, equity matters as much as cash

---

## Opening Anchor

Wait for them to make the first offer if at all possible. If they press you for a number:

"I'm targeting $190k-$200k base for a senior platform engineering role at this scope, based on what I've seen for Staff-level engineers in similar early-stage companies. On top of that, equity is an important part of the conversation since the upside is a real component of the package at this stage."

If they ask for a range: say $185k-$210k. The bottom of that range should be your actual walk-away number plus buffer, not your real floor.

---

## If the First Offer Comes In Low (below $160k base)

"I appreciate the offer. I want to make this work. That number is below the range I've been operating in for this scope, though. Based on market comp for senior platform engineers at early-stage VC-backed companies, I was expecting something closer to $185k-$200k. Is there room to move on base, or is the equity structure something we could adjust to bridge the gap?"

Then stop talking. Let them respond. Don't fill the silence.

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Targets:**
- 0.5-1.0% for a founding-team-adjacent senior hire at pre-Series A
- 0.25-0.5% is acceptable if the strike price is low and the valuation is reasonable
- Below 0.1% at this stage: ask why the pool allocation is that low, or walk

**Questions to ask before accepting:**
- "What's the current 409A valuation and the strike price for this grant?"
- "Is this ISO or NSO?"
- "What percentage of the fully-diluted cap table does this represent?"
- "What's the total employee option pool, and how much has been granted?"
- "Is there a double-trigger acceleration on change of control?"

**On vesting:** Standard is 4-year vest, 1-year cliff. If they offer worse terms, push back:
"I'd want the vesting schedule to reflect the risk of joining at this stage. A one-year cliff is standard; anything longer puts too much of the risk on my side."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they try to apply a discount because you're based in Geneva:

"The platform work I'd be doing has the same scope and the same complexity from Geneva as it would from San Francisco. I've been benchmarking against US market rates for senior remote platform engineers because that's the relevant comparison for this role. I'd prefer not to build a geography-based discount into the offer."

Hold this line. If they push back: "I understand budgets have constraints. If the base is fixed, let's talk about equity, because that's where I can see the full picture of what this is worth."

---

## Competing Offer Template

If you're in process elsewhere (or have an offer):

"I want to be straightforward: I have another process at [company] that's moving forward at a similar stage. I'd rather be at GovSignals for the problem you're working on, but I want to make sure the comp is comparable so I can make a clear decision. Can you give me a sense of where you're thinking on base and equity?"

If they ask for the specific other offer: give a range, not a number. "They're looking at $190k-$200k base with roughly [X]% equity." Keep it vague enough to be true even if the other offer isn't finalized.

---

## Scenario Planning

**At a $50M acquisition:** 0.5% = $250k gross (pre-tax, pre-dilution)
**At a $200M acquisition:** 0.5% = $1M gross
**At a $500M acquisition:** 0.5% = $2.5M gross

Government contracting AI is a sector with real enterprise revenue potential. Ask them where they see the company in 3-5 years, and what exit scenarios they're building toward. That framing helps you judge whether 0.25% or 0.75% is the right ask.

---

## Key Rules

1. Always negotiate. The first number is not the final number.
2. Negotiate each axis separately: base, equity, benefits, start date, remote policy.
3. At a startup, cash is constrained but equity is not. If base is capped, push hard on options.
4. Get everything in writing before giving notice at your current role.
5. If they give a deadline on an offer, ask for a short extension. "I want to be fully committed when I say yes. Can I have until [date] to finalize?" A day or two is almost always granted.
