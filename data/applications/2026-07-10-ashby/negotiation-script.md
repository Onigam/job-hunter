Negotiation Script: Ashby, Staff Platform Engineer, EU

## Context worth naming up front

Ashby posted the band itself: €141,000 to €226,000 annually, which is unusually transparent and worth using rather than fighting. That's an EU-wide range though, not a Swiss one, and it almost certainly wasn't built around Geneva cost of living. The company is growth-stage but not early-stage-risky: tens of millions in ARR, over 100% year-over-year growth, 2,500-plus customers, low churn, and clients like Notion, Linear, Shopify and Snowflake. That combination, real revenue plus real growth, usually means more room to move on cash than a pre-revenue startup, and less need to load everything into speculative equity. Worth confirming early whether the €141k-€226k band already reflects level (staff vs senior) or whether staff-scoped work should be anchored at or above the top of it by default.

## Market anchors

Staff-level platform and infrastructure engineers in Switzerland typically land in the CHF 170,000 to 220,000 base range depending on canton and company stage, with Geneva and Zurich running at the higher end of that. Converting the top of Ashby's posted EU band, around €226,000, lands close to CHF 210,000 to 215,000 at current rates, which is a reasonable ceiling to anchor near rather than assume as the floor. Given Magino's track record, founding-engineer ownership through an acquisition by a unicorn, plus platform and cross-team architecture depth, the right opening anchor sits at or slightly above the top of the posted band, with equity discussed as a separate line rather than folded into base to make the number look bigger.

## Opening anchor

"The posted range gives us a useful starting point. Given the scope here, infrastructure guardrails, SLOs owned with the business side, and mentoring product engineers, that reads as staff-level ownership rather than senior, so I'd want to anchor near the top of the €141k-€226k band, call it CHF 210,000 base equivalent, with equity discussed on its own."

If pushed for a number regardless: "Somewhere around CHF 205,000 to 215,000 base, depending on how the equity and any sign-on shake out."

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

If the offer lands in the lower half of the posted band: "I appreciate the offer, and I want to flag that the scope described, infra guardrails, SLOs with business stakeholders, mentoring across product engineering, reads closer to the top of the range you posted than the middle. Is there room to move closer to CHF 205,000 to 210,000 on base, or is part of the gap something equity or a sign-on could close instead?"

If they cite the DSL-to-SQL compiler work or GraphQL/Apollo depth as a gap: "That's fair to name directly. My depth is in platform architecture, distributed systems, and scaling infra under growth, not query-compiler internals specifically, and that's ground I'd close fast given the SQL and database work I've already done. I'd rather agree on what good looks like at 90 days than take a discounted number for a gap that's meant to close quickly."

## Equity negotiation

Ask what the current strike price is based on, the most recent valuation, since the metrics being shared publicly, tens of millions in ARR and triple-digit growth, suggest the company may be raising or has recently raised at a higher mark, and an earlier strike price is worth more. Confirm standard four-year vesting with a one-year cliff and flag it if it's longer. The ten-year post-termination exercise window mentioned in the posting is genuinely good and rare, so no pushback needed there, just confirm it applies uniformly and isn't tied to a minimum tenure condition. Ask whether early exercise is allowed, since that matters more with a ten-year window if the strike price is low enough to make it worth doing.

## Geographic and structure pushback

If the EU-wide band gets used to justify pricing toward the middle rather than the Swiss-adjusted top: "Geneva isn't a discount market inside that EU range, cost of living here runs above most of the EU cities that band likely averages across, so I'd want to be anchored toward the top rather than the midpoint for that reason alone, separate from the scope question."

If the on-call and mentoring load gets framed as extra rather than core scope: "Follow-the-sun on-call and mentoring product engineers on infra aren't side tasks from where I sit, they're a meaningful part of why this is a staff-scoped role rather than a senior one, and I'd want comp to reflect that rather than treating them as goodwill asks on top of the base job."

## Competing offer template

Use only if a real competing process exists.

"I'm further along in another process at a comparable level, with a base offer in a similar range to what we've discussed. I'd rather land this one, the compiler and guardrail work is the more interesting problem and the on-call model tells me the team actually protects reliability, but the offer needs to be close enough that I'm not leaving something real behind."
