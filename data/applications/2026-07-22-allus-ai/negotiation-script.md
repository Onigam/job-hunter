# Negotiation Script: Allus AI, Founding AI Agent Engineer

## What's on the table

Posted range: $100K to $200K base, 0.30% to 1.00% equity. Location listed as Cupertino, Atlanta, or remote within CA/GA, with visa sponsorship offered. That last detail matters: the range and the sponsorship language both assume a US-based hire. Geneva doesn't fit that assumption, so the comp conversation and the location conversation need to happen together, not sequentially.

## Anchor point

At 17+ years of experience, a founding-engineer track record with an exit to a unicorn, and the specific skill set they're asking for (agent orchestration, prompt and memory infrastructure, eval systems), the right anchor is the top of their posted band: $190K to $200K base, plus equity toward the high end of 0.75% to 1.00%. The justification isn't Geneva cost of living, since this isn't a Swiss role, it's that "founding engineer" at a 3-person company with no infra team means doing the work of a staff engineer and an early infra hire at once. That's worth top-of-band regardless of where the person sits.

Opening line: "Given the scope here, this is really a founding infra hire, not just an agent engineer, I'd want to talk about the top of your posted range, call it $195K base and equity closer to 1%, with the structure open to discussion."

## If they come in low

If the offer lands mid-band or below (say $130K to $150K), the counter isn't emotional, it's about scope: "The responsibilities you listed, orchestration, eval, memory infra, human feedback loops, are usually split across two or three hires at this stage. I'm glad to own all of it, but that's exactly why I'd ask you to move toward the top of the range rather than the middle." If they cite the seed-stage cash constraint, that's the moment to push equity up instead of holding the line on cash: trade $10K to $15K of base for an extra 0.15% to 0.25% equity, since seed-stage cash is genuinely scarce and equity is the lever they can actually move.

## The remote/location problem

This has to be addressed directly before comp gets finalized, not glossed over. Two honest options to raise with them:

1. Remote from Geneva as a contractor or through an employer-of-record setup, with comp paid in USD at the agreed number. This avoids the visa sponsorship question entirely since there's no relocation, but it means confirming up front whether "remote (CA or GA)" is a hard requirement or a soft preference tied to time zone overlap.
2. If on-site presence in Atlanta or Cupertino turns out to be non-negotiable, that changes the calculus completely, at which point visa sponsorship, relocation support, and a cost-of-living adjustment for a US move all need to be on the table as separate line items, not folded into the base number.

Script: "Before we get into exact numbers, I want to flag that I'm in Geneva. If on-site presence is a hard requirement, let's talk about relocation and visa terms as their own conversation. If remote is genuinely open, I'd rather lock that in first since it changes what a fair number looks like."

## Equity notes

- 0.30% to 1.00% is a wide band for a reason, ask directly where within it they see a founding infra hire landing, rather than guessing.
- Confirm the fully diluted share count the percentage is calculated against; a headline number means little without the denominator.
- Standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff is fine, but push for monthly vesting after the cliff rather than a second annual cliff.
- Ask about anti-dilution protection or at least a commitment to disclose future round terms before they close, since seed-stage equity moves fast and silently.
- At a 3-person company, ask what the option pool refresh plan looks like for the next 12 to 18 months, since early hires often get diluted by pool top-ups nobody flagged at signing.

## Competing offer leverage

Even without a live competing offer, the honest framing works: "I'm mid-conversation with a couple of other platform and agent-infra roles at similar stage companies, so I'd like to move quickly, within a week or two, on both the comp and the location question." Use this to compress the timeline, not to bluff a number that isn't real; overstating a competing offer is easy to catch and expensive to be caught doing.
