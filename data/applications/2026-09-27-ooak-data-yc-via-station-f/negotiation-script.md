# Negotiation Script: Ooak Data, Head of Engineering

## Context before talking numbers

Ooak Data is a Y Combinator company founded in 2024 by Pierre-Louis Vouteau (CEO), Grégoire (CPO) and Thomas Aubry (CTO). It turns a company's internal data (Gmail, Slack, Notion, Jira, Drive, SharePoint) into anonymized, structurally identical digital twins and RL environments for AI agents, and sells them to frontier labs. The posting mentions 8-figure contracts signed with frontier labs; YC materials say revenue is well above 7 figures and the plan is to ingest 300+ full data ecosystems within six months. The team is small and the role is employee number one on the engineering leadership side.

The posting is explicit: **Paris city center, on-site with 1-2 days WFH, CDI, €100k-130k base plus BSPCE.** That changes how the Swiss numbers get used.

1. **This is a French contract in EUR.** A Swiss-level base isn't realistic here, and pushing for one will read as not having read the posting. Swiss numbers are useful as a reference for what I'm giving up, which supports asking for the top of the band plus more equity, not a bigger base.
2. **Location decides everything else.** Geneva to Paris is about three hours by TGV. Before any numbers, settle which of these it is: relocating to Paris, weekly commuting (e.g. 3 days in Paris), or something else. A Geneva resident employed in France raises cross-border tax and social security questions (France-Switzerland tax treaty, where social charges are paid, where BSPCE gains are taxed). Get a fiduciary's opinion before signing.
3. **Equity is the real lever.** At this stage the BSPCE grant is where the value is. The base band is fixed-ish; the grant, vesting terms and exit window are not.

Questions for the first call:
- "How strict is on-site? Would a setup of three days in Paris and the rest remote from Geneva work in the first year?"
- "Is there a relocation or travel budget for this role?"
- "What's the BSPCE pool size, and how much of it is set aside for this hire?"
- "What was the last round, at what valuation, and when is the next one planned?"
- "Is the band €100k-130k fixed, or is there room for a Head of Eng who also sets direction with Thomas?"

## Market reference (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough ranges from general market knowledge for 2025-2026. Check levels.fyi, Figures.hr and Welcome to the Jungle salary data for Paris, the Swissdevjobs salary report for Switzerland, and ideally one or two YC founders in Paris.

| Reference | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Head of Engineering, Paris seed / post-YC startup | €110k-140k |
| Head of Engineering / VP Eng, Paris Series A-B scale-up | €130k-170k |
| Head of Engineering, Geneva or Zürich startup | CHF 190k-240k |
| Staff+ / Eng Director, Zürich big tech, base only | CHF 230k-290k |

The posting's €100k-130k is consistent with the Paris seed range. The Swiss rows exist to justify going to the top of the band and to frame an equity ask, nothing more.

Typical BSPCE for a first Head of Engineering at a post-YC company with real revenue: roughly **0.5%-1.5%** fully diluted, depending on how late in the round the hire comes. Check against the pool size before quoting a number.

**My numbers**

- **Opening anchor:** €135k base plus 1.25% in BSPCE.
- **Target:** €125k-130k base plus 0.8%-1.2% BSPCE, with a travel or relocation budget.
- **Walk-away:** below ~€115k base with under 0.5% equity, or a hard five-days-in-Paris requirement with no relocation support. At that point the move from a Geneva Senior Staff salary doesn't hold up.
- **Trade-offs I'd accept:** €120k base for a bigger grant (1.25%+), or the band's top with a smaller grant if the next round is close and the valuation is high.

## Opening anchor script

Use this when they ask for expectations, ideally after they've shared the band (they already have, in the posting).

> "I've read the €100k-130k range and I'm comfortable working inside it. Given the scope, leading the ingestion squad, building the team to 15-30 and setting direction with Thomas, I'd be at the top: €135k base. For context, the Geneva roles I'm looking at are in the CHF 190k-240k range, so I'm accepting a real cut in cash to do this. That's fine if equity reflects it. I'd be looking at around 1.25% in BSPCE."

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low

**Scenario: €110k-115k base, 0.4% BSPCE.**

> "Thanks, I can see the logic. The base I can work with if the equity moves. At 0.4% the package is a lot less than I'd get staying where I am, and I'd be the person hiring most of the engineers who come next. Could we get to €125k and 1%? If base is truly capped, I'd rather take €120k and 1.25%."

**Scenario: they say the band is fixed and the grant is standard.**

> "Understood. Then let's look at the terms rather than the size. Could we agree on an extended exercise window after departure, and single-trigger acceleration on a change of control? Those cost you nothing today."

**Scenario: they want full-time on-site in Paris.**

> "I can be in Paris most of the week, especially during the first months while the team is small. What I need is either three days in the office with the rest remote from Geneva, or a relocation package that makes a full move work. Which of those is easier for you?"

## Equity: what to ask about BSPCE

- **Percentage, not just count.** Ask for fully diluted percentage and the current total share count. A number of BSPCE means nothing on its own.
- **Strike price and last valuation.** Know the exercise price and the last priced round's valuation.
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask whether the cliff can be 6 months given the "hands-on from day one" scope.
- **Exit window:** French BSPCE often lapse fast after departure. Ask for an extended exercise period (e.g. several years), since a 90-day window forces a choice between paying a lot of cash or losing vested equity.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (acquisition plus termination), and try single-trigger on a portion (e.g. 25%).
- **Anti-dilution:** employees don't normally get anti-dilution protection, and asking for it signals inexperience. Ask instead about refresh grants after each round and at the 15-person and 30-person milestones.
- **Tax residency:** BSPCE get favourable French tax treatment under conditions tied to the holder and the company. If I stay a Swiss tax resident, the treatment may differ. Confirm with a fiduciary before accepting.

## Geography: pushback in both directions

The usual "Geneva is expensive" argument doesn't apply the normal way, because the role is Paris-based. Use it this way instead:

> "I'm not asking for a Geneva salary for a Paris job. I'm pointing out that I'm taking a cut in cash to join, which is why the equity matters so much to me."

If they suggest a lower base because I'd be partly remote:

> "The scope doesn't change with the number of days I'm on-site. Team size, hiring and delivery are the same job either way. I'd rather talk about how the in-office days work than about discounting the role."

## Competing offer template

Only use this if it's true, or reword it as "other conversations" if it isn't at offer stage.

> "Being transparent: I'm in late stages with a Geneva company for a Staff+ role at around CHF [X] base. Ooak is my first choice because of the problem and the stage, and I'd rather make this work. If we can land at [€ base] and [% BSPCE] with [on-site arrangement], I'm ready to sign this week."

If there's nothing concrete:

> "I'm talking to a couple of other teams, mostly Staff+ roles in Switzerland. None of them are as interesting as this problem, which is why I'm spending time on getting the terms right here."

## Before the call

- Confirm the on-site arrangement I actually want (relocate, commute, or hybrid).
- Get a fiduciary's view on France-employed, Geneva-resident tax and BSPCE treatment.
- Check the EUR/CHF rate the morning of the call.
- Have the list of what I'd hire first and in what order; it makes the equity ask credible.
