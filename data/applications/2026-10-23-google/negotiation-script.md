# Negotiation Script: Google, Staff Software Engineer, Shopping Graph (Zürich)

## The shape of this offer

The LinkedIn listing shows Zürich, Switzerland, full-time, on the Feeds Features team inside Shopping Graph Data Acquisition. The page title shows a US location instead ("Des Moines, IA" on one fetch, "Boydton, VA" on another), which contradicts the header, so **confirm with the recruiter that the role really sits in Zürich** before anything else. No salary is posted for Switzerland.

"Staff Software Engineer" at Google means **L6**. Big-company offer, so there's no equity percentage, cliff or anti-dilution to argue about. The package is made of:

1. **Level.** L6 versus L5 is the biggest number in the whole negotiation. Google is known for down-levelling experienced hires after the hiring committee, especially when the candidate hasn't worked at big-tech scale before. Expect this risk and plan for it.
2. **Base salary**, inside the L6 Zürich band.
3. **On-hire GSU grant** (Google Stock Units, i.e. RSUs), set in USD and vesting over four years. Google has changed its vesting schedule more than once, so ask for the exact schedule in writing.
4. **Sign-on bonus**, cash, sometimes split over two years, usually with a clawback if you leave within a year.
5. **Annual bonus target** (around 20% at L6) and **annual refreshers**, both set by level and performance and hard to move at offer time.

How Google's process affects timing: the recruiter can't discuss real numbers until after the hiring committee and team match. Comp is then proposed by the recruiter and approved centrally. Most of the movement comes from **competing offers and level**, not from arguing about base.

## Three things to settle early

1. **Level calibration.** Ask the recruiter directly whether the loop is calibrated for L6, and what happens if the committee sees an L5 signal. Point to 17+ years, a cofounder exit, Staff-level cross-team work at Benevity and the multi-agent/prompt engineering experience the posting lists as a minimum qualification.
2. **Hybrid pattern and commute.** Google generally expects around three office days a week. Geneva to Zürich is roughly 2h45 each way by train, which isn't sustainable three times a week. Decide in advance between relocation (ask for the full relocation package) and a weekly-commute arrangement, and raise it before the offer, not after.
3. **Vesting schedule and refresh policy.** Ask exactly how the on-hire grant vests (front-loaded or even, monthly or quarterly) so year-one comparisons with other offers are fair.

## Market anchors

These are rough estimates. **Verify every figure on levels.fyi (Google, Zürich, L5 and L6), Glassdoor CH, and with one or two people at Google Zürich before any comp call.** Google Zürich is the company's largest engineering site outside the US and pays at the top of the Swiss market.

| Role / market | Base (CHF) | Bonus | Stock per year (approx.) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google Zürich L6 Staff | 220k to 270k | ~20% target | CHF 100k to 200k | Estimate, verify |
| Google Zürich L5 Senior (down-level case) | 180k to 220k | ~15% target | CHF 50k to 100k | Avoid if possible |
| Microsoft Zürich Principal (posted band) | 184k to 310k | 0 to 25% | varies widely | Live application, real reference |
| Swiss scale-up, Staff / Principal IC | 170k to 220k | 0 to 15% | small equity | Realistic non-big-tech alternative |

Takeaway: at L6, expect base around CHF 240k to 265k and year-one total compensation somewhere around CHF 350k to 450k depending on the stock grant and sign-on. Most of the room is in **level, GSUs and sign-on**.

Swiss specifics worth raising:

- **GSU taxation.** In Switzerland RSUs are generally taxed as income at vest, at market value. Geneva rates apply if you stay resident in Geneva; Zürich rates if you move. Ask HR how withholding works.
- **Pension (BVG/LPP).** Ask about the employer contribution and whether it goes above the legal minimum. At Google it usually does, and it's real money.
- **Relocation.** If you move to Zürich, ask for the full relocation package (temporary housing, moving costs, a relocation allowance).
- **Notice period.** Swiss notice periods are often three months. Make sure the start date and sign-on payout schedule reflect that.

## Scripts

### When they ask for expectations before the committee

> "I'd rather not put a number on it before we know the level and the team. What I can say is that I'm comparing against Staff and Principal roles in Zürich, including one at Microsoft, so I'd expect the offer to be competitive with L6 in Zürich."

If pushed:

> "For L6 in Zürich, I'm thinking around CHF 275k base, with a strong GSU grant. I'm flexible on how base, stock and sign-on split, as long as the total reflects the level."

### When the offer comes in low

> "Thanks, I appreciate the detail. The team and the problem are a great fit for me, so I'd like to make this work. The total is below what I'm seeing for L6 in Zürich, though, mostly on the stock side. If the grant could move to around USD 500k, and we added a sign-on of around CHF 60k to cover what I'm leaving on the table, I'd be ready to sign."

Then stop talking. Let them come back.

### When they say the base is fixed

> "Understood. In that case, can we look at the GSU grant and the sign-on instead? Those matter more to me than base anyway."

### If they down-level to L5

> "I'll be honest, L5 surprises me. The posting asks for multi-quarter roadmaps, technical leadership and mentoring, which is what I've been doing as a Staff engineer for five years and as a cofounder before that. Could you share what signal led to L5? I'd like to understand whether there's a way to address it, maybe with one more design conversation, before we talk numbers."

### Geographic discount pushback

If anyone suggests a lower number because you'd work partly from Geneva:

> "Geneva's cost of living is about the same as Zürich's, and I'll be in the office on the same schedule as the rest of the team. I don't think a location adjustment applies here."

### Using a competing offer (real or in progress)

The Microsoft Zürich Principal application from the same day is the obvious reference. Only mention numbers you actually have.

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late stages with another Zürich team at a similar level. Google is my first choice because of the Shopping Graph work, but the other offer is ahead on total compensation, mainly stock. Is there room to close that gap?"

If asked for proof, Google recruiters usually want the written offer. Share it only if you're comfortable; redact what you don't want to share.

### Closing

> "If we can land at [base] with [GSU grant] and [sign-on], I'm ready to accept this week."

Get everything in writing: level, base, GSU grant and vesting schedule, sign-on and clawback terms, bonus target, relocation, and the agreed hybrid pattern.
