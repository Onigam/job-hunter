# Negotiation Script: VP Engineering, Daedalean AI (Destinus Group)

## Context

Daedalean is a Zurich-based, safety-critical AI avionics company (computer vision, deep learning, robotics for aircraft autonomy) that was acquired by Destinus, a European aerospace and defense group with 500+ employees, in a deal worth roughly CHF 180 million (~$225M), completed around the end of 2025. This is no longer a scrappy pre-seed startup negotiation. It's a post-acquisition VP role inside a funded, multi-hundred-person group with defense and civil aviation contracts, so comp should be benchmarked closer to a Series C/growth-stage or small-cap public company, not an early seed startup.

## Switzerland market anchors

- VP Engineering / Head of Engineering at a funded Zurich or Geneva tech company (50-500 employees): base CHF 220,000-280,000, with total comp (base + bonus + equity value) often reaching CHF 280,000-380,000.
- Defense and aerospace-adjacent tech in Switzerland tends to run at or slightly above general tech VP bands, given the specialized talent pool and security clearance requirements.
- Geneva and Zurich cost of living is comparable; no geographic discount should be accepted for either location.
- Reference points to cite if pushed for justification: Swiss tech salary surveys (Léon & Cie, Handelszeitung tech comp reports) and comparable VP-level roles at Swiss scale-ups like SonarSource, Nexthink, or Scandit.

## Opening anchor

"Based on what I'm seeing for VP Engineering roles at funded Swiss aerospace and deep-tech companies post-Series-C-equivalent, and given the scope here spans software, ML, and hardware-adjacent teams across two merged orgs, I'm anchoring around CHF 260,000-300,000 base, plus a meaningful equity or phantom share grant in the combined Destinus-Daedalean entity. I'd want to understand the full comp structure, including how equity converted through the acquisition, before we get more specific."

## If they come in low

"I hear the number. Here's what's making me pause: this role isn't just running an existing engineering team, it's integrating two organizations with genuinely different disciplines, software, ML, and hardware, right after a nine-figure acquisition. That's a harder and higher-stakes mandate than a standard VP Engineering scope, and I'd expect comp to reflect that. Is there room to move on base, or should we look at structuring more of the gap through equity or a signing component?"

## Equity and vesting notes

- Daedalean's own equity, if any legacy grants are being discussed, would have converted into Destinus equity or cash as part of the acquisition; ask directly how prior Daedalean option holders were treated, since that sets precedent for new grants.
- Push for a standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff, and ask whether Destinus has already priced a post-acquisition 409A-equivalent valuation (Swiss companies often use a comparable formal valuation for option pricing).
- For a VP-level hire joining after the deal closed, target 0.15%-0.4% of the combined entity, adjusted for how diluted the pool already is post-acquisition, plus acceleration language (single or double trigger) in case of a future sale or restructuring.
- If equity feels thin given the deal's size, ask about a cash-equivalent long-term incentive plan instead, which is common in larger, acquisition-backed European companies that don't want to keep diluting the cap table.

## Geographic discount pushback

"I want to flag that Geneva and Zurich are both high cost-of-living markets, among the highest in Europe, so I wouldn't expect any adjustment downward relative to the Zurich-based band you're already working from. If anything, the fact that this role needs to bridge two engineering cultures across locations argues for the higher end of whatever range you have."

## Competing offer leverage template

"I'm in a few later-stage conversations right now for engineering leadership roles at comparable Swiss and European companies, so I do have a timeline I'm working against. I'd rather make this work here if the scope and comp line up, but I wanted to be transparent that I'm not evaluating this in isolation."

Use only if there's a genuine parallel process running; keep it vague on company names and firm on the timeline rather than inventing specific numbers.
