# Negotiation Script — Staff Engineer, AI Evals @ Sema4.ai

---

## Market Context

**Sema4.ai profile:** Series B-stage enterprise AI agent company (formerly Robocorp, rebranded 2024). US-headquartered, with roles posted in Atlanta and remote. Staff Engineer, AI Evals is an early, high-impact position, likely one of the first to own this function.

**US Staff Engineer (AI/ML specialty), 2025-2026 benchmarks:**
- Total compensation: $220k-$320k (base + equity + bonus)
- Base salary: $170k-$230k
- Equity: 0.05%-0.25% for staff-level at Series B, 4-year vest, 1-year cliff
- Geographic note: if the role is remote-friendly from Geneva, expect them to apply a geographic adjustment of 20-30% below US rates unless you push back

**Geneva/Switzerland Staff Engineer, 2025-2026 benchmarks:**
- Senior Staff IC at scale-up: CHF 200k-260k total comp
- If paid in USD via EOR (employer of record): conversion to CHF and local employment costs apply
- Geneva cost of living index is higher than most US cities outside NYC/SF; the "geographic discount" argument cuts both ways

---

## Opening Anchor

If they ask about compensation expectations early:

> "I've been looking at Staff-level roles in AI infrastructure and evals. The range I'm seeing for this level and specialty, in USD, is roughly $220k-$270k total comp. I'm flexible on the split between base and equity depending on stage and vesting terms, but that's the range I'm working from."

If they push back with a lower number:

> "I appreciate you being direct. The number you've mentioned is below what I've been seeing for Staff roles with this scope, especially in the evals space where the talent pool is small. Can you walk me through how that's structured: base, equity, and bonus separately? That'll help me understand where there's room."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

Likely scenario: they quote a number based on Geneva being "cheaper" than SF/NY.

> "I want to make sure we're comparing the right things. Geneva is consistently ranked as one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and often in the world. The cost-of-living adjustment argument usually runs the other way here. The benchmark I'm using reflects what engineers at this level are earning in the Swiss market, which doesn't come in below US rates."

If they cite a specific geographic policy:

> "That makes sense as a framework. What I'd ask is: given that Geneva isn't a discount market, could we treat this more like a tier-1 European market rather than applying a blanket adjustment? The talent you're hiring for this kind of role here isn't cheaper than what you'd find in Seattle."

---

## Equity Negotiation (if startup/early-stage offer)

**Opening position:** ask for the full cap table context before evaluating any equity offer.

> "Before I can evaluate the equity piece meaningfully, I'd want to understand the basics: the last 409A valuation, total shares outstanding, the series B post-money valuation, and any liquidation preferences that would affect common shareholders on exit. I'm not asking for a legal document, just the headline numbers."

**If they offer 0.1% or below:**

> "Given that this is an early, founding-level role for the evals function, I'd expect the equity to reflect that. The people who define how you measure quality at this stage have disproportionate influence on the product trajectory. I'd want to see something in the 0.15%-0.25% range to feel like the upside reflects the contribution."

**Vesting terms to push for:**
- 4-year vest, 1-year cliff (standard, accept this)
- Post-termination exercise window: push for at least 2-5 years (many startups default to 90 days, which is punishing)
- Ask whether options are ISOs or NSOs (ISOs have better US tax treatment; for Swiss residents, the tax treatment differs anyway, but it signals seriousness about equity design)

---

## Competing Offer Script (if applicable)

If you have another offer in hand:

> "I want to be transparent: I'm at the final stage with another company, and they're moving on a similar timeline. The offer there is structured differently but lands in a similar range. I prefer this role for reasons I'm happy to explain, but I want to make sure we can close the gap if there is one."

If you don't have a competing offer but want to create urgency:

> "I'm in final conversations with two other teams right now and expect to have something concrete within two weeks. I'd rather not make a decision based purely on timeline pressure, but it would help to know if there's flexibility to move quickly once we align on comp."

---

## Red Lines

- Don't accept below CHF 200k / $200k USD total comp for a Staff-level evals role at a funded company
- Don't accept equity without understanding the cap table and liquidation stack
- Don't accept a 90-day post-termination exercise window; push for 2+ years minimum
- If the role isn't remote-friendly from Geneva, clarify before investing further in the process
