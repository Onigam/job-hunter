# Negotiation Script — n8n / Sr Cloud Engineer

---

## Market Context

**n8n's indicated range:** Job listings suggest up to ~€192K for the top of their engineering band (European remote). For a Sr Cloud Engineer role specifically, expect the realistic offer range to sit between €110K and €160K depending on location-adjustment model used.

**n8n's compensation model:** Berlin-based company, pays in EUR for European hires. n8n has raised $240M and is scaling aggressively (500k to 10M+ user target). This is a well-funded Series C-equivalent company, not a scrappy seed stage.

**Geneva vs. Berlin cost-of-living delta:** Geneva consistently ranks 40-50% more expensive than Berlin on housing, taxes, and general cost of living. Swiss social contributions add another layer. A €130K salary in Berlin is a materially different lifestyle than €130K in Geneva.

**Switzerland market rate for this profile (Sr Staff / Platform Engineer):**
- Sr Cloud / Platform Engineer at funded scale-up: CHF 140,000 to 185,000 base + equity
- Staff+ IC with founding-engineer background: CHF 160,000 to 210,000 base + equity
- EUR/CHF rate is approximately 0.95, so €1 = CHF 1.05 (minor adjustment, not a material FX risk)

**n8n equity:** Company valuation likely in the $1B range given $240M total raised. Options/ESOP is standard. At this stage, a Sr Engineer grant is typically 0.05-0.10% fully diluted.

## Negotiation Strategy

### Opening Anchor

If n8n asks for salary expectations before making an offer:

"Based on market data for this level in Switzerland, I'm targeting €155K to €165K base, or CHF equivalent. Geneva sits in the same cost-of-living tier as Zurich or London, which is meaningfully different from Berlin, so I'd expect the package to reflect that. On top of base, I'd want to discuss a meaningful equity stake given the growth stage."

If they push for a single number: "€160K base is where I'd start the conversation."

### When They Make an Offer

**If the offer lands at €140K or above:** A reasonable starting point. Counter up:

"I appreciate the offer. Given Geneva's cost structure relative to Berlin, I'd expect to be closer to the €155-160K range. Can you move on base, or alternatively, is there flexibility on the equity grant size?"

**If the offer lands between €120K and €140K:** This is a meaningful Geneva discount and worth pushing back directly:

"That's a bit below what I'd expect for this profile in Switzerland. The cost of living here is significantly higher than Berlin, and the Swiss tax burden is real at this income level. I'd need to see base move to at least €145K to feel the package is calibrated for Geneva, not Berlin. Alternatively, if the equity grant is substantial, I'm happy to have that conversation."

**If the offer is below €120K:** This signals a blanket European discount without Geneva adjustment. Push back firmly:

"To be direct, that's below the range I'd expect for a senior profile with a full founding-to-acquisition cycle in Switzerland. The Swiss market for this level runs CHF 140-185K base. I'd need to see the offer move materially before I could seriously consider it."

## Equity Negotiation

n8n offers equity to all employees. Given the $240M raised and growth trajectory, these options could be meaningful on an exit or IPO.

**Questions to ask before signing:**
- "What's the current strike price and the last 409A / independent valuation?"
- "What percentage of fully diluted shares does this grant represent?"
- "What's the vesting schedule? Is there a cliff?"
- "Is there single or double-trigger acceleration on acquisition?"
- "What was the price per share in the most recent primary round, and how dilutive was it?"

**What to look for:**
- Standard: 4-year vest, 1-year cliff. Acceptable. Anything beyond 4 years total, flag it.
- Acceleration on acquisition: important given n8n's stage and potential strategic interest.
- The $100/month OSS fund and unlimited AI budget are perks, not comp. Don't let them substitute for equity or base.

**If base is firm but equity feels light:**
"I'd consider taking a lower base if the equity grant is materially larger. What does a competitive equity package look like at this level compared to a standard senior IC grant? I want to be genuinely invested in the outcome."

## Geographic Discount Pushback

n8n is a Berlin-based company. They may default to a Berlin or "European average" salary band. Geneva is not an average European city.

"I understand the rationale for geographic bands. For context, Geneva ranks alongside Zurich and London as one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and the Swiss tax structure at this income level is materially different from Germany's. I'd ask you to use Geneva-specific data rather than a European average when calibrating the offer. I'm happy to share cost-of-living comparisons if that's helpful."

---

## Remote Premium Angle

n8n is remote-first for European hires. They don't have a Geneva office. That means no office costs, no local HR overhead, no facilities costs associated with Magino. This is a net positive for n8n that can be used as a lever:

"You don't carry any local office costs for a Geneva hire. The cost savings relative to a Berlin-based employee are meaningful. I'd expect that to translate into a package that reflects Geneva's market, not Berlin's."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in hand:

"I want to be transparent: I have an offer from [Company] at a comparable stage, with a base around CHF 155-165K. n8n is the more interesting technical challenge for me, but I need the package to be close enough that I'm not leaving significant comp on the table."

If you don't have a competing offer:

"I've been deliberate in this search, which is why I'm at a late stage with only a few companies. n8n is at the top of my list, but I want to make sure the package reflects my level before I close off other conversations."

---

## Timing and Closing

Once an offer is verbal, ask for it in writing and take 48 hours before responding. Use that window to:

1. Verify the equity details: strike price, grant size as percentage of fully diluted shares, vesting schedule
2. Confirm currency denomination (EUR or CHF) and check for any FX mechanism
3. Review any non-compete or IP assignment clauses specific to Swiss employment law
4. Confirm start date flexibility (n8n sponsors German visas; Swiss work rights should be confirmed as a formality)

Do not accept verbally before seeing the written offer document.
