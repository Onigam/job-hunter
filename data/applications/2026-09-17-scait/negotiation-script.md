# Negotiation Script: SCAIT, Founding Engineer (Frontend Focus)

## Context

SCAIT is pre-seed/seed stage (oversubscribed pre-seed, plus a CHF 150k seed extension and CHF 40k Venture Kick grant), based in Risch-Rotkreuz, canton Zug. The posted range is CHF 105,000-125,000 for the role. This is early enough that cash is genuinely constrained by runway, not just negotiating posture, so the real room to negotiate is mostly equity and title, not squeezing the last 10k out of salary.

Market context: a Senior Staff-level engineer in Zurich or Zug-area tech companies typically sees CHF 130,000-160,000 base at a funded Series A+ company, and CHF 150,000+ at a bank or big tech satellite office. Zug canton has some of the lowest cantonal tax rates in Switzerland, which partly offsets a lower gross figure, worth mentioning if they lean on "but Zug is tax-friendly" as a reason to lowball. Rotkreuz is not Geneva or Zurich rent-wise, so cost of living is not a strong lever either way.

At pre-seed, a founding engineer's real comp is the equity, not the base. The negotiation should spend more energy there than on the last few thousand francs of salary.

## Opening anchor

Base salary: anchor at CHF 125,000, the top of their posted band, and hold that as the starting point rather than splitting the difference downward before they've even made an offer.

Talking points to justify the anchor:
- 17+ years including architecture ownership, not just execution; the role needs someone who can make frontend architecture decisions unsupervised, which is a senior rate even at pre-seed.
- A completed founder-to-acquisition cycle (Alaya to Benevity) means less ramp time spent learning what "founding engineer" actually requires day to day.
- If they push back on budget, ask directly what the constraint is: total headcount budget, investor covenant, or a genuine ceiling. The answer changes what's negotiable.

## Equity ask

At this stage, equity should be the center of the conversation, not an afterthought.

- Ask for the fully diluted percentage, not just a share count or option pool number; a raw number of shares is meaningless without knowing the cap table.
- For an early (not first) engineering hire at a company past pre-seed with a small existing team, 0.5%-1.5% fully diluted is a reasonable opening range to discuss, depending on how many people are already on the cap table and how much has been raised.
- Standard vesting: 4 years, 1-year cliff. Push back on anything longer than 4 years or a cliff longer than 12 months.
- Ask whether there's an early-exercise option and what the strike price would be based on the last 409A or Swiss equivalent valuation; exercising early, if affordable, matters a lot for the eventual tax treatment.
- If Swiss-style options or an ESOP-lite structure is used instead of US-style equity, ask specifically how it converts on an acquisition, since that's exactly the scenario Magino has lived through once already and knows to ask about.
- Anti-dilution: ask what protection, if any, exists for the initial grant in a future down round. At pre-seed this is unlikely to be strong, but it's worth asking so there are no surprises later.

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

"Thanks for sending this over. The role and the problem you're solving are genuinely the reason I'd want to join at this stage, so I want to find a structure that works for both sides rather than walk from a good fit over a gap. On the base, I was anchoring closer to CHF 125,000 given the scope, mostly because the role needs someone making frontend architecture calls solo from early on, not ramping into it. If cash is tight given where you are in the raise, I'd rather talk about closing that gap through equity or a compensation review tied to the next funding milestone than push on base alone."

If they hold firm on base:

"That's fair, I understand runway is the real constraint here. In that case, can we talk about the equity grant and whether there's room there, or a written commitment to revisit base at the next round?"

## Geographic discount pushback

If they try to lower the offer citing Rotkreuz/Zug being cheaper than Geneva or Zurich:

"I'd actually push back a little there. Zug's cantonal tax rate is lower, which helps take-home, but the role itself doesn't get easier because of the postal code, and I'd be commuting or relocating either way. I'd rather anchor comp to the scope of the role than to where the office happens to sit."

## Competing offer template

Use only if there's an actual competing process in flight; don't bluff a specific number.

"I want to be upfront that I'm in later-stage conversations with another team at a similar stage, so I do have a timeline I'm working against. I'd rather move forward with SCAIT if we can land on a structure that works, but I wanted to be transparent about the parallel process so there aren't surprises on timing."

## Things to get in writing before accepting

- Exact fully diluted equity percentage and current fully diluted share count, not just an options number.
- Vesting schedule and cliff, in writing, not verbal.
- Strike price and whether early exercise is available.
- Any commitment to a compensation or equity review tied to the next funding round.
- Notice period and any non-compete or IP assignment terms, standard for Switzerland but worth reading closely at a startup drafting its own contracts for the first time.
