# Negotiation Script: Espa Labs, Founding Software Engineer

## Context
Espa Labs is pre-launch, San Francisco HQ, hybrid or remote-first, backed by Village Global, Neo, and SV Angel with angels from Anthropic, Microsoft, Cognition and Waymo. Founding-level equity is explicitly called out in the posting, "competitive salary" is not defined, and the role reports directly to the CTO. This is a true seed-stage founding hire, not a Series B "founding engineer" title inflation, so equity matters more than usual and salary will likely be below Swiss market unless pushed.

## Market anchors

**Geneva / Switzerland reference (for framing, not the actual offer currency):**
- Senior/Staff backend or platform engineer, Geneva market: CHF 150,000 to CHF 190,000 base
- With founder-track / architecture-ownership scope: CHF 180,000 to CHF 210,000 base equivalent

**US early-stage seed comp reference (what Espa will actually be anchored to):**
- Founding engineer, seed-stage AI startup, SF: USD 160,000 to USD 210,000 base, 0.5% to 2.0% equity depending on how early and how funded
- Since this is explicitly pre-launch with a small founding team already assembled from Meta/Palantir/Dropbox/Databricks, expect the equity offer to land toward the lower half of that range unless the round is unusually large

Frame the ask in USD since that's what they'll pay in, but use the Geneva numbers internally as the floor for total comp, not base salary alone.

## Opening anchor
"Thanks for putting this together. Based on what I'm seeing for founding engineer roles at seed-stage AI companies with a comparable team pedigree, and factoring in that I'd be doing this from Geneva rather than relocating to the Bay Area, I was expecting something in the USD 190,000 to 210,000 base range plus a founding-level equity grant in the 1% to 1.5% band before dilution from this round. Where does your offer land against that?"

## If they come in low on base
"I get that early-stage cash is tight, and I'm not looking to make this only about base. If the equity number moves up to compensate, I can flex on cash. But if both are below what I quoted, I'd want to understand how the round is structured, because right now the offer reads more like an early employee package than a founding one."

## Equity negotiation
- Ask directly: current fully diluted share count, and whether the stated percentage is pre- or post- the current raise
- Push for a standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff, nothing longer
- Ask whether early exercise is allowed, and whether there's a post-termination exercise window longer than the standard 90 days, this matters a lot at a company this early since the stock could easily be illiquid for 5+ years
- If there's an anti-dilution or pro-rata clause for the next round available to founding hires, ask for it explicitly, it's sometimes offered informally to early engineers and never volunteered
- Sample line: "Can you tell me the current fully diluted cap table percentage this grant represents, and whether it's before or after the round you're currently raising on? I'd also want a 10-year exercise window if that's something you can do for founding hires."

## Remote / geography pushback
The listing itself says hybrid or remote-first, so don't let this get framed as a discount conversation. Script: "I noticed the listing says remote-first. I want to be upfront that I'm Geneva-based and not planning to relocate, but I'm used to working async with US teams and can overlap mornings Pacific into my evening. If remote-first means what it says, my rate shouldn't be adjusted down for location, since the value of the systems work doesn't change based on my timezone."

## Competing offer leverage (use only if true, or hold this in reserve)
"I'm in later-stage conversations with another platform-focused role right now, so I'd like to move relatively quickly on this. If you can get me a formal offer this week, I can prioritize responding to it before finalizing anything elsewhere."

## Closing framing
Keep the tone collaborative, this is a five-person-or-fewer founding team and the person negotiating is likely the CTO or a cofounder directly, not an HR generalist. Over-optimizing for base salary at a seed-stage company usually signals the wrong priorities to a founding team; anchor hard on equity terms and vesting protections instead, and treat base salary as the secondary lever.
