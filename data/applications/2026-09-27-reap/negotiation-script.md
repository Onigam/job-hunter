# Negotiation Script: Reap, Staff Engineer/Tech Lead, Agentic Payments

## Context before talking numbers

Reap is a stablecoin-native card issuer and payments infrastructure company headquartered in Hong Kong, backed by Index Ventures, Acorn Pacific and HashKey Capital. It's one of the first licensed issuers live on Visa Intelligent Commerce. In May 2026, Payward (Kraken's parent company) signed a definitive agreement to acquire Reap in a deal worth up to $600m. The role is listed in Ireland but the team is described as fully remote and async.

That changes the conversation in three ways:

1. **Equity is the big unknown.** Any Reap options granted now will probably be converted, cashed out or replaced by Payward equity. Find out which before talking about equity at all.
2. **Pay bands may be moving.** Mid-acquisition companies often align bands with the acquirer's levels. Kraken/Payward pays at crypto-exchange rates, which are usually above HK or Irish fintech startup rates. Ask whose levelling framework applies.
3. **Contract entity.** Ireland-listed, Geneva-based candidate. Find out early whether they'll employ in Switzerland (their own entity or an employer of record such as Deel or Remote), or only via Ireland or as a contractor. That decides social charges, the pension (LPP/BVG) and how equity is taxed.

Questions to ask the recruiter in the first call:
- "Is this role open to someone employed in Switzerland, and if so, through which entity?"
- "Post-close, will this role sit on Reap's levels or Payward's?"
- "What form will equity take for new hires: Reap options, Payward RSUs/options, or a retention cash component?"
- "Is any part of compensation paid in stablecoin or tokens?" (Plausible given the business. Want it in CHF or EUR, with any USDC portion treated as optional.)

## Market reference (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough ranges from general market knowledge for 2025-2026. Check levels.fyi (Kraken, Stripe, Revolut, Adyen), Glassdoor, the Swissdevjobs salary report, and anyone at Reap or Kraken before any call.

| Reference | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Staff Engineer, Dublin fintech/scale-up | €130k-165k |
| Staff Engineer, fully remote EU fintech/crypto | €130k-175k |
| Staff Engineer, Kraken-level crypto infra (remote) | $180k-230k equivalent |
| Staff/Senior Staff, Geneva or Zürich scale-up | CHF 180k-230k |
| Staff+, Zürich big tech, base only | CHF 230k-280k |

The Irish number is where they'll start. The Swiss scale-up number, backed by the Kraken-level band, is what I'm arguing for. "Tech Lead" in the title plus "shape the roadmap" also supports sitting at the top of Staff.

**My numbers**

- **Opening anchor:** CHF 220k base (about €235k at current rates; check before the call) plus equity.
- **Target:** CHF 195k-210k base plus a meaningful equity or retention grant.
- **Walk-away:** below ~CHF 175k base on Swiss payroll or EOR. Below that it's under Geneva Staff market and doesn't cover the risk of joining mid-acquisition.
- **If contractor only:** about CHF 1,000-1,150 per day. Confirm social charges (AVS/AHV self-employed), LPP and tax with an advisor first, and make sure the contract doesn't create a permanent-establishment problem for them, because that's usually their reason to refuse.

## Opening anchor script

> "Before I give a number, could you walk me through the whole package: base, bonus, equity and what happens to it after the Payward close, and the pension setup for someone in Switzerland? [Listen.] For a Staff and Tech Lead role owning agentic payments end to end, based in Geneva, I'm looking at around CHF 220k base. That's in line with Staff roles at Swiss scale-ups and with Kraken's bands for similar work. I'm flexible on the split between cash and equity once I understand what the equity actually is."

Say the number, then stop talking.

## If they come in low (for example €130k-150k, or a HK/Irish band)

> "Thanks, I understand that fits your Irish band. The thing is, I'm in Geneva, and my alternatives here start above that for Staff-level roles. Plus, joining during an acquisition carries some risk that a stable offer doesn't. To make it work I'd need at least CHF 195k base. If base is truly fixed, let's look at a sign-on bonus, a retention grant tied to the Payward close, and a written review at six months."

If base doesn't move, trade in this order:
1. A retention or equity grant in the acquirer's instrument (Payward), with the vesting schedule in writing.
2. A signing bonus to cover the gap in year one.
3. A written six-month review with a pre-agreed raise if agreed goals are met.
4. Title and scope in writing: Staff Engineer and Tech Lead for agentic payments, owning the roadmap input the posting describes.
5. Remote setup: a home-office budget, and travel to team meetups (Dublin, Hong Kong, wherever Payward puts them) paid by the company.

## Pushing back on the geographic discount

> "I get that the role is listed in Ireland. The work isn't tied to Dublin, though, since the team is fully remote and async, and Geneva's cost of living and social charges are among the highest in Europe. Paying below the Swiss market means I'd be taking a pay cut to do the same job I could do for a Swiss company. I'd rather we price the role and the scope, not the postcode."

If they insist on a location-based band, ask for the formula in writing, and check whether they already have a Swiss band (Kraken has hired in Switzerland before; verify).

## Equity notes (acquisition-specific)

- **Pre-close Reap grant:** ask directly whether it accelerates, converts into Payward equity at a fixed ratio, or gets cancelled and replaced. A grant that simply disappears at close is worth nothing.
- **Payward equity:** find out whether Payward is public or private at offer time (check this, don't assume). If private, ask for the latest 409A or fair-market value, the strike price, liquidity history (tender offers, secondary sales) and the post-termination exercise window. Push for 5-10 years instead of 90 days.
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask to drop or shorten the cliff given the acquisition timing, or for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination without cause). The first change of control is already under way, but Payward itself could be acquired or go public.
- **Anti-dilution:** there's little room for an employee here. Focus on the instrument, the ratio and the vesting instead.
- **Tax:** employee options are taxed differently in Switzerland, often at grant or exercise depending on the plan. Get the plan documents and have them read by a Swiss tax advisor before signing.
- **Tokens/stablecoin pay:** treat any USDC or token part as a bonus, never as base. Base salary stays in CHF (or EUR if unavoidable), paid through normal payroll.

## Competing-offer template (use honestly)

Only say this if it's true, even if the other process is at an early stage.

> "I want to be upfront: I'm also in late-stage conversations for a Staff role at a Swiss company, with a base in the CHF 200k range. Reap is my first choice because of the agentic payments work and the scope. If we can get to CHF [target] base with a clear equity or retention grant, I'm ready to commit and close this quickly."

If there's no other offer:

> "I'm speaking with a few companies at Staff level and I'd like to make a decision within the next two weeks. What flexibility do you have on base or a retention grant?"

## Walk-away checklist

Walk away (or pause) if:
- Base is below ~CHF 175k on Swiss payroll, with no sign-on or retention to make up for it.
- They can't explain what happens to equity at the Payward close.
- The only option is contractor status with no adjustment for social charges and pension.
- The "Tech Lead" part of the title disappears when the offer arrives.

## Before the call

- Check current EUR/CHF and USD/CHF rates and update the anchors.
- Check the status of the Payward deal (closed? regulatory approvals?) and Payward's IPO status.
- Look up Kraken Staff Engineer comp on levels.fyi.
- Have the STAR stories ready, especially #2 (integrating after an acquisition), since it lines up directly with where Reap is now.
