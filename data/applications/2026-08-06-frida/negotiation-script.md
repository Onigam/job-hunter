# Negotiation Script: Head of Engineering, Frida

## Context to keep in mind

The listing posts EUR 80k to 100k base plus 1.0 to 2.0 percent equity for a Zurich role, either onsite or remote, requiring around six years of experience. That number reads like it was benchmarked against a lower-cost market or a more junior band than the title implies. A Head of Engineering hire at an early-stage consumer AI company in Zurich should not land meaningfully below Swiss market for senior engineering leadership, even accounting for a startup discount, and the gap between "80 to 100k" and what a founding-level engineering lead commands locally is the first thing worth naming out loud rather than negotiating around quietly. The equity range, 1 to 2 percent, is plausible for an early Head of Engineering hire if the company is genuinely seed or pre-seed stage, but it needs a cap table conversation before it means anything: percentage alone tells you nothing without valuation, dilution history, and vesting terms.

## Switzerland market anchors (protect the floor, don't lead with these)

- Head of Engineering / VP Engineering, Zurich or Geneva, seed to Series A startup: CHF 160k to 200k base is the realistic local range for someone owning both the technical org and hands-on architecture.
- Staff-level engineering leads with founding or cofounder-level ownership on their resume tend to sit at or above the top of that band, especially when the hire is expected to build the engineering function rather than manage an existing one.
- Zurich cost of living is high enough that a EUR 80 to 100k base converts to noticeably less purchasing power than the CHF figures above, even before currency conversion friction.
- Early-stage Swiss startups often can't move much on cash but have real room on equity percentage, vesting acceleration, or a title and scope that reflects actual ownership of the engineering org.

Anchor to scope: owning engineering hiring, architecture, and technical direction for the company's core product, not a generic senior engineer number. Use the CHF figures to explain, plainly, why the posted range undersells the role.

## Opening anchor

Wait for a real number before naming one. If asked to go first:

"For a Head of Engineering role owning hiring, architecture, and technical direction from this early a stage, I'd expect a base closer to 160 to 180k CHF, along with an equity grant sized for a founding-level engineering hire rather than a senior IC. I'd want to understand the current cap table and valuation before putting a number on the equity side specifically."

## If they hold to the posted range

"I saw the range in the listing, and I want to be upfront that it sits well below what a Head of Engineering role typically pays in Zurich, even at an early stage. If the base genuinely can't move because of runway, I'd want the equity grant, vesting terms, or title and scope to reflect that gap instead, since I'd effectively be subsidizing the difference with sweat equity, and I want that to be a conscious trade both of us are making, not an assumption."

Fallback if cash truly cannot move: ask for equity above the posted 2 percent ceiling, a shorter cliff, or acceleration tied to a future raise, and ask whether a review at six or twelve months with a defined comp step-up is on the table.

## Equity and cap table questions

- Ask for the current fully diluted share count and most recent valuation, not just a percentage, since 1.5 percent of a seed-stage cap table means something very different once a Series A dilutes everyone.
- Confirm the vesting schedule and whether there's a standard one-year cliff with four-year vesting, and ask if there's an acceleration clause tied to acquisition, given the acquisition outcome is exactly what happened with Alaya.
- Ask how much runway the company has and when the next raise is expected, since that shapes both the near-term cash flexibility and how much the equity grant might dilute before it means real money.
- Ask whether prior engineering hires, if any exist, have equity at similar terms, to get a sense of whether the offer is standard or a starting point still up for discussion.

## Remote versus onsite leverage

The listing allows either onsite or remote in Zurich. If relocating from Geneva isn't the plan, remote status is worth confirming explicitly and in writing before comp is finalized, since a fully remote arrangement sometimes correlates with a lower internal band even when the posted range doesn't distinguish between the two. If onsite presence would strengthen the offer or the equity conversation, that's worth raising as a lever: "I'm open to relocating or spending regular time in Zurich if that changes how the role and comp are scoped."

## Competing offer template

Use only if a real competing process exists. Never invent a number or company name.

"I'm also further along in conversations for a comparable engineering leadership role. I'd rather make this work here given the product and the stage, so if there's room on base, equity percentage, or vesting terms, this is the point where I'd want to know."
