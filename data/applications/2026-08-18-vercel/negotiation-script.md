## Negotiation Script: Member of the Technical Staff, Internal Agent, Vercel

Context that changes the math here: Vercel closed a $300M Series F in September 2025 at a $9.3B valuation, roughly a 3x step-up from the $3.25B Series E in May 2024, and opened a $300M tender offer alongside it. Revenue run-rate reportedly hit around $340M by March 2026, up from $144M at the end of 2024. This isn't an early equity bet, it's a late-stage private company with real liquidity events already happening, so comp should be benchmarked as senior/staff IC at a well-capitalized scale-up, not discounted like a seed-stage gamble. The posted band is $208,000 to $312,000, SF-based, explicitly adjusted for location, fully remote, "Anywhere in the World." That band adjustment is the first thing to pin down, since "adjusted for location" from a US company can mean anything from a modest haircut to a full local-market reset.

## Market anchor

- US remote senior/staff engineer at a late-stage, well-funded developer tools company: the posted $208K-$312K band is the real anchor, not a hypothetical.
- Geneva-based remote staff-level engineer, comparable scope, benchmarked against Swiss cost of living: roughly CHF 170,000-210,000 base equivalent, which sits within a fair read of the posted band once currency and location adjustment are applied honestly rather than to the low end.
- Given 17+ years of experience, a founding-engineer track record, and an acquisition exit, the target is the upper half of the band, not the midpoint. Opening anchor: $270,000 base equivalent (or CHF equivalent), plus equity, framed as staff-level scope given the ownership this role implies over V's architecture.

## Opening anchor script

"The posted range is $208,000 to $312,000, adjusted for location. Given 17 years of experience, a founding-engineer background, and an exit through acquisition, I'd expect to land in the upper part of that range, somewhere around $270,000 base equivalent if I'm being benchmarked fairly against Geneva cost of living, plus a meaningful equity grant given where the company sits post-Series F. Can you walk me through how the location adjustment actually works for a Swiss-based remote hire, so we're both working from the same number?"

## If they come in low

"I hear the number. Is that adjustment based on Geneva specifically, or a broader 'international remote' bucket? I ask because Geneva's cost of living is close to San Francisco's on most indices, and I want to make sure the adjustment reflects that rather than a generic international discount."

If they hold the line: "I can flex on base if the equity grant makes up the difference, especially given the tender offer program already running, that's real near-term liquidity most companies at this stage don't offer. What I'd rather not do is anchor below $230,000 equivalent, that undervalues the scope of owning V's architecture end to end."

## Equity notes

- Ask for the equity grant's basis explicitly: is it priced off the $9.3B Series F valuation, or an internal 409A that may sit below the last round.
- Standard vesting is 4 years with a 1-year cliff, confirm that's what's being offered rather than something back-loaded.
- Ask directly about the $300M tender offer program opened alongside the Series F: is it a recurring program, and would a new hire's grant be eligible after the first vesting milestone, or is it a one-time event tied to that specific round.
- Given the 3x step-up from Series E to Series F, ask whether refresh grants are tied to a fixed annual cycle or to performance reviews, since the answer changes how much the initial grant size actually matters long term.

## Geographic and remote pushback

"The listing says the role is fully remote, anywhere in the world, with the band adjusted for location. I'd want that adjustment benchmarked against Geneva specifically rather than a flat international rate, since Swiss cost of living runs close to major US tech hubs, not a discount market. Happy to share comparable data on that if it's useful for whoever sets the band."

## Competing offer, if genuinely in play

"I'm in later-stage conversations with another company at a comparable scope, in a similar range to what I mentioned. This one is the one I'm most drawn to, mostly because of how early-stage the internal agent problem still is here, but I want to be upfront about timing so we can move at a pace that works for both sides."

## Close

A late-stage, well-capitalized company with an open tender offer program is not the place to accept a low-ball "we're adjusting for your location" number without pushback, the cash and liquidity are both already there. Get the base, the equity basis and valuation it's priced against, the vesting schedule, and whether the tender offer program applies to new hires, all in writing before committing. Confirm the location-adjustment methodology specifically, since that's the single number most likely to move if asked directly rather than assumed.
