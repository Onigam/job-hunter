# Negotiation Script — Linear, Full Stack Engineer

---

## Market Context

Linear is a US-incorporated company that hires remotely across Europe. They're Series B (raised $52M in 2022) with 25,000+ paying companies including OpenAI, Coinbase, and Ramp. By 2026 they likely have significant ARR and may have raised a Series C — but they're still private, which makes equity meaningful and harder to price.

They're known for lean teams and high output per engineer, which means they're calibrating to the top of the market to attract the right people. Their published benefits include "competitive salary and equity" with "employee-friendly equity terms" and early exercise options — the kind of framing that signals they've thought about this more than average.

**US market benchmarks for Staff / Senior Staff Full Stack Engineer (2025-2026, USD):**
- Top growth startups (Series B-D): $220K-$320K base, $300K-$450K OTE with equity
- Linear tier (premium remote product company): likely $250K-$340K base for a staff-level hire

**Switzerland / Geneva market benchmarks (CHF):**
- Senior Engineer (5-8 yrs): CHF 120K-160K base
- Staff / Principal Engineer: CHF 160K-210K base
- Senior Staff / Founding-level scope: CHF 200K-240K base

Linear probably denominates in USD for European hires. Do not let them default to a "European discount" on top of an already USD-denominated offer. Geneva is not Berlin.

**Your anchor target: USD 290K-310K base**, which puts you in the upper third of the staff band at a premium remote-first company. Justify it by the scope (full-stack product ownership, distributed systems experience, founding-engineer track record) not by personal need.

If they prefer CHF: target CHF 230K-260K base, calibrated to Geneva's cost of living and the 2026 CHF/USD rate.

---

## Opening Anchor Script

When they ask for your expectations or make a first offer:

> "Based on comparable roles at remote-first product companies at Linear's tier, I'm targeting around $300K base. That's calibrated to the staff engineer level and to my background, which includes building and owning a full-stack platform from zero through acquisition. I'm flexible on the structure if equity is meaningful, but I'd want the base to reflect the scope rather than just the title."

**Why this works:**
- It references a market benchmark, not personal need.
- It signals you've done the research on their tier specifically.
- It opens the equity conversation without pre-emptively trading base for it.
- The founding engineer reference anchors your value above a "strong senior" framing.

---

## If They Come In Below Your Anchor

If the initial offer is below $250K base (or CHF equivalent below CHF 200K):

> "I appreciate the offer. The role is genuinely interesting and I'd like to find a way to make this work. That said, the number is below what I'd need to move from my current position, given both my seniority level and Geneva's cost of living. Could you walk me through whether there's flexibility, either on base or on the equity component? I want to understand the full picture before saying no to something I'm interested in."

This keeps the door open without accepting a below-floor number. Don't counter immediately with a specific number when they're below your floor — get them to reveal whether room exists first.

**Hard floor: $240K base** (or CHF 195K equivalent). Below that, the equity would need to be exceptional to justify the move.

---

## Equity Negotiation

Linear's "employee-friendly equity terms" and early exercise language are meaningful signals. Early exercise means you can buy shares at grant time, starting the clock on long-term capital gains treatment earlier. Extended exercise windows after departure mean you're not forced into a "quit and immediately buy or lose your options" situation.

**What to ask:**
- Grant size: for a staff-level hire at a Series B/C company, expect 0.05%-0.2% depending on valuation. Push toward the higher end if the base is at the lower end of your range.
- Vesting: standard 4-year with 1-year cliff. If they offer anything shorter, ask why.
- Early exercise: confirm this is available and at what strike price. Ask for the current 409A fair market value and the most recent preferred share price. The spread between them tells you the option upside at a reasonable exit multiple.
- Acceleration: ask whether there's single-trigger acceleration on acquisition. Given that you've been through an acquisition, this is an informed ask, not a negotiating tactic.

**Equity ask script:**

> "On equity, I'd want to understand what percentage you're considering for this level and what the current fair market value looks like versus the preferred price. I've been through an acquisition from both sides, so I know how vesting timing can work against you if the structure isn't right. I'd also want to confirm the early exercise option is available from day one."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If Linear references a "Europe rate" or applies any discount relative to US-based hires:

> "Geneva is consistently one of the three most expensive cities in Europe, on par with Zurich and London, not Berlin or Lisbon. If you're calibrating to European cost of living, I'd ask that it be Geneva specifically, not an average. A geographic adjustment calibrated to the wrong city works out to a significant real-terms difference."

Supporting data: Geneva's cost of living index is 20-30% higher than London on housing and 15-20% higher overall. It's the most expensive city in Switzerland in most years.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have or receive another offer during the process:

> "I want to be straightforward with you: I'm in late-stage conversations with [Company X] and I'm close to an offer there. I'd prefer Linear because [specific reason], but I do have a timeline forming. Can we accelerate the process on your end, or move to the offer stage if you're ready?"

If no competing offer exists, do not fabricate one — Linear's team is small enough that they may probe specifics. Use time pressure honestly instead:

> "I'm actively in final-stage conversations elsewhere and expect to have an offer in the next two to three weeks. I want to give this process proper attention, so it would help to know your typical timeline from this stage."

---

## Non-Salary Items Worth Negotiating

- **Equipment budget**: CHF 3K-5K for hardware setup is standard in Swiss remote roles. Ask for it if not offered.
- **Co-working stipend**: Linear already covers this in their benefits. Confirm it applies to Geneva and ask about the amount.
- **Conference / learning budget**: CHF 2K-3K/year is reasonable. Often forgotten in the offer stage.
- **Start date**: Swiss notice periods are typically 3 months at this level. Linear should expect this. If they push for faster, propose a financial bridge or a phased start.
- **Title**: confirm whether "Staff Engineer" is the internal level or just the posting title. If "Senior Staff" is the actual level, get it in the contract.

---

## What to Never Do

- Don't accept the first number without at least one counter.
- Don't reveal your current salary before getting their offer range. If pressed: "I'd rather not anchor the conversation to my current package since the scope here is quite different."
- Don't negotiate against yourself by proposing a range. Give a single number and defend it.
- Don't treat equity as a bonus. Price it at a conservative 5-10x revenue multiple exit, not the optimistic case. That gives you a floor, not a fantasy.
