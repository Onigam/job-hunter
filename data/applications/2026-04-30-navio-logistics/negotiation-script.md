# Negotiation Script — Navio Logistics, Founding Engineer

---

## Market Context

Navio is a VC-backed early-stage startup, remote from Berlin. "Founding engineer" means position #1-3 on the engineering team: architectural ownership, hiring bar setting, direct CTO collaboration. That's a different compensation profile than a senior hire at Series B.

**Berlin founding/staff engineer market (2025-2026):**
- Senior SWE (5-8 years): €95-120k
- Staff engineer / founding engineer: €120-145k
- Founding engineer with prior exit and architecture ownership: €135-155k

**Your profile justification:** 17 years of experience, including building and selling a platform (Alaya → Benevity, $1B+ valuation). This isn't a senior engineer stepping up; it's a proven founder doing it again. The upper end of the range is the right anchor.

**Geneva cost-of-living note:** You're remote from Geneva where living costs are substantially higher than Berlin. If they raise a location discount, the counter is straightforward (see below).

---

## Anchor Position

**Base salary target:** €140,000  
**Opening range if pushed:** €135,000 - €150,000  
**Equity target:** 0.75% - 1.5% depending on funding stage  
**Walk-away base floor:** €115,000 (only if equity is meaningful, 1%+)

---

## Opening — When They Ask for Expectations

> "For a founding engineer role at this stage, I'm looking at a base in the €135-145k range. Equity matters here as much as cash because this is a genuine founding-team position. Before I anchor on a number, I'd want to understand the cap table and what a typical grant looks like. What have you budgeted for this role?"

Letting them reveal first is reasonable if they ask for flexibility. But don't move your anchor without new information.

---

## If They Come In Low (under €110k base)

> "Thanks for being upfront about that. That's below where I need to land given the seniority of this role and what I bring to it. I've built and sold a platform before — the founding engineer contribution is structurally different from a senior hire joining a larger team. I'd need to be at €130k minimum, and I'd want the equity to reflect the founding-team position clearly. Is there room to move on either of those, or would it make sense to talk equity structure first and come back to base?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Target:** 0.75% - 1.5% of post-round cap table, depending on stage.  
**Minimum acceptable:** 0.3% at early seed with clean terms; less than that and the risk/reward math doesn't hold.

**Questions to ask before accepting any equity number:**

- "What percentage of the post-round cap table does this grant represent?"
- "What's the current funding stage and how much has been raised?"
- "Is there an option pool refresh planned before the next round?"
- "What's the vesting schedule and cliff?"
- "What's the exercise window post-termination — 90 days or extended?"
- "Is there any provision for double-trigger acceleration in an acquisition?"

**Standard terms to push for:**
- 4-year vest, 1-year cliff (industry standard — insist on this)
- 10-year exercise window post-termination, not 90 days (the 90-day window is the most founder-unfriendly clause in startup comp; most engineers lose options they've earned because of it)
- Double-trigger acceleration: if the company is acquired and you're let go within 12 months, unvested shares accelerate

**Script for exercise window:**

> "One thing I'd like to discuss is the exercise window. The standard 90-day window post-termination is quite restrictive, especially for early employees who might hold significant unvested options. A 5-10 year window is becoming more common at founder-friendly companies. Is that something you can offer?"

---

## Geneva Location Discount Pushback

If they try to apply a discount because you're not in Berlin:

> "I understand the logic, but I'm fully remote and the work output is identical regardless of where I sit. Geneva's cost of living is significantly higher than Berlin's — if anything, the direction of any location adjustment would run the other way. I'd rather focus on the value of the role and avoid geographic adjustments that don't reflect how this arrangement actually works."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

**If you have a real competing offer:**

> "I want to be transparent: I'm at the offer stage with another company at a similar stage. I'm more interested in what Navio is building, but the timeline is compressing. Is there a point this week where you'd be ready to move to a formal offer?"

**If no competing offer — create urgency without fabricating one:**

> "I'm actively in several conversations and want to be respectful of everyone's time, including yours. I'd like to get to a clear picture of whether this is a mutual fit within the next two weeks. Can you give me a sense of your decision timeline?"

---

## Total Comp Framing

If cash is genuinely constrained (early-stage reality), you can negotiate a structured trade-off:

> "If the cash component is capped around €120k, I'd want the equity to reflect that trade-off explicitly — closer to 1.5% with clean vesting terms. The way I think about it: the founding engineer discount on cash needs to show up clearly in the cap table."

---

## Walk-Away Conditions

- Base below €115k with no path to revisit after Series A: decline
- Equity below 0.25% with standard 90-day exercise window: decline
- No willingness to discuss exercise window extension: flag as cultural red flag and factor into decision, not automatic walk-away but worth noting
- Cap table opacity (won't share basic structure): walk away — this is a sign of future misalignment
