# Negotiation Prep, Turiq (Technical Co-Founder / Founding Engineer)

## Context worth naming up front

Some of what's public about this opening (job-board postings referencing it) frames the role as hourly, around CHF 35/hour plus "equity potential," with flexible hours between 60-100%. That's a junior-intern comp structure, not a technical co-founder or founding engineer structure, and it's worth addressing directly and early rather than letting it anchor the whole conversation. If the actual offer on the table matches that framing, the fix isn't to negotiate the hourly rate upward, it's to reject the hourly frame entirely and move the conversation to salary plus equity, the way any real co-founder or first engineer hire gets structured.

## Market anchors, Switzerland

- Senior Staff / Principal-level engineers in Zurich or Geneva at funded startups: roughly CHF 150,000-190,000 base, sometimes higher at Series A+ with real revenue.
- Founding Engineer (first 1-5 hires, pre-seed to seed): base is often discounted from that, roughly CHF 110,000-150,000, offset by meaningful equity (commonly 1-3% for an early non-founder hire, more if the title genuinely means co-founder).
- Technical Co-Founder (equal or near-equal footing with existing founders): this isn't a salary negotiation so much as an equity split negotiation. A true technical co-founder joining pre-product or pre-traction should expect somewhere in the 15-35% range depending on what's already been built and how many other co-founders exist, plus a below-market or deferred salary until there's funding or revenue to support one.
- If Turiq is already onboarding paying institutions onto a live product, as their own materials suggest, that changes the math: there's a functioning product and early revenue, which justifies a real base salary even at founding-engineer level, not just equity and hope.

The opening move is to find out which of these three shapes the role actually is before naming a number. A "technical co-founder" title with an hourly junior rate attached is internally inconsistent, and it's fair to ask them to resolve that inconsistency before further conversation.

## Opening anchor

"Before we talk numbers, I want to understand the actual shape of this: am I joining as a co-founder with real ownership, or as employee number one or two with founder-level responsibility but employee-level equity? Those are different roles with different comp structures, and I want to make sure we're both picturing the same thing."

If they confirm it's genuinely co-founder-level: anchor equity conversation at 20-25%, salary at CHF 80,000-100,000 (below market, appropriate for true founder stage) unless there's already revenue, in which case push toward CHF 120,000+.

If they confirm it's founding-engineer-level (not co-founder): anchor at CHF 140,000 base plus 2-3% equity with a standard four-year vest and one-year cliff, citing the fact that they already have paying institutional users, not just a prototype.

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

"I hear the number. Given that Alaya went from zero to acquisition by a unicorn, and that I'd be stepping into a role with founder-level scope and expectations, I'd want the comp structure to reflect that, either in cash or in a bigger equity stake if cash is genuinely constrained right now. What I don't want is a structure where the responsibility is founder-level but the downside protection is intern-level. Where's your flexibility, cash or equity?"

## Equity negotiation notes

- Confirm strike price and 409A-equivalent valuation basis before agreeing to any percentage; a percentage means nothing without knowing the company's actual valuation and share count.
- Standard vesting: four years, one-year cliff. For a true co-founder role, push for acceleration on a change of control (single or double trigger) since that protects against being pushed out post-acquisition, something I've seen from the inside at Benevity.
- Ask directly about anti-dilution provisions and whether there's already an ESOP pool carved out that would dilute a founder-level grant before it's even issued.
- Get clarity on whether "equity potential" in any earlier posting meant a formal option grant or an informal promise; informal promises aren't worth modeling into a decision.

## Geographic pushback

Zurich and Geneva are both expensive by any European standard, and Geneva rents and health insurance in particular run well above the Swiss average. If there's any suggestion of a discount for being based in Geneva rather than Zurich, the response is straightforward: the commute is under three hours and the cost of living difference doesn't favor Zurich enough to justify a lower number, especially for a role that's remote-compatible several days a week.

## Competing offer advantage template

"I'm also mid-conversation with a couple of other opportunities at a similar stage, including one where the base comp is already in the range I mentioned. I'd rather build with a team and a product I actually believe in, which is why I'm here and not just taking the highest number, but I do need this offer to be in a comparable range for me to prioritize it over those conversations."

Use this only if genuinely true or close to true; the specific stories above (Alaya, the strangler-fig migration, the AI adoption story at Benevity) are the real edge here, not a bluffed offer.
