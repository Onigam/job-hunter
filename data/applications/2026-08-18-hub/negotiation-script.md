# Negotiation Script: Founding Engineer, Hub

Context that changes the math here: Hub is YC P26, about $2M raised, roughly two years old, building a multimodal data pipeline for physical AI with a network of 150,000+ contributors. The posted range, $80,000 to $120,000 base plus 1.0% to 2.0% equity, is written for the profile they describe in the listing: 6 months to 3 years of experience, typically fresh out of a French grande école. That is not the level this application is coming in at. Going into this conversation means being upfront that the scope on offer, owning critical systems including anti-fraud, payments across 100+ countries, and backend infrastructure scaling to a million users, is closer to a staff-level founding engineer mandate than an early-career one, and the comp conversation needs to start there, not from the posted band.

## Market anchor

- The posted $80,000 to $120,000 band reflects junior hiring economics at a $2M-raised, two-year-old company, not the market rate for 17+ years of experience with a proven founding-engineer track record, including a platform built from zero to acquisition by a company valued over a billion dollars.
- Staff-level founding or first engineers at seed to Series A companies in Europe typically see base ranges from CHF 140,000 to 180,000 equivalent when the role covers this much surface area, plus an equity grant sized for someone joining pre-scale rather than a standard early hire.
- Geneva cost of living is high even relative to Paris, so if the role stays remote-friendly with occasional on-site time, that is a reasonable basis for keeping comp closer to the Swiss end of the range rather than a French junior band.
- Opening anchor: base around CHF 150,000 (or the USD equivalent, roughly $170,000), with equity toward the top of a founding-engineer range given the ownership described, not the 1.0% to 2.0% posted for the junior track.

## Opening anchor script

"I want to be direct about where I'm coming from: the listing describes a scope, anti-fraud, payments infrastructure, and backend systems scaling toward a million users, that reads as a staff-level founding engineer mandate, not the early-career profile the posted range is built for. Given 17 years of experience and having built and scaled a platform through to acquisition, I'd expect a base closer to $170,000 or the CHF equivalent, with equity sized for that level of ownership. Is there flexibility to structure the offer around scope rather than the posted band?"

## If they come in low

"I hear that the posted range is fixed for this req. Would it make sense to bring in this role at a senior or staff track instead, with comp to match, given the systems I'd actually be owning? If the budget genuinely can't move on base, I'd want to talk about a larger equity grant and a defined comp review tied to concrete milestones, say the first two quarters, rather than accepting a junior band for staff-level scope."

If they hold the line entirely: "I understand budget constraints at this stage. If the base has to stay within the posted range, the equity has to do more of the work, I'd be looking at the higher end of what you've described, closer to 2.0%, plus clear vesting terms, before this makes sense given the level of ownership involved."

## Equity notes

- Ask for the current valuation basis (last priced round or a 409A-equivalent for a French structure, likely BSPCE if incorporated in France), the strike price, and whether the grant is options or a direct instrument.
- At $2M raised, dilution so far has likely been limited, so ask what percentage or share count a 1.0% to 2.0% grant actually represents in absolute shares, not just the headline percentage.
- Standard vesting is four years with a one-year cliff, confirm Hub follows that and ask whether early exercise is permitted, which matters for the tax treatment of a French BSPCE-style grant versus US-style options.
- Ask about the post-termination exercise window; early-stage grants sometimes carry short windows that quietly erode the value of an otherwise generous-looking grant.
- Given the ownership scope described (anti-fraud, payments, infra to a million users), ask directly whether the equity grant reflects a founding-team level of trust or a standard early-hire slice, since those numbers should differ meaningfully.

## Scope and seniority pushback

"The role as described, owning multiple production platforms, anti-fraud systems, and payment infrastructure across 100+ countries, with strict deadlines tied to top-5 AI company deliverables, is a lot of surface area for a single early-career hire to carry alone. I'd want to understand whether this is genuinely a solo mandate at my level or part of a small founding engineering team, since that changes both the comp conversation and how I'd structure the first few months."

## Competing offer, if genuinely in play

"I'm in later conversations with another company at a comparable scope and a materially higher base. Hub is the one I keep coming back to because of the physical AI data problem specifically, but I want to be upfront about the gap so we can figure out together whether there's a way to close it."

## Close

Being early and YC-backed is a reason to negotiate for real equity, not a reason to accept a junior comp band for a staff-level mandate; the systems described in this listing, fraud detection, payments, and infrastructure at scale, are the kind of ownership that commands a premium once the scope is named out loud instead of left implicit in a generic salary range. Get the base, the exact equity percentage or share count, the vesting and cliff terms, and the exercise window in writing before accepting anything, and if the company genuinely can't move past the posted band for a role with this much scope, that mismatch is worth treating as real signal about how the company prices ownership, not just an opening position to push past.
