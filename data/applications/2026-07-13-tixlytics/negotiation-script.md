# Negotiation Script: Tixlytics, Founding Engineer, Trading Infrastructure

## Context
Role is remote, based out of New York, backed by an already profitable brokerage (eight-figure revenue). Comp structure per the posting includes founding-team equity and an annual bonus tied to trading outcomes and system performance, not a pure salary play. Magino is Geneva-based, Senior Staff level, 17+ years, founder-to-acquisition track record (Alaya, sold to Benevity). Negotiate from Swiss cost-of-living and Staff+/founding engineer market rates, not NYC junior founding-engineer numbers, since the level and scope here are closer to a technical co-founder than an early hire.

## Market anchors (Switzerland, remote-for-US-company context)
Base salary for a Senior Staff or founding engineer working remote from Switzerland for a US-backed startup typically lands CHF 160,000 to CHF 210,000, depending on how much of the comp is carried by bonus and equity. Pure Swiss market comparables (Google, Meta, UBS engineering) for Staff-level ICs sit CHF 180,000 to CHF 240,000 base, but early-stage startups usually can't match that in cash and lean harder on equity and upside. Given Tixlytics ties a real bonus to trading performance rather than offering only a discretionary bonus, the base can reasonably sit lower than a pure-cash Staff role, provided the bonus formula and equity are well defined and not vague promises.

Opening anchor: CHF 195,000 base, plus the performance bonus structure as posted, plus founding-level equity (see below). Justify with Swiss cost of living, the founder-to-acquisition track record, and the fact that this role is scoped as an owner of critical trading systems, not a supporting engineer.

## Opening the conversation
Something like: "Before we get into numbers, can you walk me through how the trading-performance bonus is calculated today, and what founding-level equity has meant in practice for people who've joined so far?" Get the bonus formula and equity mechanics on the table before anchoring on base. A vague "meaningful equity participation" is not a number, and the base negotiation should reflect how firm or fuzzy that equity commitment actually is.

Once the structure is clear: "Based on what a Senior Staff engineer with a founding and exit track record commands, and factoring in Swiss cost of living, I'd be looking at a base around CHF 195,000, plus the bonus structure you've described, plus a founding equity grant in the range we can discuss. Does that fit where you're positioned for this hire?"

## If they come in low on base
"I hear that the bonus and equity are meant to carry a lot of the upside here, and I'm genuinely fine with that if the mechanics are solid. But I'd want the base itself to reflect the level of ownership this role has, given I'd be the one designing the valuation and execution systems from scratch. Could we land closer to CHF 175,000 to 185,000 on base, with the bonus and equity structured the way you've described?" Never accept a lowball on base as a way to "make it up" on equity unless the equity terms (vesting, cliff, strike price if applicable) are in writing.

## Equity negotiation
Ask directly: percentage or share count, vesting schedule (standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff), whether there's acceleration on acquisition or on being let go without cause, and whether there's any anti-dilution protection for early hires in future rounds. For a true founding engineer role, 0.5% to 2% is a reasonable range depending on stage and how much has already been raised or is bootstrapped from brokerage revenue. Since this company is backed by profitable trading operations rather than pure VC capital, ask explicitly whether that changes the equity pool size or the timeline to any liquidity event. Push back gently if they offer options with a short exercise window after departure; ask for at least a 7 to 10 year post-termination exercise window if this is a private company grant.

## Geographic discount pushback
If they try to anchor lower because Geneva "should be cheaper" than New York: "Geneva is actually one of the more expensive cities globally, comparable to or above Manhattan on cost of living, and Swiss market rates for Staff-level engineers reflect that. I'm not asking for a NYC premium, I'm asking for a rate that matches Zurich or Geneva comparables for this level." Have a specific number ready (CHF 195,000 anchor) rather than debating the principle in the abstract.

## Using a competing process
Even without a live competing offer in hand, it's fair to say: "I'm currently in later-stage conversations with a couple of other platform and infrastructure roles at a similar level, so I'd want to move toward a decision in the next two to three weeks if the fit here is right." Keep it honest and non-specific rather than inventing details that could unravel under a direct question.

## Bonus structure clarity
Before agreeing to anything, get in writing: what metrics the trading-performance bonus is tied to, how often it pays out, whether it's capped, and what happens to it in a slow trading period versus a strong one. A bonus tied to system uptime or execution quality is very different from one tied purely to trading P&L, and the negotiation should reflect which one it actually is.

## Closing
"I want to make this work, the problem is genuinely interesting and the fact that it's backed by real revenue rather than a pure thesis is a big part of why I'm here. Let's get the base, bonus mechanics, and equity terms aligned this week so we can move to a decision." Set a soft deadline to keep momentum without sounding like an ultimatum.
