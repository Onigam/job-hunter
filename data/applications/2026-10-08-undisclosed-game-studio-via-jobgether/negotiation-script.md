# Negotiation Script: Technical Director, Applied AI (undisclosed game studio, via Jobgether)

## Context that shapes the numbers

- **The employer is hidden, and so is the band.** Jobgether is the intermediary; the partner studio runs interviews and makes the decision. The posting says only "competitive compensation" depending on experience and contract structure. Your first job is to find out who the studio is (or at least its size and HQ country) and what the budget is, before you name any number.
- **Location is contradictory.** The LinkedIn header says Phoenix, AZ, the body describes a role based in Switzerland, and it's also listed as fully remote and international. Confirm whether they'd employ you in Switzerland, hire you as a contractor, or go through an employer of record (EOR). Each one changes what a given number is worth.
- **"Flexible contract options depending on location" usually means contractor.** Price a contract much higher than a salary, because you'd carry AHV/AVS, pension, LAMal, holidays and downtime yourself.
- **"A large creative and game-development environment"** suggests an established studio or publisher rather than an early-stage startup. Don't expect much equity. In games the extra levers tend to be bonuses (often tied to project milestones or company results), profit share, and sometimes royalties.
- **Technical Director in games is a senior leadership title**, usually between Lead and CTO. Negotiate against Staff/Principal and Director bands, not senior engineer ones.
- **Be realistic about the gap.** You have no shipped games and no Unreal/Houdini experience, and they'll know it. Don't let that become a discount on the whole package. Handle it with a ramp-up plan or a six-month review, not a lower number.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

These are rough ranges from public comp data. Check levels.fyi, Glassdoor, swissdevjobs.ch and the recruiter's band before you quote any of them. The Swiss games industry is small, so most comparables are tech roles rather than studio roles.

| Benchmark | Base (approx.) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Technical Director, mid-size European studio (FR/DE/UK/Nordics) | €110k to €160k | plus bonus; Paris and Stockholm at the top |
| Technical Director / AI lead, large US or global studio, remote | USD 180k to 260k | plus bonus or RSUs if public |
| Staff/Principal engineer, Geneva, scale-up or mid-size | CHF 170k to 220k | plus equity or bonus |
| Director-level engineering lead, Switzerland, established company | CHF 200k to 250k | plus 10 to 20% bonus |
| Senior AI/platform contractor, Switzerland | CHF 1,100 to 1,500 per day | no benefits, no pension |

Geneva rent, health insurance you pay yourself and a salary-linked pension are why Swiss numbers sit well above EU ones. Bring that up whenever someone mentions "remote parity".

**Employee (Swiss payroll or EOR):**
- Opening anchor: CHF 235k base plus bonus.
- Target: CHF 210k base plus 10 to 15% bonus.
- Walk-away floor: CHF 180k base. Below that with no bonus, it pays less than a Geneva Staff IC role and carries more scope.

**Contractor:**
- Opening anchor: CHF 1,400 per day.
- Target: CHF 1,250 per day, or about CHF 22k to 25k a month on a monthly retainer.
- Walk-away floor: CHF 1,050 per day. Ask for a minimum 12-month term and 30 days' notice on both sides.

If they only quote in EUR or USD, convert at the day's rate (CHF/EUR has been around 0.93 to 0.95; check it) and say the CHF figure out loud, so it becomes the reference point.

## Step 1: find out who and how (first Jobgether or studio call)

"Before we get into numbers, a couple of practical questions. Could you tell me which studio this is, or at least its size and where it's based? The posting mentions Phoenix, Switzerland and fully remote, so I'd like to understand how you'd actually employ me in Geneva: payroll here, an EOR, or a contract. And what's the budget range for the role? I'd rather we work from the same numbers from the start."

If they push you to name a number first:

"For a Technical Director role leading applied AI across a large studio, and assuming I'm employed in Switzerland, I'm looking at around CHF 235k base plus a bonus. That's based on 17 years of engineering, a platform I built from zero through an acquisition, and the last few years spent getting AI tooling into how engineers actually work. If you're structuring it as a contract, my rate is CHF 1,400 a day. If your range is in that area, I'm very interested."

## Step 2: if they come in low

"Thanks for being straight about it. CHF [their number] reads more like a senior engineer band than a Technical Director one, and for someone in Geneva it's below market. The scope here is research, production tooling and the AI standard for the whole studio, across engineers and artists. Could we get to CHF 210k base? If base is fixed, I'd want to close the gap with a higher bonus target or a signing bonus."

If they bring up the games gap:

"That's fair, and I raised it myself. Rather than a lower starting number, I'd suggest a six-month review with concrete goals: say, two AI tools in production use by artists and an adoption standard the team has signed off on. If I hit those, the base moves to [target]. That way you're not paying up front for something you haven't seen yet."

If it's a contract and the rate is low:

"I understand the budget. Keep in mind that as a contractor I'm covering my own pension, social charges, health insurance and holidays, which in Switzerland is roughly 20 to 30% on top of a salary. At CHF [their rate] a day, that works out below a Geneva salary for the same work. Could we meet at CHF 1,250, or agree a lower rate with a guaranteed 12-month term?"

## Step 3: pushing back on a geographic discount

If they offer an "EU remote" or lower-cost-country number:

"I can see why the band was set that way for a distributed team. But I'd be living, taxed and insured in Geneva, which is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and the posting itself describes the role as based in Switzerland. The fair comparison is a Swiss band for this level."

If the budget really is set for one market:

"Understood. Then let's look at what else can move: bonus, signing bonus, an extra week of holiday, or a learning budget for the Unreal and Houdini side. I'd rather find a structure that works than walk away over the base alone."

## Step 4: equity, bonus and profit share

An established studio probably won't offer startup equity. Ask anyway, and get every answer in writing.

- **Bonus.** What's the target, what's it tied to (project milestones, company results, personal goals), and what actually paid out in the last two years? A studio bonus tied to a game's launch date can quietly slip to zero.
- **Profit share or royalties.** Some studios share revenue from shipped titles. Ask whether it applies to central tech and AI roles or only to game teams.
- **If equity is offered (private studio):** ask the type (real shares, options or virtual/phantom), the strike price, the number of fully diluted shares outstanding so you can work out your percentage, and the last valuation. Standard vesting is four years with a one-year cliff; ask for monthly vesting after the cliff and acceleration on change of control (double trigger at minimum). Ask about liquidation preferences on any preferred shares, because they decide whether common equity is worth anything in an exit. Anti-dilution protection is rare for employees, but you can ask for refresh grants after future rounds.
- **If the parent is public:** RSUs, so ask about grant size, the price used to convert a currency amount into units, the vesting schedule and refreshers.
- **IP and side projects.** AI tooling work often overlaps with open source and personal projects (OpenClaw, Givernance). Ask for a clear carve-out in the IP clause for prior and personal work.

## Step 5: competing offer template (use only if real)

Use it only if you actually have another process at a similar stage. Never invent an offer.

"I want to be transparent with you. I'm also in late-stage conversations for a [Principal/Staff AI role] at a [public tech company / funded startup], at around CHF [their number] base plus [RSUs/equity]. This role is my first choice because of the scope, owning applied AI across a whole studio is rare, but I need the package to be in the same range. Is there room to get closer to CHF [target]?"

If you don't have a competing offer, use the market instead:

"For context, Staff and Principal roles I'm talking to in Switzerland are sitting at CHF 200k to 230k base. I'm not asking you to beat that, just to be in the same range for a Director title."

## Before you sign

- Check the employing entity, the country of the contract and which law governs it.
- If it's an EOR, find out who pays the EOR fee. It should be them.
- Get remote-work terms in writing, including how many retreats or trips a year and who pays.
- Read the notice period, the non-compete (Swiss non-competes need to be limited in scope and time) and the IP clause.
- Check that the title in the contract says "Technical Director", since it matters for your next negotiation.
