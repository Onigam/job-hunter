# Negotiation Script: AI Architect / AI Engineering Lead (via Jobgether)

## Context that shapes the numbers

This is a fully remote **contract**, Switzerland-based, for an undisclosed client that's most likely in insurance or health claims processing. That changes the whole negotiation compared with a permanent role:

- **You're negotiating a day rate, not a salary.** There's no equity to speak of, no paid holidays, no employer pension contribution unless it runs through payroll. The rate has to cover all of that.
- **Find out the contracting structure first.** Ask whether you'd be on the payroll of an agency or employer of record (portage / Personalverleih), or invoicing as self-employed. Swiss AVS/AHV self-employed status is hard to get with a single client, so an agency payroll is the likely route. If so, ask for the **gross rate billed to the client** and the agency margin, and negotiate the rate paid to you, not just the headline.
- **Contract roles are usually budgeted.** The client has a ceiling and the intermediary takes a cut (often 15 to 25%). Your room to move is mostly inside that cut and inside the client's ceiling, which is why naming a number first, and high, matters.
- **Scope is senior.** "Lead architecture of production multi-agent systems" plus "technical leadership across enterprise AI" is architect-level work in a regulated domain. Price it as a lead, not as a senior developer.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough figures from Swiss contractor rate surveys and recruiter data. Re-check on freelancermap.ch, Hays and Michael Page contractor guides, swissdevjobs.ch and by asking the recruiter directly for the budget.

| Benchmark | Rate (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Senior software engineer, Swiss contract | CHF 110 to 140/h |
| Solution / software architect, Swiss contract | CHF 130 to 165/h |
| AI / GenAI architect or lead, Swiss contract | CHF 145 to 190/h |
| Same role, if they apply a "remote EU" rate | €90 to 130/h (push back, see below) |
| Permanent Staff/Principal AI engineer, Geneva or Zurich (comparison) | CHF 190k to 250k base + bonus/equity |

Day rates are typically 8 to 8.4 hours: CHF 145/h ≈ CHF 1,160/day, CHF 175/h ≈ CHF 1,400/day.

**Target:** CHF 170/h (≈ CHF 1,360/day) paid to you.
**Opening anchor:** CHF 185/h (≈ CHF 1,480/day).
**Walk-away floor:** CHF 140/h (≈ CHF 1,120/day). Below that, you're earning less than a permanent Geneva Staff role once you count holidays, pension and gaps between contracts.

Sanity check on the floor: CHF 140/h × 8h × ~210 billable days ≈ CHF 235k gross. Take off the employer and employee social charges you now carry (roughly 12 to 15% if self-employed, or the agency's deduction), no paid holidays, a self-funded second pillar and the risk of bench time, and it lands close to a CHF 190k to 200k permanent package. That's the bottom of what your profile earns in Geneva.

Why anchoring high is defensible: you've actually rolled AI-assisted engineering out across two teams at a scale-up (about 70% adoption in three months, no mandate), you've architected a platform from zero through an acquisition, and you have 17+ years across Java-based and TypeScript stacks. Contract clients pay for people who can be productive in week one; say that out loud.

## Opening the rate conversation

Raise it with the recruiter before the first client interview:

"Before we go further, could you tell me the budget for this contract, and how it's structured? I'd like to know whether it's through your payroll or invoiced, and what the client is billed, so we're working from the same numbers."

If they push you to name a number first:

"For lead-level AI architecture work in Switzerland, I'm at CHF 185 an hour, paid to me. That reflects hands-on production experience with agentic tooling across an engineering org, and a domain where getting the guardrails wrong is expensive. If the budget is in that area, I'm very interested."

## If they come in low

"Thanks, I appreciate you being straight with me. CHF [their number] is closer to a senior developer contract than an architect lead. The scope here is designing multi-agent systems for the whole SDLC and setting practice across teams, in a regulated domain. I could get to CHF 170 if we agree on [one of: a 6-month minimum term / a 40-day notice period / a rate review after 3 months]. Is there room to get there?"

If they say the client's ceiling is fixed:

"Understood. Then can we look at the agency margin? I'd rather the client pay the same and a bit more reach me than walk away from a role I think fits well. Alternatively, we could reduce the scope: four days a week at the same day rate."

(The four-day option is a real lever if you want time for Givernance or other work.)

## Pushing back on a "remote" or EU discount

"I'm Geneva-based and paying Geneva costs, and the posting itself is listed for Switzerland. The comparable contracts I'm looking at are Swiss-paid. If the client is fine with Swiss remote, I'd expect a Swiss rate."

If they cite EU rates:

"I understand some of your bench is in lower-cost countries. The client is hiring for Swiss timezone and Swiss context, and a regulated-domain architect who can be in a room in Zurich or Basel at short notice is worth the difference."

## Contract terms beyond the rate

Equity isn't on the table for a contract, so put the effort into terms:

- **Term and renewal.** Ask for an initial 6 months with renewal, not 3. A short first term plus a long notice for you is a red flag.
- **Notice symmetry.** Same notice period for both sides.
- **Rate review.** A written rate review at the first renewal, ideally +5 to 10% if extended.
- **Payment terms.** 30 days maximum; ask if the agency pays you when the client pays them ("pay when paid") and refuse that if possible.
- **IP clause.** Make sure it's limited to work done for this client. Carve out pre-existing work and side projects (Givernance, open-source repos on your GitHub) explicitly.
- **Tooling costs.** Who pays for Claude, Copilot and model API usage during development? It should be the client.
- **Conversion clause.** If there's a chance they'd convert you to a permanent hire, ask what the agency's conversion fee is and whether it drops after 6 to 12 months. A low conversion fee makes a later permanent offer far more likely.
- **Non-compete.** Push back on anything that stops you working for other insurers or health-tech firms after the contract ends.

## If it becomes a permanent offer

If the client wants to convert, switch frames to the permanent market: CHF 210k to 230k base anchor for a Staff/Principal AI Architect in Switzerland, plus bonus. If the client is a startup or scale-up, ask about equity specifically: grant size as a percentage of fully diluted shares, 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff at most, acceleration on change of control (double trigger is a reasonable ask, you've been through an acquisition and know why it matters), and the latest preferred share price so you can value it.

## Competing offer template

Use only if true, or framed as a pipeline rather than an offer:

"I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations for a couple of other roles, one a Swiss-based Staff position. This contract is the one I'm most interested in because of the agentic SDLC work. If we can get to CHF 170 an hour, I'm ready to commit and stop the other processes."

## Before you hang up

Get the agreed rate, term, notice, start date and contracting structure in writing (an email from the recruiter is fine) before you stop other conversations.
