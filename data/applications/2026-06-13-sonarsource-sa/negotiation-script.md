# Negotiation Script — SonarSource SA, Staff Full Stack Engineer (Java, React)

---

## Market Context (Geneva, Staff Engineer, 2026)

Staff Software Engineer at a scale-up with 75%+ Fortune 100 penetration, Geneva-based, Java/TypeScript, AI focus:

- **Market range:** CHF 180,000–230,000 base (Staff/Senior Staff IC in Geneva tech, 2025–2026)
- **SonarSource positioning:** Series-level scale-up with strong ARR and international footprint. Not a cash-constrained startup, not a FAANG payer. Expect mid-to-upper market range, not top-of-band.
- **Benefits offset:** Their pension contribution is meaningful (60% of total cost, roughly 13.5–15.5% of base saved on your behalf). Factor this into total comp math.
- **Company Growth Bonus:** Discretionary, so treat it as a bonus, not guaranteed comp. Don't anchor on it.

**Target opening anchor:** CHF 215,000 base
**Acceptable floor:** CHF 190,000 base (if offset by equity or strong bonus structure)

---

## Opening Anchor Script

When they make an offer or ask for your range:

> "Based on the market for Staff-level IC roles in Geneva with this scope, I'm targeting around 215,000 CHF base. That's in line with what I've seen for Staff engineers at scale-ups with similar product complexity. I'm flexible on structure if the total comp picture makes sense."

If they push back immediately:

> "I'm happy to work through the numbers together. Can you walk me through the full comp package, including the pension contribution and any equity component? That helps me evaluate the total picture rather than just the base."

---

## If They Come In Low (below CHF 185,000)

> "I appreciate the offer. The base is a bit below where I'd need it to be for this to make sense given the scope and my background. I'm at [X] currently and a move would need to reflect the level of the role. Is there flexibility to get closer to 205,000? I'm not trying to be difficult; I just want to make sure we're structuring this in a way that works long-term."

If they hold firm on base:

> "If the base ceiling is fixed, are there other levers? A signing bonus for year one, an accelerated first review, or additional equity would all help bridge the gap. What does the equity program look like for Staff-level hires?"

---

## Equity (if applicable)

SonarSource is not a public company as of 2026. If they offer equity (RSUs, options, or profit-sharing):

- Ask for the strike price and last-known 409A valuation (if options)
- Ask about the vesting schedule: standard 4-year with 1-year cliff is baseline
- Ask about anti-dilution protection and what happens at acquisition (given Alaya/Benevity history, you can speak credibly about acquisition outcomes)

> "I've been through an acquisition on the founding side, so I know how equity terms matter when a liquidity event happens. Can you share the current cap table structure and what Staff hires typically receive in terms of grant size relative to the last funding round?"

If they deflect:

> "Even a ballpark is helpful. I'm not expecting a detailed breakdown, just enough to factor it into the total comp picture."

---

## Geneva Cost-of-Living Pushback

If they cite EU market comps or try to anchor below Geneva rates:

> "I want to flag that Geneva is a different cost structure than Paris or Berlin. Rent, health insurance, and tax on a gross salary here work differently. I'd rather anchor to Swiss market data for this conversation, which is why my range reflects CHF, not EUR. That's not me being inflexible; it's just that the comparison doesn't translate directly."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in hand:

> "I should mention I'm at the offer stage with another company as well. The SonarSource role is genuinely more interesting to me given the AI/IDE focus and the Geneva location, but I want to make sure I'm making a fair comparison. Can we get to a final number this week so I can give you a clear answer?"

If you don't have a competing offer (hypothetical leverage only, use carefully):

> "I'm in conversations with a couple of other companies at the Staff level, and I want to be straightforward with you: I'd prefer to join SonarSource, but I need the offer to be competitive enough that I'm not leaving money on the table. What's the ceiling on the base for this role?"

---

## Timing

- Don't accept on the first call. Always say: "I'd like a day to look at the full package in writing before I give you a formal answer."
- Written offers matter. Get it in writing before making any decisions.
- First review timing: ask what the standard first performance review cycle looks like. If it's annual, try to negotiate a 6-month checkpoint.

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

- Vague bonus language ("we've historically paid bonuses") with no floor or structure
- Equity described as "meaningful" but no numbers shared
- Pressure to decide within 24 hours on first offer
- "We're a flat org" used to justify below-market comp
