# Negotiation Script, Head of Engineering, Platform @ Keyrock

## Market anchors

Keyrock's own posting for this role in Brussels lists roughly $122k-$150k, which is a London/Brussels fintech band, not a Switzerland one. That gap is the whole negotiation if the conversation happens on Keyrock's default scale.

Switzerland reference points for a hands-on Head of Engineering / Staff+ leadership profile, base only, cash comp:
- Geneva/Zurich fintech or crypto scale-up, VP/Head of Eng level: roughly CHF 190k-240k base
- Geneva/Zurich Senior Staff or Principal IC-leaning leadership: roughly CHF 170k-210k base
- Total comp (base + bonus + equity value) at this level in a well-funded scale-up: often 1.3-1.6x base

Keyrock has raised meaningfully and has a Deutsche Bank partnership, so this isn't an early seed-stage comp conversation; there's room to anchor closer to the higher end of a European fintech band even if the base office isn't Switzerland.

If the role is remote or hybrid from Geneva rather than relocation to Brussels, the honest anchor is Swiss market rate adjusted down slightly for the fact that Brussels cash comp typically runs 15-25% below Zurich/Geneva for equivalent seniority, not all the way down to a straight Brussels number.

## Opening anchor

Lead with a number, not a range, and justify it in one sentence:

"Based on what I'm seeing for hands-on engineering leadership roles at funded fintech and crypto infrastructure companies in Switzerland and comparable European hubs, I'm targeting CHF 210k-230k base, plus meaningful equity given the stage. I'd want to understand your full comp structure, cash and equity, before we talk about where in that range makes sense."

Let them respond first. Don't self-discount before hearing their number.

## If they come in low

Script for a lowball or a Brussels-scale offer:

"I appreciate the offer. The number is below what I'd expect for this scope, owning a trading data platform end to end with team leadership attached, and it looks like it's benchmarked against a Brussels cash band rather than a Zurich/Geneva one. Given the relocation or the remote-from-Switzerland setup we discussed, is there flexibility to move the base closer to CHF [X], or to make up the gap through equity and a signing component?"

Don't justify the ask with personal expenses (rent, cost of living as a sob story). Justify it with market data and scope of the role.

## Equity

Keyrock is a scale-up with real revenue and a known market position, not a two-person seed startup, so equity questions should focus on structure, not survival odds:
- Ask for the current valuation basis and the last round size, even informally
- Ask about vesting schedule (standard 4-year with 1-year cliff is the floor, push back on anything longer)
- Ask whether there's an early-exercise option and how long post-departure exercise windows are, this matters more than the headline percentage
- If equity is offered as a substitute for a competitive base rather than in addition to one, treat that as a flag worth asking about directly rather than accepting silently

## Geographic discount pushback

If they anchor to Brussels or London pay bands because that's the primary office:

"I understand the default band is set around the Brussels office. If the arrangement is remote-from-Geneva or a relocation, I'd want the comp conversation to reflect Swiss market rates rather than the local Brussels band, since that's the market I'd actually be hired out of and compared against."

Keep it matter-of-fact. This is a scoping question, not a complaint.

## Competing offer template

Use only if there's a real second conversation in motion; don't bluff a specific number that can be checked.

"I'm currently in later-stage conversations with another platform engineering role at a similar comp level. I'd rather move forward with Keyrock if we can get the offer aligned, since the problem space here, the multi-venue liquidity layer and the Rust-based trading infra, is the more interesting one to me. Where's the flexibility on your end?"

## Closing move

Once a number is close: "That works for me. Can we get the offer in writing this week so I can wrap up the other conversation cleanly?" Creates gentle urgency without threatening anything.
