# Negotiation Script: 5U AI, Founding AI Engineer (Munich)

## Context that shapes everything

5U AI is pre-seed. They raised $3.2M (about €2.7M) in July 2026, led by Emerge Capital, with angels from DHL, GEODIS, DSV, Maersk and Ceva. Founded 2025 by Yagiz Abik (CEO) and Fehmi Şener (CTO). The posting gives no salary range, and it's Munich onsite, not remote.

That means two things. Cash is tight: a €2.7M round probably funds 18 to 24 months for a small team, so base salary has a hard ceiling. Equity is the real lever, and as a founding engineer with 17 years and an exit behind you, you're in a position to ask for meaningful ownership.

The other thing to keep in mind is that the job is in Germany, but your reference point is Geneva. A Munich offer will look low next to Swiss numbers. That's not something to hide; it's your main argument.

## Market reference points (estimates, verify before the call)

These are working estimates from public salary data and typical early-stage packages. Check them against current data (Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Index Ventures' OptionPlan, German startup comp reports) before any number leaves your mouth.

| Reference | Base | Equity |
|---|---|---|
| Geneva, Senior Staff / Principal at a scale-up | CHF 190k to 240k | RSUs or options, varies |
| Zurich, Staff+ at big tech | CHF 230k to 300k+ total comp | RSUs |
| Munich, senior/staff engineer at funded scale-up | €95k to 130k | small options pool |
| Munich, founding engineer at pre-seed (employees 1 to 3) | €85k to 120k | ~0.5% to 2%, sometimes more |
| Munich, founding engineer with lead/architect scope | €110k to 140k | ~1% to 3% |

The 5U role owns the whole platform architecture and helps hire the team. That's the top two rows of the Munich table, not the middle.

## Your numbers

**Walk-away floor:** decide this before the first call and write it down. Given a move from Geneva, something like €105k base plus at least 1% is a reasonable place to start thinking, but it's your call, not a formula.

**Target:** €125k to 135k base, 1.5% to 2.5% equity, 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff.

**Opening anchor:** €140k base and 2.5%, or a lower base with more equity if they push back on cash.

## Scripts

### When they ask for your expectations first

"I'd rather hear how you're thinking about the package first, since you know your runway better than I do. For context, I'm coming from a Senior Staff role in Geneva, so my current cash is well above what a pre-seed company can match. I'm not expecting you to match it. I'm expecting the equity to make up the gap."

If they insist on a number:

"For a founding role that owns the platform end to end and helps build the team, I'm thinking around €140k base and 2.5%. I'm flexible on the split between the two."

### If the base comes in low (say €95k to 105k)

"Thanks, I appreciate you being straight about the runway. The base is below what I'd need to relocate from Geneva comfortably, and I'm taking a real cash cut already. Could we get to €125k? If the base really can't move, I'd want the equity to reflect that, closer to 2.5% than 1%."

### If the equity comes in low (say 0.25% to 0.5%)

"That's where I'd expect a strong senior hire later on, maybe post-seed. For the first engineer next to Fehmi, who's owning architecture, reliability and the first hires, I'd expect something between 1.5% and 2.5%. Can you walk me through how you sized it and what the pool looks like?"

### If they say "this is what everyone else at this stage gets"

"Understood. I'd just point out that I've already done this once, from a blank page to an acquisition by a unicorn. That lowers your risk on the platform side, and that's what I'm asking you to price in."

### Competing offer template (only use if it's real)

"I want to be transparent: I'm in late stages with another company, and their offer is [base] plus [equity/RSUs]. 5U is my first choice because of the problem and the team, and I'd like to make it work. Is there room to close the gap on [base or equity]?"

Don't invent a competing offer. Founders at this stage talk to each other and to the same investors.

## Equity: questions to ask before signing

German startups usually use VSOP (virtual stock options) rather than real shares, because real equity for employees is expensive and slow under German corporate law. Know which one you're getting.

**Instrument.** Is it VSOP, real options, or real shares? A VSOP is a cash payout promise tied to an exit, not ownership. It usually pays out only on an exit event and carries no voting rights.

**Percentage, fully diluted.** Ask for the fully diluted share count and the current pool size. A percentage without a denominator means nothing.

**Vesting and cliff.** Standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.

**Leaver terms.** This matters most with VSOPs. Ask for the good leaver and bad leaver definitions in writing. Watch for clauses where vested virtual shares are forfeited if you leave before an exit, even as a good leaver. Push for vested shares to stay vested.

**Acceleration.** Ask for double-trigger acceleration: if the company is acquired and your role is terminated or materially changed, unvested equity vests. You've lived through an acquisition, so you can explain why this matters.

**Strike price and exercise.** For real options, what's the strike and how long is the post-departure exercise window? Push for longer than 90 days.

**Dilution.** Anti-dilution protection for employees is rare and hard to get at pre-seed. Don't spend capital on it. Instead ask how much dilution they expect in the seed round and whether there's a refresh policy for early employees.

**Tax.** VSOP payouts in Germany are taxed as employment income at exit, not capital gains. If you'd stay Swiss tax resident for any period, get proper advice before signing.

## Relocation and the Geneva question

Munich is cheaper than Geneva, so the usual "Geneva is expensive" argument works against you here. Flip it: the point isn't cost of living, it's what you're giving up.

"Munich costs less than Geneva, I know. What I'm weighing is a Senior Staff salary in Swiss francs against a pre-seed package, plus a move. That's why equity and a relocation budget matter to me more than squeezing the base."

Things to ask for, all cheap for them compared with base:

- a relocation budget, something like €5k to €10k, or covered temporary housing for the first month or two
- a start date that gives you time to handle the move properly
- any flexibility on onsite days in the first few months while you relocate (they've been clear it's an office role, so ask for a transition, not permanent remote)
- a salary review tied to the seed round, written into the offer

## Things not to do

- Don't argue about the base for more than one round. Pre-seed cash is genuinely limited, and haggling signals you're there for the salary.
- Don't accept an equity number before seeing the fully diluted share count and the VSOP document.
- Don't bring up Swiss salary figures as a demand. Use them as context for why the equity has to be real.
- Don't negotiate with Fehmi and Yagiz separately. Settle the package with whoever makes the offer, usually the CEO.
