# Negotiation Script: Kuro, Head of Engineering

## Context that shapes the numbers

Kuro Technology was founded in Berlin in November 2024 by Lea Scherer (CEO) and Jonas Minkler (CPO), who both held leadership roles at Capmo and alcemy before. They've raised €10M in a round led by UVC Partners, with Foundamental, Realyze Ventures and helloworld also in. They have 16 people, five of them engineers, and 12 customers, six of which are among the 20 largest general contractors in the German-speaking region (Goldbeck, Hochtief Infrastructure, Köster, Implenia, Geiger, Riedel).

What that means for the negotiation:

- **This is a Berlin, on-site role, and the employer is German.** Kuro will think in EUR and Berlin startup bands. Swiss numbers below are for your own reference, so you know what you'd be giving up, and as a pushback point. Don't expect them to match Geneva pay.
- **A €10M round at 16 people means cash is real but limited.** They can pay a proper Head of Engineering salary. They can't pay Zurich big-tech money. Equity is where the upside is, and it's also where there's the most room to negotiate.
- **German startups usually grant VSOPs (virtual shares), not real equity.** Since the 2024 Zukunftsfinanzierungsgesetz, real ESOPs have become more attractive under §19a EStG, and some startups have switched. Ask which they use.
- **Repeat founders know the playbook.** They'll have a standard VSOP agreement and a sense of what a Head of Engineering gets. Expect a reasonable first offer and limited willingness to move far from their template, except on grant size.
- **Location is the biggest variable.** Full relocation to Berlin, a hybrid arrangement from Geneva, or a commute pattern each change the maths. Settle this before money.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

These are rough figures from public surveys and recruiter data. Re-check on levels.fyi, Glassdoor, the Michael Page and Hays salary guides (Germany and Switzerland), swissdevjobs.ch, and Index Ventures' OptionPlan for equity norms.

**Berlin (what Kuro will benchmark against)**

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Head of Engineering, Berlin, seed or early Series A, 5 to 15 engineers | €120k to 150k |
| Head of Engineering / VP Eng, Berlin, well-funded Series A to B | €140k to 175k |
| Staff / Principal Engineer, Berlin scale-up | €110k to 140k |
| Equity, first engineering leader hired after founding, seed to Series A | ~0.5% to 1.5% (VSOP) |

**Switzerland (your reference point)**

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Head of Engineering, Geneva startup or scale-up | CHF 190k to 240k |
| Head of Engineering / VP Eng, Zurich scale-up | CHF 210k to 260k |
| Staff Engineer, Geneva | CHF 180k to 230k |

A CHF 220k Geneva salary is roughly €235k at current rates, but Berlin's cost of living and rent are far lower and German social contributions and taxes work differently, so a straight conversion isn't a fair comparison. What you're really comparing is take-home after rent, and the upside from equity.

**Target (full relocation to Berlin):** €155k base plus ~1.0% to 1.25% in VSOP or ESOP, with relocation support.
**Opening anchor:** €170k base plus 1.5%.
**Walk-away floor:** €140k base, and only with at least ~0.8% equity on good terms (see below).

**If they agree to a Geneva-based hybrid:** cost of living is anchored in Geneva, so push the base toward €165k to 175k, or ask that travel and Berlin accommodation be covered separately. Expect them to resist both.

The reasoning for anchoring high: you've done this exact job before, co-founding the tech side of a B2B SaaS that went to acquisition by a unicorn. Then you spent five years at Senior Staff level inside a large org, which is the stage Kuro is heading into. Founder-level experience combined with scale experience is hard to find in Berlin.

## Location before money

Raise this in the first call with the founders, not with a recruiter at offer stage:

"Before we go further, I'd like to be straight about location. I'm in Geneva today. I understand why you want the team together in Mitte at this stage, and I'm not trying to turn this into a remote role. What I'd like to understand is whether you'd consider [relocation with support / three weeks a month in Berlin for the first year]. It changes how I'd think about the package, so it's better to settle it now."

If they say it's full on-site or nothing, and you're willing to relocate, ask for relocation support in the offer (see below).

## Opening anchor script

"Looking at what heads of engineering at well-funded Series A companies in Berlin are paid, and given I've built and run the engineering side of a B2B SaaS from zero through acquisition, I'm looking at around €170k base and about 1.5% equity. I know that's at the top of the Berlin range. It's below what I'd get in Geneva, and the equity is the part that makes the move worth it for me. How does that compare with what you had in mind?"

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low

**Script A, base below range (for example €125k to 135k):**
"Thanks, I appreciate you being direct. I understand you're protecting runway, and I'd do the same in your seat. That number reads more like a senior IC salary than someone owning the roadmap, infrastructure and hiring. I'd need the base closer to €155k. If cash is the constraint, I'm open to closing part of the gap with more equity, as long as the terms are solid."

**Script B, they apply a 'we're a startup' discount:**
"That makes sense, and it's why I'm focused on equity. But a lower base and a standard grant together don't reflect the trade-off. If the base stays where it is, the grant needs to be at the top of what you'd give this role, not the middle."

**Script C, the band is fixed:**
"Understood. Then let's look at what's flexible: grant size, a compensation review after six months tied to agreed outcomes such as the AWS migration and the first hires, a relocation package, and a sign-on bonus. Any combination of those would work for me."

**Script D, relocation pushback:**
"Moving from Geneva means giving up a Swiss salary level and moving my life to Berlin. A relocation package covering the move, a few months of temporary housing and help with registration and paperwork isn't unusual for a hire at this level. Is that something you've done before?"

## Equity: what to ask and what to push on

Get answers in writing to each of these:

1. **Instrument.** VSOP (virtual shares paying out cash on exit) or a real ESOP under §19a EStG? A VSOP is taxed as income when it pays out and only pays on an exit or liquidity event. Real ESOP shares can get more favourable treatment under the 2024 rules. Ask which, and why.
2. **Percentage, not just units.** Ask for the grant as a percentage of fully diluted share capital, plus the total number of shares and the size of the pool. A grant of "X virtual shares" means nothing without the denominator.
3. **Strike or base price.** For a VSOP, the base price is usually the last round's price per share. Ask what it is, because your payout is the exit price minus that base.
4. **Vesting and cliff.** The standard is four years with a one-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff. As a leadership hire you can ask for a shorter cliff (six months) or a small amount vesting immediately.
5. **Leaver clauses.** This is the most important part of German VSOPs. Read the good leaver / bad leaver definitions closely. Push for: vested shares kept as a good leaver if you're let go without cause or leave after the cliff, bad leaver limited to real cause (such as fraud or serious breach), and no clause that cuts your vested shares just because you resign.
6. **Change of control acceleration.** Ask for double-trigger acceleration: if Kuro is acquired and your role is eliminated or materially reduced within 12 months, unvested shares vest. You've been through an acquisition, so you can explain why this matters from experience.
7. **Dilution.** Anti-dilution protection is very rare for employees, so don't spend goodwill asking for it. Instead ask whether refresh grants are planned after the next round, and get a commitment to review your grant after Series A or B.
8. **Exit payout mechanics.** In a VSOP, check how the payout works in an exit and whether there's any cap.

**Cross-border tax.** If you keep a Geneva residence while working in Berlin, or move partway through vesting, both Swiss and German tax rules can apply to the equity. Get a short opinion from an adviser who handles CH–DE cases before signing.

Script if the grant looks small:
"The equity is the main reason this makes sense for me, coming from a Swiss salary level. For someone owning engineering end to end at 16 people, I'd expect something closer to 1.5%. If that's above what you can do, I'd want the leaver terms and change of control acceleration to be strong, and a commitment to review the grant after the next round."

## Competing offer template (use only if real)

"I want to be transparent. I'm also in late-stage conversations for a [Staff / Head of Engineering] role based in Switzerland, and the base there is around CHF [X]. Kuro is my first choice because of the problem and the stage, and I'd rather make this work. Is there room to close the gap, either on base or equity?"

Don't invent a competing offer. If there isn't one, use the Swiss market anchors instead: "the roles I'm otherwise looking at in Switzerland pay CHF 200k-plus."

## Other things worth negotiating

- **Relocation package**: moving costs, two or three months of temporary housing in Berlin, and help with Anmeldung and the tax ID.
- **Sign-on bonus** if you're leaving anything behind.
- **Title and scope in writing**: Head of Engineering reporting to the CEO, ownership of the engineering roadmap and hiring budget, and an agreed path to CTO or VP Eng as the team grows.
- **Hiring budget and headcount plan**: not compensation, but it determines whether the role is doable. Ask how many engineers they plan to hire over the next 12 months.
- **Learning and conference budget**, and time for AI and construction-tech events.
- **Vacation**: Berlin startups usually offer 28 to 30 days. Ask for 30.
- **Review timing**: a compensation review after six months, not twelve.
