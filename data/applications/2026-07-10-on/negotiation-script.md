# Negotiation Script, Staff Engineer AI Security @ On (Zurich)

Context: On (NYSE: ONON) is a public company, not a pre-IPO startup, so equity here means RSUs on a real stock with daily liquidity, not options with a strike price to worry about. That changes the negotiation shape: no cliff-and-vesting-schedule anxiety, no anti-dilution clause to chase, just base, bonus, RSU grant size, and sign-on. Focus energy there.

## Market anchors (Zurich, Staff+ security / platform, 2026)

- Base salary for a Staff Engineer in Zurich tech generally lands CHF 155,000 to CHF 195,000, with security specialists and AI-adjacent roles trending toward the upper half of that band given how thin the talent pool is for people who've actually shipped both platform work and security judgment.
- Total cash (base plus target bonus) for Staff-level security ICs at well-funded Swiss or Swiss-listed companies often reaches CHF 190,000 to CHF 230,000.
- Zurich cost of living runs close to Geneva, so there's no geographic discount to concede on, unlike roles based in cheaper EU hubs. If a recruiter opens with a number benchmarked against Berlin or Lisbon, that's worth naming directly.
- RSU grants at public companies of On's size for Staff-level hires typically vest over four years, often with a one-year cliff on the first tranche and quarterly or annual vesting after. Ask for the grant value in CHF and the vesting schedule in writing before comparing it to any other offer.

## Opening anchor

Aim for the top quartile of the range above, roughly CHF 190,000 base plus a meaningful RSU grant, and justify it with the actual scope: this role owns security strategy across every AI agent, chatbot, and LLM workflow the company runs in production, which is a broader mandate than a typical appsec or cloud security Staff role. Something like: "Given this role covers governance and production security for every agentic system across the company, not a single product line, I'd expect base in the CHF 185,000 to CHF 200,000 range, with an RSU grant that reflects a Staff-level, cross-org mandate."

## If they come in low

Don't reject a number on the spot. Ask what it's benchmarked against first: "Is that figure based on general Staff Engineer bands, or does it account for the AI security specialization specifically? I'd want to understand where the comp committee placed this role before responding." That question alone often moves the next offer, since it forces them to justify the anchor rather than just defend it.

If the number still lands low after that, counter with scope, not desperation: "I hear the number. Given the breadth of what's in scope here, threat modeling, governance, and operational security across every AI workflow the company ships, I'd want to close the gap on base or make it up meaningfully on the RSU side. What flexibility exists there?"

## Equity notes (public company specifics)

- Ask for the RSU grant's dollar or CHF value at time of offer, not just a share count; share count without a strike or grant-date price tells you nothing.
- Confirm the vesting schedule (typically 25% after year one, then quarterly) and whether there's an annual refresh grant program, since that's where a lot of the real multi-year comp lives at public companies.
- Ask whether refresh grants are formulaic (tied to performance review bands) or discretionary; formulaic is easier to plan around.
- No anti-dilution language needed since these are RSUs on a publicly traded, liquid stock, but do ask about blackout periods around earnings if selling shortly after vesting matters.

## Geographic pushback

Not really needed here since Zurich is a genuine high-cost hub with no discount narrative available to them. If anything, use Geneva-to-Zurich the other direction: "I'm not asking for a relocation package or remote flexibility that costs you anything, so there's no offsetting discount to apply on my end."

## Competing offer template (use if real)

"I want to be upfront that I'm further along in conversations with another company for a comparable Staff-level security role, and the numbers there are in the CHF X range. On is my preferred option because of the scope of the AI security mandate here, but I do need the offer to be competitive with what's already on the table."

Only use this if there's an actual competing process; a bluffed number is easy to expose if they ask for details, and it burns trust with a company you might actually want to join.
