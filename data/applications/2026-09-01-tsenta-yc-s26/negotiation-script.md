# Negotiation Script: Tsenta (YC S26), Founding Engineer

## Market Context

**Posted comp:** "Competitive salary" and "meaningful founding-level equity," no numbers in the listing. Two founders (Pulkit Gupta, Agnay Srivastava), YC S26 batch, this would be one of the very first engineering hires. Location isn't stated explicitly in the posting, worth confirming directly whether they expect SF-based or are remote-open.

**YC S-batch founding-engineer bands (2026), US-anchored:** For a post-YC, early-traction company (45,000+ users, meaningful paid conversion, not pre-revenue), a realistic first-hire package runs USD 120,000-160,000 base plus 1%-3% equity, depending heavily on how much of that traction is monetized yet. Tsenta's numbers, tens of thousands of users and real paid conversion with interview outcomes to point to, put them past the riskiest pre-PMF end of that range.

**Geneva context:** Magino's current market rate as Senior Staff Engineer in Geneva sits around CHF 180,000-220,000. A US-anchored founding-engineer base will very likely land under that in raw terms. The frame here isn't "match Geneva," it's that founding-level equity plus real ownership over the matching engine and ATS integration layer is what closes that gap, provided the grant is substantive and the base doesn't drop so low it signals underpricing.

**Why anchor high anyway:** This isn't a two-person team with an idea and no users. Tsenta already has tens of thousands of users and a documented interview-rate outcome for paid users. That's traction a first hire can price against, not just conviction.

---

## Opening Anchor

When asked about expectations:

> "Coming from Senior Staff at Benevity with a founder background at Alaya, I'd be looking at something in the USD 140,000-155,000 base range, plus equity in the 1.5%-2.5% band as the first or second engineering hire. I know early-stage packages vary a lot, so before I get more specific, where does your range actually sit?"

Why this works:
- Anchors near the top of a defensible band without inventing a number they haven't offered
- Ties the equity ask to being an early hire, the strongest lever available pre-Series A
- Gets them to reveal their number first, useful since the posting gave none

---

## If They Come In Low on Base

If the offer lands in the USD 80,000-100,000 range:

> "I get that cash is tight this early, even with the traction you've got. That said, this is a meaningful gap from what I'd need to make this work long-term from Geneva. Could we get to USD 120,000-125,000? If the cash isn't there, I'd want the equity to move to compensate for it."

If they hold firm on base:

> "Understood. If USD 120,000 isn't reachable now, let's talk about equity moving up instead, into the 2.5%-3% range, with a compensation review built in at six or twelve months once we've both seen how the role plays out and the next raise lands."

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Standard terms to confirm:**
- 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff, standard, no pushback needed
- Double-trigger acceleration on acquisition or founder departure, worth raising explicitly this early
- Vesting start date tied to actual start date, not offer date
- Post-money safe or priced round status, ask directly what the current cap table and dilution picture looks like before agreeing to a percentage

**Opening ask:**

> "As one of the first engineers, joining this early in a YC S26 company, I'd want to be in the 2%-3% range. That's roughly where the ownership stake actually reflects the risk of joining before a priced round, rather than being early in name only."

**If they offer 0.5%-1%:**

> "That's on the low side for a first or second engineering hire at this stage, especially with the user traction you already have. I'd want to get to at least 1.5%-2% to feel like the grant matches what I'd be taking on."

---

## Geographic and Remote Considerations

The posting doesn't say whether they expect someone in SF or are open to remote. Worth testing directly rather than assuming either way.

> "Before we get into numbers, I want to understand how firm any in-person expectation is. I'm in Geneva now. I'm open to relocating for the right founding role, but I'd also want to know if a remote arrangement, or a hybrid start while I work out logistics, is realistic given the team is still this small."

If relocation is expected:

> "I'm willing to relocate for this. I'd ask for a one-time relocation contribution, in the USD 8,000-12,000 range, to cover flights, temporary housing, and visa costs. That's fairly standard for an ask like this and not something I'd expect to revisit later."

If timezone-remote is workable:

> "If we can make a Geneva-based remote setup work with a few overlap hours against SF each day, that's my preference. I'd want that confirmed explicitly before I give notice anywhere, since 'remote-friendly' means different things at different early-stage companies."

I'd rather build the engineering foundation at an early-stage company with real traction than join something later-stage, but I do need to make a call in the next couple of weeks. If we can move on terms, that helps me prioritize this."

## Final Close

> "You've already got users who trust this enough to pay for it and land interviews off it, this isn't a bet on an idea, it's a bet on a team that's found something that works and now needs someone to build the reliability layer under it at scale. I want to be that person. Let's get the terms settled so I can start."
