# Negotiation Script — Lead Fullstack Engineer (Undisclosed Startup via Pragmatike)

## Market Anchors

### Switzerland / Geneva context
- Senior Fullstack Engineer (5-8 yrs): CHF 120-145k base
- Lead Fullstack Engineer (tech lead, 8+ yrs): CHF 145-175k base
- Staff / Principal Fullstack (architecture ownership): CHF 175-210k base
- Magino's profile (17 yrs, founding engineer, Staff-level at Benevity) justifies the upper end of Lead or lower end of Staff range.

### Remote / international context (if paid in EUR or USD)
- "Work from Anywhere" roles for Lead-level engineers in Europe typically pay EUR 100-140k or USD 130-170k depending on company location and funding stage.
- Geneva cost of living is among the highest in Europe: push EUR/USD rates to reflect that.
- If the company is US-based and paying in USD: target USD 150-170k base + equity.
- If the company is European and paying in EUR: target EUR 110-135k base + equity.

---

## Opening Anchor

When asked for expectations, lead with range, not a single number:

> "Based on my background, including 17 years of experience, co-founding and exiting a platform that was acquired by a $1B+ company, and my current Senior Staff level at Benevity, I'm targeting CHF 160-175k base for a Lead role in Switzerland, or the equivalent in EUR/USD if the comp is structured internationally. I'm open to discussing how equity factors in, especially at early stage."

**Why this anchor works:**
- Sets a high but defensible floor backed by concrete credentials.
- Signals flexibility on structure (base vs. equity trade-off) without conceding on total comp.
- "Equivalent in EUR/USD" avoids the trap of accepting a lower number because the currency feels different.

---

## If They Come In Below Target

If the first offer is below CHF 145k (or EUR 105k / USD 140k):

> "I appreciate the offer. The role is genuinely interesting and I want to make this work. That said, the number is below what I'm seeing for Lead-level fullstack engineers in this market, particularly with my background. Would there be room to move to [target number], or if base is constrained, could we discuss how equity or a signing bonus could bridge the gap?"

**Do not:**
- Accept immediately to avoid awkwardness.
- Counter with a single number — give them a range so they feel they're choosing, not being dictated to.
- Anchor downward by saying "I'm flexible" before they've made a move.

---

## Equity Negotiation (Early-Stage Startup)

If this is a seed or Series A company, equity matters more than base. Key questions to ask:

1. What is the current cap table structure? How many shares outstanding?
2. What is the most recent 409A valuation (or equivalent for European entities)?
3. What percentage of the fully-diluted cap table does this grant represent?
4. What is the vesting schedule? Standard 4-year with 1-year cliff is baseline.
5. Is there acceleration on change of control (single trigger or double trigger)?
6. Are options or RSUs? What's the strike price relative to current FMV?

**Target for a Lead-level early-stage hire:**
- Seed stage: 0.5-1.5% fully diluted
- Series A: 0.2-0.7% fully diluted
- Series B+: 0.1-0.3% fully diluted

**Script if equity feels low:**
> "The equity percentage feels a bit light given the lead role and the stage of the company. Is there flexibility there, or could we discuss accelerated vesting for the first tranche given what I'd be bringing in immediately?"

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

Some companies apply a "location factor" discount for remote hires. Push back on this directly:

> "I've seen companies apply location adjustments for remote roles, and I want to flag upfront that I'm not expecting a Geneva employer to cover Geneva rent on an Austin salary. My reference point is what a strong Lead Fullstack engineer in a major tech hub would earn, because that's the market I'm competing in for this kind of role. If the budget is built around a location discount, I'd want to understand that before we get further into the process."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or a strong late-stage process), use it:

> "I should mention I'm in late stages with another company. I'd prefer this opportunity, but I need to make a decision in the next [5-7 days]. If we can align on comp, I'd be happy to close this quickly."

**If the offer is hypothetical / you're projecting:** don't fabricate specific numbers. Use the framing:

> "I'm in active processes elsewhere and have reason to believe I'll have offers in the CHF 160k+ range shortly. I want to be straightforward about that because I'd rather give you the chance to compete than have a timing problem later."

---

## Fallback Position

If base is truly constrained (startup budget limited):

- Request a signing bonus to bridge the gap (ask for CHF 15-25k one-time).
- Request a 6-month review with a clear comp adjustment trigger.
- Negotiate additional PTO, equipment budget (CHF 3-5k), or a professional development budget.
- If equity upside is real and the company is compelling, a CHF 10-15k base discount in exchange for an additional 0.25-0.5% equity can make sense at seed/Series A.

---

## Red Flags to Watch

- No clarity on cap table or valuation when asked directly.
- Equity quoted as number of shares without telling you the total shares outstanding.
- "We'll revisit comp after you start" with no concrete timeline or trigger.
- Location discount applied without acknowledgment.
- Vesting cliff longer than 12 months.
