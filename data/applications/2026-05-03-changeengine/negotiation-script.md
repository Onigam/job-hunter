# Negotiation Script — ChangeEngine VP/Head of Engineering

Role: VP / Head of Engineering
Company: ChangeEngine (Series A, $18.5M raised, ~211 employees)
Location: London (Magino based in Geneva)

---

## Market Context

**London VP Engineering market rate (2025-2026):**
- Series A startup, 50-250 employees: £140,000-£185,000 base
- With equity, total comp target: £160,000-£210,000+
- VP/Head with founding-engineer remit (hiring, architecture, IC contribution): toward the upper end

**Geneva/Switzerland equivalent (if remote arrangement):**
- CHF 185,000-240,000 base for equivalent seniority
- Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe — cost-of-living argument is legitimate
- If working remotely from Geneva, there may be a contractor/EOR structure discussion (this changes the comp frame)

**Key leverage points:**
- This is a founding-level engineering role (first or near-first engineer in London), not a standard VP hire
- Magino brings a direct comp: co-founded and sold a company. That's harder to price than a VP who joined a company at Series B.
- Acquisition outcome (Benevity $1B+) is a verifiable data point, not a claim

---

## Opening Anchor

When asked about compensation expectations, do not give a range. Give a single number at the top of what's justifiable.

**Script — opening:**

> "Based on the seniority of this role and the founding-engineer scope — hiring, architecture decisions, hands-on shipping in the early phase — I'd expect something in the range of £165,000-175,000 base, with meaningful equity reflecting the early-stage risk. I'm open to discussing how the total package is structured."

If the role is remote from Geneva, adjust:

> "If the arrangement is remote from Geneva, the base would need to reflect that geography — Geneva cost of living is roughly 30-40% above London. I'd be targeting CHF 210,000-225,000 in that structure."

---

## If They Come In Low

If the first offer is below £140,000 / CHF 185,000:

**Script — counter:**

> "I appreciate the offer. The base is lower than I'd expected for a VP-level role with the founding scope you described — hiring the initial team, setting the architectural foundation, shipping hands-on in the early months. That's a different kind of commitment than a standard management hire, and I think the comp should reflect that. I'd need to see at least [£155,000 / CHF 200,000] to make this work, ideally closer to [£165,000 / CHF 215,000]. Is there room to move on base, or should we look at how equity can bridge the gap?"

Key principle: always give them a path (equity bridge) if they can't move on cash. Don't make it a dead end.

---

## Equity Negotiation

ChangeEngine is Series A ($18.5M total raised). Typical equity for VP Engineering at this stage:

**Market range:**
- 0.5% to 1.5% depending on company size, stage, and how "founding" the role really is
- If truly one of the first engineers with founding-level scope: 0.75%-1.25% is defensible
- Standard vesting: 4 years, 1-year cliff

**Questions to ask:**
1. "What is the current fully diluted share count?" (determines what your % actually means in dollars)
2. "What was the post-money valuation on the Series A?" (gives a baseline for current paper value)
3. "Is there a standard refresh policy after year 1 or 2?"
4. "What are the exercise terms for options if I leave — standard 90 days, or extended?"

**Script — opening equity ask:**

> "On equity, given the founding-engineer scope of this role, I'd expect something in the 0.75-1.0% range, vesting over four years with a one-year cliff. I'd want to understand the current cap table structure before we finalize anything — specifically the fully diluted count and the Series A post-money valuation. Happy to work through the numbers together."

**If the equity offer is low (e.g., 0.2-0.3%):**

> "That's below what I'd expect for a role with this scope and stage. At Series A with genuine founding-level risk and responsibility, 0.75% is the floor I'd be comfortable with. If you can't move there, I'd want to revisit the base to compensate. What's the flexibility on either side?"

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If the hiring team tries to apply a "Geneva remote discount" (paying less because you're not in London):

> "I understand the logic, but I'd push back on framing it as a discount. Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe — my cost of living is comparable to or higher than London's. A geographic reduction doesn't hold up from that angle. The relevant comparison is market rate for this role and level, not where my apartment is."

If they persist: anchor to market rate data, not personal cost of living. You can reference Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, and LinkedIn Salary for VP Engineering at Series A companies in Western Europe.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer or are at late-stage conversation elsewhere, use it:

**Script:**

> "I want to be transparent with you — I'm at final stages with another company. I'd prefer ChangeEngine, which is why I'm having this conversation. But I need to make a decision in the next [5-7 days] and the other offer is at [£X / CHF X]. If you can get to [your target], this is an easy yes for me."

If you don't have a competing offer: don't fabricate one. Instead, use timeline pressure:

> "I've got a few other conversations that are progressing and I'd expect to have a clearer picture of my options within two weeks. I'd rather move quickly with you if we can get aligned on comp."

---

## Non-Negotiables to Establish Early

1. **Equity vesting cliff:** 1-year cliff is standard. Anything above 1 year is a red flag.
2. **Option exercise window:** Push for 5-year post-departure exercise window if they offer less than 90 days.
3. **Title clarity:** Confirm whether this is VP Engineering or Head of Engineering — matters for future job searches.
4. **Location flexibility:** Get in writing whether the role is London-based, hybrid, or remote-friendly from Geneva.
5. **Reporting line:** Who does this role report to? CEO or CTO? Answer changes the scope and influence.

---

## Final Framing

Before signing anything:

> "Can you walk me through the cap table structure — specifically what percentage remains in the option pool after this grant, and what dilution has looked like across previous rounds? I want to understand what a realistic exit scenario looks like for someone at my equity level."

If they can't answer this, or are evasive, that's useful signal about how they approach transparency with leadership hires.
