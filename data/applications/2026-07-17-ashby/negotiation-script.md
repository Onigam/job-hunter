# Negotiation Script — Ashby / Staff Product Engineer, EU

---

## Market Context

**Ashby's stated range:** EUR 141,000 to 226,000, applied as a single band across a long list of eligible countries: Germany, Belgium, France, Netherlands, Spain, Italy, Finland, Sweden, Switzerland, Portugal, Norway, Poland, or remote within those countries.

**What a flat EUR band across that country list actually means:** the same number has to cover Warsaw and Geneva. That's not a small gap. A EUR 180,000 offer is a very strong number in Poland or Portugal and a middling one in Switzerland once cost of living and taxes are accounted for. If Ashby has any internal country-based adjustment on top of the posted band, that adjustment is the whole conversation, and it's worth asking about directly rather than assuming the top of the range is available everywhere.

**Geneva cost of living vs. the rest of the listed countries:**
- Geneva and Zurich are consistently the most expensive cities in this entire list, by a wide margin over Berlin, Lisbon, or Warsaw.
- A pre-tax EUR 190,000 in Geneva (paid in CHF terms) leaves meaningfully less purchasing power than the same number in most of the other eligible countries, once Swiss health insurance premiums and cantonal tax are factored in.
- There's a real, defensible argument that Swiss-based candidates should land at or near the top of the stated band, not the midpoint.

**Switzerland market rate for this profile (staff+ product engineer, ex-founder, full-stack ownership):**
- Staff+ product engineer at a profitable, high-growth SaaS company: CHF 170,000 to 220,000 base, often with meaningful equity given the seniority and scope.
- Founding-engineer-caliber IC at a funded scale-up: CHF 180,000 to 230,000 base plus equity.
- Ashby's top band of EUR 226,000 converts to roughly CHF 215,000 to 220,000 at current exchange rates, which lands inside that range and is the number to anchor toward.

---

## Negotiation Strategy

### Opening Anchor

If asked for salary expectations before an offer is made:

"I've seen the posted range, 141 to 226 thousand euros. Given that I'm based in Geneva, which is one of the more expensive cities in that country list, I'd expect to land in the upper part of that band, something in the 200 to 226 thousand euro range, or the CHF equivalent if that's easier to administer."

If pushed for a single number: "Somewhere around CHF 210,000 to 220,000 base, with equity that reflects a staff-level, full-ownership role."

### When They Make an Offer

If the offer lands near the top of the range (~EUR 215k-226k / ~CHF 205k-215k): This is a fair outcome for the level and location. Shift the conversation to equity terms and the exercise window rather than pushing base further.

If the offer is mid-range (~EUR 180k / ~CHF 172k): Push toward the top using the cost-of-living argument directly.

"I appreciate the offer. Given that this is a single band covering countries with very different costs of living, and Geneva is on the expensive end of that list, I'd expect to be closer to the top of the range than the middle. Is there room to move toward 210 to 220 thousand euros?"

If the offer is below EUR 160k: Push back on it as a level mismatch, not just a location one.

"To be candid, that number reads more like a senior-level offer than a staff-level one, before even factoring in Geneva cost of living. Between the scope of this role and where I'm based, I'd need to see this closer to 200,000 euros to feel the offer matches the level."

---

## Equity Negotiation

Ashby's posting mentions equity with a 10-year exercise window, which is already a strong, candidate-friendly term worth acknowledging rather than re-litigating.

**Questions to ask:**
- "What's the strike price, and when was the most recent 409A or equivalent valuation done?"
- "What percentage of fully diluted shares does this grant represent at my level?"
- "Is the vesting standard four years with a one-year cliff, or something else?"
- "Does the 10-year exercise window apply regardless of when I leave, or only under certain conditions?"

**What to look for:**
- Given Ashby is profitable-adjacent (tens of millions in ARR, growing over 100% year over year per their own numbers) rather than burning cash on a speculative bet, the equity is likely worth taking seriously rather than treating as a lottery ticket.
- Ask directly how the option pool size and recent growth numbers translate into value for a new staff-level grant, since a fast-growing, low-burn company is a genuinely different risk profile than a typical early-stage startup.

**If base lands mid-range and equity feels light:**
"If there's less flexibility on base, I'd want to understand whether the equity grant can move instead. Given the growth numbers you've shared publicly, I'd rather have a larger stake in that than push hard on the cash number alone."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If the offer explicitly discounts Switzerland relative to the top of the band:

"I understand wanting one simple band across a lot of countries, but Geneva's cost of living is genuinely in a different tier than most of the rest of this list, closer to what you'd see in a major US city than to Lisbon or Warsaw. I'd ask that the Swiss end of this range reflect that rather than defaulting to the middle of the posted band."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If there's a real competing conversation in progress:

"I want to be straightforward: I'm in a later-stage conversation with another company at a comparable level, with a base in the CHF 200 to 220 thousand range. Ashby is my first choice given the scope of the role and how you run engineering, but I'd need the numbers to be close enough that it's not a difficult call."

If there's no real competing offer, don't invent one:

"I've been deliberately selective in this search because I want the right scope and team, and this role is close to the top of that list. I'm also further along with a couple of other companies, so I'd like to move at a reasonable pace if we can land on the right package."

---

## Timeline and Closing

Given Ashby's own description of a fast hiring process (three rounds, roughly six hours of interviews total, quick feedback), expect the offer stage to move quickly too. That's fine, but still take the time to get it right.

Once there's a verbal offer, ask for it in writing before responding with a number. Use 48 hours to:
1. Confirm the equity grant details and exercise window in writing, not just verbally
2. Check whether the base is paid in EUR or CHF, and how currency conversion is handled going forward
3. Re-read the vesting schedule for anything unusual

Don't verbally accept before the written offer is in hand.
