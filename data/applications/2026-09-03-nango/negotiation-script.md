# Negotiation Script: Staff Backend Engineer, Nango

Context that changes the math here: the posting states the band openly, $140,000 to $220,000 plus equity, for remote hires across a long list of countries including the US, Canada, and much of Europe. That's a wide single band rather than country-specific tiers, so the negotiation is less about proving Geneva is expensive and more about proving the top third of that range fits an eleven-plus-year backend hire coming in at staff level. Nango is YC W23, roughly 14 people, mid seven-figure ARR, 500+ paying customers including Replit and Ramp, so this reads as an early-stage cap table that's already proven the product, not a pre-revenue bet.

## Market anchor

Staff-level backend hires with 10+ years and a track record of owning an API layer end to end typically land CHF 155,000-180,000 base in the Swiss market, or the equivalent $170,000-200,000 in a US-anchored remote band. The posted $140,000-220,000 range is wide enough that opening below the upper third would undersell both the seniority bar (staff, 10+ years) and the fact that a fourteen-person company doesn't have room for a staff hire who isn't already load-bearing within the first quarter. Opening anchor: $200,000 base plus an equity grant sized for a staff-level hire at a company this early, with room to discuss the split between base and equity depending on how Nango weighs each.

## Opening anchor script

"Given the scope here, owning backend architecture decisions that the rest of the platform lives with for years, I'd anchor around $200,000 base, plus an equity grant that reflects joining at 14 people rather than 140. Where does that land relative to how you're thinking about the role, and is the equity number typically a fixed grant or tied to a percentage of the cap table at this stage?"

## If they come in low

"I hear the number. Can you walk me through where that sits in your $140,000 to $220,000 range and what's driving that placement, is it geography, or how you're scoping the role itself? I'm anchoring to the ownership level, ten-plus years shipping and owning backend systems in production, not just the range midpoint."

If they hold the line: "I can work with a lower base if the equity grant reflects joining this early, or if there's a structured comp review at six months tied to what actually ships. I'd rather not settle below $175,000 total base, that undervalues the staff-level ownership this role is asking for."

## Equity notes

- Ask for the fully diluted percentage, not just share count, and get the current valuation used to price it.
- At 14 people and mid seven-figure ARR, this is likely a Series A or pre-Series A cap table. Push for standard 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff, and ask directly whether there's an early-exercise option and what the post-termination exercise window is (90 days is standard and worth negotiating up if it's shorter).
- Ask whether there's anti-dilution protection tied to future rounds for early hires, most startups won't offer it beyond founders and lead investors, but it costs nothing to ask and signals you've done this before.

## Geographic and remote-band pushback

Since the band is already unified across countries rather than geo-adjusted, there's no discount to push back against here, the leverage point is proving staff-level scope instead. Worth stating directly that Geneva-based doesn't mean discounted rate expectations, and that availability across European and US working hours is a plus for a small, fully remote team.

## Competing offer leverage template

"I'm currently weighing this against another staff-level backend conversation that's landed in a similar range. What I care about more than the marginal dollar is the ownership scope and the team, so if Nango can move on either the base or the equity split, I'd rather close here than keep the other process running longer than it needs to."
