# Negotiation Script: Senior Fullstack Developer, Claude Code Expert (undisclosed coaching marketplace via Jobgether)

## Context that shapes the numbers

The employer isn't named. What the posting tells us: a small, experienced team that has "built successful platforms" before, a life coaching and counseling marketplace finishing development and heading into launch, a Firebase/GCP + SvelteKit stack, and the role is advertised as based in Switzerland with remote or hybrid options. The "(m/w/d)" in the title and the German "Vollzeit" point to a German-speaking employer, which could be Swiss-German or German. The LinkedIn header also showed "Québec, QC", which contradicts the body. **Before any salary talk, ask Jobgether three things: the company's country, the contract type (Swiss employment, employer of record, or freelance), and the band.**

What that means:

- **Early-stage, pre-launch.** Cash is probably tighter than at a scale-up. Expect a lower base with some equity, or a freelance mandate. Base still matters most, because pre-launch equity is a long shot.
- **The title is Senior, the scope is tech lead.** Leading launch prep, owning deploy and maintenance processes, setting best practices and contributing to the roadmap is lead work. Price it at the top of Senior or as Lead, not mid-Senior.
- **Possible German employer.** If the company is in Germany and pays German rates, a Geneva salary will look like a big jump to them. Settle on a Swiss band or a Swiss day rate early.
- **Part-time is worth raising.** The posting says full-time, but with Givernance running in parallel, a 50% to 80% arrangement or a freelance contract would suit you better. Small pre-launch teams are often more open to this than the posting suggests, especially after launch when the work settles.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough figures from public surveys and recruiter data. Re-check on swissdevjobs.ch, levels.fyi (Switzerland), Glassdoor, the Hays and Michael Page Switzerland salary guides, and ask the recruiter for the band directly.

| Benchmark | Amount (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Senior Fullstack, Swiss startup (Zurich/Geneva), 100% | CHF 115k to 145k base |
| Tech Lead / Lead Fullstack, Swiss startup, 100% | CHF 135k to 165k base |
| Senior Fullstack, German startup, German band (what to avoid) | €70k to 95k |
| Freelance senior TypeScript/Node, Switzerland | CHF 900 to 1,300 per day (roughly CHF 110 to 160 per hour) |
| Equity, early lead engineer at a pre-seed/seed startup | ~0.2% to 1% of fully diluted shares, 4-year vest |

**Target:** CHF 150k base at 100% (pro rata if part-time), plus options.
**Opening anchor:** CHF 160k base, or CHF 1,200 per day freelance.
**Walk-away floor:** CHF 130k at 100%, or CHF 950 per day. Below that, Geneva roles pay more for less risk.

Why the top of the range is defensible: 17+ years, co-founder who took a platform from zero to an acquisition by a $1B+ company, the exact backend stack (TypeScript, Node.js, Express, REST), and the AI-assisted workflow they're making the headline of the role. Expect pushback on the gaps (Firebase, GCP, Svelte, Stripe, Twilio) to pull you down. See the gap script below.

## Getting the facts first

To the Jobgether recruiter, before the hiring manager round:

"Before we go further, could you tell me where the company is registered, whether this is a Swiss employment contract, an employer of record, or a freelance setup, and the salary range for someone based in Switzerland? I'm in Geneva, so I want to make sure we're working from a Swiss range."

## Opening anchor

When they ask for your expectations:

"For this scope, leading the launch, owning the deploy and maintenance process and setting engineering practice for the team, I'm looking at around CHF 160,000 base at full time, based in Geneva. I'm also open to discussing a part-time or freelance arrangement, which might suit a pre-launch budget better. How does that compare with what you had in mind?"

If they ask for a number first and you'd rather not give one: "I'd rather start from your range. Once I see it, I can tell you quickly whether we're close."

## Counter-offer if they come in low

"Thanks, I appreciate the offer and I'm keen on the role. The number is below what Senior and Lead roles pay in Switzerland right now, and the scope here is closer to tech lead than a standard senior seat. I'd be comfortable at CHF 150,000. If base is the hard constraint before launch, I'd look at a smaller gap in base with a defined review six months after launch, tied to concrete milestones like uptime and release cadence, plus a meaningful option grant."

If they hold firm and you still want it: trade base for time. "If CHF X is the ceiling, I'd do it at 80% for the same total, which keeps your cost the same and gives me room for my own project."

## Pushing back on the gap argument

If they argue you're new to Firebase, GCP, Svelte, Stripe or Twilio:

"That's fair, and I said so in my application. What you're paying for is someone who's taken a platform from zero to production and through an acquisition, who writes TypeScript and Node every day, and who uses Claude Code to get up to speed on an SDK in days rather than weeks. The stack differences are real, but they're the cheap part of this job. Launch judgment is the expensive part."

## Geographic discount pushback

If they want to pay a German or "European" band:

"I understand you may have people elsewhere on a different scale. I'm in Geneva, though, and costs here are among the highest in Europe. Rent and health insurance alone are well above German levels. I'm happy to be flexible on the structure, part-time or freelance, but the rate has to be a Swiss one."

## Equity notes

At a pre-launch startup, options are a lottery ticket, so they shouldn't replace base. If they offer them anyway, ask for:

- The percentage of fully diluted shares, not just a number of options, and the latest valuation or strike price.
- Standard vesting: 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Push for a shorter cliff (6 months) given the launch focus.
- Acceleration on change of control (at least single-trigger partial, or double-trigger).
- Exercise window after leaving: ask for longer than 90 days.
- If it's a Swiss company, ask whether it's options or a phantom/virtual plan, and how it's taxed. Swiss tax treatment of employee options can be unfavourable if the plan isn't set up carefully.
- Good-leaver terms that protect vested shares.

## Competing offer template

Use only if true, or keep it general:

"I'm in late-stage conversations with another Swiss company for a Staff-level role. I'd prefer this one because of the launch scope and how central Claude Code is to how you work, but I need the numbers to be within reach. If you can get to CHF X, I'm happy to commit this week."

## Contract terms to check

- **IP clause.** Carve out prior and personal work explicitly: Givernance, OpenClaw, and the open-source repos on your GitHub.
- **Side activity.** If it's a Swiss employment contract, get written consent for Givernance as an allowed outside activity.
- **Remote and hours.** The posting promises flexible hours and remote or hybrid; get the expected on-site days, if any, and where, in writing.
- **Tooling.** Ask who pays for Claude Code (Max plan or API usage). For a role built around it, the company should.
- **Launch on-call.** Ask how post-launch on-call is shared and compensated, since you'd be the person owning stability.
