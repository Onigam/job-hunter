# Negotiation Script: Nexthink, Staff Software Engineer (Lausanne, Hybrid)

## Market anchor

Lausanne pays below Zurich (typically 10-15% lower) but above Geneva for engineering roles, and Staff level with 10+ years of experience and 3+ years already at Staff/Principal should sit well above the generic "Software Engineer" bands you'll see quoted online (roughly CHF 95k-125k for Lausanne, CHF 106k median for Geneva). Those numbers describe mid-level IC roles, not this one. For a Staff Software Engineer at a ~1,000 person, profitable B2B SaaS company like Nexthink, a realistic base range is:

- **Target range:** CHF 165,000-190,000 base
- **Floor (walk-away consideration point):** CHF 155,000 base
- **Stretch anchor (opening ask):** CHF 195,000 base

Total comp including bonus and any equity should land CHF 15k-30k above base. Nexthink isn't an early-stage startup, so don't expect hypergrowth-style equity upside, but do expect the base to reflect that stability.

## Opening anchor

When asked for a number first, anchor high and justify it with market data rather than a bare figure:

"Based on Staff-level compensation benchmarks for Lausanne and the scope of this role, I'm looking at a base in the CHF 190,000-195,000 range, plus the standard bonus structure. That reflects 10+ years of backend and distributed systems experience, three-plus years already operating at Staff level, and the AI integration work this role specifically calls for."

If pushed to give a single number, give the top of the target range (CHF 190,000), not the midpoint. It's easier to come down than to claw back up later.

## Counter-offer script (if they come in low)

If the initial offer lands below CHF 160,000 base:

"I appreciate the offer. The number is a bit below where I expected to land given the Staff scope and the AI-native platform work described in the role, that's typically where compensation moves up rather than holds flat. Based on what I'm seeing for comparable Staff roles in the Lausanne and greater Geneva area, I was anchored closer to CHF 180,000-190,000 base. Is there room to move closer to that, either on base or through the bonus structure?"

If they hold firm on base but have room elsewhere:

"If base is fixed at this stage, I'd want to look at the sign-on bonus or the annual bonus target to close the gap. A one-time sign-on in the CHF 10,000-15,000 range would make the total package work for me this year."

## Equity notes

Nexthink is a mature private company, not an early-stage startup, so equity here (if offered at all) is more likely a modest RSU-style or phantom equity grant than founder-level option pools. Treat any equity offer as a bonus on top of a solid base, not a substitute for one:

- Ask directly whether the grant is RSUs, stock options, or phantom shares, the tax treatment in Switzerland differs meaningfully between them.
- Ask for the vesting schedule (standard is 4 years with a 1 year cliff) and whether any acceleration applies on acquisition.
- If options, ask for the strike price and the most recent 409A or equivalent valuation so the number means something.
- Don't let an equity grant justify accepting a below-market base. Base is what compounds into pension contributions and is the number you can actually rely on.

## Geographic discount pushback

Some companies try to apply a "remote from Geneva, office in Lausanne" discount, or benchmark against lower cost-of-living countries for a hybrid role. Push back directly:

"This role is based in Lausanne with in-office expectations, so I'd expect Lausanne market rates to apply, not a discounted remote band. Geneva and Lausanne are both high cost-of-living areas, and I'm not looking for a remote-adjusted number for a hybrid role that expects me on site."

## Competing offer leverage template

Use only if there's a genuine second process in motion. Vague or fabricated leverage tends to backfire with experienced recruiters:

"I'm currently further along in another process at a similar level, so I do have a timeline I'm working against. I'd like to keep things moving on both sides, and if Nexthink can get closer to the range we discussed, I'd be glad to prioritize this one."

## Practical notes

- Negotiate total comp as a package (base, bonus target, sign-on, any equity) rather than line by line, it gives more room to trade.
- Ask about the unlimited PTO policy's actual norms in practice, "unlimited" policies sometimes average out lower than a fixed 25+ day policy elsewhere.
- Confirm whether the relocation package applies if moving within Switzerland (Geneva to Lausanne) or only for international relocation.
- Get any verbal agreement in writing before signing, especially sign-on bonus and start date.
