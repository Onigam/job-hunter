# Negotiation Script - Swayable / Staff Engineer: AI and Scientific Computing

---

## Market Context

**Company stage:** Swayable is self-funded past its original YC seed, roughly $3.5M ARR, and somewhere between 35 and 60 employees depending on the source. There's no recent priced round on record. That cuts both ways in a negotiation: no VC pressure forcing thin comp to protect runway, but also no fresh 409A to anchor an equity number against, so the strike price conversation needs to be asked for directly rather than assumed.

**No posted salary range** on the YC or LinkedIn listing. Levels.fyi shows Swayable software engineer pay around $115K to $153K, which is a mid-level band, not a staff one. For a staff-level architect role reporting directly to the CTO, the number should sit well above that.

**US staff engineer market (NYC/SF), small profitable-adjacent startup:** roughly $190,000 to $240,000 base, plus equity, for someone owning architecture for an entire technical domain and mentoring both engineers and data scientists. Swayable's benefits are genuinely strong on paper (100% covered health, dental, vision, generous parental leave, a dev stipend), which is worth factoring in but not worth letting substitute for base.

**Switzerland comparison, for my own anchor, not necessarily theirs:** a Geneva-based staff+ engineer with founding-engineer-level ownership would command roughly CHF 180,000 to 220,000 base locally. At current exchange rates that's close to $205,000 to $250,000, which lines up with the US staff range above. That overlap is useful. It means I don't need to argue for a "location premium," I can just point at what staff-level work costs in either market and let the numbers agree with each other.

---

## Negotiation Strategy

### Opening Anchor

If asked for expectations before an offer is made:

"For a staff-level role owning the AI and scientific computing architecture and mentoring the team around it, I'd expect a base in the $210,000 to $230,000 range, plus a meaningful equity grant given the ownership involved. I'm remote from Geneva, so I'm flexible on how that's structured, but that's the ballpark for the scope described."

If pushed for a single number: "Around $220,000 base, with equity commensurate with a staff-level architecture role."

### When They Make an Offer

If the offer lands at $210K or above: This is a fair outcome for the scope. Shift focus to equity mechanics, the exercise window, and the vesting start date rather than pushing base further.

If the offer is mid-range (~$180K to $195K):

"I appreciate the offer. Given the scope here, being the architect and technical lead for the whole AI and data analysis surface, I'd expect to land closer to $210,000 to $220,000. Is there room to move the base, or is there flexibility on the equity side to make up the difference?"

If the offer is below $170K:

"To be candid, that reads closer to a senior-level number than a staff one, particularly for a role that reports directly to the CTO and covers architecture plus mentoring across engineers and data scientists. I'd need to see this closer to $200,000 to feel it matches the level described in the posting."

---

## Equity Negotiation

Because there's no recent outside round, the standard "what's the 409A" question matters more here than at a freshly-funded startup, since the last formal valuation could be old or informal.

**Questions to ask:**
- "What's the current strike price, and when was the most recent 409A valuation done?"
- "What percentage of fully diluted shares does this grant represent?"
- "Is vesting the standard four years with a one-year cliff?"
- "What's the exercise window after departure, the standard 90 days or something longer?"
- "Since there hasn't been an outside priced round recently, how is the company thinking about valuation for equity grants right now?"

**What to look for:**
- A bootstrapped, profitable-leaning company at $3.5M ARR is a real business, not a moonshot bet, but the equity is illiquid with no clear path to a near-term liquidity event. Don't let the equity story substitute for a fair base.
- If the exercise window is the standard short one, that's worth flagging. A longer post-departure window is a reasonable, low-cost ask for a company this size.

**If base lands mid-range and equity feels thin:**

"If base has less room to move, I'd want to understand whether the equity grant itself can be larger, or whether the exercise window can be extended. Given there's no recent outside valuation, I'd rather have clarity on the mechanics than assume the grant is generous."

---

## Geographic and Remote Discount Pushback

The team is centered in NYC and SF. If there's any suggestion that a Geneva-based remote hire should be paid less than a US-based one for the same role:

"I understand wanting a simpler comp structure, but Geneva isn't a lower cost-of-living market than New York or San Francisco, it's comparable or higher once Swiss health insurance and cantonal tax are factored in. I'd ask that the offer reflect the role and level rather than a blanket remote discount."

If they raise currency or payroll complexity as the reason for a lower number, separate that from compensation level explicitly: "I'm fine working through whatever payroll or contractor structure is simplest on your end. That's an administrative question, it shouldn't be the reason the number itself is lower."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If there's a real competing process in flight:

"I want to be straightforward: I'm further along with another company at a comparable staff level, with a base around $210,000 to $220,000. Swayable is genuinely interesting to me given the scientific rigor behind the product, but I'd need the numbers to be close enough that it's not a hard call."

If there's no real competing offer, don't invent one:

"I've been intentionally narrow in this search, looking for roles where the technical scope matches staff level and the product itself is worth the ownership. This is one of a short list, so I'd like to move at a reasonable pace toward the right package."

---

## Timeline and Closing

Given the size of the team, expect a shorter, more direct hiring process than a larger company, likely a handful of conversations with the CTO and a couple of engineers rather than a long panel loop. Once there's a verbal offer:

1. Get it in writing before responding with a counter, including base, equity percentage, and vesting start date
2. Ask directly about the 409A date and strike price if it wasn't already covered
3. Confirm whether pay is USD and how that gets handled for a Switzerland-based hire, tax equalization, contractor structure, or direct employment
4. Re-read the PTO and parental leave terms since those were called out as above-market

Don't verbally accept before the written offer is in hand.
