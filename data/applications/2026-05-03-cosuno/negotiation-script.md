# Negotiation Script — Cosuno Senior AI Engineer

## Market Context

**Important dynamic to understand first:** Cosuno is a Berlin-based company offering remote. German market rates for a Senior AI Engineer at Series B stage typically range €100,000–€130,000 base. Swiss market rates for the equivalent role are significantly higher: CHF 160,000–210,000 at Staff/Senior level in Geneva. This gap is the central tension in any negotiation.

Cosuno will likely anchor to the German or pan-European remote range. The goal is to be recognized as a Geneva-based senior individual contributor whose cost of living, tax burden, and market alternatives reflect Swiss rather than German benchmarks.

**Role level:** Senior AI Engineer, effectively IC5/Staff-equivalent given 17+ years and founding-engineer profile.

**Realistic target range:**
- Minimum acceptable: CHF 155,000 / €145,000 gross annually
- Target: CHF 175,000 / €165,000 gross + equity
- Aspirational: CHF 190,000+ with meaningful equity or bonus

---

## Opening Anchor

When asked about salary expectations, do not give a range. Give a number.

> "For a senior IC role at this scope, I'm targeting CHF 175,000 gross annually. That reflects Geneva market rates for Staff-level AI engineering work, which is where I've been benchmarking given that I'm staying in Switzerland. I'm happy to talk about the full package including equity, but that's my starting point on base."

If they immediately say "we pay in EUR and our range is €100,000–€120,000":

> "I appreciate the transparency. The gap is mostly geographic, not a disagreement about the role. An engineer in Berlin at €115,000 and an engineer in Geneva at CHF 165,000 are in roughly the same purchasing power position after taxes and cost of living. I'd want to find a number that works for both sides rather than a German-market rate applied to a Swiss location."

---

## Counter-Offer Scripts

**If they offer €110,000 or below:**

> "I have to be honest, that's materially below what I need to make this work from Geneva. I'm not trying to be difficult about it — it's a straight cost-of-living calculation. My current compensation is in the CHF 170,000–180,000 range. If you can get to €135,000–€140,000 I think we can close this."

**If they offer €120,000–€130,000:**

> "We're getting closer. I want to make this work, so let me be specific: I need CHF 155,000 minimum to leave my current role, and I think the work justifies more. Can you get to €140,000 plus equity? At that point I'd be ready to move quickly."

**If they say the range is fixed and they can't go higher:**

> "If the base is fixed, I'd like to talk about what else is in the package. Equity, signing bonus, hardware, and conference budget all matter. A meaningful signing bonus and a clear equity grant could bridge the gap for the first year while we demonstrate the value of the arrangement."

---

## Equity Notes (Series B Company)

Cosuno has raised $52.7M across Series A ($17.6M, 2021) and Series B ($35.1M, 2022). They are post-Series B and likely approaching profitability focus or a Series C path.

**What to ask:**
- What percentage of the company does the option pool represent?
- What is the current valuation (or last-round valuation) for reference?
- What is the standard vesting schedule? (4-year with 1-year cliff is standard)
- Is there acceleration on acquisition?
- Are these ISOs or NSOs (if EU-structured, likely phantom equity or warrants)?

**Script for equity conversation:**

> "Can you walk me through what the equity grant looks like? I want to understand the number of options, the vesting schedule, and what the strike price is relative to the last-round valuation. I'm not expecting a founding-level grant, but equity matters to me and I want to understand what's realistic here."

**Minimum acceptable equity for this stage:**
- 0.1%–0.3% fully diluted for a senior IC at a Series B company is realistic
- Ask for the higher end; 0.05% or less is a red flag for "senior" positioning

**Anti-dilution note:** At Series B, subsequent rounds will dilute. Ask whether future financing comes with pro-rata rights for employees or employee option pool top-ups. This is a legitimate question and signals you understand how equity works.

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they try to apply a geographic discount to your Geneva location (lower salary because "it's remote from Switzerland not Berlin"):

> "I understand the logic, but it works the other way from my perspective. I'm not taking a discount on the Swiss market rate because the role is remote. If anything, a Geneva-based engineer means you're getting someone in a stable, high-cost jurisdiction who won't be moving to Berlin and asking for a raise six months in. The location is a feature, not a discount."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have or can produce a competing offer, use it late in the process:

> "I want to be straightforward with you. I have another process moving in parallel at a company offering CHF 168,000. I'd prefer to work on the Cosuno problem because I think the domain is more interesting, but I need the gap to be smaller than it currently is. Can you get to CHF 160,000 and we close this?"

If you don't have a competing offer but have had conversations, you can reference it more loosely:

> "I've had conversations with other companies in the AI space in the CHF 160,000–175,000 range. I'm not trying to create a bidding war, but I want you to know that's the market I'm comparing against."

---

## Timing and Process Notes

- Don't accept on the first call. "Let me look at the full package in writing" is always appropriate.
- If they push for a quick answer, it's okay to say: "I need 48 hours to review the full offer properly. I'll have an answer by Thursday."
- If they ask what would make you accept immediately: "Get me to CHF 165,000 base and I'll sign this week."
- Get everything in writing before giving notice anywhere.
