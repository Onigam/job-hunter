# Negotiation Script: Sonar, AI Systems Engineer (Geneva)

Posted location: Geneva, on-site with three anchor days (Mon, Tue, Thu). Relocation support offered, candidate already in Geneva.
Posted comp: no figure given. LinkedIn tags the role "Management level", department IT / Business Technology.
Company: Sonar (SonarQube), founded in Geneva, privately held, $400M+ revenue and profitable per the posting.

---

## 1. Read the situation first

**This is a Business Technology role, not a product engineering one.** It reports into internal IT, and half the scope is Atlassian administration, ITSM and CMDB. IT bands at most companies sit below product engineering bands for the same seniority. Expect Sonar to anchor to an "IT systems engineer / Atlassian lead" band, while the AI agent and MCP work is closer to a senior or staff engineering profile. The negotiation is mostly about which band you land in.

**The gap cuts both ways.** Magino doesn't have the 5+ years of JSM administration they list, which weakens the argument for the top of the IT band. But the Claude, MCP and agent engineering experience is the scarce part of this profile, and few Atlassian admins have it. Lead with that.

**Decide the floor before the call.** Coming from a Senior Staff role, this may be a step down in scope. Worth asking early what the career path looks like (does it grow into an AI platform lead for internal systems?) and how big the Business Technology team is.

**Questions to ask on the first recruiter call:**
- What's the salary band, and which internal level does the role map to?
- Is there a bonus, and is it paid as a 13th month, a target bonus, or both?
- Does the package include equity (options or RSUs), and what liquidity has there been for employees of a private company?
- Who does the role report to, and is there budget for Atlassian certifications?

## 2. Market anchors (Geneva base, 2026, working estimates)

Re-check against Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, the Swiss salary calculator (Salarium) and recruiter feedback before any call. Sonar is Geneva's best-known software employer and tends to pay at or somewhat above local market for engineering, but internal IT roles are usually more conservative.

| Profile in Geneva | Typical base (CHF) |
|---|---|
| Atlassian / JSM administrator, senior | 105k to 130k |
| IT systems or automation engineer, senior | 115k to 145k |
| AI / ML engineer, senior (product side) | 140k to 175k |
| Staff engineer at a scale-up | 170k to 210k |

A realistic band for this role is probably CHF 130k to 165k base, with the upper half reachable if they value the AI side.

**Targets**

- Walk-away: CHF 150k base, unless the equity or growth path is unusually strong.
- Target: CHF 160k to 170k base plus bonus.
- Opening anchor: CHF 180k.

Geneva costs are high (rent, health insurance, childcare), so anything under CHF 150k at this experience level is a real step back from what Magino earns now. There's no geographic discount argument for them to use since the role is in Geneva, but be ready if they bring up "internal equity" with existing IT staff.

## 3. Opening the conversation

If the recruiter asks for expectations early:

> "I'd like to understand the band first, since this role straddles IT administration and AI engineering and those are priced quite differently. For context, I'm coming from a Senior Staff engineering role, and the part of this job I'd be strongest at is the agent and MCP work. For that scope in Geneva, I'm targeting around CHF 180k base. I'm open on structure, so bonus and equity matter to me too."

If they insist on one number:

> "Around CHF 180k base. I'll adjust once I see the full package."

## 4. If they come in low

**Scenario A: pure IT admin band (for example CHF 125k).**

> "Thanks for sharing it. I think that band fits the Atlassian administration part of the role, but the AI systems side, building MCP integrations and agents that meet audit requirements, is a different skill set and it's the one that's hard to hire for. I've built production platforms from scratch and shipped LLM and multi-agent work at Benevity. To make this work I'd need to be at CHF 160k or above. Is there a level that covers both sides of the job?"

**Scenario B: close but under target (for example CHF 150k).**

> "That's close. I know I'm lighter on JSM administration than some candidates, and I've already planned to get certified in the first few months. Could we get to CHF 165k, or agree on 155k with a written review at six months tied to the Atlassian certification and the first agents in production?"

**If base is truly capped:**

> "If the base is fixed, could we look at a sign-on bonus, a higher bonus target, or an equity grant? A paid certification budget would help as well."

## 5. Equity

Sonar is private and has raised large rounds in the past, so equity may be offered as options or RSUs. Things to ask for in writing:

- **Instrument and strike.** Options vs RSUs, and the strike price relative to the last valuation. Options priced at a high past valuation can be worth little.
- **Vesting and cliff.** Four years with a one-year cliff is standard. Ask whether refreshers exist.
- **Liquidity.** Has Sonar run tender offers or secondary sales for employees? Without liquidity, treat equity as a lottery ticket and don't trade base for it.
- **Leaving.** Exercise window after departure (90 days is common and painful; longer is better) and what happens on acquisition (single or double trigger acceleration).
- **Swiss tax.** Options are usually taxed at exercise in Switzerland, RSUs at vesting. Check with a tax adviser before accepting a large grant.

Script:

> "Could you share the equity details in writing, including the instrument, the valuation it's priced against, vesting, and whether there's been any liquidity for employees? That will help me compare base against upside properly."

## 6. Competing offer template

Use only if true. Don't invent an offer.

> "I want to be open with you. I'm in late stages with another company for a staff-level platform role at around CHF [X] base. Sonar is my preference, because the problem of governing internal AI agents is one I really want to work on, and it's in Geneva. If we can get closer to CHF [target], I'd be ready to sign quickly."

## 7. Other items worth negotiating

- Start date and notice period (Swiss notice is often three months; say so early).
- Certification budget and time for ACP-120 and ACP-620.
- Home office days beyond the three anchor days.
- Title. If the scope includes AI platform ownership, "Senior" or "Lead" in the title helps both level and future moves.
- Pension (LPP) contributions above the legal minimum; some Geneva employers pay significantly more than 50%, which is worth several thousand CHF a year.
