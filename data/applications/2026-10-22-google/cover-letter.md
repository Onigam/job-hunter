Most platforms treat policy as a gate you hit after the fact. The HCE posting turns that around: residency and budget become decorators on a developer's intent, compiled into an environment that's compliant from birth, with AI agents hosted as tenants under tightly bounded credentials. That last part is the problem I keep running into: an agent becomes useful the moment it can provision things, and dangerous for exactly the same reason.

Seventeen years of platform work sits behind that view. At Alaya, which I co-founded in 2016, I built the platform from a blank page and took it to 50+ enterprise clients and an acquisition by Benevity in 2021. Every client brought its own security review, so isolation and access boundaries shaped most of the architecture. As a Senior Staff Engineer at Benevity with no reports, I got roughly 70% of engineers on two teams I didn't manage using AI-assisted workflows within three months. Most of my own engineering now runs through multi-agent setups, so scoping what an agent may touch is a daily concern for me.

To be straight about the gaps, I haven't built a policy compiler at Google's scale, and my cloud depth is AWS more than GCP. I live in Geneva, so Zürich is a train ride. Happy to sketch how I'd tie an agent's credentials to a single environment's lifetime, if that helps.
