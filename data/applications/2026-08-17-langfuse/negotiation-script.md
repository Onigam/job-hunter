# Negotiation Script: Product Engineer, Langfuse

Context that changes the math here: Langfuse was acquired by ClickHouse in January 2026 as part of ClickHouse's $400M Series D, which put ClickHouse's valuation at roughly $15B. This is no longer a scrappy seed-stage equity bet, comp is likely being set (or at least reviewed) against ClickHouse's own bands now, and equity, if any is still offered at this level, is probably ClickHouse stock or RSU-like instruments rather than a Langfuse cap table that technically still exists. Confirm which regime applies before anchoring on either number below. Role is based in Berlin (one week per month in person per their remote-first policy), IC product engineer level, not a leadership hire, so comp should be benchmarked as senior/staff IC, not exec.

## Market anchor

- Senior product/full-stack engineer in Berlin at a well-funded, high-growth post-acquisition startup: roughly EUR 85,000-110,000 base, sometimes higher for someone at Staff level with founder-grade experience.
- Geneva-based remote senior/staff engineer comps for comparable scope: roughly CHF 140,000-170,000, reflecting Swiss cost of living rather than the Berlin local market.
- Given the ClickHouse acquisition and the jump in company scale, there's a real chance the band moves closer to a US-style senior IC package (base plus meaningful equity/RSUs), especially if ClickHouse is folding Langfuse comp into its own structure. Worth asking directly early on rather than guessing.
- Opening anchor: CHF 150,000 equivalent if working from Geneva, or EUR 100,000 plus equity if the role is priced on the Berlin band. Frame it as a Staff-level IC number given 17+ years of experience and a founder track record, not a generic senior engineer rate.

## Opening anchor script

"Given the scope here and that this is now part of a company that just closed a $400 million round, I'd expect a base somewhere in the CHF 145,000 to 165,000 range if it's priced against where I'm based, or the equivalent if you're running a Berlin-anchored band, plus a meaningful equity or RSU component. I'm flexible on the exact structure as long as the total lands in that zone. Can you tell me which band this role is actually priced against now, Langfuse's old scale or ClickHouse's?"

## If they come in low

"I hear the number. Is this priced against Langfuse's pre-acquisition band or ClickHouse's current one? I ask because the range I'm working from assumes a Staff-level IC at a company this size post-round, and I want to make sure we're comparing apples to apples before I move."

If they hold the line: "I can flex on base if the equity or RSU grant makes up the gap, or if there's a defined review date tied to the post-acquisition integration work specifically. What I'd rather not do is anchor below CHF 140,000 equivalent, that's under what this scope pays elsewhere in my market."

## Equity and RSU notes (post-acquisition specifics)

- Ask explicitly whether any grant offered is Langfuse legacy equity, newly issued ClickHouse equity, or a hybrid conversion, the mechanics and vesting reset can differ a lot between those.
- If it's ClickHouse equity: ask for the current 409A or last-round valuation basis, the vesting schedule (likely 4-year, 1-year cliff is standard), and whether unvested Langfuse grants (if any existed pre-acquisition) were converted 1:1 or at a discount.
- Ask if there's a refresh grant tied to performance reviews, since a $15B company usually has a more structured refresh cycle than a seed-stage one did.
- Get clarity on liquidity: is there a tender offer program, or is this fully illiquid until a future ClickHouse exit event.

## Geographic and remote-cadence pushback

"The role is Berlin-based with a week a month in person. If I'm working from Geneva the rest of the time, I'd want the base benchmarked against Swiss cost of living rather than the Berlin local market, since that's where most of my cost of living actually sits. Happy to share comparable data if useful."

## Competing offer, if genuinely in play

"I'm in later-stage conversations with another company at a comparable scope, in a similar range to what I mentioned. This role is the one I'm most drawn to because of the specific integration challenge, but I want to be upfront about where things stand so we can move at a pace that works for both of us."

## Close

Don't let the acquisition news function as a discount, the acquisition strengthens the pitch for a strong base since Langfuse now sits inside a company that just raised $400M, so cash constraints are less of an excuse than they'd be at a typical seed-stage startup. Get the base, equity type, vesting terms, and review cadence in writing before committing, since integration-era roles sometimes have comp structures that shift again once org changes settle.
