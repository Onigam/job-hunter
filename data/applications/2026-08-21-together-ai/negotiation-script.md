# Negotiation Script: Together AI, Staff Engineer (Distributed Storage & HPC/AI Infra)

Context to keep in mind going in: the posting discloses a base range of $250,000 to $300,000 plus equity for the San Francisco location, which is the anchor to work from. The role also sits well outside my core depth (Ceph, Weka, Lustre, RDMA/InfiniBand, Go at production scale), so I'm in a weaker position than usual to push the top of a disclosed band. This script assumes the process gets far enough to reach an offer discussion despite that gap.

## Market context

- California pay transparency puts $250k-$300k base as the company's own stated range for this level in SF, likely a Staff/L5-equivalent band. Total comp with equity at a company of Together AI's stage (well-funded, high-profile AI infra) can commonly run 1.5x-2.5x base once equity is priced in, depending on valuation assumptions.
- Geneva-based comp for a Staff Engineer at a comparable scale-up or infra company runs roughly CHF 170,000-220,000 base, but that figure isn't directly relevant unless the role converts to a remote-from-Switzerland arrangement, since the disclosed band is tied to SF.
- If relocation to SF is on the table, use the disclosed range as the primary anchor and negotiate within it rather than trying to argue past it, given the gap on core storage requirements works against a request to exceed the stated ceiling.

## Opening position

"Thanks for sharing the range. Given the disclosed band, I'd want to be talking near the top of it, around $290,000 base, given the platform and distributed-systems experience I bring even though the storage-specific stack here is new to me. I'd also want to understand the equity grant in more detail before we land on a full number, since that's likely to matter more than the base at this stage of the company."

Don't open below the middle of a disclosed range. Anchoring at $290k leaves room to land around $270k-$280k if there's pushback, still solidly in the upper half of the posted band.

## If they come in low (below $250k or heavily equity-weighted)

"I noticed the number's below the range posted for the role. I understand there's a real gap between my background and the deep storage requirements here, but I'd expect that to affect ramp time more than it affects the value of the platform and Kubernetes experience I'd bring on day one. Is there room to move closer to the posted range, or is the equity meant to make up the difference? If it's the latter, I'd want to see the numbers on vesting and strike price before I can compare it to base."

Key move: separate the "storage gap" conversation from the comp conversation. Acknowledge the gap once, then redirect to what's actually being paid for.

## Equity and vesting questions to ask before agreeing to anything

- What's the current 409A valuation, and when was it last set.
- Standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff, or something non-standard.
- Refresh policy: does equity typically refresh at year 2, or only on promotion/re-up.
- Double-trigger acceleration in case of acquisition (relevant given my own experience being on the acquired side at Alaya).
- Whether the grant is ISOs, RSUs, or something else, and what the exercise window looks like after departure.

## Relocation and remote framing

If SF relocation is required: ask for a relocation package (flights, shipping, temporary housing for 4-6 weeks) as a separate line item, not folded into base. Geneva to SF moves are expensive and this is a normal ask, not a stretch one.

If remote-from-Switzerland is possible: expect the company to apply something closer to a US remote band rather than the SF-specific range, and be ready to hear that the storage/HPC nature of the role (hands-on hardware, GPU cluster proximity) may make full remote a harder sell than it would be for a pure software role. Worth asking directly early rather than assuming.

## Competing offer template (use only if real)

"I'm in later-stage conversations with another company for a comparable infrastructure role, and I want to be transparent that comp there is tracking toward [$X]. I'd rather make this work here if we can get close, since the technical problem is the one I actually want, but I do need the numbers to be in the same neighborhood."

Only use this if there's an actual competing process. A fabricated offer is easy to expose in a follow-up question and burns credibility permanently.

## Closing line for any round

"I want to get this right for both of us. Send me something in writing and I'll turn it around quickly."
