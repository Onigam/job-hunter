# Negotiation Script: Lead Engineer, AI Platform (undisclosed company via Jobgether)

## What the posting already tells you

- **Cash is published:** USD 170,000 per year, "benchmarked to U.S. levels regardless of location". That reads like a single global band, so the base number itself may be the hardest thing to move.
- **Equity** with ongoing refresh grants, **35 days PTO**, twice-yearly retreats, health and learning benefits.
- **Fully remote**, async, distributed across countries. LinkedIn shows "Des Moines, IA" in the header while the text says the partner hires in Switzerland; confirm the Swiss hire is real in the first call.
- **Hybrid role:** hands-on engineering plus managing the AI Quality team. That's lead/manager scope, which is your strongest argument for a higher level.
- The company isn't named yet. Jobgether shortlists, the partner runs interviews. Ask for the company name and stage as soon as you reach the partner; you can't value the equity without it.

## Convert the number before anything else

At roughly 0.80 CHF per USD (check the rate on the day you negotiate), USD 170k is about **CHF 136k gross**. Two things change what that's really worth in Geneva:

1. **How you'd be employed.** Ask directly: Swiss entity, Employer of Record (Deel, Remote, Oyster and similar), or contractor?
   - Entity or EOR: the employer pays its share of AHV/AVS, unemployment insurance and BVG/LPP pension. CHF 136k is your real gross.
   - Contractor: you pay both sides of social charges, your own pension, accident and loss-of-earnings insurance, and you lose unemployment cover. Treat CHF 136k as roughly CHF 110-115k employee-equivalent, and ask for a gross-up of 20-25%.
2. **Currency risk.** Paid in USD, a weaker dollar cuts your salary in CHF. Ask for payment in CHF at a fixed rate, or a yearly FX true-up.

## Switzerland market reference (Geneva / Zurich, 2026, indicative)

| Benchmark | Base (CHF) | Variable / equity |
|---|---|---|
| Senior / Staff Engineer, Swiss product company | 150k-190k | 0-15% + equity |
| Lead or Staff AI / ML Platform Engineer, Zurich big tech or scale-up | 170k-230k | bonus + RSUs |
| Engineering Manager / Lead (hands-on), Geneva scale-up | 165k-210k | 10-15% + equity |

CHF 136k sits below Geneva market for a lead engineer who also manages a team. That's not a reason to walk away, since global remote roles with real equity, 35 days off and full async can be worth it. But it means the negotiation should focus on level, equity and employment terms, not just base.

## Targets

- **Opening anchor:** USD 195k base (about CHF 156k), or the next level up in their band, plus a meaningful equity grant
- **Realistic landing:** USD 175-185k, or USD 170k with a larger equity grant and a signing bonus
- **Walk-away:** below USD 170k as a contractor without gross-up, or no clarity on equity (number of shares, total outstanding, last valuation)

## Opening anchor script

If they ask for your expectations before an offer:

> "I saw the USD 170k in the posting, and I appreciate that it's transparent. For the level of this role, leading the AI Quality team while staying hands-on on evals and observability, and with 17 years behind me including a founding role through an acquisition, I'd expect to sit at the top of the band or one level up. Something around USD 195k would reflect that. I'm flexible on how it's split between cash and equity."

If they ask about your current salary:

> "I'd rather anchor on this role. It combines people management with building the evaluation platform itself, so I'm benchmarking against lead and staff-level roles that do both."

## If they come in at the posted number with no flexibility

Don't argue the base harder. Move to the levers a global band usually allows:

> "Understood that the cash band is global, and I respect that. Then let's look at the rest of the package. Is there room on the level, on the size of the initial equity grant, or a signing bonus for the first year? And since I'd be paid in USD while living in Geneva, could we discuss paying in CHF at a fixed rate, or an annual adjustment?"

If they're hiring you as a contractor:

> "If it's a contractor setup, I'd be covering both sides of Swiss social insurance plus my own pension and accident cover, which is around 20-25% on top. Could we either go through an EOR, or gross the rate up to cover that?"

## Equity questions (ask all of them before accepting)

- What instrument: stock options or RSUs? If options, what's the strike price and the latest 409A or equivalent valuation?
- How many shares, and what's the total fully diluted share count? Without that, the grant is just a number.
- Last round size, valuation and date, plus liquidation preferences above common (1x non-participating is standard; anything above that eats into your outcome).
- Vesting schedule and cliff. Four years with a one-year cliff is normal; ask if the cliff can drop to six months given the role scope.
- Exercise window after leaving. Ask for an extended window (5-10 years) instead of 90 days; it matters a lot if you're paying Swiss tax on exercise.
- Acceleration on change of control. Double-trigger is a fair ask for a lead role.
- How refresh grants are sized and when they start, since the posting mentions them explicitly.
- Swiss tax treatment: options are generally taxed at exercise as employment income, so check whether the company has a Swiss tax ruling for its plan or has handled Swiss employees before.

Founders usually can't give individual employees anti-dilution protection; that's an investor term. Your protection is a larger initial grant and real refresh grants, so push there instead.

## Geographic discount pushback

They say "regardless of location", which means they shouldn't apply a discount. If one appears anyway (for instance a European band lower than the posted figure):

> "The posting says compensation is benchmarked to U.S. levels regardless of location, so I'd expect the same number. And Geneva is among the most expensive cities in Europe, so a discount wouldn't match the cost side either."

## Competing offer template (only if real)

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late stages with another company, a Swiss-based role at around CHF [X] base plus [equity / bonus]. This role is my first choice because of the focus on evaluating agents in production, which is exactly where I want to spend the next few years. If we can close the gap on [level / equity / signing bonus], I'm ready to commit quickly."

Never invent a competing offer. If you don't have one, use market data and the scope of the role.

## Non-cash items worth asking for

- Learning budget amount (the posting lists professional development), including conference travel
- Hardware and home office budget, plus coworking in Geneva if needed
- Explicit hands-on time: confirm what share of the week stays engineering versus management
- Model and tooling budget for experiments (API credits for testing open-source and frontier models against production baselines, which the role explicitly calls for)
- Start date and any notice period you need to serve
