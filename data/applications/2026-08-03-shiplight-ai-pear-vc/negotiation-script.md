# Negotiation Script: Shiplight AI, Founding Engineer

## Context to anchor on
- Posted range on the job board is roughly $99,000 to $137,300 base, which reads like a band built for the 1-3 year experience floor mentioned in the listing, not for a founding engineer bringing 17+ years and one acquisition. Don't accept that band as the starting point for this conversation; treat it as the floor of a much wider range a founding-stage AI company can construct once equity, seniority and remote/relocation flexibility are on the table.
- Role is San Francisco Bay Area, hybrid-to-onsite preferred, backed by PearX/Pear VC. Team size is small (roughly 2-10 people), which means comp is negotiated directly with the founders, not HR, and equity matters more than at a later-stage company.
- Home market reference (Geneva/Switzerland): a Senior Staff-level engineer with founding and cofounder experience in Geneva typically commands CHF 160,000-220,000 base cash equivalent, before considering Swiss social charges and the generally higher local cost of living. That's roughly $180,000-245,000 at current exchange rates. This isn't the number to quote directly to a US seed-stage startup, but it's the internal floor below which taking a lower-cash, higher-equity SF offer only makes sense if the equity is real upside, not a token grant.
- Bay Area market reference for a seed/PearX-backed founding engineer with senior-plus experience: base cash typically $170,000-220,000, plus 0.5%-2.0% equity depending on how early the round is and how senior the hire. A candidate with a prior exit is usually a case for the higher end of both bands.

## Opening anchor
Say this once you have signal they want to move forward, not before:

"I want to be upfront that the posted range looks scoped for someone earlier in their career. Given seventeen years building and shipping, including founding Alaya through an acquisition by Benevity, I'd expect a founding engineer offer here to land around $190,000 to $210,000 base, plus a founding-level equity grant, somewhere in the 1.0 to 1.5 percent range depending on how you're thinking about the cap table at this stage. I'm flexible on the exact split between cash and equity if the reasoning behind the split makes sense to me."

## If they come in low on cash
"I hear that early-stage cash is tight, that's normal at this stage and not a dealbreaker for me. What I'd want to understand is whether the equity grant is calibrated to make up the difference, and over what time horizon that actually pays off. If cash lands closer to $160,000, I'd want equity closer to 1.75 to 2 percent to make the total package make sense, along with a real conversation about milestone-based comp reviews at six and twelve months rather than waiting a full year for the first adjustment."

## Equity negotiation checklist
- Confirm the standard 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff; push back if the cliff is longer than 12 months for a founding-level hire.
- Ask directly what percentage of the company the grant represents on a fully diluted basis, not just a share count. A share count without the cap table context is close to meaningless.
- Ask about the strike price and whether early exercise is allowed; early exercise plus an 83(b) election matters a lot for tax treatment if you believe in the upside.
- Ask about acceleration on acquisition. A single-trigger or double-trigger acceleration clause protects you if the company gets acquired quickly, which is a live scenario for a PearX company.
- Ask what anti-dilution protection, if any, exists for early employee grants in future rounds. Most seed-stage companies won't offer much here, but it's worth knowing the answer going in.

## Geographic and remote considerations
The role is framed as SF-based and hybrid. Since the base case is relocation or a remote arrangement from Geneva, address it directly rather than letting them assume a discount:

"I'm not asking for a Bay Area premium given I'd be joining remotely from Geneva at least initially, but I'd push back on any discount tied to location too. My market rate in Switzerland is already at the high end of what you'd pay a senior engineer in the Bay Area, so location isn't a reason to go lower, if anything it's a reason the base number should reflect senior-market rates rather than junior-market ones. If relocation to SF becomes part of the plan later, I'd want a relocation allowance and a timeline built into the offer rather than an assumption I'll just handle it."

## Competing offer as negotiating power (use only if real, or clearly framed as a general market read)
"I'm currently the strongest name in another process for a platform-level founding role with a comparable equity structure, and the cash number there is close to $200,000. I'd rather join Shiplight if the mission and the team are the right fit, but I want the offer to reflect that I have other paths at a similar level, not just this one."

## Closing move
Always end by restating enthusiasm for the actual work, separate from the number:

"None of this changes how much I want to build the QA agent side of this with you, I just want the offer to reflect what I'd bring on day one rather than where the band started before we talked."
