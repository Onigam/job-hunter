# Negotiation Script — Technical Co-Founder / CTO at AI for Operations

---

## Context

This is a co-founder role, not an employee role. Negotiation here is about equity structure, governance rights, and cash runway management as much as salary. The framing shifts from "what am I worth in the market" to "what's a fair split given what each founder brings."

**Key facts to anchor on:**
- Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in the world (top 5 globally for cost of living)
- Senior Staff / Staff+ engineers in Switzerland earn CHF 160k-220k base
- A technical co-founder taking below-market salary is accepting implicit compensation via equity
- Any equity below 10% for a founding CTO joining pre-product is undervalued given the risk

---

## Opening Position

**If asked "what are you expecting?"**

> "Before I give you a number, I want to understand the structure. I'm coming from a senior staff role at Benevity where I was well compensated. Taking a co-founder path means accepting some salary discount in exchange for meaningful equity upside. That tradeoff makes sense to me if the equity is real. So let's talk about that first: what stage are you at, do you have a term sheet or is this pre-funding, and how are you thinking about the cap table?"

This surfaces the information you need before committing to a number. It also signals that you've done this before.

---

## Salary Anchoring

**If they push for a salary number early:**

> "My market rate at Benevity as Senior Staff in Geneva is in the CHF 180k-200k range. For a co-founder role I'd expect to sit below that to reflect equity upside, but not dramatically below. I'm thinking CHF 120k-150k as a base depending on runway and round size, with the difference between that and market rate reflected in meaningful co-founder equity."

**Anchor logic:**
- CHF 120k is the floor for someone with this profile in Geneva (below this is not credible)
- CHF 150k is reasonable for a seed-funded startup with real runway
- Anything above CHF 160k starts to look like a senior hire, not a co-founder (less equity expected)

---

## Equity Positioning

**Swiss early-stage norms:**
- Solo technical co-founder joining a non-technical founder: 30-50% is standard
- Technical co-founder with one other technical co-founder: 20-35%
- Joining at seed (post-first-funding): 10-20% depending on dilution already baked in
- Joining post-seed with product in market: 5-15%

**Opening ask (pre-funding / blank page):**

> "If we're building this from zero together and I'm the person writing the first line of code and making all the architecture calls, I need to be at 25-40% at minimum. That's not a negotiating position, that's what the market data shows for founding CTOs in this profile. I've seen too many technical co-founders accept 10-15% on the premise that something is 'just the beginning' and find themselves misaligned when the first institutional round comes in."

**If they counter low (below 15%):**

> "I understand why you'd frame it that way, but I want to be direct: below 15% on a blank-page co-founder role isn't a co-founder structure, it's an early employee structure with a better title. If that's the model, the economics shift and we'd be having a different conversation about base compensation. I'm not saying that's wrong, but let's be clear about which conversation we're having."

---

## Equity Structure Details to Negotiate

**Vesting (non-negotiable minimum):**
- 4-year vest, 1-year cliff is standard
- Push for a double-trigger acceleration clause: if the company is acquired AND you're let go, unvested shares vest immediately

**Anti-dilution:**
- Ask for broad-based weighted average anti-dilution, not full ratchet (more investor-friendly, more likely to be accepted)
- Confirm that founder shares are not diluted in the first institutional round below a pre-agreed threshold

**Good leaver / bad leaver terms:**
> "I want to make sure we define good leaver and bad leaver terms before any paperwork. In Swiss law these are not automatic. A bad leaver clause that lets the company claw back vested shares on termination is unacceptable. I need those to be earned and protected."

**IP assignment:**
- Standard: all IP built during the role belongs to the company
- Edge case: any pre-existing IP you bring in (tools, frameworks you built before) should be explicitly carved out or licensed in writing

---

## If They Have a Competing Offer or Try to Rush

**Pressure close ("we need an answer by Friday"):**

> "I take closing dates seriously, but I'm also not going to sign a co-founder agreement under time pressure. If this is the right fit for both of us, two or three extra days to read the documents carefully doesn't change anything. If that window matters more to you than getting this right, that's worth understanding before we go further."

**If you have another offer in process:**

> "I want to be transparent with you: I have another conversation active for a staff-level role. The compensation there is closer to CHF 180k with standard equity. I'm not using that as a threat, but it's context. A co-founder path needs to make sense relative to what I'm walking away from."

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

- Cap table with more than two founders pre-product (complexity you don't need)
- No term sheet and no clear funding plan within 6 months
- Equity offer framed as "options" with a strike price at or above current fair market value (this reduces real value)
- Vesting that starts before you sign (backloading is a warning sign)
- No mention of a shareholders' agreement (in Switzerland, verbal agreements on equity mean nothing)

---

## Post-Offer Checklist

1. Get the cap table before signing anything (current shareholders and amounts)
2. Have a Swiss employment lawyer review the shareholders' agreement (cost: CHF 800-1500, worth it)
3. Confirm IP assignment carve-outs in writing
4. Confirm double-trigger acceleration is in the shareholders' agreement, not just discussed verbally
5. Get a signed term sheet for the next funding round if one is in progress
