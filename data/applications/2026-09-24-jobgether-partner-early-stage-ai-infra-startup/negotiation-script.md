# Negotiation Script: Founding Engineer (path to CTO), early-stage AI infra startup (via Jobgether)

## Context
- First engineering hire at a startup that already has a product and revenue. You'd own backend, infra, frontend and the agent runtime, reporting straight to the founders, with a stated path to CTO.
- Posted band: **EUR 80k to 140k**, plus founding equity. Remote from Europe at first, then **relocation to San Francisco within the first year**, with relocation support and US visa sponsorship.
- That band is a European remote band. It's low for Geneva and very low for San Francisco. The real negotiation is about three things: where you land in the band now, what your pay becomes once you move to SF, and how much equity a founding engineer with a path to CTO gets.
- Jobgether is a matching platform, not a traditional retained recruiter. Once you're shortlisted, negotiate with the founders directly.

## Market anchors (2026, gross annual, estimates)

| Profile | Base | Equity |
|---|---|---|
| Senior/Staff engineer, Geneva or Zurich startup | CHF 150k to 200k | small |
| Founding engineer, funded Swiss startup | CHF 160k to 210k | 0.5% to 2% |
| Founding CTO / Head of Engineering, funded Swiss startup | CHF 180k to 230k | 1% to 3%+ |
| Founding engineer, seed / Series A, San Francisco | USD 170k to 230k | 0.5% to 2% |
| First engineer with a CTO track, SF, revenue-stage seed | USD 190k to 250k | 1% to 3% |

For reference, EUR 140k is roughly CHF 130k at current rates, which sits below a senior engineer salary in Geneva.

Sources to cite if challenged: Levels.fyi (Zurich, SF Bay Area), Carta and Pave startup compensation data for founding hires, Swissdevjobs, Glassdoor Geneva. These are estimates; check the latest data right before the call.

## Your target

**Phase 1, remote from Geneva (before the move)**
- Walk-away: EUR 130k base, and only if the equity and the SF reset below are strong and in writing
- Target: top of the band, EUR 140k, or its CHF equivalent if you're employed locally
- Opening anchor: CHF 175k (about EUR 185k), framed around 17 years of experience and Geneva cost of living

**Phase 2, after relocating to San Francisco**
- Target: USD 200k to 230k base, with the number (or a floor) written into the offer letter now, not "we'll figure it out"
- Opening anchor: USD 240k

**Equity**
- Target: 1% to 2% of fully diluted capital, depending on the latest valuation and round
- Push toward 2%+ if the CTO title comes with a timeline and there's no senior engineer above you

## Script: when they ask for your expectations

"You've posted 80 to 140k euros, and I'm guessing that's calibrated for a European remote hire. I'm in Geneva, with 17 years of experience and a platform I built from nothing through to acquisition. For the remote phase, I'm looking at about CHF 175k. What matters just as much to me is what the package looks like after the move to San Francisco, and the equity for a first engineer on a CTO track. Can we talk through all three together?"

If they push you to give a single number first:
"I'd rather not negotiate against myself. Where in the band did you have in mind for someone who'd own the whole technical side from day one?"

## Script: counter-offer if they come in low

If they offer, for example, EUR 110k and 0.5%:

"Thanks, and I mean it when I say I want this to work. Right now the package prices the role like a senior hire, though, and you're asking for the person who redesigns the agent runtime, sets engineering standards and grows into your CTO. A few ways we could close the gap: base at the top of the band now, equity closer to 1.5%, and an SF salary written into the offer so it resets automatically when I move. If cash is tight at this stage, I'm fine putting more weight on equity, but it needs to be meaningful."

If they say cash is capped:
"Understood, early cash is precious. Then let's move the difference into equity, add a guaranteed salary review at the SF move or after the next round, whichever comes first, and a signing bonus that covers the move itself."

## The San Francisco move: get this in writing

This is where most of the value is, and where "we'll sort it out later" costs you the most.
- **Salary reset:** US salary number, or a floor, written into the offer, triggered on relocation.
- **Visa route:** ask which one they're planning. H-1B is a lottery and can't be counted on. L-1A needs a year of employment with a related foreign entity, which fits a "move within a year" plan only if they have a European entity employing you. O-1 is possible given your founder and acquisition history, but it takes preparation. Ask who pays for lawyers and fees (it should be them), and what happens to your job if the visa is refused.
- **Relocation package:** flights, temporary housing (typically 1 to 3 months), shipping, and a tax gross-up on the relocation payments. Ask for a lump sum or a cap in writing.
- **Timing control:** "within the first year" should mean a mutually agreed date, not an instruction with two weeks' notice.
- **Cost of living:** SF rents and health insurance are high; make sure US benefits (medical, dental, 401k) are spelled out.

Script:
"I'm open to the San Francisco move, and I'd like us to agree the terms now while everything's calm. Specifically: the US base on arrival, which visa route you're planning and who covers it, the relocation budget, and what happens if the visa doesn't come through. Once that's written down, I can commit with confidence."

## Equity: questions to ask

Ask, in writing:
- What's the instrument? For a US company, usually ISOs or NSOs; ask about early exercise and whether an 83(b) election is possible, since at a low valuation that can matter a lot.
- What percentage of fully diluted capital does the grant represent, and how many shares are outstanding including the pool?
- Last valuation or SAFE cap, and the strike price (latest 409A).
- Vesting: standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff. As a founding hire, ask for a shorter cliff (6 months) or partial credit for the remote phase if it's employed through a different entity.
- Change of control: ask for double-trigger acceleration. You've been through the Alaya acquisition, so you know how much this matters.
- Exercise window after leaving: push for 5 to 10 years instead of 90 days.
- CTO path: ask for the criteria and timing in writing, and whether promotion comes with an equity refresh.
- Anti-dilution: employees rarely get it, but ask how the pool will be refreshed in the next round and roughly how much dilution to expect.

Script:
"Equity is a big part of why this role is interesting to me. To weigh it properly I need the percentage of fully diluted capital, the instrument, the strike and last valuation, and the vesting and change-of-control terms. I've been on the founder side of an acquisition, so I'd like double-trigger acceleration included, and a longer exercise window than 90 days."

Tax note: while you're resident in Geneva, Swiss rules apply, and options are generally taxed as income when exercised. Once you're a US tax resident, US rules apply, and ISOs, NSOs and 83(b) elections are handled very differently. Moving between the two regimes mid-vesting is exactly where people get caught out, so talk to a cross-border tax advisor before signing, and again before the move.

## Employment setup during the remote phase

A US startup can't simply pay you as a US employee while you live in Geneva. Ask how they'll employ you: an Employer of Record (Deel, Remote, Oyster), a European subsidiary, or a contractor agreement. As a contractor you'd carry Swiss social charges (AVS/AHV) and have no pension or accident coverage, so the gross should be around 15 to 20% higher to make up for it. An EOR is usually cleaner for you.

Script:
"How are you planning to employ me while I'm in Switzerland? If it's a contractor setup, the number needs to account for social charges and benefits I'd be covering myself."

## Geographic pushback (if they say "the band is for Europe")

"I get that the band is set for Europe. But Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in the world, and you're hiring someone who'll be in San Francisco within the year anyway. I'm not asking for SF pay from day one. I'm asking for the top of your band now, and a written SF number for when I get there."

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

"I want to be straight with you. I'm in late-stage conversations for a Principal Engineer role at an AI scaleup in Switzerland, at around CHF [X] base plus equity. This role interests me more because of the ownership and the CTO path, but I can't ignore a gap that large. If you can get to [target], I'll stop the other process and commit."

Only use this if there's a real process behind it, and never invent numbers. If you're also progressing with the Zurich agentic AI role (NWS Management AG), that can be the honest reference point.

## Priorities if you have to trade

1. SF salary and visa terms in writing (the biggest financial lever)
2. Equity percentage and double-trigger acceleration
3. Base during the remote phase
4. Relocation package and signing bonus
5. CTO title criteria and timeline

## Final check before signing
- Offer letter includes: base, SF salary or floor, equity % of fully diluted, vesting and cliff, acceleration, exercise window, visa sponsorship commitment, relocation budget
- Clear employing entity and contract type for the remote phase
- Cross-border tax advice obtained
- Clarity on what "path to CTO" means in practice
