# Negotiation Script: Senior Software Engineer, Agentic Risk & Posture (Check Point Software, Zurich)

## Context that shapes the numbers

Check Point (NASDAQ: CHKP) is a large, profitable public company, so this isn't a startup offer with options of uncertain value. The comp will follow a corporate band: base, an annual bonus target, and RSUs. The team itself is part of the former Lakera unit, which Check Point now runs as its AI security R&D center in Zurich. That gives you two things to point to: the parent company has an established budget, and the team is recruiting for a scarce skill set (AI agents plus security) in Switzerland's most expensive tech labour market.

The title is "Senior Software Engineer", which sits below your current Senior Staff level. The posting also asks for "experience in building up or leading teams while contributing to the core tech", and that's a lead or staff expectation. The **level** is the most important lever you have, more than base. Raise it before numbers come up.

Note: the LinkedIn page also showed Chicago, IL. Confirm early that the role is Zurich-based (or Swiss remote/hybrid) and on a Swiss contract.

## Zurich market reference points (CHF, annual, gross)

| Level | Base | Bonus | RSUs/yr | Total comp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Senior SWE, mid-size tech / scale-up | 140k to 170k | 0 to 10% | small | 150k to 190k |
| Senior SWE, large public tech (Check Point-type band) | 160k to 190k | 10 to 15% | 20k to 50k | 200k to 260k |
| Staff / Lead SWE, large public tech | 190k to 225k | 15% | 50k to 90k | 260k to 340k |
| Big Tech Zurich (Google L6 equivalent, upper reference) | 210k to 240k | 15 to 20% | 100k+ | 350k+ |

These are estimates pieced together from public salary data and recruiter ranges. Check them against Levels.fyi (Check Point, Zurich) and any Lakera alumni you can reach before the first comp conversation.

**Targets**
- Ideal: Staff/Lead level, base CHF 205k, total comp around CHF 290k
- Realistic target: base CHF 190k to 200k, total comp CHF 250k to 270k
- Walk-away floor: base CHF 170k with meaningful RSUs (below that, the level gap plus commute cost doesn't add up)

## Before numbers: levelling

Say this when the recruiter first describes the role:

> "Before we talk about comp, I'd like to talk about level. The posting asks for someone who has built and led a team while still writing core code. I did exactly that as co-founder and head of technology at Alaya through to the acquisition, and I've been Senior Staff at Benevity since. I'm fine with the title on the posting if the scope is right, but I'd want the offer levelled as staff or lead, not mid-senior. Is that possible within this req?"

If they say the level is fixed: "Understood. Then I'd expect us to land at the top of the senior band, and I'd like a written path to the next level within twelve months, tied to concrete criteria."

## Opening anchor

If asked for expectations, don't give a single number, and don't go first if you can avoid it:

> "I'd rather understand the band for this level first. What range has been approved for the role?"

If they push:

> "From what I've seen of Zurich comp for engineers working on AI security at public companies, and given that I'm coming in at Senior Staff, I'm looking at total compensation in the CHF 270k to 290k range, with base around CHF 200k to 210k. How that splits between base, bonus and RSUs is flexible."

## If they come in low

Don't react to one component. Ask for the full breakdown first:

> "Thanks. Could you walk me through it piece by piece: base, bonus target, RSU grant in shares and over how many years, and any sign-on? I'd like to see the full picture before I respond."

Then counter on scope, not personal need:

> "I appreciate it. Where I'm stuck is the gap between this number and the scope we discussed: designing the core detection features, scanning code and configs for vulnerable agents and MCP servers, and helping build the team. Given that, I was expecting closer to [anchor]. Is there room on base, or failing that, on the RSU grant or a sign-on bonus?"

If base is capped:
- Ask for a larger initial RSU grant (it's usually the easiest component for a public company to move)
- Ask for a sign-on bonus of CHF 20k to 40k to cover unvested Benevity compensation you'd be leaving
- Ask for a guaranteed review at 6 months, with level and comp both on the table

## Equity notes (public company RSUs)

Questions to ask before accepting:
- Grant size in **shares**, plus the reference price used to convert it. Work out the value at today's CHKP price and don't negotiate on a CHF figure they've picked.
- Vesting schedule and cliff. Confirm whether it's quarterly or annual after the cliff, and whether a three- or four-year schedule applies.
- Refresh grants: is there an annual refresh, and what does it look like for a strong performer?
- Swiss tax treatment: RSUs are taxed as income when they vest. Ask whether payroll withholds through sell-to-cover.
- Any retention pool attached to the Lakera unit, and whether new hires into the team can access it.

There's no early-stage dilution or liquidity risk here, so don't spend time on anti-dilution. The question is purely how much and how fast.

## Location and commute

You're in Geneva and the role is in Zurich, about 2h45 each way by train. Raise it early and frame it as a working arrangement, not a hardship:

> "I'm based in Geneva. I can be in Zurich regularly, and I'd suggest two days a week to start, more during onboarding. Can we agree on that hybrid setup in writing, and would Check Point cover a GA travelcard or the equivalent commute cost?"

A GA 2nd class costs around CHF 4,000 a year, a small ask for a company this size.

If anyone hints at a lower number because you're not in Zurich every day, push back:

> "The comp should reflect the role and the Zurich market it's hired in. Where I sleep doesn't change the work, and Geneva's cost of living is on par with Zurich's."

## Competing process template

Use only what's true at the time. The search is broad and active, so this framing is usually honest:

> "I'm in conversations with a few other companies working on AI infrastructure and agent tooling, some of them at later stages. Check Point is high on my list because of the problem this team is working on. That said, I need to make a decision within [timeframe], and I'd like to make sure the offer is competitive before then."

If there's a real written offer: give the total comp figure and the level, but not the company name unless that helps you.

## Closing

> "The problem this team is working on is the main reason I'm here. I see it every week from the other side. If we can get the level and the total package to where we've discussed, I'm ready to move quickly."

Get the final offer in writing (level, base, bonus target, RSU share count and vesting, sign-on, hybrid arrangement, commute cover) before giving notice anywhere.
