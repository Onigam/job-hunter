# Negotiation Script — Hatchet Founding Engineer

## Situation Summary

- Posted range: $160K–$200K USD base + 1.00% equity
- Company: YC W24, ~7 people, founded 2023, New York-based
- Role type: Founding Engineer, full-stack/infra, TypeScript/Python/Go
- Location: Magino is in Geneva, Switzerland. Switzerland is NOT listed in the remote eligibility list (US, NL, GB, DK, SE, DE, NO, FI, ES, FR, BE). This is the first thing to resolve before negotiating anything else.

---

## Step 0: Resolve the Location Question First

Switzerland is not listed as an eligible remote country. Raise this early, ideally before a first technical screen, so you don't invest time on both sides and hit a hard stop at offer stage.

Script for intro or screening call:

"Quick practical question before we go further: I'm based in Geneva, and I noticed Switzerland isn't in the listed remote locations. Is that a hard constraint on your end, or is it something you've worked around before for the right person?"

Two likely responses:
- "We can hire contractors internationally" — then you're negotiating as a contractor, which changes the math significantly (see below).
- "We're only set up for those listed countries" — then you either pass, or negotiate a move/relocation, which would need to be part of the offer.

Most YC-backed startups at this stage hire internationally via contractor agreements (Deel, Remote.com). That's fine but factor it in.

---

## Contractor vs. Employee — Why It Matters Here

If Hatchet hires you as a contractor (likely for a Geneva-based hire):

- No employer-side Swiss social contributions, meaning your gross is your gross
- You handle your own AVS/LPP contributions (~20% of income as a self-employed person in CH)
- No paid leave by default; you negotiate this explicitly or price it in
- Rule of thumb: multiply the employee equivalent salary by 1.25–1.35 to get a contractor rate that puts you at parity after self-employment overhead

So if the role is "worth" CHF 200K as an employee in Geneva, the equivalent contractor rate should be CHF 250K–270K, or ~$275K–$300K at current rates (~0.90 CHF/USD).

## Market Anchors — Geneva / Swiss Market

For a Staff-level founding engineer with 17 years of experience and a successful exit:

- Senior Staff Engineer (IC) at a mid-size company in Geneva: CHF 160K–200K
- Staff/Principal at a FAANG-adjacent or well-funded scale-up in CH: CHF 200K–240K
- Founding engineer at a startup with real equity: CHF 140K–170K base + meaningful equity (lower base is acceptable if equity is genuine)

Hatchet's $160K–$200K USD range translates to roughly CHF 144K–180K at current exchange rates. That's below median Geneva market for this profile on base alone. The equity (1%) needs to do real work to justify the gap.

---

## Equity Analysis — 1% at a YC W24 Seed Company

1% at a seed-stage YC company is a strong founding-engineer grant. Context:

- YC invests $500K at a $5M post-money cap (standard YC SAFE). Current valuation is likely between $5M and $20M at this stage.
- At a $10M current valuation, 1% = $100K today (illiquid).
- At a $100M Series A valuation (achievable if the product takes off), 1% pre-dilution = $1M, post-dilution likely 0.6–0.7% = $600K–$700K.
- At a $500M exit, your diluted stake (estimate 0.4–0.5%) = $2M–$2.5M.

Questions to ask before accepting equity terms:

1. What is the current valuation / last round price per share?
2. What is the total option pool size? What percentage of the company is the pool?
3. Standard 4-year vest with 1-year cliff — confirmed?
4. Is there an acceleration clause (single-trigger or double-trigger) on acquisition?
5. What is the exercise window if I leave? (Standard is 90 days; push for 5–10 years.)
6. What is the strike price on the options?

A founding engineer grant should include double-trigger acceleration and an extended exercise window. Push for both.

## Opening the Negotiation

### If they come in at the bottom of the range ($160K):

"I appreciate the offer. I want to work through this with you. The $160K base is at the low end of what I was expecting given the profile and the founding engineer scope. Based on my research on comparable roles and my own market rate in Geneva, I was targeting the $195K–$210K range on base. Is there room to move on that, or should we look at the total package together?"

Then wait. Don't fill the silence.

### If they come in mid-range ($180K):

"That's closer. I'll be honest with you — when I factor in the contractor overhead for operating out of Switzerland (no employer-side social contributions, which I cover myself), the effective number is closer to $145K in real terms. To get to parity with what I'd net as an employee at that level, I'd need to be in the $200K–$215K range. Does that make sense to you, and is there flexibility?"

### If they come in at the top ($200K):

"That's a solid number. Before I respond to the full package, I want to make sure I understand the equity terms clearly. Can we walk through the vesting schedule, current valuation, and exercise window? Those details matter quite a bit at this stage of the company."

## Counter-Offer Framework

If they push back on base, shift the conversation to total comp:

"If the base is capped at $X, I'd want to revisit the equity component. A larger grant or an accelerated vesting schedule on the first tranche would help make the total picture work. What's your flexibility there?"

Levers to negotiate beyond base salary:
- Equity: push from 1.00% toward 1.25%–1.50% given the founding scope
- Signing bonus to offset the first-year contractor setup costs (CHF 10K–15K is reasonable)
- Equipment budget (standard: $2K–$3K for a remote engineer)
- Coworking / home office stipend (CHF 300–500/month for a Geneva-based remote employee is standard)
- Vacation days explicit in the contract (aim for 25 days minimum)
- Extended exercise window on options (5 years post-departure, not 90 days)

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have other offers in play, use this framing:

"I want to be transparent with you: I'm in late-stage conversations with [company type, not necessarily the name] for a role at a similar level. Their package is at [X]. Hatchet is genuinely where I want to focus my energy, so I'd rather find a number we can both feel good about than have this come down to a comparison. What's the most you can do?"

If you don't have a competing offer, don't fabricate one. Use market data instead:

"I've done research on Geneva-market and remote-startup comps for a founding engineer with this background. The range I'm seeing is $190K–$220K for roles with this scope. I'm not trying to be difficult; I want to land somewhere we're both comfortable."

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

- Options granted as a fixed number of shares with no disclosure of total shares outstanding (you can't calculate percentage without this)
- No acceleration clause of any kind
- 90-day exercise window with no negotiation
- Vague language about "standard vesting" without written confirmation
- Delay in sending an offer letter ("we'll sort the paperwork once you start")

Any of these is a reason to slow down, ask the question explicitly, and wait for a written answer before signing anything.
