# Negotiation Script: Ayunis, Principal Engineer (Product, Platform, Infrastructure)

## The shape of this offer

Ayunis (formerly Locaboo, rebranded in July 2025) is a Munich GovTech company serving 800+ municipalities in the DACH region. The role is listed as Munich, hybrid, with EU workations. No salary is published, only "competitive salary", a €2,000 yearly learning budget and 30 vacation days. The listing doesn't mention equity.

Settle three structural questions before any number:

1. **Where Magino would be employed.** The role is Munich hybrid and Magino lives in Geneva. Options, in order of likelihood: relocation to Munich, a split arrangement (e.g. one week a month in Munich, the rest from Geneva), or full remote from Switzerland. Working remotely from Switzerland for a German employer raises Swiss social security and payroll questions (an employer of record or a Swiss contracting setup is usually needed). Ask early so it doesn't kill the deal at the offer stage.
2. **German.** The posting asks for German and English. Be upfront about Magino's actual level before the first call, since it affects both fit and your bargaining position.
3. **Funding and equity.** Ayunis has an existing revenue base from the Locaboo product, but its funding stage isn't public. Ask directly whether there's a VSOP and how big the pool is.

The honest gap: a strong Munich Principal offer around €140k is roughly CHF 130k, well below a Geneva Principal base. After German taxes and social contributions, €140k gross in Steuerklasse I nets something like €78k to 80k. A Geneva salary of CHF 200k nets roughly CHF 135k to 140k. Check both with a current calculator before the call, because this comparison is the core of any location argument.

## Scripts

### Opening (when they ask for expectations)

> "For a Principal role that owns product architecture, platform and infrastructure together, I'm looking at around €150k base, plus equity. That's based on what Principal roles at funded Munich scale-ups are paying right now, and it reflects that I'd be covering three areas that some companies split across two or three senior people. I'm flexible on the mix if equity is meaningful."

If they push for a number first and you'd rather not give it:

> "I'd rather understand the scope and the equity side first. What's the band you've set for this role?"

### If they come in low (e.g. €115k to 120k)

> "Thanks, I appreciate it. I'm still keen on the role, and the gap is real though. The posting combines product architecture, the agent runtime and production infrastructure, and that's a Principal scope. Can we get the base to €140k? If the budget is fixed on base, I'd want to look at a larger VSOP grant and a signing bonus to close the difference."

### Pushing back on a location discount (Scenario B)

> "I understand the band is built for Munich. Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, though, and Swiss social costs apply if I'm employed from here. I'm not asking for a Zurich big-tech salary. I'm asking for a number that makes the move from a Geneva Senior Staff role neutral, which is roughly CHF 170k. I'd travel to Munich as often as the team needs."

### Bringing up a competing process (use only if true)

> "I'm being transparent: I'm in late stages with another company, and their offer is around [X]. Ayunis is my preference because of the product and the open-source angle. If we can get closer on base or equity, I'd like to make this work."

Keep it calm and factual. Don't name the other company unless asked, and never invent an offer.

## Equity notes (German VSOP)

German startups almost always use virtual share options (VSOPs), not real shares. Points to check:

- **Pool size and grant as a percentage**, not just a number of "virtual shares". Ask for the fully diluted share count.
- **Vesting.** Standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Ask for credit if any probation period overlaps the cliff.
- **Payout only on exit.** VSOPs usually pay out only on an exit or sale. Ask what happens in a secondary round or a long private period, and whether there's any buyback.
- **Leaver clauses.** Good-leaver and bad-leaver definitions matter a lot. Push to keep vested options as a good leaver, and watch for clauses that let vested options decay over time after leaving.
- **Tax.** VSOP payouts are taxed as ordinary income in Germany at exit, not as capital gains. If Magino stays tax-resident in Switzerland, check how the payout would be taxed there too.
- **Dilution.** Ask whether the strike price or the percentage is protected in future rounds. Full anti-dilution is rare for employees, but asking signals seniority.

## Other levers if base is stuck

- Signing bonus to bridge the first-year gap (€10k to 20k)
- A guaranteed salary review after 6 or 12 months, with written criteria
- Larger learning budget (conference talks on the open-source product are good for both sides)
- Fixed number of remote weeks per year beyond the workation policy
- Title confirmation as Principal Engineer in the contract, with a clear path to Head of Engineering if the team grows
