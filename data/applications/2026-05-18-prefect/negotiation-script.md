# Negotiation Script — Staff Product Engineer @ Prefect

---

## Market Context

Prefect is a US-based remote-first company. Compensation will likely be quoted in USD. Key variables:

**US market rates for Staff Product Engineer (2026):**
- Total compensation range: $200,000-$280,000 USD
- Base salary typical: $160,000-$220,000 USD
- Equity: 0.1%-0.5% for staff-level at a growth-stage startup (depends on Prefect's current valuation and stage)

**Geneva purchasing power adjustment:**
- Cost of living in Geneva is among the top 3 globally
- CHF 1 = ~$1.10 (strong franc)
- Staff engineers at Swiss tech companies (local) typically earn CHF 180,000-240,000 base
- Remote USD pay requires a ~10-15% premium over "geographic discount" baseline to be CHF-equivalent after taxes and cost of living

**Prefect context:**
- Growth-stage startup (launched Horizon January 2026, actively hiring)
- Likely Series B or equivalent funded
- This is an early, high-leverage hire for Horizon specifically

---

## Opening Anchor

When asked about compensation expectations, the goal is to anchor high and justify it:

> "Based on staff-level market rates and my background building platforms that went from zero to acquisition, I'm targeting a base in the range of $200,000-$220,000 USD, plus meaningful equity. I'm remote from Geneva, so the Swiss cost of living context is real, but I'm not asking for a hardship premium, just market rate for the level. What's the range you've built for this role?"

Key moves:
- State a range that anchors at the top of market
- Acknowledge geography without making it the whole argument
- Immediately turn it back to them to get their number

---

## If They Come in Below Your Floor

If their offer is below $180,000 base:

> "I appreciate you putting a number on the table. The base is a bit lower than I'd expected for staff-level work, especially given that I'd be taking full end-to-end ownership of product slices from day one. The platform experience I'm bringing, founding Alaya, shipping it to acquisition, the full-stack work across multiple production systems, sits above senior and I'd want the comp to reflect that. Can we look at whether there's room to move the base to [target], or adjust the equity to bridge the gap?"

If they cite budget constraints:

> "I understand there are constraints. One thing that sometimes helps at this stage is front-loading some of the equity value or structuring a 6-month review with a committed step-up if I hit agreed milestones. Would that kind of structure be on the table?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

For a growth-stage startup like Prefect, equity is often worth more than the base adjustment. Push for specifics:

**Questions to ask:**
- "What's the current valuation and how is it calculated?" (post-money cap table)
- "What's the total option pool and what percentage would my grant represent?"
- "What's the vesting schedule?" (Standard: 4-year with 1-year cliff. Anything shorter cliff is a yellow flag.)
- "Is there any acceleration on acquisition or change of control?" (single-trigger vs double-trigger)
- "When were options last repriced, and what's the current strike price vs estimated 409A fair market value?"

**Target for Staff-level at growth-stage startup:**
- 0.15%-0.35% of the company
- 4-year vest, 1-year cliff
- Double-trigger acceleration on acquisition (standard, reasonable to ask for)

**If they offer 0.05% or less:**

> "For a staff-level hire who's taking ownership of a core product surface, I'd expect to be somewhere in the 0.15-0.25% range. That's the level where the equity upside is actually meaningful for the person taking on the responsibility. Is there room to revisit that number?"

---

## Geographic Pushback Script

If they use "geographic pricing" to discount the offer below market:

> "I understand remote companies sometimes tier by location. My concern with that approach here is practical: Geneva has one of the highest costs of living globally, the CHF is strong, and staff engineers at companies based here earn CHF 180,000-230,000 equivalent. If you price me at a discount to US-based staff engineers, you're not saving money, you're just making it harder to keep me long-term. I'd rather align on a rate that works for both of us from day one."

Do not apologize for where you live. This is a factual argument.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have or are expecting an offer from another company (or need to create urgency):

> "I want to be transparent with you: I'm in late-stage conversations with [Company / 'another company in the developer tools space']. Their offer is at [X / 'market rate for the level']. Prefect is where I want to be given the Horizon direction and the stage you're at, but I need the offer to be competitive to make this the clear choice. What can you do?"

Even without a hard competing offer, this framing is legitimate if you're actively interviewing elsewhere.

---

## What to Get in Writing Before Signing

- Total equity grant in shares (not just percentage)
- 409A valuation at grant date
- Cliff and vesting schedule
- Acceleration clause language (exact wording matters)
- Any performance review timelines with salary step-ups
- Remote work policy (hardware budget, home office stipend, time zone expectations)
- Health coverage or stipend for non-US employees

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

- Equity offered as "profit sharing" or "bonus" rather than options/RSUs: ask why
- No acceleration clause at all: means your unvested equity evaporates on acquisition
- Offer contingent on relocating to US "later": clarify in writing that the role is permanently remote
- Base well below $160,000 with no clear equity offset: the risk-reward doesn't work for a staff role
