# Negotiation Script — Staff Software Engineer, AI Infrastructure (Undisclosed via Jobgether)

---

## Market Rate Context

**Role level:** Staff Software Engineer (10+ years required; Magino has 17+)
**Location context:** Remote EU. Company is undisclosed; posting covers Germany, Austria, Netherlands, Switzerland, and other EU countries.

**Reference ranges (2026):**

| Market | Base salary (Staff SE) |
|--------|------------------------|
| Switzerland (Geneva) | CHF 185,000-230,000 |
| Germany (remote) | EUR 130,000-165,000 |
| Netherlands (remote) | EUR 125,000-160,000 |
| EU remote average | EUR 120,000-155,000 |

**Magino's justification for the top of range:**
- 17 years of experience vs. the 10-year minimum
- Cofounder-engineer background: built a platform acquired by a $1B+ unicorn
- Hands-on AI infrastructure experience at Staff level (LLMs, embeddings, semantic search in prod)
- Current role at Benevity (post-acquisition Staff IC) is already at or near this level

---

## Opening Anchor

When asked for salary expectations, anchor high and justify immediately:

> "Based on my current level at Benevity and comparable Staff-level roles in the EU market, I'm targeting CHF 200,000-220,000 base — or EUR equivalent around EUR 150,000-165,000 depending on how compensation is structured for my location. Given 17 years of experience including a founding-engineer background and hands-on AI infrastructure work, that's consistent with what I see at Staff+ level in the European market."

**Why anchor in CHF first:** Geneva cost of living is substantially higher than Germany or Netherlands. If they use a location-based pay model, Switzerland anchors the frame. If they pay a single EU rate, EUR 150,000+ is a defensible ask.

---

## If They Come In Below Target

**Scenario A — They offer EUR 110,000-125,000:**

> "I appreciate the offer, and I'm genuinely interested in the role. The range is a bit below what I was targeting based on market comparables at Staff level. I came in at EUR 150,000 as my anchor — is there flexibility to get closer to that? I'm also open to discussing equity or a performance review at six months if that helps bridge the gap."

**Scenario B — They offer EUR 125,000-140,000:**

> "That's closer. My target was EUR 150,000-165,000, so there's still a gap. I'd want to at least close to EUR 145,000 base if we can, and I'd also want to understand what equity or long-term incentive looks like before I make a final call."

**Do not accept the first offer.** A counter is expected at this level. Even a small move signals confidence.

---

## Equity Negotiation (if startup or scale-up)

Since the company is undisclosed, equity structure is unknown. Ask early:

> "Before we get deep into base compensation, can you tell me more about the equity component? I'd want to understand the instrument (options vs. RSUs), the current valuation or most recent round, vesting schedule, and cliff."

**Standard asks for a startup at this level:**
- 0.05%-0.15% for a Staff IC, depending on stage (seed vs. Series B+)
- 4-year vest, 1-year cliff
- Full acceleration on acquisition (at minimum, double-trigger)
- Anti-dilution protection on early tranches

**If they push back on equity percentage:**

> "I understand the range is constrained at this stage. If equity is below 0.1%, I'd need base compensation to fully carry the market rate — so I'd want to be at EUR 155,000-165,000 to account for the lower upside."

---

## Geneva Cost-of-Living Pushback

If the company uses geographic pay and tries to set Magino's rate based on a lower-cost EU country:

> "Geneva has one of the highest costs of living in Europe, comparable to Zurich or London. If the benchmark is the German market, I'd expect a 25-30% uplift to reflect that — which puts a EUR 130,000 German-market rate at around EUR 160,000-165,000 for a Switzerland-based employee. I'd want to land there before signing."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If there are other active processes, reference them without fabricating:

> "I have a few other processes in late stages right now at Staff level, one with a Geneva-based company and one with another EU remote role. I'm not using that as pressure, but I do want to make sure we can align on comp quickly if this is the right fit on both sides."

**If they ask for specifics:** decline politely. "I'd rather not share company names while those processes are still live." That's a normal and respected position.

---

## Closing the Negotiation

Once you're close on base, always confirm the full package before accepting:

- Vacation days (EU standard is 25-30 days minimum)
- Hardware budget
- Learning and development budget (the JD mentioned this)
- Health insurance coverage (if applicable for Switzerland)
- Contract type: employee vs. contractor (Switzerland has different treatment)

> "Before I sign, I'd want to confirm the full package in writing — base, equity details, benefits, and whether this would be an employment contract or a consulting arrangement."
