# Negotiation Script — BetterDB Founding Engineer

---

## Context

BetterDB is bootstrapped and profitable. Compensation is described as all-cash from company profits, no VC involved. This changes the equity conversation significantly — you're likely looking at founder-style equity or a profit-sharing arrangement, not standard startup options with a 409A valuation. The team is small, the role is founding-level, and they're bringing you in to own technical direction.

Switzerland market rates apply even if the role is remote, because your cost of living and tax basis are Geneva.

---

## Switzerland Market Anchors (2026)

**Founding Engineer / Staff+ level in Geneva:**
- Large tech (FAANG-adjacent): CHF 200,000-240,000 base
- Series A-B startups with significant equity: CHF 150,000-185,000 base + 0.5-2% equity
- Bootstrapped profitable companies: CHF 160,000-210,000 base (no equity discount justified — they're not offering upside in lieu of salary)
- Geneva cost-of-living premium: ~15-20% above Zurich rates

**Your anchor:** CHF 200,000 base. You have 17 years of experience, a successful founding engineer exit (acquired by a unicorn), and staff-level architecture credentials. This is not an inflated ask.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for your expectations, give a number, not a range.

> "I'm targeting CHF 200,000 base. That reflects my experience at this level — I've done this before as a founding engineer, not just as a senior IC. If there's a profit-sharing or equity component on top, I'd want to understand the structure before factoring it in."

Don't soften this. Don't say "somewhere around" or "I'm flexible." State it, then go quiet.

---

## If They Come In Below CHF 160,000

This is a signal they haven't benchmarked Geneva rates or are anchoring to a non-Swiss cost of living.

> "I appreciate the offer. The number is below what I'd expect for this role at this level, especially given the Geneva market. I've looked at what founding engineer positions pay here and I'm not finding much below CHF 180,000 for someone with this profile. Is there room to move closer to CHF 195,000?"

If they push back on the Geneva angle:

> "The role is remote, but my tax and cost basis are Geneva. I'd be taking on the same financial commitments regardless of where the company is incorporated. That's part of what I'm factoring in."

---

## If They Come In Between CHF 160,000-185,000

This is negotiable territory. Don't accept on the first call.

> "That's in the right direction. I'd want to land closer to CHF 195,000. If there's a profit-share or equity structure I haven't seen yet, I'm open to discussing how the total package fits together — but I'd want the cash component to reflect the market."

Ask for 24-48 hours to consider before responding. Use that window to reinforce your position in writing.

---

## Equity / Profit-Sharing Negotiation

Since BetterDB is bootstrapped with no VC:

- Standard startup options don't apply — there's no cap table in the traditional sense
- Ask directly: "What does ownership or profit participation look like for a founding engineer hire?"
- Reasonable asks for this stage: 1-3% equity stake, or a profit-sharing arrangement tied to EBITDA growth
- Push for anti-dilution protection or a right of first refusal if they raise in the future
- Vesting: 4-year vest, 1-year cliff is standard — don't accept less on the cliff

Script for the equity conversation:

> "I want to understand the ownership side before I can evaluate the total package. You're bootstrapped and profitable, which I see as a positive — you're not burning runway. But it also means there's no options structure I can model from a standard 409A. What does a founding engineer's stake typically look like in how you've thought about this?"

If they haven't figured this out yet:

> "I'd suggest we agree on a cash number now and come back to the ownership piece within 30 days. I'm comfortable with that timeline if the base reflects the market."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in process (or want to create urgency without fabricating):

> "I have a couple of conversations at a similar stage right now. Nothing signed, but I'd prefer to close this one first if we can get aligned on the number. What's your timeline for a decision?"

This creates urgency without lying. You don't need to name the other company.

---

## Geographic Pushback Defense

If they suggest a "remote discount" because you're not in their city:

> "I understand the logic, but a remote discount assumes my cost structure is lower. Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in the world — healthcare, taxes, housing. I'd actually argue the reverse: if I were based in the Bay Area, you'd be paying significantly more. The Geneva rate is a reasonable basis."

---

## What Not to Do

- Don't give a range. Ranges anchor to the bottom.
- Don't volunteer flexibility before they've given a number.
- Don't say "I'm open to whatever is fair" — it hands them the frame.
- Don't negotiate on the first call. Get the offer in writing, then respond.
- Don't accept same-day. Even if you're going to say yes, sleep on it and come back with at least one counter.
