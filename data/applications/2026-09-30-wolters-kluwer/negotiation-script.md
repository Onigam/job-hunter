# Negotiation Script: Wolters Kluwer, Lead/Staff Full Stack Engineer, AI Platform & Agents

## Context that shapes everything

**The posting is based in Hürth, Germany, and the team is remote-first.** You live in Geneva, so the whole negotiation depends on one question to settle early: which entity would employ you, and whose pay scale applies? There are three possibilities:

1. A German contract with German pay while you live in Switzerland. This is the worst outcome: German bands are well below Geneva's, and cross-border social security and tax get messy.
2. A Swiss contract through a Wolters Kluwer entity in Switzerland or an employer of record, with Swiss pay. This is the one to aim for.
3. A German contract with you relocating to the Cologne area. Only consider this if you actually want to move.

Ask the recruiter directly on the first screen, before any number comes up: "Can this role be hired in Switzerland, and if so, is it benchmarked to Swiss or German pay?"

**This is a large listed company, not a startup.** Wolters Kluwer trades on Euronext Amsterdam. You won't get options, cliffs or anti-dilution terms. Pay is usually base plus an annual bonus with a target percentage, and at senior grades sometimes a long-term incentive plan (performance shares). What you can move: **grade/level**, base, bonus target, whether LTIP applies, sign-on, and the remote and travel setup.

**"Lead/Staff" is a range, and the posting lists the career level as "Management."** Push to be graded at the Lead end. With 17+ years, a cofounder exit and five years as Senior Staff, you have a real case.

No salary is published.

## Market reference points (estimates, verify before the call)

These are rough working numbers from public compensation data. Check them on Levels.fyi, Glassdoor and kununu (Wolters Kluwer Germany), and against Swiss salary surveys for Staff-level engineers, before saying any number aloud.

| Reference | Base | Bonus | Long-term incentive | Total per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wolters Kluwer Germany, Staff/Lead engineer | ~€105k to 140k | ~10% to 15% | maybe, at senior grades | ~€115k to 165k |
| Geneva, Staff/Senior Staff at a scale-up or corporate | ~CHF 170k to 220k | varies | small | ~CHF 180k to 240k |
| Zurich, Staff at large tech/corporate (non-FAANG) | ~CHF 180k to 230k | ~10% to 20% | varies | ~CHF 200k to 260k |
| Swiss-based remote hire at a large corporate | ~CHF 165k to 210k | ~10% to 15% | varies | ~CHF 180k to 240k |

Takeaway: a German-scale offer converted into CHF lands around CHF 100k to 150k, which is a large cut from the Geneva market. The gap is structural, so the fix is to get the role benchmarked to Swiss pay, not to haggle within a German band.

## Your numbers

**Walk-away floor:** write it down before the first call. As a starting point, something around CHF 170k base on a Swiss contract is a reasonable place to begin thinking, but it's your call.

**Target (Swiss contract, Lead level):** base around CHF 205k, 15% bonus target, eligibility for the LTIP if the grade allows it, and a travel budget for regular trips to Hürth. Total cash around CHF 235k.

**Opening anchor:** CHF 210k to 220k base, stated as a Swiss-market figure for a Lead engineer.

**If it has to be a German contract:** don't accept it at German pay without also relocating. If you're open to relocating, the anchor becomes around €150k base plus bonus, and you'd negotiate relocation support as well.

## Scripts

### First screen: settling where you'd be employed

"Before we get into numbers, one practical question. I'm based in Geneva and the posting says the team is remote-first. Can Wolters Kluwer employ me in Switzerland, either through a local entity or an employer of record? That decides which market the compensation should be benchmarked against."

### When they ask for your expectations

"For a Lead-level role hired in Switzerland, I'm expecting a base in the range of CHF 210k to 220k, plus the standard bonus. That's in line with Staff and Lead roles in the Geneva and Zurich markets. I'm flexible on the mix, and I'd also like to understand whether the long-term incentive plan applies at this grade."

If they push back that the role is budgeted in euros:

"I understand the budget was set for Germany. The cost of living in Geneva is far higher than in Cologne, and the Swiss market for this level is priced accordingly. If the team is remote-first, I'd expect the pay to follow where the person is employed. Is there flexibility on that, or should we look at the grade instead?"

### Pushing on level

"The posting covers Lead and Staff. My background is cofounding a platform and taking it to acquisition, then five years as Senior Staff doing cross-team architecture and AI adoption without a formal mandate. I'd like to be considered at the Lead end, and I'd like to understand what separates the two levels in scope."

### If the offer comes in low

"Thanks, I'd like to make this work. The base is below what I see for this level in Switzerland. I'd be ready to accept at CHF 205k base with the bonus as offered, and a travel budget for time in Hürth. Is that something you can take back?"

Then stop talking. Let them answer.

### Geographic discount pushback

"I know remote hiring sometimes comes with location adjustments. In this case the adjustment would go the other way; Geneva is one of the most expensive places in Europe to live. I'm not asking for a premium, just to be paid at market for where I'm based."

### Competing offer template (use only if true)

"I want to be transparent. I'm in late-stage conversations with another company, and their offer is at CHF [X] base plus [bonus/equity]. Wolters Kluwer is my preference because of UpToDate Expert AI and the platform scope. If you can get closer to [number], I'd be ready to commit this week."

Never invent a competing offer. If you don't have one, say you're "in other conversations" and leave it at that.

## Non-salary items worth negotiating

**LTIP eligibility.** Ask whether performance shares apply at the offered grade. At a listed company this is often the biggest lever after base.

**Travel.** In-person interviews are required, and there'll be on-site time later. Ask for a written travel budget and a clear expectation of how often you'd be in Hürth.

**Title.** "Lead" rather than "Staff" if the scope is the same. It matters for the next move.

**Vacation.** Swiss standard is 25 days; ask for 25 or more if it's a Swiss contract.

**Learning budget and conferences.** A cheap ask for them and useful for you.

**Start date.** Leave yourself enough runway to finish anything in progress.

## Before you sign

Get the employing entity, contract jurisdiction, social security arrangement and tax treatment in writing. If it's a cross-border setup, have a Swiss fiduciary check it before you accept, since fixing a bad payroll setup later is painful.
