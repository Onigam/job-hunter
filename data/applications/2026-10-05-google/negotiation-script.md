# Negotiation Script: Google, Staff Software Engineer, Generative AI, Commerce (Zürich)

**Context.** Staff Software Engineer is Google's L7. The role sits in Zürich, full-time and onsite or hybrid, and no salary is published. Magino lives in Geneva, so this is a commute or a partial move rather than a relocation. Every figure below is an estimate drawn from public comp data (Levels.fyi Zürich entries, Swiss salary surveys, recruiter-reported ranges) and none of it has been verified with Google. Check the latest Levels.fyi Zürich L6/L7 data points before the recruiter call.

The biggest lever here isn't base salary, it's **level**. Google's bands are fairly rigid within a level, but the gap between L6 and L7 is large. Given the ML infrastructure gap in the posting, there's a real risk of being down-leveled to L6 (Senior). Most of the negotiation energy should go into protecting L7.

## 2. Opening anchor

Google recruiters will ask for current or expected comp early. Avoid giving a number before the level is confirmed.

> "I'd rather not anchor on a number before we've settled the level, because that drives everything else at Google. Once the hiring committee has a view, I'm happy to talk numbers. What I can say is that I'm interviewing for Staff scope, and I'd expect to be in the L7 band for Zürich."

If they insist:

> "For L7 in Zürich, I'm looking at total comp around CHF 500k, with base around 265k. That reflects seventeen years of experience, having been the technical cofounder of a platform through to an acquisition by a $1B+ company, and Staff-level cross-team scope at Benevity."

Why this holds: it sits in the upper half of the estimated L7 band without being out of it, and it's tied to concrete proof rather than a wish.

---

## 3. If they come in low

### Scenario A: L7 offer, but at the bottom of the band

> "Thanks, I'm glad we're aligned on L7. The base is close, but the equity is well below what I'm seeing for Staff in Zürich. Is there room on the initial GSU grant? A sign-on bonus would help too, especially to bridge the first year before stock vests in volume."

Push on, in this order: **initial GSU grant** (most flexible), **sign-on bonus** (cash, one-off, easier for them to approve), then **base** (least flexible within a band).

### Scenario B: Down-leveled to L6

> "I appreciate the offer and the team is the right one. I want to be honest that L6 doesn't match the scope I've been working at. At Benevity I was Senior Staff, driving architecture across teams, and before that I built and led the whole platform at Alaya. Could we look at whether the committee would reconsider L7, or set clear criteria and a timeline for an L7 promo case?"

If L6 holds, get the top of the L6 band, a larger sign-on, and a **written** expectation about the promo timeline from the hiring manager. Be clear with yourself: an L6 offer at Google may still beat most Swiss alternatives on cash, but think about whether the scope is worth it.

---

## 4. Equity notes (public company, GSUs)

This isn't startup equity, so there's no cliff-and-dilution conversation and no anti-dilution clause to negotiate. What matters:

- **Vesting schedule.** Google has changed its GSU vesting more than once in recent years (front-loaded, then monthly with no cliff). Ask the recruiter to spell out the exact schedule for this grant, with year-by-year amounts in CHF.
- **Refreshers.** Annual refresh grants depend on performance and level. Ask what a typical refresher looks like at L7 so you can model years 2 to 4.
- **Year-one dip.** If vesting is back-loaded, push for a larger sign-on to smooth year one.
- **Currency.** GSUs are denominated in USD. A weaker dollar against the franc cuts real comp, so model it at the current rate and at 5% to 10% worse.
- **Swiss tax.** RSUs are taxed as income at vest in Switzerland. Check the tax in your canton of residence (Geneva vs Zürich canton) before signing, because it changes the net number noticeably.

---

## 5. Geography pushback

There's no geographic discount to fight here, since Zürich is Google's top Swiss band. The questions are practical:

> "I'm based in Geneva. Is there flexibility on how many days a week in the Zürich office? If the team expects three or more, I'd want to talk about relocation support or a commuting allowance."

Geneva to Zürich is around 2h45 by train each way. A GA travelcard runs around CHF 4,000 a year and is worth asking for if regular commuting is expected. If moving to Zürich makes more sense, ask for the standard relocation package (temporary housing, moving costs, agent fees).

---

## 6. Competing offer template

Use only with a real offer, or real advanced-stage conversations. Never invent numbers.

> "I want to be transparent. I'm also in late-stage conversations with [Company] for a [role], and their package comes to around CHF [X] in total comp, with [scope detail, e.g. founding-level ownership of the platform]. Google is my first choice for the work itself, the agentic commerce problems in particular. If we can get the initial grant to around [Y], I'm ready to sign this week."

At Google, competing offers mostly move the **initial GSU grant** and **sign-on**, and only in documented form. The recruiter will probably ask for the offer letter.

---

## 7. Final checklist before accepting

- Level confirmed in writing (L7 vs L6)
- Base, bonus target, GSU grant and vesting schedule spelled out year by year
- Sign-on bonus amount and any clawback terms (usually prorated if you leave within 12 months)
- Office expectations for Zürich and commuting or relocation support
- Start date that leaves at least two weeks between roles
