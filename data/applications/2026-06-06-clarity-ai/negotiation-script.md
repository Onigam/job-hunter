# Negotiation Script — Clarity AI Senior MLE Staff Engineer

---

## Market Context

Clarity AI is a Madrid-headquartered company with remote hiring across CET ±2h. As a Swiss-based candidate, expect a tension between their Spanish/European comp benchmarks and what Geneva actually costs.

**Swiss market anchor (Senior Staff MLE, 2026):**
- Senior Staff / Principal ML Engineer in Switzerland: CHF 175,000-215,000 base
- Geneva specifically: add 10-15% cost-of-living premium over Zurich
- Senior Staff platform/infrastructure roles at AI scaleups in Switzerland: CHF 180,000-200,000 is a realistic midpoint

**Spanish/EU market anchor (for comparison):**
- Same role in Madrid: EUR 95,000-140,000 base
- Pan-European remote (anchored in Western Europe): EUR 120,000-160,000

**The gap is real.** A company anchoring on their Madrid comp band will offer significantly below what Geneva requires. That pushback needs to be anticipated and scripted.

---

## Opening Anchor

**When they ask "what are your salary expectations?"**

Don't anchor first if you can avoid it. Try:

> "I'd rather understand the full package before naming a number. Can you share the comp band for this level?"

If they push for a number:

> "Based on senior staff ML engineering roles in Switzerland and the scope of what you're describing, I'm looking at CHF 185,000-200,000 base, plus equity. I'm flexible on structure if the total package makes sense."

Why CHF 185k as the floor: it's the realistic lower bound for a Senior Staff role in Geneva once you account for Swiss taxes and cost of living. Opening at 185k leaves room to land at 175k if needed without feeling like you gave up too much.

---

## Counter-offer Script

**Scenario A: They come in at EUR 110,000-130,000 (Spanish/pan-EU range)**

> "I appreciate the offer. I want to make this work, but I need to flag a structural issue: I'm based in Geneva, where the cost of living is roughly 40% higher than Madrid, and Swiss income taxes operate differently than Spanish ones. EUR 120k translates to less take-home here than it would to a Madrid-based hire. To be at the same net position, I'd need something closer to CHF 170,000-180,000. Is there flexibility in the band for candidates in high-cost locations?"

If they say the band is fixed:

> "Understood. Is there room to make up the gap on equity, signing bonus, or an annual review trigger at six months rather than twelve? I want to find a path here."

**Scenario B: They come in at CHF 140,000-160,000 (partial recognition of Swiss rates)**

> "That's closer. My target is CHF 185,000 and I think that's fair given the scope: this role is building foundational GenAI platform infrastructure, not operating existing systems. The Staff+ designation typically reflects that. Is there room to move to 175,000 as a firm number with a performance review at six months?"

---

## Equity Notes

Clarity AI is backed by BlackRock, SoftBank, and Deutsche Börse. It's a late-growth-stage company (300+ employees), which means:

- Equity is likely RSUs or stock options, not common stock with a $0.01 strike
- At this stage, liquidity depends on IPO or acquisition; neither is imminent but both are plausible
- The valuation has likely been through multiple rounds, so early-employee dilution is already baked in

**Questions to ask:**
- "What's the current 409A valuation / fair market value per share?"
- "What's the vesting schedule? Is there a cliff?"
- "What happens to unvested equity in an acquisition?"
- "Has the company done any secondary sales or tender offers for employees?"

**Target equity for Senior Staff:**
- 0.05%-0.15% fully diluted is realistic at this stage for a senior individual contributor
- Below 0.03% at a 300-person company is thin; worth flagging

**Script if equity feels light:**

> "The base works, but the equity feels light relative to the scope and the stage. If I'm building foundational platform infrastructure for the next two years, I'd expect to be a bit more aligned with the long-term outcome. Is there room to increase the grant, or to add an acceleration clause if there's a change of control?"

---

## Geographic Pushback Script

If the recruiter uses geography as a discount lever:

> "I understand the company is based in Madrid and that affects the comp bands you're used to working with. But remote hiring cuts both ways: you get access to talent that wouldn't relocate, and I'm operating from a Swiss cost base. I'm not asking for a Swiss premium on top of your band; I'm asking to be paid at the market rate for the actual work I'd be doing, which is senior staff-level infrastructure engineering. That rate is the same whether I sit in Madrid or Geneva."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or want to create urgency):

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company in the AI infrastructure space. I'd prefer Clarity AI because the problem domain is more interesting to me, but I need to make a decision within the next two weeks. Can we move the conversation forward?"

If asked what the other offer is:

> "I'd rather not get into specifics on their end, but it's a Staff-level role in a comparable domain. The total comp is higher, which is why I want to resolve this now rather than let it drift."

---

## Non-negotiables to Protect

- Remote status from Geneva: if they ask you to relocate to Madrid, the answer is no (unless the package accounts for full relocation + cost of living differential + housing support)
- Timezone flexibility: CET ±2h is fine; avoid any role that requires regular US East Coast hours
- Equity cliff: standard 1-year cliff is fine; anything longer than 1 year is a red flag
