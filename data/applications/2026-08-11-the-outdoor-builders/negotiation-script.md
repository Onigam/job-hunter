# Negotiation Script: The Outdoor Builders (Founding Full Stack & AI Engineer)

## Context
Early-stage startup (San Francisco HQ, remote-friendly), founding-engineer role. Product is already live with 100+ models and 4 AI pipelines in production; company is pushing toward ~12M ARR within a year and starting the B2B SaaS build. Likely comp structure: below-market base plus meaningful equity, since this is a founding-team slot, not a backfill. No published salary range found publicly, so anchor on market data and adjust once they name a number.

## Market anchors (Switzerland-adjusted, remote-from-Geneva)
- US-based founding/early engineer roles at seed to Series A typically land $140k-$180k base plus 0.5%-2% equity, depending on how "founding" the title really is (pre-seed vs. funded).
- Geneva cost of living runs 25%-40% above the US national average and roughly on par with or above SF, so a straight US base number needs the same geographic weight an SF hire would get, not a discount for remote-from-Europe.
- Swiss senior/staff engineer base pay (permanent contract, Geneva market) sits around CHF 140k-180k. As a remote contractor or on a US payroll structure paid in USD, target the equivalent: roughly $150k-$190k base.
- Reference point: my last staff-level comp at Benevity, an acquiring company at meaningful scale, sets a real floor. This isn't a first job, it's a lateral move into more risk, so total comp should not go down from a stable staff-engineer base without equity upside that clearly compensates for it.

## Opening anchor
"Given the founding-engineer scope, I'd want to talk base in the $170k-$190k range plus equity, calibrated to how early this really is. I know that's likely above what founding roles at this stage usually open with, but I'm coming in at staff level with a company I built and sold as the closest comparable, so I'd rather start the conversation there and work from real numbers than anchor low and try to catch up later."

## If they come in low (e.g., $110k-$130k base, heavy equity weighting)
"I get why the base might be lighter at this stage, equity is supposed to do a lot of the work here. Can you walk me through the strike price, the fully diluted share count, and where the last valuation landed? If the equity math genuinely gets me to a number I'd take on the risk for, I'm open to a lower base than my anchor. If it doesn't, I'd need the base closer to $150k to make the move work financially, especially relocating from or working remotely at Geneva cost of living."

## Equity negotiation checklist
- Ask for the actual share count and fully diluted percentage, not just a headline equity number, before agreeing to anything.
- Confirm strike price and 409A valuation (or Swiss equivalent) so the real cost of exercising is clear upfront.
- Standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff is the floor to accept; push for monthly vesting after the cliff instead of quarterly.
- Ask about acceleration on acquisition. Given the Alaya-to-Benevity path, this is a real scenario worth planning for, not a hypothetical.
- If early enough (pre-Series A), ask whether early exercise with an 83(b) election is available, and whether the company covers legal review of the grant.
- Confirm anti-dilution protection language, or at minimum ask how future rounds are expected to affect the founding-engineer pool.

## Geographic discount pushback
"I want to flag that Geneva isn't a discount location. Cost of living here tracks close to SF, and I'm not asking for SF-plus, just parity with what the role would pay a US-based hire at the same level. If there's a reason the offer assumes a lower cost base for Geneva specifically, I'd like to understand it, because the data I'm working from doesn't support that."

## Competing offer as a lever (use only if real, keep vague otherwise)
"I'm in later-stage conversations with another team at a similar comp band, so I do have a timeline I'm working against. I'd rather make this work if the scope and comp line up, this role is genuinely the more interesting build."

## Closing framework
- Don't accept verbally on the first call. Ask for the offer in writing with base, equity grant size, vesting schedule, and any relocation or remote-work stipend spelled out.
- Take 48-72 hours minimum before responding, even if the number is good.
- If the gap after one counter is small (under 10%), close it rather than re-anchoring a third time, since founding-team goodwill matters more than squeezing the last few thousand.
