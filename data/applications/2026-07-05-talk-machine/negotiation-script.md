# Negotiation Script: Talk Machine (Founding Engineer)

## Context

Talk Machine is a seed-stage, roughly seven to fifteen person team, London-based with a remote-friendly setup that already spans Europe and Dubai. A founding-engineer offer this early is going to skew toward equity and below-market cash rather than a competitive base, and the negotiation has to treat those as two separate conversations: what the cash needs to be to make Geneva workable, and what the equity actually needs to look like given the risk of joining pre-Series A.

## Market anchors

- Founding engineer cash comp at UK/EU seed startups typically lands GBP 70k-100k base equivalent, sometimes lower when the pitch leans heavily on equity upside. For someone coming in as a Senior Staff-level hire rather than a junior founding engineer, the right anchor is closer to CHF 130k-150k base if based in Geneva, or the GBP equivalent near the top of the London range if remote comp is banded to London.
- Geneva cost of living doesn't support a discounted "remote from a cheap location" framing. Rent, health insurance, and general costs put Geneva at or above London, so any attempt to band comp lower because it's remote should get pushed back on directly.
- Equity at seed stage for an early, senior technical hire (not the first hire, but early enough to matter) commonly runs 0.5%-2%, with the number depending heavily on how much has already been allocated to the founders and any earlier hires. Given the small headcount already in place, ask directly where in the cap table this hire sits before anchoring a number.
- Because this is a tiny team building a live consumer product already in the App Store, there's a credible argument that the risk profile is a notch better than pure pre-launch seed, which supports pushing the cash side harder than a typical founding-engineer offer would allow.

## Opening anchor

"I want to be upfront that I'm evaluating this the way I'd evaluate any founding-level role, cash plus equity plus scope together, not just the base number. Given the seniority I'd be coming in at, I'd want a base near CHF 140k if I'm staying in Geneva, and I'd like to understand the equity band you have in mind for a hire at this level before we get further into it. Can you walk me through how you've structured comp for the team so far, and where headcount sits on the cap table today?"

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

"I appreciate the offer, and I get that seed-stage cash is tight, most founding-engineer roles are. But the number here is meaningfully below what I'd need to make the move work from Geneva, and I want to be direct rather than let that sit unspoken. My expectation going in was closer to CHF 130k-140k base. If cash is genuinely capped at this stage, I'm open to that gap being closed on the equity side, but I'd want that traded explicitly rather than just accepted as a discount."

If they cite remote/location as the reason for a lower number: "I'd push back gently there. Geneva isn't a cheaper market than London, if anything it runs higher on housing and health costs, so I don't think location is the right lever to pull. If the constraint is genuinely about seed-stage runway, let's talk about how equity or a comp review at the next raise can bridge it instead."

## Equity negotiation

- Ask for the fully diluted share count, the most recent valuation or 409A-equivalent, and how much of the pool has already been allocated across the existing seven-to-fifteen person team before agreeing to a percentage.
- Standard four-year vest with a one-year cliff is the baseline; for an early senior hire, it's reasonable to ask whether a shorter cliff or monthly vesting after year one is on the table, since seed-stage teams sometimes have more flexibility here than they lead with.
- Given the company is UK-incorporated, ask specifically about EMI option scheme eligibility, since EMI options carry meaningful UK tax advantages that a standard option grant wouldn't, and confirm how that translates for someone based in Switzerland rather than the UK.
- Ask what happens to the equity number at the next raise: is there a refresh mechanism tied to the Series A, or is the initial grant meant to be the whole story until a future negotiation. Don't leave that as an assumption.

## Geographic discount pushback

If location comes up as a reason to lower the offer: "I've looked at the actual numbers on this. Geneva rent and healthcare put it in the same range as London, sometimes above it depending on the neighborhood, so I'm not asking for a discount to be waived, I'm asking that Geneva not get treated as a lower-cost market it isn't. I'm happy to work London hours or overlap with Dubai, that's not the issue."

## Competing offer leverage template

Use only if there's an actual process in motion elsewhere, even an early one: "I want to flag that I'm also further along in a conversation with another team at a comparable stage, so I'd like to move quickly here if there's real interest on your side. I'd rather decide based on where this lands than let it drag."

If there's no competing offer, don't invent one. Lean on scope instead: "Given this is a founding-engineer role and I'd be one of the more senior technical people in the building, I want the offer to reflect that from day one rather than treating it as a standard early hire and revisiting it after the first raise."
