# Negotiation Script: Staff Platform Engineer, Infrastructure, Wispr Flow (San Francisco)

## Context to keep in mind

This is an onsite San Francisco role at a company that went from $2M to $50M ARR in twelve months, posted at $270K-$350K base plus equity with employee-friendly terms (extended exercise window). The posted range is already a real US Staff-level number, not a placeholder, so the negotiation here is less about proving the number is too low and more about where in that band to land, how much equity on top of it, and whether relocation from Geneva gets any support. The base question to resolve early, same as always with a US onsite role: what's the actual relocation and visa picture, since a $300K SF offer with no relocation help and a slow visa process is a materially different deal than the same number with both handled.

## Switzerland market anchor (context, not the primary lever)

A Staff-level platform or infrastructure engineer in Geneva or the broader Swiss market would typically anchor around CHF 170k-210k base at a well-funded scale-up, sometimes higher at a company with Wispr's growth trajectory. That's useful context but not the number to import here, US comp structures for Staff-level infrastructure roles at fast-growing, well-funded startups routinely clear $270K-$350K base alone, before equity, which is already above Swiss-market cash comp once currency and cost of living are accounted for. The point of knowing the Swiss anchor isn't to demand it on top of the US number, it's to have a credible floor if the conversation ever drifts toward "we can go lower for someone relocating."

## Opening anchor

Let them name a number first if possible. If asked directly:

"Given the scope here, owning platform components end to end and going after production problems that don't have an obvious owner yet, I'd anchor toward the top of your posted range, around $330K-$350K base, plus an equity grant that reflects Staff-level ownership rather than a standard IC grant. I'm open on the exact split between cash and equity if the total package reflects that."

## If they come in low on cash

"I get that the range has real constraints on your end. If base can't move past the low-to-mid part of the posted band, I'd want that offset with a larger equity grant, or a defined comp review at nine to twelve months tied to the scope I've actually taken on by then, not a vague 'we'll revisit it.'"

Fallback if base truly can't move: ask for a bigger initial equity grant, confirm the extended exercise window applies in full, and get a relocation package that covers the actual cost of a Geneva-to-San-Francisco move, shipping, temporary housing, and a lead-time buffer for visa processing, spelled out in writing rather than assumed.

## Equity negotiation notes

Ask what stage the company priced its most recent round at and what the current strike price implies about dilution before the next one. Confirm it's a standard four-year vesting schedule with a one-year cliff, and ask directly whether the extended exercise window mentioned in the posting is capped at a fixed number of years post-departure or genuinely open-ended, since "extended" can mean very different things company to company. Given the ARR growth from $2M to $50M in a year, it's fair to ask how the option pool and strike price reflect that trajectory versus where they were priced a year ago, since a lot of the easy upside may already be priced in.

## Work authorization and relocation

Raise this before comp gets detailed, not after: "Before we get into numbers, I want to understand the visa and relocation process, since I'm currently in Geneva. What's the typical timeline for a role like this, and what does Wispr typically cover for an international relocation, flights, shipping, temporary housing, immigration legal fees?" If the timeline is long or sponsorship isn't straightforward, that has to factor into the overall calculus, not just get resolved as a footnote after the offer is signed.

## Geographic framing

"I'm coming from Geneva, which isn't a cheap market either, so this isn't about needing a premium to make the move work financially. It's about the offer reflecting that I'd be uprooting a life in one country to be onsite in San Francisco full time, which is a real cost beyond the number on the offer letter."

## Competing offer template

Use only if a real competing process exists. Never invent a number or company name.

"I'm also further along in conversations for another Staff-level infrastructure role. I'd rather build this at Wispr given the scale of the latency problem and the amount of ownership on offer, so if there's room to move on base or the equity grant, this is the point where that would help me decide."
