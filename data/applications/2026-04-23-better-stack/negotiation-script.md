# Negotiation Script — Better Stack, Founding Product Engineer

---

## Market Context

Better Stack lists two compensation bands publicly:
- Standard mode: $150K-$400K/year + equity
- Hardcore mode: $300K-$800K/year + equity

The wide bands are deliberate: they're calibrating to the individual, not the range. The right framing for this role is not "Staff Engineer at a Series B" — it's "person who will run a product line independently." That maps to a founding-team compensation model, not a leveled IC band.

**Switzerland / Geneva market benchmarks (2025-2026, CHF):**
- Senior Engineer (5-8 yrs): CHF 120K-160K base
- Staff / Principal Engineer: CHF 160K-210K base
- VP Engineering / Director of Engineering: CHF 200K-280K base
- Founding Engineer (startup, with equity): CHF 140K-180K base + meaningful equity

Better Stack's role is unusual: the scope is VP/Director-level autonomy with a founding-engineer execution mandate. That justifies anchoring above the Staff band.

**Your anchor target: CHF 220K-260K base (or USD equivalent at current rate: ~$240K-$285K)**, landing in the upper third of their "standard" range. The hardcore mode bracket exists as leverage if you want to signal maximal commitment.

---

## Opening Anchor Script

When they ask for your expectations or make a first offer, respond with:

> "Based on the scope you've described, where I'd be the founding engineer on a new product line with full ownership from concept to go-to-market, I'm thinking in the range of CHF 250,000 base. That's calibrated to Geneva's cost of living and to the fact that this is effectively a founding-team role, not a leveled IC position. I'm flexible on the structure if equity is meaningful, but I'd want the base to reflect the scope."

**Why this works:**
- It's specific and justified, not a number pulled from thin air.
- It frames the ask around the role scope, not personal need.
- It opens the equity conversation without ceding base.
- CHF framing signals you've done your homework on the Geneva market.

---

## If They Come In Below Your Anchor

If the initial offer is below CHF 200K (or ~$215K USD):

> "I appreciate the offer. The role scope is genuinely interesting and I'd like to make this work. That said, the number is below what I'd need to move from my current position, especially given Geneva cost of living and the fact that I'd be taking on a founding-level scope. Could you walk me through whether there's flexibility, either on base or on the equity component? I want to understand the full picture before I say no to something I'm genuinely interested in."

This keeps the door open without accepting a below-market number. Never counter immediately with a specific number when they're below your floor — get them to reveal whether there's any room first.

**Counter floor:** CHF 200K base is the minimum to seriously consider, given Geneva CoL and the seniority required. Below that, the equity would need to be exceptional to compensate.

---

## Equity Negotiation

Better Stack is $28.6M funded (2021-2026). They're Series A / growth stage. The founding product engineer role implies building a new product that could become a standalone product line.

**What to ask for:**
- Options or equity: expect 0.1%-0.5% for a senior/founding role at this stage. Push for 0.3%-0.5% given the founding nature.
- Vesting: standard 4-year with 1-year cliff. Anything shorter (3-year) is a red flag.
- Anti-dilution or acceleration: ask about single-trigger acceleration on acquisition. Given Magino's experience being acquired, this is a well-informed ask, not a negotiation tactic.
- Strike price: ask to see the current 409A/fair market value and the most recent preferred price. The gap between them tells you the current option upside.

**Equity ask script:**

> "On equity, I'd want to understand the current cap table structure and what percentage you're considering for this role. Given that this is a founding position on a new product line, I'd want to make sure the equity reflects that scope — I'm thinking in the 0.3%-0.5% range. I'm also curious about the acceleration provisions on acquisition, since I've been through an acquisition and I know how the timing can work against you if it's not structured correctly."

The acquisition reference signals sophistication without aggression.

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

Better Stack may default to USD-denominated offers and apply a geographic discount for European candidates. Push back clearly:

> "I'd note that Geneva is one of the more expensive cities in Europe, comparable to London or Zurich rather than, say, Berlin or Lisbon. If you're using a cost-of-living adjustment model, I'd ask that it be calibrated to Geneva specifically, not to a generic 'Europe' bracket."

Supporting data if needed: Geneva's cost of living index is consistently 20-30% higher than London on housing and overall expenses.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have or receive another offer during the process:

> "I want to be straightforward with you: I've been in conversations with [Company X] and I'm close to an offer there as well. I'd prefer Better Stack because of [specific reason], but I do have a timeline. Can we accelerate the process on your end?"

If no competing offer exists: don't fabricate one. The Better Stack founders will likely probe specifics. Instead, use time pressure honestly:

> "I'm actively in late-stage conversations elsewhere and expect to have clarity in the next two to three weeks. I want to give this process the attention it deserves, so I'd appreciate knowing your typical timeline."

---

## Non-Salary Items Worth Negotiating

- **Equipment budget**: CHF 3K-5K for hardware setup is standard in Swiss tech. Ask for it.
- **Conference / learning budget**: CHF 2K-3K/year is reasonable and often forgotten.
- **Location flexibility**: confirm Geneva is genuinely accepted, not just tolerated. Get it in writing.
- **Title**: "Founding Product Engineer" is fine externally, but ask whether "Engineering Director" or "Head of Product Engineering" is available internally for hierarchy purposes.
- **Start date**: standard in Switzerland is 3-month notice period. Better Stack should expect this. If they push for faster, negotiate a financial bridge or a defined overlap period.

---

## What to Never Do

- Don't accept the first number without at least one counter.
- Don't reveal your current salary before getting their offer range. If pressed: "I'd rather not anchor the conversation to my current package, since the scope here is quite different."
- Don't negotiate against yourself by proposing a range. Give a single number.
- Don't treat the equity as a bonus — it's part of the total compensation and should be evaluated at a realistic exit multiple, not the best-case scenario.
