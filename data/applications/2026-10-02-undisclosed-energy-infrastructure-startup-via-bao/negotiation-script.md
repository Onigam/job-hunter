# Negotiation Script: Founding Software Engineer (2nd engineer), undisclosed energy-infrastructure startup via BAO

## Context that shapes the numbers

The company automates the administrative, financial and regulatory work behind renewable energy projects in Europe. About 10 people, 900+ projects served, €4M just raised. You'd be the second software engineer. The role is in Paris, hybrid, on a French CDI, and the search is run by Yann Prigent at BAO, a dedicated headhunter.

**The posting publishes the band: €70k to €100k base, plus BSPCE.** That's a wide band, and it tells you two things. The top end is meant for someone senior, and with 17+ years and a founding-to-acquisition track record you should be negotiating at the top, not the middle. It also tells you base won't go much past €100k, so equity is where the real conversation is.

What actually moves here:

- **Base:** €95k to €100k is a fair ask. Above €100k usually means a different title.
- **Equity (BSPCE):** second engineer at a ~10-person company after a €4M round is still a founding-level grant. This is your main lever.
- **Title:** "Founding Engineer" is fine, but if the scope includes setting practices and hiring the next engineers, ask whether "Lead" or "Head of Engineering" is on the table now or at a defined milestone. Title shapes your next refresh.
- **Location:** Geneva to Paris hybrid is the first thing to settle. It changes everything below.

Decide before the first call whether a Paris package works for you at all. If your all-in floor is above what this band plus equity can reach, say so to the recruiter early; headhunters respect it and it saves four rounds.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough figures from public surveys and recruiter data. Re-check on levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Welcome to the Jungle, the Michael Page and Hays salary guides (France and Switzerland), swissdevjobs.ch, and Index Ventures' OptionPlan for equity norms.

**Paris (what they benchmark against)**

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Senior full-stack engineer, Paris seed startup | €65k to 85k |
| Founding / first engineers, Paris seed to Series A | €80k to 100k |
| Staff / Lead engineer, Paris scale-up | €90k to 120k |
| BSPCE, senior hire, seed stage | ~0.1% to 0.3% |
| BSPCE, engineer #1 or #2, seed stage | ~0.5% to 1.5% |

**Switzerland (your reference point)**

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Senior software engineer, Geneva | CHF 140k to 175k |
| Staff engineer, Geneva | CHF 180k to 230k |
| Founding engineer, Swiss seed startup | CHF 130k to 170k + 0.5% to 1.5% |

A straight conversion isn't fair either way. Paris costs less to live in, but French employee social contributions take a much bigger bite of gross than Swiss ones, and you'd be paying for TGV trips and possibly a pied-à-terre. Compare take-home after housing and travel, then add a realistic view of the equity.

**Target:** €100k base, 0.75% to 1% BSPCE, a written on-site pattern, and a travel budget if you stay in Geneva.
**Walk-away (decide before the call):** something like €90k base with under ~0.4% equity is a senior-hire package, not a founding one.

## Opening anchor

When the recruiter or a founder asks for your expectations, don't give a range that starts low. Something like:

> "Given the band you've published, I'm looking at the top of it, so €100k base. The bigger part for me is equity. As the second engineer, I'd expect a founding-level grant, somewhere around 1%, because I'd be shaping the architecture and hiring the next engineers, not just shipping tickets. I'm flexible on how the two balance out if the equity side is strong."

Why this works: you're anchoring inside their own published band, so it doesn't sound unrealistic, and you're moving the real negotiation to equity, where they have more room.

## If they come in low

**Base offered at €80k to €85k:**

> "Thanks, I appreciate it. I'll be straight with you: that's mid-band, and the scope you described is the senior end of it. I've built a platform from zero to acquisition and hired the first engineers there. I'd need to be at €100k, or close to it with more equity to make up the gap. Can we look at that?"

**Equity offered at 0.2% to 0.3%:**

> "That's in line with a senior hire at a later stage. For engineer number two at ten people, the grants I've seen are closer to 0.75% to 1.5%. What would it take to get to 1%? If the pool is tight right now, I'd consider a smaller initial grant with a defined top-up at the next round or at a milestone we agree on."

**They say the band is fixed:**

> "Understood on base. Then let's spend the flexibility on equity and on the setup: the on-site pattern, a travel budget, and a review at six months against agreed goals."

## Equity: what to ask about

BSPCE are French stock warrants. Ask for these in writing:

- **Percentage on a fully diluted basis**, not just a number of warrants. Ask for the total share count and the size of the pool.
- **Strike price** and the latest valuation (the €4M round sets it).
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Exercise window after leaving.** Many French plans give 90 days or less; ask for longer, or at least know the number.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (acquisition plus termination). You've been through an acquisition, so you know why this matters.
- **Refresh grants:** ask whether there's a policy for refresh grants after the next round. Full anti-dilution protection is almost never granted to employees on BSPCE, so a refresh commitment is the realistic substitute.
- **Liquidation preferences** on the investors' shares, so you know what the equity is actually worth in a modest exit.

**Swiss resident caveat.** BSPCE are generally reserved for employees of the French company, and the tax treatment depends on where you're resident when you exercise and sell. If you stay in Geneva and work for a French employer, payroll, social security and BSPCE taxation get complicated. Talk to a cross-border tax advisor before you sign, and ask the company how they'd employ you (French CDI with French payroll, or something else).

## Geneva and the Paris hybrid question

Settle this before money. A script:

> "I'm in Geneva, and Paris is just over three hours by TGV. I can be in the office regularly, say two days every week or a full week each month, and fully remote the rest. Would that work for how the team runs? If you need someone there three or four days a week, I'd rather know now."

If they want more presence than you'd like, negotiate a travel and accommodation budget as part of the package. Something like €500 to €1,000 a month is a reasonable ask for regular Geneva to Paris trips.

If they push the idea that a Geneva-based candidate should cost less: you're not asking for a Swiss salary, you're asking for the top of their own Paris band, and you're absorbing the travel. Say that plainly.

## Competing offer template

Use only what's true. If you have other processes running (Swiss platform roles, other founding roles), mention them honestly without inventing numbers:

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late stages with a couple of other companies, including roles in Switzerland where the base is noticeably higher. This one is my preference because of the mission and the stage. If we can get to €100k and around 1% equity, I'd be ready to commit and stop the other processes."

If you don't have competing offers, don't claim them. "I'm talking to a few other companies" is enough, and it's true for anyone actively searching.

## Order of the conversation

1. Recruiter call: confirm location pattern and give the anchor (top of band, ~1% equity).
2. Founder interviews: talk about scope, title and the roadmap. Don't negotiate there.
3. Offer stage: negotiate equity first, then base, then setup (travel, title, review date).
4. Get everything in writing, including the BSPCE plan rules, before signing.
5. Before signing, run the tax and employment setup past an advisor if you're staying in Geneva.
