# Negotiation Script — Head of Engineering at David AI

## Context

- **Stated range:** $200K - $280K (USD, San Francisco)
- **Stage:** Series B, $50M raised, FAANG + AI lab customers
- **Location:** San Francisco, CA (relocation or remote TBD)
- **Bonus qualifier:** Founder experience (you have it; most candidates don't)

David AI has already disclosed their range. This puts you in an unusual position: the ceiling is visible, but the floor is negotiable. Your goal is to land at or above $280K with meaningful equity, not just the base.

---

## Opening Anchor — First Number

If they ask what you're looking for before making an offer:

> "Based on the scope of the role and what I'm seeing for comparable positions in San Francisco at Series B companies, I'm targeting $290K to $310K in base. I'm flexible depending on how the equity is structured."

**Why above the stated range:** You have direct founder experience (their stated bonus qualifier), 17+ years of experience, and a verifiable acquisition outcome. Anchoring above the ceiling is reasonable; it signals confidence and gives room to land at $280K as a "win" for both sides.

---

## When They Make an Offer

**If they come in at $280K (top of stated range):**

> "I appreciate the offer at the top of your stated range. Given the scope of the role and the founder experience you flagged as a bonus qualifier, I'd want to make sure the equity reflects that contribution too. Can you walk me through the equity package, the strike price, and where the company is currently valued?"

Then evaluate the equity before deciding. $280K + strong equity at Series B is a good package. $280K + thin equity is not.

**If they come in at $220K-$240K (mid range):**

> "I was hoping to be closer to $280K on the base, which is the top of the range you published. The role as described, managing the engineering org and partnering with the CTO on technical vision, is closer to a VP Engineering scope than a typical engineering manager position. Can we revisit the base, or adjust the equity to bridge that gap?"

**If they come in at $200K (bottom of range):**

> "I appreciate the offer, but $200K doesn't reflect the market rate for the responsibilities in this role, particularly at Series B stage in San Francisco. I'm targeting $270K-$280K. I'd rather find a way to get there than walk away from something that's otherwise a strong fit."

---

## Equity Negotiation

David AI is Series B with $50M raised. For a Head of Engineering at this stage, typical equity is 0.3% to 0.8% depending on dilution, vesting, and valuation.

**Questions to ask:**

1. "What's the current preferred share price from the last round, and what's the post-money valuation?"
2. "What's the option pool as a percentage of fully diluted shares?"
3. "What vesting schedule are you using, and is there a cliff?"
4. "Does the company have a refresh grant program?"
5. "Is there any acceleration on acquisition or change of control?"

**Minimum acceptable structure:** 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff, some form of double-trigger acceleration. If they offer less than 0.3% at current valuation for a Head of Engineering, push back.

**Equity counter script:**

> "I'm looking for something in the 0.4% to 0.6% range, fully diluted. Given that I'd be joining as a founding-stage leader at Series B, the equity needs to reflect the risk I'm taking and the value I'm bringing. What's possible in that range?"

---

## Geography Lever (If Role is Remote)

If the role can be done remotely from Geneva:

> "I'm based in Geneva, which means I'm already calibrated to European costs of living, not San Francisco. I'd be open to adjusting the base slightly if the equity is structured well, but I'd need the base to stay above $260K to account for the currency exposure and the expectation that I'm available across US and European time zones."

If they push for SF relocation:

> "Relocation to San Francisco adds meaningful personal and financial friction. If the company can support a relocation package (at minimum $25K-$30K), and the base is at $280K or above, I'm open to the conversation. Otherwise, remote is strongly preferable."

---

## Competing Offer Framing

If you have (or receive) another offer:

> "I want to be transparent. I'm at the final stage with one other company and expect to have a number by [date]. I'd prefer to close with David AI if we can get to alignment on comp. Is there flexibility to move quickly?"

If you don't have a competing offer but want to create urgency:

> "I'm in conversations with two other companies. I'm not playing games here, but I do want to be honest that I need to make a decision within the next two weeks. If David AI is the right fit, I'd rather close it now than let the process drag."

---

## Final Line — If They Can't Move

> "I've thought about it. The base is lower than what I was targeting, but if you can improve the equity by [X%] and include acceleration on acquisition, I can make this work. That's my best ask."

If they still can't move: accept, decline, or ask for a first performance review at 6 months with a defined path to $280K. Get it in writing.

---

## What Not to Do

- Don't reveal your current salary from Benevity as an anchor.
- Don't say "I need" anything. Frame asks around market rate and role scope.
- Don't accept verbally until you have the offer in writing.
- Don't negotiate against yourself by offering a lower number first.
