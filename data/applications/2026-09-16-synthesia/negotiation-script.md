# Negotiation Script: Principal/Staff Software Engineer, Synthesia (Seattle)

Context that shapes this one: the posting itself states base starts at USD 200,000 plus a stock option plan, full-time only, employed through Synthesia's US entity, no contractors, workplace type hybrid out of Seattle, and the applicant location requirement is the United States. This is not the remote-from-Geneva conversation that came up on the earlier Synthesia ML Platform role. This is a relocation, and the math, visa sponsorship, moving costs, and what I'm walking away from in Geneva, needs to get resolved before comp does.

## Market anchor

- "Starts at USD 200,000" is a floor language, typically the bottom of a published band rather than the number for someone with 17 years and a founding-engineer, post-acquisition track record.
- Staff and Principal engineers in Seattle at well-funded, late-stage private companies (Synthesia raised $200M in its Series E at a $4B valuation) commonly land base salary in the USD 220,000 to 260,000 range, with total comp including options pushing well past that once the option grant is priced in.
- Geneva is consistently ranked among the most expensive cities in the world, more expensive than Seattle on most cost-of-living indices, so this isn't a candidate moving from a cheap market to a pricier one looking for a discount; if anything the opposite framing applies, and it's worth having that number ready in case anyone tries to anchor low assuming a European candidate will take less.
- Opening anchor: USD 235,000 base, plus an option grant sized for Principal-level ownership of a system that sits on the security, compliance, and multi-tenant surface for the company's largest customers, plus a relocation package that covers moving costs, temporary housing, and visa/immigration fees.

## Opening anchor script

"Given the scope, owning core enterprise platform systems and the new external-facing API surface for the Avatar technology, I'd expect a base closer to USD 235,000, with an option grant that reflects Principal-level ownership post-Series E. I'd also want to understand the relocation package early, since this move only makes sense for me if the visa sponsorship and moving costs are handled cleanly. Where does this role currently sit within your bands, and what does the relocation support typically look like?"

## If they come in low

"Is 200,000 the actual target for this hire, or the floor of a published range? I want to make sure we're negotiating from the same starting point." If they hold the base: "I can work with a base closer to your standard number if the option grant moves up to reflect Principal scope, and if the relocation package is comprehensive enough that the move itself isn't a financial step backward. What I'd want to avoid is landing at the bottom of the cash band and also absorbing relocation costs out of pocket."

## Equity / option plan notes

- Confirm whether it's an option plan or RSUs; at this valuation and stage, options are more likely, so ask about the strike price, the 409A valuation used, and whether early exercise is available.
- Confirm standard four-year vesting with a one-year cliff, and ask directly whether there's a refresh grant policy tied to performance review or the next funding event.
- Given the $4B valuation and investors like NVentures, Kleiner Perkins, and GV, ask where the company sees itself in eighteen to twenty-four months, since that shapes how much weight the option grant should carry relative to base in the overall offer.
- Ask what happens to unvested options if the hybrid arrangement changes or if the role's location requirement shifts after the first year.

## Relocation and visa framing

"I'm Geneva-based today, and relocating to Seattle is something I'm genuinely open to, it's on my list of places I'd want to build next, but I need clarity on two things before I can properly evaluate an offer: visa sponsorship timeline and category, and what the relocation package actually covers, flights, shipping, temporary housing, and any tax equalization for the move itself. I don't want the excitement about the role to paper over a move that ends up costing me money in year one."

## Competing offer, if genuinely in play

"I'm in parallel conversations for platform and infrastructure leadership roles at comparable scope, some of which don't require a relocation. That's not a pressure tactic, it's context for why the relocation package and the base both need to be real numbers rather than a starting offer I'm expected to negotiate up from scratch."

## Close

A profitable-trajectory, post-Series E company at a $4B valuation has room to make a relocation offer that doesn't cost the candidate money in the first year. The published USD 200,000 base is a floor to negotiate from, not a ceiling to accept. Get the visa sponsorship category and timeline in writing, get the relocation package itemized, and get the option grant's strike price, vesting start date, and refresh policy documented before signing anything. For a move this disruptive, the relocation terms deserve as much scrutiny as the base salary number.
