# Negotiation Script, Director of Engineering @ n8n

## Context to keep in mind
This is a Berlin-headquartered, remote-first, VC-backed (Sequoia, Accel) company at roughly $40M ARR, hiring a Director of Engineering to run a manager-of-managers structure over enterprise and cloud-facing teams. The posting asks for people based in Berlin or within about one hour of Berlin time. Geneva is the same timezone as Berlin (CET/CEST), so there's no timezone discount to fight, but there is likely a geographic pay-band question since n8n is a German company and may default to German or "European remote" bands rather than Swiss ones.

## Market anchors (Switzerland-adjacent, Director-of-Engineering level)
- Geneva/Zurich in-house Director/VP Engineering at a scale-up: roughly CHF 220,000 to CHF 280,000 base, before equity, for someone managing managers.
- Berlin-based Director of Engineering at a well-funded Series C/D startup (n8n's approximate stage): roughly EUR 150,000 to EUR 190,000 base, often with meaningful equity to compensate for lower Berlin base pay.
- The gap between the Swiss anchor and the likely Berlin-scale offer is real and worth naming directly rather than pretending it doesn't exist. Swiss cost of living, especially Geneva, is a legitimate lever, but only after they've shown interest, not in the first conversation.
- Total comp framing to use throughout: base plus equity plus benefits, not base alone. n8n is still private, so equity has to be priced as a range with real assumptions, not treated as a rounding error.

## Opening anchor
When asked for a number first, don't lead with a single figure. Say something like:
"Based on what I'm seeing for Director-level engineering leadership at companies your size and stage, comparable roles land between CHF 230,000 and CHF 260,000 base in Switzerland, or the equivalent EUR 170,000 to EUR 195,000 range for Berlin-based hires with a meaningful equity component. I'd want to understand your bands before I anchor on a single number, but that's the range I'm working from."

If pushed for a single number: aim for the 65th to 75th percentile of the range you just gave, not the midpoint. For this role that's roughly CHF 250,000 base or EUR 185,000, plus equity.

## If they come in low
Don't react to the number itself; react to the gap. Script:
"That's below the range I'd need to make this work, and I want to be upfront about that rather than let it sit unaddressed. Can you tell me more about how the band was set, whether there's room in base, or whether the difference is meant to be made up in equity or other comp?"

This does three things: signals the offer is not accepted as-is, invites them to explain their constraints (useful information), and keeps the door open for a structured counter instead of a flat rejection.

## Counter-offer script
"I'd like to counter at [X], which reflects the Director-level scope here, running a manager-of-managers structure across enterprise and cloud reliability, and the fact that this isn't a first management role for me. I've built teams from zero and driven adoption of new engineering practices across teams that didn't report to me, which is close to what this job actually needs day one. If base is genuinely fixed at your current offer, I'd want to talk about closing the gap through equity vesting terms, a signing bonus, or an earlier compensation review, say at nine months instead of the standard twelve."

## Equity negotiation (startup, private, VC-backed)
- Ask for the strike price, the last 409A or equivalent valuation, and the total fully diluted share count before evaluating any equity number in isolation; a percentage or unit count means nothing without those.
- Standard vesting is four years with a one-year cliff; push back if theirs is longer or has a cliff beyond twelve months.
- Ask directly whether there's an early-exercise option and what the post-termination exercise window is; a 90-day window after a four-year vest is a real financial risk worth naming, and some companies now offer extended windows (as long as ten years) as a differentiator.
- Ask whether the option pool has been refreshed recently or is likely to be refreshed before your grant is finalized; a stale pool dilutes new grants immediately.
- Given n8n is Series C-ish and growing fast, equity upside is plausible but shouldn't be treated as a substitute for a competitive base. Treat it as a multiplier on top of a base that already clears the anchor range above.

## Geographic discount pushback
If they try to apply a "remote discount" below the Berlin band because you're not in Berlin itself:
"Geneva and Berlin are the same timezone, so there's no collaboration cost being solved by a lower number here. If the concern is cost of living, Geneva runs higher than Berlin on most indices, not lower, so I'd push back on a downward adjustment on that basis specifically."

## Competing offer leverage (use only if genuinely true, or hold in reserve)
"I'm in later-stage conversations with another company at a similar level, and I want to be transparent that comp is one of the factors I'm weighing between the two. I'd rather make a decision based on n8n's best offer than force a bidding situation, so if there's flexibility, now's the moment to use it."

## Close
Whatever number lands, confirm the full package in writing before verbally accepting anything: base, equity (unit count, strike price, vesting start date), any signing bonus, benefits, and the timing of the first compensation review. Verbal agreements on comp have a way of getting reinterpreted once legal drafts the offer letter.
