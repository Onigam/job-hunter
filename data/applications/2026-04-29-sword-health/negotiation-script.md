# Negotiation Script — Sword Health, Senior AI Infrastructure Engineer

---

## Market Context

**Role level:** Senior AI Infrastructure Engineer (equivalent to Staff Engineer for infra-specialised roles)
**Location:** EU Remote (Porto/Germany/EU listed; Magino is Geneva-based)
**Listed salary range (third-party aggregator):** $120,000-$150,000 USD

**Switzerland market rates for this level:**
- Senior AI/ML Infrastructure Engineer in Geneva: CHF 140,000-180,000 base
- Staff-equivalent AI Infra roles at health-tech or AI companies: CHF 160,000-200,000
- Benevity-equivalent Senior Staff: CHF 150,000-170,000 range

**Key comp variable:** Sword Health is a US-incorporated company (San Francisco HQ) likely paying EU employees through a local entity or EOR (Employer of Record). Geneva as a work location means Swiss statutory costs (AVS/AI, LPP pension, health insurance) are on top of salary. If they pay a "EU rate" rather than a Swiss rate, there's a legitimate gap to address.

**Equity consideration:** Sword Health raised Series D ($163M) in 2023. Still pre-IPO. Options or RSUs could be meaningful if valuation continues to grow, but current liquidity is uncertain. Treat equity as upside, not base comp.

---

## Opening Anchor

Target: **CHF 160,000-170,000 base** (or USD equivalent at ~1.10 rate: $175,000-$185,000)

This is anchored above the listed range for three reasons you can cite:
1. Geneva cost of living is among the highest in Europe (Mercer 2025: Geneva ranks top 5 globally)
2. The AI infra specialisation commands a premium over general SRE/platform roles
3. 17 years of experience including founding-engineer and Staff-level track record

Do not lead with the third-party salary listing. Let them open first if possible.

---

## Script: First Offer Call

**If they open with a number below CHF 150,000 / $155,000:**

"I appreciate the offer. I want to be direct about where I'm anchored. For a Senior AI Infrastructure role with my level of experience, working from Geneva, the range I'm targeting is CHF 160,000-170,000 base. Geneva's cost structure is meaningfully different from Porto or Berlin, and I want to make sure we're calibrated to the same market. Is there room to move closer to that range?"

**If they push back on Switzerland premium ("we pay EU rates"):**

"I understand the pay structure, and I'm not trying to make this difficult. But from my side, my costs are Geneva costs: housing, healthcare, AVS contributions. If the base can't move, I'd want to revisit the equity component or add a cost-of-living adjustment to bridge the gap. What flexibility is there on those levers?"

**If they're at CHF 150,000-160,000:**

"That's closer to where I need to be. If we can get to 160,000 I'm ready to move forward without needing time to evaluate further. I'd rather close this quickly than drag it out."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in process (or can credibly say you're in final stages):

"I want to be straightforward with you: I have another process at a similar stage for a staff infra role in the CHF 155,000-165,000 range. I'm more drawn to what Sword Health is building, specifically the real-time AI agent infrastructure problem, but I need the numbers to be in a comparable range to make the choice easy. If you can get to 160,000 base, this is the one I choose."

If no competing offer: do not fabricate one. Use market data instead.

---

## Equity Negotiation (if stock options or RSUs are on the table)

**Questions to ask before evaluating:**
1. What is the current valuation (last round or 409A)?
2. What is the strike price for options?
3. What is the vesting schedule (4-year / 1-year cliff is standard)?
4. Is there a liquidity plan: IPO timeline, secondary market access, or buyback program?
5. Are these ISOs (incentive stock options) or NSOs? Relevant for US tax treatment if you ever work from the US.

**If they offer options valued at <1% of package:**
"I appreciate including equity, but at the current stage and with no clear liquidity timeline, I'd weight it as upside rather than current compensation. I'd like to make the base work first, and treat the equity as the long-term bet."

**If they offer meaningful equity (0.05-0.2% for a senior hire at this stage):**
"That's meaningful. I'd want to understand the vesting terms and current 409A valuation before putting a number on it, but this does move the overall package picture. Let me review and come back to you by end of week."

---

## What to Prioritise (in order)

1. Base salary: non-negotiable to close quickly; CHF 155,000 minimum, CHF 160,000+ target
2. Remote flexibility: confirm Geneva is acceptable even if the listed locations are Porto/Germany
3. Contract type: employee vs. contractor (if contractor, add ~20-25% for self-employment costs and absence of benefits)
4. Equipment budget and home office stipend (standard in Swiss remote roles: CHF 2,000-3,000/year)
5. Equity: upside only; don't trade base for unvested options at a pre-IPO company

---

## Things to Verify Before Signing

- Does the Swiss contract comply with Swiss employment law (LPP pension enrollment, accident insurance, etc.)?
- Who is the local employer entity in Switzerland, or will they use an EOR?
- What is the notice period? Swiss standard is 1-3 months depending on years of service.
- Is there a non-compete clause and how broad is it?
