Negotiation Script: Founding Engineer, FinTech Start-up (via Oliver Bernard)

## Context worth naming up front

This one's unusual because the employer is unnamed in the posting itself, it's a stealth-mode AI fintech being recruited for by Oliver Bernard, with only "top-tier global investors" and "significant funding already secured" as data points. That means anchoring hard on a number before the first call would be premature. The role is titled Founding Engineer but listed as Remote-EU, Mid-Senior level, which is a slightly odd combination since founding-engineer scope usually tracks with earlier-stage, smaller teams, while "Mid-Senior" as a level label undersells what full ownership from day one usually pays. Before naming numbers, it's worth getting the recruiter, Jonah Brookes, to confirm funding stage (seed, Series A, later), headcount, and whether "Founding Engineer" here means employee two or employee twenty, since that swings both base and equity meaningfully.

Geneva is home base, and Switzerland isn't in the EU even though the role is scoped Remote-EU, so it's worth confirming early whether that's a hard legal-entity constraint or just a loose description of the working-hours band the company wants. If it's the latter, Swiss cost-of-living anchors are fair game.

## Market anchors

Founding or early engineer roles at well-funded AI startups in Europe typically land base salary in the CHF 140,000 to 190,000 range when adjusted for Swiss cost of living, with the wide band reflecting how much "founding" varies in practice, equity usually carries more of the total comp story than base at this stage. Given 17+ years of experience and a track record that includes a founding role which ended in an acquisition by a billion-dollar company, the anchor should sit at the top of that band rather than the middle, closer to CHF 175,000 to 195,000 base, with equity layered on meaningfully given the early stage and "meaningful equity stake" language already in the posting.

## Opening anchor

"Before I put a number down, I want to understand where this sits: is this genuinely one of the first engineering hires, or closer to a strong early-team seat that's using the founding-engineer title loosely? That changes how I think about the equity-to-base ratio."

If pushed for a number regardless: "Given the ownership and speed the posting describes, and assuming this is a genuinely early seat, I'd want to start the conversation around CHF 180,000 to 195,000 base, with equity discussed as a separate, serious conversation rather than an afterthought."

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

If the offer lands well under that range: "I appreciate the offer, and I want to flag that a founding-engineer title with full ownership from day one usually comes with comp closer to what I'd expect from prior staff-level and cofounder experience, not a standard mid-level number. I was expecting something closer to CHF 180,000 base or above. Is there room to move there, or is the gap meant to be closed by equity or a sign-on instead?"

If they lean on the "Mid-Senior" level label to justify a lower band: "I get that the internal leveling says Mid-Senior, but the scope described, full ownership, frontier-level work, shipping fast with no playbook, reads like a staff or founding-level seat regardless of the label. I'd rather we align the level to the actual scope than negotiate around a title that doesn't match the job."

## Equity negotiation

Ask plainly what percentage of the company the offered equity represents, not just a share count, since share counts are meaningless without knowing total shares outstanding. For a genuinely early founding-engineer hire at a funded startup, 0.25% to 1% is a reasonable starting range depending on how early and how much has already been raised, push toward the higher end if this is closer to employee two or three than employee twenty. Confirm standard four-year vesting with a one-year cliff, and ask directly whether there's any double-trigger acceleration on acquisition, since the Alaya story makes clear that acquisitions happen and vesting terms matter when they do. Ask about the strike price and whether a 409A or equivalent valuation has been done recently, and get the post-termination exercise window in writing, anything short of ten years can turn a good outcome into an expensive decision later.

## Geographic pushback

If comp gets anchored down assuming remote-from-Switzerland is cheap: "Geneva runs at or above most Western European cost of living, so I wouldn't expect the number to shrink because I'm not sitting in an EU capital. I'm also not asking for an EU-specific package, just parity with what the role's scope would pay anywhere in Western Europe."

If the EU-only framing becomes a real blocker rather than a loose description: "If Remote-EU turns out to be a hard legal requirement rather than a timezone preference, I'd rather know that now than after we've aligned on comp, since it would change whether this is workable at all."

## Competing offer template

Use only if a real competing process exists.

"I'm further along in another process at a comparable level, with a base in a similar range to what we've been discussing. I'd rather land this one, an unnamed early-stage AI fintech with real funding and a genuinely open mandate is the more interesting problem to me than a later-stage role would be, but the offer needs to be close enough that I'm not walking away from something real."
