# Negotiation Script: Crossref, Head of Infrastructure Services

## Context specific to this offer
- Posted band: 120k USD or local equivalent, converted using a 5-year average USD rate for stability. This is a stated, largely fixed non-profit band, not a typical startup negotiation where the number is soft.
- Non-profit, 60-some distributed staff, reports to the Director of Technology. There's no equity here and the org almost certainly has real budget constraints tied to member dues, not VC funding.
- Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in the world; a flat 120k USD converted to CHF will land meaningfully below Geneva-market rates for a Staff+/leadership infrastructure role. That gap is the main thing worth raising, not the base number itself.
- Timeline is public: advertised in September, offer expected by November. No urgency to rush a counter before other candidates are through the pipeline.

## Opening anchor
Because the band is published and framed as fixed, don't open with a number pulled from thin air. Anchor instead on the cost-of-living mismatch and scope:

"I saw the role is banded at 120k USD equivalent globally. I want to be upfront that Geneva is one of the higher cost-of-living markets you're likely hiring into, so I'd like to understand whether there's any flexibility for geographic cost of living, or whether the 120k is genuinely flat regardless of location. If it's flat, I'd like to talk about scope, on-call load, or professional development budget as places we could still find value."

This does two things: it tests whether the band is actually rigid before wasting a counter-offer, and it reframes the conversation toward total package rather than just base.

## If they confirm the band is flat
Non-profits with public, dues-funded budgets often genuinely can't move base pay. Don't push on the number past one attempt. Instead:
- Ask about remote work stipend, conference/training budget, and hardware allowance, which are usually more flexible than base.
- Ask about review cadence: "Is there a formal comp review at 6 or 12 months tied to performance, or is 120k the number for the life of the role until org-wide adjustments happen?"
- Ask about PTO and flexible time off specifics beyond what's in the handbook, since Crossref already advertises unlimited sick time and paid parental leave, there may be room in vacation structure even if base is fixed.

## If there's any flexibility
Counter once, clearly, tied to scope rather than just "I'm worth more":

"Given the scope here includes both the infrastructure roadmap and people leadership for the team, and given Geneva's cost of living, I'd ask you to consider something closer to 135-140k USD equivalent. I'm flexible on exactly how that's structured if there's a ceiling on base."

Don't anchor above 140k for this specific role; the org's public band and non-profit structure make a large ask look tone-deaf rather than confident.

## Geographic cost-of-living pushback
If they push back with "the band is the same everywhere," the honest response is:
"That makes sense for keeping things fair internally. I'd just flag that at Geneva rates, 120k USD is closer to a mid-level engineering salary than a leadership one, so I want to make sure expectations on scope match what the comp reflects. I'm still very interested either way, just want to go in with clear eyes."

This keeps the door open without threatening to walk, which would be a bluff here given the mission fit is genuinely strong.

## Equity / non-profit equivalent
There's no equity in a non-profit structure. Don't ask for it. If there's any deferred comp, bonus pool, or org-wide 13th-month-style payment common in some non-profits, ask directly:
"Is there any annual bonus, profit-share, or 13th-month structure outside the base, or is 120k the full cash comp?"

## Competing offer leverage
Only use this if genuinely true at the time. Template if applicable:
"I'm currently a few weeks into conversations with another team, so if there's any flexibility on your side, I'd rather make a decision on this one, the mission fit here is stronger for me. I don't want to create false urgency, just wanted to be transparent about timing."

Do not fabricate a competing offer. Crossref is a small, tightly networked community (scholarly infrastructure, open access) and a bluff that gets checked would do real damage.

## Closing framing
Because this is mission-driven work with a fixed band, the strongest negotiating position is honesty about fit plus one clear, well-justified ask, not aggressive anchoring. The likely outcome is a small adjustment or none at all on base, with more room in start date, review cadence, or remote setup budget.
