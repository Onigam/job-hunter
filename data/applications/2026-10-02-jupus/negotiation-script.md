# Negotiation Script: JUPUS, Engineering Manager, Applied AI

_Advertised: remote (CET ±2), up to €160k base. Company: Cologne, ~85 people, €19.5M raised (€13M Series A, June 2026, led by Semapa Next)._

## 1. Market context

The posted ceiling of €160k is strong for a German startup EM. Most Series A companies in Germany pay engineering managers somewhere around €110k to €140k, so JUPUS is already at the top of its local market.

Geneva is a different story. An engineering manager or Staff-level lead in Geneva usually earns about CHF 170k to 220k base at a funded startup, and more at large tech or banks. At today's rate (roughly 0.93 to 0.95 CHF per euro, so check it on the day), €160k comes to about CHF 150k. That's below Geneva market, and Geneva living costs are some of the highest in Europe.

Employment structure also matters. A German company employing a Swiss resident has to run Swiss payroll or go through an EOR, and pay Swiss social charges either way. Ask early how they've handled this for other non-German hires, because the answer changes what your number actually means.

## 2. Opening anchor

Don't name a number first if you can avoid it. If pressed:

> "Based on what I've seen for this scope, five reports and ownership of the whole Applied AI direction, I'm looking at the top of your band, so €160k base, plus a meaningful VSOP grant. I'm in Geneva, where comparable roles sit closer to CHF 190k, so I'm already meeting you partway by working in euros."

The goal is to lock in the ceiling as the starting point and push the extra value into equity and terms.

## 3. If they come in low (e.g. €135–145k)

> "Thanks, I appreciate the offer and I'm keen on the role. The base is below what I'd planned for, though. The posting goes up to €160k, and given the founder experience and the time I've spent on LLM systems in production, I think I'm at the top of that range rather than the middle. Can we get to €160k? If base is truly fixed, I'd want to look at a larger VSOP grant and a review at six months with a defined step-up."

If they cite the management or agentic-AI gap:

> "That's fair, and I raised it myself. That's why I'd suggest a six-month review tied to concrete outcomes, an eval framework in place, and the team's retention and delivery. If I hit them, the base moves to €160k. That way you're not paying up front for something you haven't seen yet."

## 4. Equity (VSOP)

German startups almost always grant virtual shares (VSOP), not real equity. Things to ask and push on:

- **Grant size**: ask for it as a percentage of fully diluted capital, not a share count. For a first EM hire after a Series A, 0.1% to 0.3% is a reasonable range to aim for.
- **Vesting**: 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Leaver clauses**: this is where VSOPs hurt. Find out what happens to vested units if you leave (good leaver vs bad leaver). Push for vested units to stay yours as a good leaver, without them decaying over time.
- **Payout trigger**: VSOPs usually pay out only at exit. Ask whether they've ever run a secondary or buyback.
- **Base valuation / strike**: ask which valuation your grant is struck at. Units struck at the Series A price are worth much less than they sound.
- **Dilution**: VSOPs normally have no anti-dilution protection. Don't spend your bargaining power fighting it, but ask whether the pool gets topped up at the next round.
- **Swiss tax**: VSOP payouts are taxed as ordinary income in Switzerland when paid. Plan around that rather than treating the paper value as net.

## 5. Geographic pushback

If they suggest a location discount, or argue Geneva shouldn't change anything:

> "I'm not asking for a Geneva premium on top of the band. I'm asking for the top of the band you've published for remote roles. Employing me in Switzerland also costs you more in social charges than a German hire, and I understand that. I'd rather solve it with the EOR setup and equity than by lowering the base."

## 6. Competing offer template (use only if true)

> "I want to be transparent. I'm in late stages with another company, and their number is around [X]. JUPUS is my first choice because of the problem and the team, but I need the package to be close. Is there flexibility on base or VSOP to get there?"

Never make up a competing offer. If nothing is live, skip this section and lean on the market data and the six-month review.

## 7. Other terms worth asking about

- Budget for team offsites, given that the company is fully remote
- Learning and conference budget, with an AI/legal-tech focus
- Hardware and home-office allowance
- Notice period (German contracts often default to 3 months; Swiss ones vary)
- Holidays: 30 days is common in German startups, so make sure the Swiss contract matches
