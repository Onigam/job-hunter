# Negotiation Script, Founding Engineer @ Kato (Pear VC portfolio)

Context: Kato is Pear-backed and pre-Series A by every signal in the posting, first engineering hire reporting straight to the CTO, based in San Francisco with a four-days-a-week in-office expectation. That's a different negotiation than a Geneva-based role. Comp will be quoted in USD, cash is likely modest for a seed-stage company, and equity is where most of the real upside sits. The Switzerland angle here isn't a local market comp, it's a bargaining chip: the cost of pulling someone out of Geneva, and the total comp bar a Swiss-based senior engineer already clears, sets a floor for what relocation needs to be worth.

## Market anchors

- Founding engineer cash comp at seed to early Series A startups in SF generally lands in the USD 150,000 to 190,000 base range for someone with 10+ years and a proven build-from-zero track record, though titles like "founding engineer" at pre-Series A companies sometimes come in lower on cash specifically because the equity is meant to carry the weight.
- Equity for a founding engineer joining as employee one or two, not a co-founder, typically runs somewhere around 0.5% to 2%, with the exact number depending heavily on stage, how much has already been raised, and how diluted the cap table is.
- A senior staff-level engineer already based in Geneva, with a founder-and-acquisition track record, is not a cheap relocation. Swiss total comp for that profile is competitive with US mid-market bands even before accounting for the fact that leaving Geneva means giving up a stable, well-compensated, low-relocation-risk position.
- San Francisco cost of living, combined with a return-to-office expectation, both increase the true cost of taking this role. Relocation, visa sponsorship if needed, and temporary housing all belong in the conversation before base salary does.

## Opening anchor

Aim for the top of whatever cash band they open with, and frame it around the Alaya outcome rather than years of experience alone: "Given that I've built a platform from zero through to an acquisition by a unicorn, and that this role is asking me to relocate from Geneva on a four-days-a-week onsite basis, I'd want a base toward the higher end of what you'd pay a founding engineer at this stage, along with an equity grant that reflects joining as employee one or two rather than employee ten."

## If they come in low on cash

Ask what the number assumes before pushing back on it directly: "Is that base benchmarked against a founding engineer with less senior experience, or does it account for the fact that I'd be relocating internationally for this?" Then anchor the counter on the relocation reality rather than a generic ask: "Moving from Geneva to San Francisco isn't a lateral move for me financially, even before cost of living. I'd want to close some of that gap through cash, and make up the rest through a larger equity grant or a relocation package."

## Equity due diligence

- Ask for the actual percentage being offered, not just a share count, and ask what the fully diluted cap table looks like so the percentage means something.
- Confirm the strike price and the most recent 409A valuation, and ask directly whether early exercise is allowed, since Swiss tax treatment of unexercised options versus exercised shares is not the same and matters for planning.
- Standard is four years with a one-year cliff. Confirm that's what's on the table and ask whether there's any acceleration on acquisition, given this is exactly the kind of company that gets acquired by a larger fintech or lending platform.
- Ask how much has been raised and at what valuation, since a founding engineer's equity is only as good as the odds the company actually gets to a liquidity event.

## Relocation and visa

- If sponsorship is needed, ask early whether Kato has done this before and who covers legal costs, since a seed-stage company's answer here says a lot about how prepared they are for an international hire.
- Ask for a relocation stipend or lump sum covering flights, temporary housing, and moving costs, and don't treat this as separate from the comp conversation, it's part of the same number.
- Clarify the actual onsite requirement in writing. "Four or more days a week" is vague enough that it's worth pinning down before accepting anything.

## Competing offer template (use only if real)

"I want to be upfront that I'm also talking with another company at a comparable founding or senior IC level, with a package in the USD X range. Kato is the more interesting technical problem to me, but I'd need the offer here to be close enough to that number that the relocation math still works."

Don't bluff this one. A seed-stage founder will often just ask for details, and losing credibility with the CTO you'd be reporting to on day one is a worse outcome than a slightly lower number.
