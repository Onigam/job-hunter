# Negotiation Script: Cosuno, Principal Full Stack Developer (TypeScript)

## Context to keep in mind
Cosuno is Berlin-based, remote-first, and pays in EUR by default. The posting mentions "competitive compensation above market average" without a number, which means the first concrete figure in the conversation sets the anchor. Cosuno has processed $4B in construction volume and looks like a healthy Series B-ish scale-up rather than a pre-seed bet, so there's real room to negotiate on both cash and equity without it reading as unreasonable.

Because this is a remote EU role rather than a Geneva-based one, salary won't hit Swiss principal-engineer numbers (roughly CHF 160k-200k+ base), and pushing for that framing would be tone-deaf. The realistic anchor is a German/EU tech-hub principal engineer band, adjusted upward for the seniority and scope this role actually carries (full-stack ownership, architecture, mentoring).

## Market anchors (EU remote, Principal/Staff-level full stack)
- Berlin/Amsterdam principal engineer base: roughly EUR 95,000-125,000, with strong candidates and scale-ups on the upper end.
- Remote-from-Switzerland premium: some EU companies partially adjust for cost of living even on remote contracts, though many don't. Worth asking directly rather than assuming either way.
- Total comp for a role at this level, including any bonus or equity, should land noticeably above pure base, given the "above market average" language in the posting.

## Opening anchor
When asked for a number first: "Based on what principal-level full stack roles are paying at scale-ups of your size in Berlin and Amsterdam, and given the added remote-from-Switzerland factor, I'd expect a base in the range of EUR 115,000 to 130,000, plus equity. I'm flexible on structure if the overall package gets there."

If they ask for a single number instead of a range: give the top of the range, not the middle. "EUR 128,000 base is where I'd want to land, with room to talk about how equity and other components fit around that."

## If they come in low
Script: "I appreciate the offer, and I'm genuinely interested in the role. The number you've shared is below what I'd expect for principal-level ownership of the full stack, including the architectural and mentoring scope described in the posting. Can you walk me through how this number was set, so I understand what flexibility exists?"

Follow-up if they cite budget constraints: "I understand budget is a real constraint. Would it help to close some of the gap with a signing bonus or an accelerated equity review at six or twelve months, if base is genuinely fixed right now?"

## Equity negotiation
- Ask directly what the current strike price and 409A-equivalent valuation look like, and how much dilution has happened since the last round.
- Standard vesting to expect: 4 years, 1-year cliff. If they offer anything shorter than a 1-year cliff, that's unusual and worth clarifying why.
- Ask whether there's an early-exercise option and whether unexercised shares get a post-termination exercise window longer than the default 90 days; that detail matters more than the headline share count.
- For a company at Cosuno's apparent stage (processing real transaction volume, likely Series B or later), equity is a real but secondary lever compared to base. Don't let equity substitute for a below-market base.

## Geographic discount pushback
If they try to anchor to a lower "cost of living" number because the role is remote-from-Switzerland but paid on a Berlin scale: "I'd push back gently on that. The role's scope and impact don't change based on where I'm sitting, and the market rate I'm anchoring to is already the Berlin/Amsterdam band for this level, not a Swiss one. I'm not asking for a Geneva premium, just parity with what you'd pay a principal engineer in your own hub cities."

## Competing offer as a bargaining point (use only if genuinely applicable)
"I'm currently in later-stage conversations with another company for a similar scope of role, and I'd like to be able to make a decision based on complete information from both sides. If there's flexibility on the number, now's the useful moment to use it."

## Close
Whatever the final number, confirm the full package in writing before responding: base, bonus structure if any, equity grant size and vesting schedule, start date, and remote work terms including any expectation of Berlin office time. Don't verbally accept before that email exists.
