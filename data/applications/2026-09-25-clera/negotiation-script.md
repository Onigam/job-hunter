# Negotiation Script: Clera, Staff Engineer (founding), Munich

The posting gives a band of **EUR 80k to 130k** base, full-time on-site in Munich, no visa sponsorship. The listing is a seed-stage AI sales automation startup. Note that getclera.com is a San Francisco AI talent agent that places candidates at startups, so this may be a client role posted through Clera. Confirm the actual employer's name, funding and team size on the first call before discussing numbers.

Two things to check early: that you have the right to work in Germany without sponsorship, and whether they offer relocation support from Geneva.

## 1. Market picture (2026 estimates)

| Reference point | Base | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Munich seed/Series A startup, Senior Engineer | EUR 80k to 105k | Small VSOP grant |
| Munich startup, Staff / founding Staff Engineer | EUR 110k to 140k | Founding grants 0.3% to 1% at seed |
| Munich big tech (Google, Apple, Microsoft), Staff | EUR 140k to 180k | Liquid RSUs on top |
| Geneva / Zurich, Staff Engineer at scale-up | CHF 180k to 230k | Your current market, for reference |

The posted ceiling of EUR 130k sits well below what you'd earn in Switzerland. After German tax and social charges (a bit over 40% combined at this level, against roughly 25 to 30% in Geneva), net pay drops further. Munich housing is cheaper than Geneva, but not by enough to close that gap. The equity has to make up the difference, or the move doesn't make sense.

**Your targets**
- Walk-away: EUR 120k base plus a meaningful founding grant, or EUR 130k with a smaller one
- Target: EUR 130k base plus 0.5% to 1% equity
- Opening anchor: EUR 140k base, 1% equity, relocation package

Anchoring above the posted band is fine here. Staff scope across several products and a founding title together justify it, and it leaves room to land at the top of the band.

## 2. Opening the conversation

If they ask for expectations first:

> "For a founding Staff role owning architecture across several products, I'm looking at around EUR 140k base, with a founding-level equity grant. I'm moving from Geneva, where Staff comp is quite a bit higher, so equity matters a lot to me. I'd rather see the full package than settle on base alone."

If they insist on a single number, repeat 140k. Don't give a range.

Justification to have ready: 17 years in production systems, five years as cofounder building a platform from zero to 50+ enterprise clients and an acquisition by a $1B+ company, hiring and mentoring the first engineers, and recent Staff-level work on LLM integration and cross-team architecture at Benevity. The posting asks for someone who can make architecture calls across multiple products and still ship most of the code. You've done exactly that job before.

## 3. If the offer comes in low

> "Thanks, I appreciate the offer. The base is below what I'd expected for a founding Staff role, and it's a real step down from Swiss comp. If we can get base to EUR 130k and the grant to 0.75%, I'm ready to move forward."

If base is capped at the band:

> "Understood. If base is fixed, I'd want that reflected in equity. Can we look at a larger grant, a relocation and signing bonus to cover the move, and a written comp review after the next funding round?"

Don't accept on the call. "Let me go through the full package and come back to you by Friday" is always fine.

## 4. Equity questions (German startup)

German startups mostly use **VSOPs** (virtual stock options) rather than real shares. Ask all of these in writing:

- Is it a VSOP or real options/shares? VSOPs usually only pay out on an exit and are taxed as income at that point. Ask whether they've looked at the newer employee-share rules in §19a EStG.
- What percentage of the fully diluted share count does the grant represent? A grant quoted as a euro "value" means nothing without the valuation behind it.
- Strike price, or the base valuation the virtual shares are measured against.
- Vesting: 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff and double-trigger acceleration on change of control.
- Leaver clauses: German VSOPs often have harsh good/bad leaver terms, and some let vested units lapse if you leave before an exit. Push for vested units to be kept as a good leaver.
- Dilution: what happens to your percentage at Series A? Ask whether a refresh or top-up is planned after the round.
- Liquidity: any chance of partial payout in a secondary before a full exit?

Anti-dilution protection is basically never given to employees. Asking for a guaranteed top-up after the next round is more realistic.

## 5. Location pushback

This role is on-site in Munich, so the pushback goes the other way. They'll pay Munich rates, and the argument is about why you're worth the top of their band.

> "I'm relocating from Geneva for this, and I'm leaving a market where Staff engineers earn well above your band. I'm not asking for Swiss pay, but I need the package to reflect founding scope, and some help with the move."

Ask for relocation support (a lump sum of EUR 5k to 15k is normal, or a few months of temporary housing), and confirm the start date works with your current notice period.

## 6. Competing offer template

Use only if it's true, or clearly as a market reference:

> "I want to be upfront: I'm in late-stage conversations for a Staff role in Switzerland with a base around CHF 200k. I'd rather join you, since the founding scope is what I'm after, but I need the package to be closer for this to work. Is there room on base or equity?"

Never invent a specific written offer. Talking in terms of the market ("roles I'm interviewing for are paying...") is honest and still works.
