# Negotiation Script — Principal ML Ops Engineer (Undisclosed AI Startup via Pragmatike)

---

## Market Anchors — Switzerland, Principal-Level ML Ops

**Relevant comps (CHF, gross annual base):**
- Principal / Staff ML Ops Engineer, Geneva/Zurich: CHF 160,000-200,000
- Senior ML Ops (IC5 equivalent), remote-friendly Swiss startups: CHF 140,000-170,000
- Principal-level AI infra at Series A-B Swiss/European startup: CHF 170,000-210,000

**Magino's positioning justification:**
- 17+ years of experience, founding engineer background
- Track record of building production infrastructure from zero to acquisition ($1B+ exit)
- Recent production AI work (RAG, multi-agent, model serving on Kubernetes) directly relevant to the role
- Geneva cost of living: among the highest in Europe, CHF 180,000 is not aggressive at this level

**Opening anchor: CHF 190,000 base.** If they push back, CHF 175,000 is a reasonable landing zone. Do not go below CHF 160,000 without meaningful equity compensation.

---

## First Conversation — When They Ask About Expectations

Use this when a recruiter or hiring manager asks for compensation expectations early in the process.

> "For a Principal-level role in Geneva, I'm targeting the CHF 185,000-195,000 range for base. That's consistent with what I'm seeing in the market for this level, and I'm open to discussing the full package once I understand the equity structure better. What does the total compensation look like on your end?"

Push the conversation toward total comp immediately. Early anchoring on base only leaves equity and bonus unaddressed.

## Counter-Offer Scripts

**Scenario A — They open below CHF 160,000:**

> "I appreciate the offer. I'll be honest: that's below what I need to make this work in Geneva, and it's also below what I've seen for Principal ML Ops roles at this level in this market. Based on my research and the scope of what you're describing, I'd need to be in the CHF 175,000-185,000 range to move forward. Is that something you have room to get to?"

Don't justify the number extensively. State it once, clearly, and ask the question back.

**Scenario B — They open at CHF 160,000-175,000:**

> "That's a reasonable starting point. Given the scope of the role and my background building production ML infrastructure at Principal level, I'd want to get to CHF 185,000 on the base. On the equity side, what does the package look like in terms of grant size and current valuation?"

Redirect to equity immediately. At an AI startup this is often where the real value is.

**Scenario C — They offer at or above CHF 180,000:**

Accept the range in principle, then negotiate the equity and vesting terms. Base is close enough; the fight is on equity.

> "That works for me on the base. I'd want to understand the equity grant in more detail before I finalize anything. What's the grant size, the current 409A or last round valuation, and the vesting schedule?"

## Equity Negotiation (AI Startup)

**Key questions to ask before accepting any offer:**
1. What's the grant size (options or RSUs)?
2. What was the last round valuation, or preferred share price?
3. Standard 4-year / 1-year cliff vesting?
4. What percentage of the company does this represent on a fully diluted basis?
5. Is there any acceleration on acquisition (single or double trigger)?

**What to push for:**
- 0.25%-0.5% fully diluted is reasonable for a Principal at an early-stage AI startup
- At Series A ($20-50M valuation), that's CHF 50,000-250,000 paper value pre-dilution
- Push for double-trigger acceleration: if the company is acquired and you're laid off within 12 months, unvested shares accelerate. This is standard and easy to grant.
- One-year cliff is non-negotiable from their side; 4-year vest is standard. Don't push on those.

**Script for equity:**

> "On the equity, I want to make sure I understand what this looks like in practice. What's the fully diluted percentage this grant represents, and what was the share price at the last round? I'm not trying to game the math, I just want to understand what the upside looks like relative to the base trade-off I'm making versus a larger company."

## Geneva Cost of Living Pushback

If they compare your ask to remote or non-Geneva salaries:

> "I hear that. The Geneva premium is real though: housing, social contributions, tax rates in the canton. CHF 185,000 in Geneva is not the same purchasing power as CHF 185,000 in Lisbon or Berlin. The market comps for Principal-level technical roles here reflect that. I'm anchoring to what's competitive locally."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

Even if you don't have a competing offer in hand, frame as follows if they're slow or lowballing:

> "I'm in conversations with a couple of other companies at this level. I'm not using that as pressure, but I do want to be transparent that the timeline matters to me. If the compensation can get to the range I mentioned, this is the offer I'd want to take. Where are you on making a decision?"

This creates light urgency without fabricating specifics.

---

## Walk-Away Conditions

Don't accept:
- Base below CHF 155,000 without very strong equity (0.4%+ fully diluted at a credible valuation)
- Standard 4-year vest with no acceleration clause at an early-stage startup (acquisition risk is real)
- Any role where the "Principal" title maps to individual contributor with no architecture authority
