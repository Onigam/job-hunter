# Negotiation Script - Orbit, Product Engineer (m/w/d)

## Context that shapes this negotiation
Orbit is bootstrapped and profitable, not VC-funded. That cuts both ways: no investor pressure forcing lowball offers to preserve runway, but also no large speculative option pool to throw at a counter. Comp discipline is more likely to sit on base salary and the "Own Orbit" profit-sharing program than on traditional equity. The role is remote-first and EU-located rather than Switzerland-based, so the anchor is a senior/staff-level remote European product engineer rate, adjusted upward for Magino's 0-to-1 founding experience and Geneva cost of living, not a pure Zurich or Geneva in-office rate.

## Market anchor
- Senior/staff-level product engineers in remote-first, profitable European tech companies: roughly CHF 130,000-160,000 total cash equivalent, sometimes expressed in EUR (EUR 125,000-155,000) depending on the employer's home currency.
- Founding or 0-to-1 experience with a successful exit typically adds a premium on top of a standard senior band, since it signals the candidate can own ambiguity without a spec handed to them, exactly what this role is asking for.
- Geneva-based remote workers can reasonably anchor near the top of a company's stated band even without a Swiss legal entity, since cost of living and the CHF exchange rate are real constraints on take-home pay.

## Opening anchor
"Based on the scope here, full feature ownership plus building out agent infrastructure and eval frameworks, I'd expect total cash in the CHF 150,000-165,000 range, or the EUR equivalent if that's how the band is structured. That reflects both the seniority of the work and that I've done the 0-to-1 version of this before, at Alaya, through to an acquisition."

## If they come in low
"I hear you on the number. Given the scope includes both feature ownership and building the agent tooling other engineers will depend on, that's closer to two roles than one, and I'd want the comp to reflect that combination rather than either half of it alone. Is there room to move the base closer to [target], or to make up the gap through the profit-sharing program instead?"

## Profit-sharing and equity notes ("Own Orbit")
Since Orbit runs profit-sharing rather than a conventional option pool, treat it like equity in the negotiation, ask the same questions a VC-backed offer would require:
- What percentage of profit does the pool represent, and how is an individual's share calculated, headcount-weighted, tenure-weighted, or role-weighted?
- Is there a vesting or tenure requirement before a share becomes payable, and what happens to unvested share on departure?
- How has the payout actually looked in the last one to two profit-sharing cycles, in absolute terms, not just as a percentage?
- Because there's no dilution risk the way there is with priced equity, the real question isn't anti-dilution, it's payout predictability: ask whether profit-sharing has been paid out consistently or only in strong years.

## Geographic discount pushback
If the offer implicitly discounts for being Geneva-based rather than Hamburg-based: "I want to flag that Geneva's cost of living is on par with or above Hamburg's, and the role is remote-first by design, so I'd expect the band to be set by seniority and scope rather than by which EU city someone happens to sit in."

## Competing offer leverage template
Use only if genuinely in play; don't bluff a number that won't survive a follow-up question.
"I'm currently weighing this against another opportunity at a similar total comp level, and honestly the work here, and the writing culture, is what's pulling me toward Orbit. If we can close the gap on [specific term], I'd rather sign here."

## Close
Confirm the final number, the profit-sharing mechanics in writing, start date, and equipment/coworking budget details before verbally accepting. Ask for the offer in writing within 48 hours of a verbal agreement.
