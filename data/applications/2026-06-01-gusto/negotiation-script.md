# Negotiation Script — Gusto, Staff Software Engineer, Platform Orchestration

---

## Market Context

Gusto is a large, well-funded US fintech company (payroll and HR SaaS, last disclosed private valuation approximately $9.5B). The JD states the compensation band explicitly:

- **Denver and remote:** $163,000-$204,000 USD base
- **San Francisco, Seattle, New York:** $197,000-$247,000 USD base

Equity (RSUs) and benefits add meaningfully on top of base. For Staff-level hires, RSU grants in the $150,000-$300,000 range at grant date over a 4-year vest are typical at this company size.

Key variable: the role is listed as SF hybrid (2-3 days/week in office). If you're negotiating as a Geneva-based remote hire rather than a San Francisco-based hire, Gusto may push you toward the remote tier ($163k-$204k). You should resist this if operating at SF parity on cost of living.

One logistical item to clarify early: does Gusto have a Swiss legal entity, or would they use an Employer of Record (EOR) service? This affects onboarding timeline and some benefits. Raise it factually, not as a blocker.

---

## Opening Anchor

**If asked for salary expectations early — deflect first:**

> "I'd rather understand the full scope of the role and the team before we get into numbers. That said, I'm not going to waste your time: for a Staff Engineer at this level, I'd expect to be at the top of your SF band or above, with base in the $240,000-$260,000 range plus equity."

If they push for a single number before you know their exact offer:

> "$250,000 base is where I'm anchoring. I'm open to conversation on the equity side if the base needs some flexibility."

Do not anchor below $220,000 at first mention, regardless of geographic pressure.

---

## If They Make the First Offer

Wait. Don't react immediately. After they share the number:

> "Thanks, I appreciate the transparency. Can you walk me through how the full package breaks down, base, equity grant, vesting schedule, and any performance component?"

This gives you time to process and confirms you're negotiating the full picture, not just the base.

---

## If They Come in at the Low End of the SF Band (below $220,000 USD base)

> "I appreciate the offer and I want to find a way to make this work, but I'll be direct: that's below what I need to move forward. Based on the role scope and my background, I'd need to see a base closer to $240,000. Is there room there, or should we look at how equity can close the gap?"

**If they apply the remote/Denver tier to a Geneva-based arrangement:**

> "I understand the geographic tier logic, but the output of a Staff Engineer doesn't have a geographic discount. The architecture decisions, the cross-team platform work, the AI-agent tooling — those are priced at Staff level regardless of where I'm sitting. Geneva's cost of living is at parity with San Francisco on most indices, so the tier applied here matters quite a bit."

Supporting data if they push back: Geneva is consistently ranked among the top five most expensive cities in the world (Mercer, ECA International rankings). Cost of living comparisons to SF show effective parity or Geneva slightly above for housing and general expenses.

---

## Equity Negotiation

Gusto may offer RSUs vesting into shares after a liquidity event, or post-IPO public shares if they've gone public by the time you're negotiating. Target:

- **Grant size:** $200,000-$300,000 USD at grant value for Staff+ joining is reasonable at this company size
- **Vesting schedule:** 4-year vest with 1-year cliff is standard; ask about quarterly vesting after cliff
- **Refresher grants:** ask explicitly whether Gusto has a refresher or top-up grant policy for Staff+ engineers and at what cadence

Questions to ask before accepting:
- "What's the current 409A or share price you're using for RSU calculations?"
- "Is there a refresher grant policy, and what does it look like for someone at Staff level?"
- "What does vesting acceleration look like in an acquisition scenario?"

If the initial equity grant feels low:

> "Can you look at increasing the equity component to offset the base? I'm flexible on the cash/equity mix as long as total year-one and year-four comp lands where it needs to."

---

## Geographic Premium Argument

If Gusto tries to apply more than a 10-15% geographic discount relative to SF rates:

> "I want to flag that Geneva's cost of living is effectively at SF parity. Whatever geographic adjustment framework you're applying, I'd want to make sure it's calibrated to actual cost of living rather than just country of residence. I'm happy to share third-party cost-of-living data if that helps the internal conversation."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

**If you have another Staff-level offer:**

> "I want to be transparent: I have another offer at the Staff level that came in at [number]. I'd prefer to join Gusto if we can align on comp, but I'm on a timeline. Is there flexibility to move quickly on the package?"

**If you don't have a competing offer, use market anchors instead:**

> "The range I'm seeing across similar-scope Staff roles at companies of comparable size and maturity is $230,000-$260,000 base. I'm not using that to pressure you, but it tells me the number I shared is grounded. I'd rather solve this with you now than wait and have to come back to it."

---

## Bottom Line / Walk-Away Thresholds

Set these before the negotiation, not during it.

| Component | Target | Minimum to Accept |
|---|---|---|
| Base salary | $240,000-$250,000 USD | $200,000 USD |
| Equity grant | $250,000 USD RSUs (4-yr vest) | $150,000 USD RSUs |
| Total year-1 comp | $270,000+ USD | $230,000 USD |

If they can't meet the base minimum but the equity is substantial, assess the risk honestly. An RSU grant at a multi-billion-dollar valuation with no clear IPO timeline is a different risk profile from one with a concrete path to liquidity within 18-24 months. Ask directly: "What's the current thinking on a liquidity event for RSU holders?"

---

## EOR / Remote Employment Note

If Gusto doesn't have a Swiss legal entity, they'll likely use an EOR (such as Deel or Remote.com) to employ you in Switzerland. This is common and generally workable, but:

- Confirm that benefits (health, pension contributions) are handled correctly under Swiss law
- Some EOR agreements subtract their fee from your gross comp; verify whether the salary quoted is gross-to-you or gross-to-Gusto
- Swiss social charges (AVS/AI/APG) are roughly 10.5% of gross, split between employer and employee; confirm whether the quoted salary is before or after employer contributions
