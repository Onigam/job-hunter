# Negotiation Script: Inworld AI, Staff/Principal ML Engineer, Serving

## Context worth naming up front

This one is unusual on the seniority axis: Magino has genuine Staff-plus scope and a founder-level track record, but the role wants specific serving-framework depth (vLLM, TRT-LLM, CUDA, quantization, speculative decoding) that isn't his core background. That cuts both ways. Inworld is a well-funded, fast-growing AI lab competing globally for scarce serving talent, and that scarcity usually pushes comp up regardless of exact profile match. On the other hand, the honest gap in framework-specific experience means the opening anchor should sit at the Staff end rather than Principal, with a clear path to closing the gap fast rather than pretending it isn't there. Get clarity early on whether they're hiring for Staff or Principal specifically, since the posting bundles both, and that distinction alone can be a six-figure swing in a market this hot. Also worth noting the posting states visa sponsorship isn't available for the Switzerland-based role, but relocation support to the Bay Area may open up later, which is a separate lever worth asking about if the conversation goes that direction.

## Market anchors (Switzerland, ML infra / serving, 2026)

Generic Swiss "Machine Learning Engineer" salary data clusters around CHF 100,000-150,000, but that reflects broad ML roles, not Staff/Principal serving specialists at a venture-funded AI lab competing on a global pay scale. That baseline is close to irrelevant here. Well-funded AI infra startups hiring remotely into Switzerland tend to benchmark closer to US or pan-European senior AI comp than to local Swiss averages, since they're competing with US labs for the same small pool of people who can actually tune a serving stack. A realistic base band for Staff level sits around CHF 190,000-230,000, with Principal running CHF 230,000-270,000 or higher, both usually paired with a meaningful equity grant given the company's stage. Since the role is likely EOR-based remote rather than a Swiss entity hire, ask early how the EOR structures base versus bonus versus equity, since those arrangements sometimes cap certain comp types or add employer-side costs that quietly show up as a lower headline number. Working anchor to open around: CHF 200,000-220,000 base if landing at Staff scope, adjusted upward if they confirm Principal-level ownership, with equity discussed separately below.

## Opening anchor

"Before I throw out a number, I want to understand two things: whether you're hiring for Staff or Principal specifically, since the posting covers both and that's a meaningful scope difference, and how compensation is structured for a Switzerland-based remote hire. That'll tell me where to anchor."

If pushed for a number regardless:

"Given Staff-level scope and how competitive the market is for serving specialists right now, I'd want to start around CHF 200,000 to 220,000 base, with equity and any bonus structured on top."

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

If the offer lands under roughly CHF 180,000 base:

"I appreciate the offer. I want to flag that this reads closer to a general ML engineer band than a Staff-level serving hire, and given how competitive the market is for this specific skill set right now, I was expecting something higher, closer to CHF 200,000 or above. Is there room to move, or is part of the gap something equity or a sign-on could close?"

If they anchor hard on the framework-experience gap to justify a lower number:

"That's fair to raise, and I'd rather be direct about it than have it surface later: my depth is in platform and distributed systems, not CUDA kernels specifically, and vLLM and TRT-LLM are areas I'd be closing fast rather than arriving with mastered. If that changes where you can land on base, I'd rather talk about a defined 90-day ramp with a comp review attached than take a permanently discounted number for a gap that's meant to close."

## Equity negotiation

Ask directly what the last priced round was and whether the option strike price reflects that valuation or an earlier, cheaper one. Standard vesting is four years with a one year cliff; push back if it's longer, and ask specifically about early exercise rights, since that matters more the higher the strike price climbs. Confirm whether it's options or RSUs, and if options, ask about the post-termination exercise window. Anything under the now-common 10-year window is worth negotiating, since a short window can force a costly early exercise decision later. Ask about refresh grant policy too, since Staff-plus hires at fast-growing companies are usually eligible for refreshers well before the initial grant fully vests.

## Geographic and structure pushback

If Swiss-based comp gets anchored down using a "remote hire" discount:

"I get that remote comp sometimes gets discounted relative to a US-based hire, but Geneva isn't a low cost-of-living market, and the role's scope doesn't shrink because I'm not in the Bay Area. I'd rather anchor on the skill set and ownership than on a location-based discount."

If they raise the framework-experience gap during the wider negotiation, not just on base:

"I'd rather solve that with a structured ramp than a discount that follows me indefinitely. If we agree on what Staff-level ownership of the serving stack looks like at 90 days, I'm comfortable being evaluated against that instead of a lower band baked in from day one."

## Competing offer template

Use only if a real competing process exists; a bluffed number falls apart the moment someone asks a follow-up question.

"I'm further along in another process at a comparable level, with a base offer around CHF 210,000. I'd rather move forward here, the problem you're solving on the serving side is more interesting to me than most of what else is out there, but the numbers need to land close enough that I'm not leaving something real on the table."
