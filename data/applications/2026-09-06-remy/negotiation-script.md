# Negotiation Script - Remy, Founding Backend/Infra Engineer

## Context
Remy (R-Space Technologies) is a New York-based, early-stage startup (founded 2025) building an AI submittal intelligence platform for construction, already live with customers like Clark Construction and Swinerton. This is a founding infra hire, not employee #1, but still early enough that comp will likely be structured around a seed or early Series A band: moderate cash, meaningful equity. The role is remote-friendly (Geneva-based candidate), which puts geography and currency on the table from the first conversation.

## Market anchors
Staff-level backend/infra engineers at US seed-to-Series-A startups typically land base salary in the $160k-200k USD range when hired remote, sometimes higher in NY given local cost of living. A founding engineer with a validated 0-to-1 track record (platform built and sold to a unicorn acquirer) sits at the top of that band, not the middle. For a Geneva-based hire, CHF equivalent context matters too: a Senior Staff Engineer in Geneva runs roughly CHF 170k-210k base at established companies, and Geneva's cost of living is among the highest in the world, higher than NYC on most indices. That's the anchor if Remy tries to apply a blanket "remote discount."

## Opening anchor
"Based on the scope, owning core backend and infra for an LLM-driven product with paying enterprise customers already live, and my track record building and scaling a platform from zero through acquisition, I'm looking at total comp in the $190k-210k base range, plus an equity grant appropriate for an early infra-owning hire, somewhere around 0.75%-1.5% depending on how the option pool is structured. I'd want to see the numbers and walk through them together rather than negotiate against a single numbers, so tell me where your band sits and we'll find the right shape."

## If they come in low
"I hear the number. Here's the thing: this role isn't a mid-level implementation hire, it's the person setting the patterns for code quality, deployment, and observability that the rest of the engineering team inherits. I've done that once already at Alaya, which is now inside a company valued over a billion dollars. I can flex on the cash if the equity or the vesting terms move to reflect that, but I'd need the total package to land closer to [target number] to make sense given where I'm relocating time and risk from."

## Equity negotiation
- Ask for the fully diluted percentage, not just the option count, and ask what the last priced round valued the company at.
- Standard is 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff; push back on anything longer (5-year vesting is a red flag at this stage).
- Ask about anti-dilution protection or at least a refresh conversation tied to the next round, since founding-adjacent equity gets diluted fast through seed and Series A.
- If they offer a lower base in exchange for more equity, get comfortable with the actual dollar value at the last valuation before agreeing, not just the percentage.

## Geographic discount pushback
"I want to flag this early: Geneva isn't a low cost-of-living market you're getting a discount on by hiring remote. Rent, health insurance, and taxes here run comparable to or above NYC. I'm not asking for a US-city premium, I'm asking that the offer reflect where I actually live rather than an assumption about remote-from-Europe pricing."

## Competing offer leverage template
"I'm in later-stage conversations with another infrastructure-focused team right now, and I like what Remy's building more, the customer traction is real and the problem is more interesting to me technically. If we can get to [target number] I'd rather stop those conversations and commit here. If we can't quite get there on cash, I'm open to talking about how the equity or start date could close the gap."

## Closing
Always confirm the full package in writing before verbally accepting: base, equity percentage and strike price, vesting schedule, benefits, and any relocation or home-office stipend. Verbal numbers move; the offer letter is what matters.
