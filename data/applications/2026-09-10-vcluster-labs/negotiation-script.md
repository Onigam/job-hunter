# Negotiation Script, vCluster Labs, Staff Software Engineer

## Market Context

**Role level:** Staff Software Engineer, reporting close to the CTO
**Location:** Geneva, Switzerland (role posted as remote-first, Europe)
**vCluster's posted range:** EUR 75,000 to 145,000 base, plus equity and bonus
**EUR/CHF rate (2026):** roughly 0.95, so the top of the posted range (EUR 145k) is about CHF 138k at face value

**Swiss market rates for equivalent role:**
- Staff Software Engineer, infrastructure/platform, Geneva: CHF 170,000 to 210,000
- Kubernetes-focused infra roles at growth-stage, venture-backed companies: CHF 180,000 to 220,000
- vCluster's own posted band sits well under Swiss market even at its ceiling

**Honest read before anchoring:** the required qualification is 3+ years deep Kubernetes and GoLang. Magino has strong production Kubernetes and Docker experience but his core languages are TypeScript, Java and Python, not Go. That gap is real and will likely surface in the technical rounds, so the opening ask should lean on the parts that are unambiguous strengths, founding-engineer ownership, platform architecture calls under pressure, CTO-level collaboration, customer-facing technical work, rather than trying to out-anchor on the Kubernetes/Go line item itself.

**Magino's justification for a high anchor:**
- 17+ years experience including a founding-engineer chapter with a $1B+ valuation exit
- Alaya scaled to 50+ enterprise clients before the Benevity acquisition
- Direct production Kubernetes and Docker experience, plus a track record of CTO-adjacent architecture decisions
- Geneva cost of living: consistently ranks among the five most expensive cities globally
- A posted range that already sits below typical Geneva market rates for this level

---

## Opening Anchor

**Target:** CHF 175,000 base (roughly EUR 184k), 0.10 to 0.15% equity

When asked for a number, anchor on Swiss market data rather than the posted EUR band:

> "The range I'm working from is Geneva market rate for a Staff infrastructure role at a venture-backed company, which lands around CHF 180-210k. Given the founding-engineer background and the Kubernetes work in production, I'd anchor around CHF 175k base. On equity, for a company at this stage with your last raise, I'd expect somewhere in the 0.1-0.15% range."

Don't open by referencing their posted EUR ceiling directly. Let them bring it up first.

---

## Counter-Offer Scripts

**If they come in near the top of the posted range (EUR 145k, about CHF 138k):**

> "I appreciate that. I want to be direct about the gap: CHF 138k is meaningfully under Geneva market rate for a Staff infrastructure engineer, even accounting for the fact that Go isn't the language I've spent the most time in. I'd need to see something closer to CHF 165-170k to make the math work living here. Is there room to move the base, or to make up the difference through equity and bonus?"

**If they cite the posted EUR band as fixed policy:**

> "That's useful context. My situation is that I'm cost-based in Geneva, not in a lower-cost European market, and the posted band doesn't really account for that spread. A Staff-level hire in Zurich or Geneva costs noticeably more than the same role in most of the rest of Europe. Can we look at whether there's a location-adjusted version of that band, or flexibility on the bonus and equity side instead?"

**If they raise the Kubernetes/Go gap as a reason to come in lower:**

> "That's a fair point to name directly. My Kubernetes experience is real and production-tested, Go is genuinely the piece I'd be ramping on fastest. I'd rather be upfront about that than oversell it. If it makes sense to structure the first few months with a slightly adjusted comp tied to a ramp period, with a defined bump once I'm shipping independently in Go, I'm open to that conversation."

**If they're firm on base and won't move it:**

Push on other levers in this order:
1. Equity increase: an additional 0.03 to 0.05% if base is capped
2. Signing bonus: CHF 15,000 to 20,000 one-time, useful for bridging the EUR/CHF gap in year one
3. Annual learning budget for Go and Kubernetes-focused conferences/training: CHF 3,000 to 4,000/year
4. Remote equipment and home office budget: CHF 2,000 to 3,000/year

> "If the base has to stay where it is, I'd want the equity to reflect the gap, maybe 0.15-0.18% instead of the opening number, or a signing bonus to cover the first year while the Go ramp-up happens."

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Ask for:** 0.10 to 0.15% (post-Series B-ish, $30M+ raised, Fortune 500 and AI cloud provider customers, so later stage than a seed-stage team)
**Structure:** 1-year cliff, 4-year vest, monthly vesting after cliff
**Key questions to ask before accepting any equity number:**

- What's the current fully diluted cap table, and how much of it is option pool versus investor preference stack?
- What's the strike price, and is there a 409A or equivalent valuation attached?
- Are these ISOs or NSOs, and what's the exercise window after departure?
- Does Khosla or the other lead investors hold participating preferred, and how does that affect common shareholder outcomes?
- Is there any anti-dilution protection or refresh policy for early non-founder hires?

**If the equity number comes in low (under 0.08%):**

> "For a Staff hire this close to the CTO on product direction, I'd expect equity that reflects real influence on where the company goes next, not just a standard IC grant. At this stage, the difference between 0.08% and 0.15% is significant in a reasonable exit scenario, and it's also part of what offsets the base being under Geneva market rate. Can we revisit that number?"

**Refresher expectation:** Ask explicitly for a refresher conversation at the 18-month mark, tied to demonstrated Go/Kubernetes ramp and delivered scope. Doesn't need to be in the offer letter, but say it out loud.

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

vCluster's band is EUR-denominated and likely built around a broader European average, not Swiss cost of living specifically. If that comes up:

> "I get that the band is meant to work across a distributed European team, and that's a reasonable default. My pushback isn't about wanting a premium for its own sake, it's that Geneva rent, healthcare and taxes sit closer to what you'd see in Zurich or a major US city than to what a EUR 90-100k midpoint assumes. I'd rather we calibrate to where I actually live than to a continent-wide average."

**Data point to cite if needed:** Swiss ICT salary surveys and local platforms like salary.ch consistently put Staff-level infrastructure engineering in Geneva at CHF 170-220k, well above the EUR-to-CHF conversion of vCluster's posted ceiling.

---

## Using Competing Offers

If other conversations are active, use them without overstating:

> "I've got a couple of other infrastructure-focused conversations moving in parallel, both anchored in the CHF 175-195k range. I'd rather join vCluster, the virtual cluster problem is a more interesting one to me than what the others are building. But I need the comp to be in that same neighborhood for it to be the obvious choice."

If there's no competing offer in hand, don't fabricate one. Fall back to market data:

> "The Geneva market data I'm working from puts this level at CHF 175-210k. That's the frame I'm anchoring to, independent of any other conversation."

---

## Timeline and Process Notes

Don't volunteer a number before understanding the scope of the CTO-facing part of the role, since that's what justifies the higher end of the ask. If pressed early:

> "I'd rather get a clearer picture of the scope, especially the product/CTO collaboration piece, before putting a number on the table. Can we come back to compensation once we're both further into the process?"

**Offer deadline:** Ask for at least 5 business days to review a written offer, more if equity documentation needs independent review.

> "I want to look at the equity terms properly rather than rush the decision. Five to seven business days works on my side, is there anything time-sensitive I should know about?"
