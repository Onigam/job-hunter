# Negotiation Script: Staff Backend Engineer, Nango

Context that shapes the numbers here: Nango is YC-backed (W23), around fourteen people, mid-seven-figure ARR, and has disclosed only a small early round, so this is an early-stage cap table rather than a company sitting on a large war chest. Public listings for this exact role have shown base ranges running from roughly $120,000 to $220,000 depending on country, which means they're running geographically adjusted bands, not one flat global number. The role is fully remote across 20+ countries, Switzerland included, with no office anywhere, so the Swiss band is the one worth anchoring on rather than the US number the posting leads with. This is a staff-level IC seat, not a management role, so comp should track scope and ownership, not headcount.

## Market anchor

Staff-level backend engineer, Geneva-based and remote, at a well-funded early-stage startup: roughly CHF 150,000 to 185,000 base is realistic, reflecting Swiss cost of living and 17+ years of experience that includes founder-level ownership. US-anchored bands for this role have topped out around $220,000, which at current exchange rates lands close to the top of that Swiss range, so there's real room to anchor high without asking for anything unusual. Early equity at a fourteen-person, YC-backed company should be sized for a staff hire joining this early, not a diluted later-stage grant. Opening anchor: CHF 165,000 base plus an equity grant sized accordingly.

## Opening anchor script

"Given the scope of this role, joining as a staff-level IC on a fourteen-person engineering team, I'd expect a base around CHF 165,000 if it's priced against Geneva, plus an equity grant that reflects joining this early. I noticed your ranges vary quite a bit by country. Can you tell me which band Switzerland falls under, and whether that figure is base only or total comp?"

## If they come in low

"I hear the number. Is that the Swiss-adjusted band, or closer to where a lower-cost country in your range would land? I'm anchoring the ask to the scope of the role, owning core backend infrastructure at staff level, not just the base geography."

If they hold the line: "I can flex on base if the equity grant is sized for a staff-level early hire rather than a standard senior slice, or if there's a defined comp review after the first six months tied to scope. What I'd rather avoid is landing below CHF 150,000 equivalent, that's under market for this level where I'm based."

## Equity notes

Ask for the current valuation basis, whether that's the last priced round or a 409A-equivalent, plus the strike price and vesting schedule; four years with a one-year cliff is standard, worth confirming Nango follows it. With only a small amount disclosed raised against mid-seven-figure ARR, the company is likely capital-efficient and hasn't diluted heavily, so it's worth asking directly what percentage or share count a staff-level grant represents rather than accepting a dollar figure alone. Ask about the post-termination exercise window too; early-stage grants sometimes carry short windows that matter more in practice than the headline grant size. Since Nango's product is open source, it's also worth asking whether any roadmap or ownership work is tied to community-facing contributions that factor into vesting or promotion criteria.

## Geographic discount pushback

"I noticed the posted ranges span from about $120,000 to $220,000 depending on country. Geneva is one of the more expensive cities globally, on par with or above most US tech hubs for cost of living, so I'd want the band here treated as high-cost rather than discounted the way some remote-first companies default to. Happy to share comparable cost-of-living data if that's useful."

## Competing offer, if genuinely in play

"I'm in later-stage conversations with another company at a comparable scope and a similar range to what I mentioned. This role is the one I'm most drawn to given the specific infrastructure problem you're solving, but I wanted to be upfront about timing so we can move at a pace that works for both of us."

## Close

Being early-stage and YC-backed is a reason to push on equity, not to accept a discounted base; a team this size running production traffic for hundreds of paying customers has already proven the product works, so the risk premium on base salary should be lower than at a true pre-revenue startup, even while the equity upside stays real. Get the base, equity percentage or share count, vesting schedule, and exercise window terms in writing before accepting. Early-stage offers move fast, and verbal numbers from a recruiter call have a way of shifting by the time the written offer shows up.
