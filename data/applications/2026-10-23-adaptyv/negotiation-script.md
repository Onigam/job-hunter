# Negotiation Script: Adaptyv, Head of Software Engineering, Lausanne

## The shape of this offer

The LinkedIn listing places the role in Lausanne, full-time, at Director seniority. Some boards also list it as hybrid or remote. No salary is published. The page header also shows "Boydton, VA", which contradicts the body, so confirm with the recruiter that it's a Swiss contract with a Swiss employer.

Adaptyv is a startup that has just raised. In 2025 it came out of beta after an $8M seed led by Ace Ventures, and in 2026 it announced a Series A of about €34M (reported elsewhere as roughly $40M) with Highland Europe. **Check the exact amount and date in the official press release before any call.** Fresh Series A money matters in two ways: there's cash to pay a real Head of Engineering salary, and the equity hasn't yet been diluted by a B round.

The package has five levers:

1. **Title and scope.** "Head of Software Engineering" with roadmap ownership. Clarify whether it reports to the CEO or CTO and whether a VP Engineering or CTO title is planned as the team grows.
2. **Base salary.**
3. **Equity**: percentage, strike price, vesting, cliff, and what happens on an acquisition.
4. **Sign-on or relocation**: small here, since Geneva to Lausanne is a commute and not a move.
5. **Work pattern**: days on-site in Lausanne versus remote from Geneva.

Three things to settle early:

1. **Level and reporting line.** A player-coach who owns the roadmap and hires the team is an executive hire at a startup this size. Make sure the offer is calibrated as one (Director / Head of), not as a senior engineer with a nicer title.
2. **Equity basis.** Ask for the fully diluted share count and the Series A post-money valuation. Without those, a number of options means nothing.
3. **On-site rhythm.** Geneva to Lausanne is about 40 minutes by train. Two or three days a week is fine; ask whether they expect more, especially if lab work means being next to the instruments.

## Market anchors

These are approximations. **Check them against levels.fyi, Glassdoor CH, the Swiss startup salary surveys and, ideally, one or two founders or Heads of Engineering at EPFL Innovation Park companies.**

| Role / market | Base (CHF) | Equity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Head of Engineering, Swiss Series A startup (Lausanne/Geneva) | 180k to 230k | 0.5% to 1.5% | Typical range for a first engineering leader |
| VP Engineering / CTO hire, Swiss Series A | 200k to 260k | 1% to 2.5% | Higher if hired before the team exists |
| Staff / Principal IC, Swiss scale-up | 170k to 220k | small | The realistic alternative path |
| Big tech Zürich, Staff / Principal | 230k to 300k base, plus stock | RSUs | Useful as an upper reference, not a direct comparison |
| Online aggregator estimate for this posting | €85k to 105k | n/a | Not from the company; looks like a generic EU estimate and is well below Swiss market for this level |

Target: **base CHF 210k to 230k, equity around 1% (fully diluted), standard four-year vesting.** Walk-away floor: roughly CHF 185k base with at least 0.6%, or a lower base only if the equity goes up substantially.

Swiss specifics worth raising:

- **Option taxation.** In Switzerland, employee options are usually taxed as income when exercised (or sometimes at grant, depending on the plan and canton). Ask how Adaptyv's plan is structured and whether it has a tax ruling with the Vaud tax office. If you stay resident in Geneva, Geneva taxes apply.
- **Pension (LPP/BVG).** Ask for the employer contribution rate. Many startups pay the legal minimum; asking for more is a quiet way to add value.
- **Notice period.** Three months is common. Make the start date and any sign-on timing account for it.

## Opening anchor

When asked for expectations, give a number tied to the role, not to past salary:

> "For a Head of Engineering role at a Series A company in Switzerland, where I'm owning the roadmap, hiring the team and still writing code, I'm looking at a base around CHF 230k, with equity around 1% on a fully diluted basis. I'm flexible on the split between the two, and I'd like to see the full picture before we lock anything in."

Why 230k: it's the top of the Head of Engineering range and the bottom of the VP/CTO range, which fits a cofounder with an exit who's being asked to build the team from the start.

If they push for a number before you've seen their range:

> "I'd rather hear how you've budgeted the role. You've just closed the Series A, so I assume there's a band. If it's in the right area, we won't have trouble agreeing."

## Counter-offer script (if they come in low)

If the offer is, say, CHF 170k base and 0.4%:

> "Thanks, I can see the thinking behind it. The base is below what Swiss Series A companies are paying for the first engineering leader, and 0.4% is closer to a senior engineer grant than to someone who'll own the roadmap and hire the team. I'd want to get to CHF 210k on base and around 1% on equity. If base is the hard constraint right now, I'm open to a lower number there in exchange for more equity, or a scheduled step-up once the team reaches a certain size."

Fallback trades, in order of preference:

1. More equity in exchange for a lower base.
2. A written base review at 12 months, or tied to a milestone (team of six hired, a specific lab automation milestone shipped).
3. A sign-on bonus to close a base gap in year one.
4. Extra vacation or a learning budget (biology courses, conferences like NeurIPS or SynBioBeta).

## Equity negotiation

Questions to ask, in this order:

1. "What's the fully diluted share count, and what was the Series A post-money valuation?"
2. "Is this options or shares, and what's the strike price?"
3. "What's the vesting schedule and cliff?" Expect four years with a one-year cliff. Ask whether the cliff can be six months, given the role is senior and you're joining right after the raise.
4. "What happens on an acquisition?" Ask for **double-trigger acceleration**: if the company is acquired and the role is eliminated or materially changed within 12 months, unvested equity vests (fully or at least 50%). Having lived through an acquisition, you can say this from experience:

> "I've been through an acquisition from the founder side. It went well, but I saw how much depends on what's written down beforehand. Double-trigger acceleration is standard for leadership hires, and I'd like it in the agreement."

5. "How long do I have to exercise vested options if I leave?" Ask for an extended window (several years instead of 90 days). Startups increasingly agree to this.
6. **Anti-dilution.** Employee options rarely get anti-dilution protection, and asking for it can look naive. Ask instead whether there's a refresh grant policy after future rounds, which does the same job in practice.

## Geographic discount pushback

If they suggest a lower number because the role is "partly remote" or because Lausanne is cheaper than Zürich:

> "I live in Geneva, so I'll be in Lausanne regularly and paying Geneva costs, which are among the highest in the country. The role is the same whether I'm at a desk in Lausanne or Geneva. I'd keep the number tied to the role and the market for Swiss Series A leadership."

If the contract ends up with a non-Swiss entity (the "Boydton, VA" mention), insist on a Swiss contract or an employer of record with Swiss social security, and don't accept a US or EU salary band for a Swiss-based hire.

## Competing offer template

Use only what's true at the time. If there's a real parallel process:

> "To be transparent, I'm also in late stages with another company for a Principal-level role. Adaptyv is my first choice because of the problem and the stage. I'd like to make this work, and it would help if we could get closer on base and equity."

If there's no offer yet but active processes:

> "I'm talking to a couple of other companies at a similar level. I'm not trying to start an auction, I just want to be honest that timing matters on my side too."

Never invent a specific competing number.

## Before the call

1. Read the Series A announcement and note the amount, lead investor and stated use of funds.
2. Ask the recruiter about team size today and the hiring plan for the next 12 months. A plan to hire five or more engineers supports the upper end of the range.
3. Prepare the 90-day plan from the STAR stories: learn the experiment flow end to end, map the software and instrument stack, then propose roadmap cuts.
4. Decide your walk-away number before the call, not during it.
