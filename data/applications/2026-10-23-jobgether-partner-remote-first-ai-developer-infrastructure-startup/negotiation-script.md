# Negotiation Script: Senior+ Full-Stack Engineer, AI Developer-Infrastructure Startup (via Jobgether)

## What the posting tells you (and what it doesn't)

- Title is **Senior+ Full-Stack Engineer**, highly autonomous, with influence on architecture, product strategy and roadmap. That's Staff-shaped scope under a Senior title. Use that gap when talking numbers.
- Remote-first, but the posting asks for someone **based in Switzerland**. Good news: it means they've budgeted for a Swiss hire, not a European one.
- Stack: TypeScript, Node.js, React. Product: APIs, SDKs, CLI and MCP integrations that connect AI apps and agents to external APIs. Customer-facing component.
- No company name, stage, funding, salary band or equity range listed. Jobgether is an intermediary that shortlists; the partner runs interviews and makes the offer. Keep numbers out of the Jobgether conversation as much as you can.

## Questions to ask before any number comes up

1. Who is the company, what stage, how much raised, and how many months of runway?
2. Team size today, and where would this hire sit? Is there a Staff level above "Senior+", and what would it take to get there?
3. How would I be employed: Swiss entity, employer of record (Deel, Remote, Oyster), or contractor?
4. Salary in CHF or USD/EUR? If not CHF, who carries the currency risk?
5. Swiss benefits included: BVG/LPP pension, accident insurance, 25 days of holiday or more?
6. Equity details: options or shares, strike price, last preferred price, fully diluted share count, vesting schedule.
7. How much of the customer-facing work falls in US hours?

## Swiss market reference

Working estimates for Geneva, Vaud and Zurich, CHF base. Verify on levels.fyi, Glassdoor and with one or two Swiss recruiters before the first comp call.

| Profile | Base salary (CHF) |
|---|---|
| Senior full-stack engineer, Swiss startup | 130k to 160k |
| Senior+ / Staff at a funded dev-tools or AI-infra startup, Swiss hire | 150k to 185k |
| Staff engineer at a US-backed AI company hiring in Switzerland | 170k to 210k + equity |

Through an EOR, the employer pays roughly 13 to 16% on top in social charges and pension. That's their cost, not a reason to lower your base, but expect it to come up.

**Your numbers (decide them before the call, don't move them during it):**

1. **Anchor:** CHF 185k base plus meaningful equity (see below).
2. **Target:** CHF 165k to 175k base, salary paid in CHF, full Swiss benefits.
3. **Walk-away:** below CHF 145k base with no equity upside to compensate.

## When they ask for your expectations first

Try to get them to show their range first:

"Before I put a number on it, could you share the band you've budgeted for this role in Switzerland? It'd help to know whether it's set for a Swiss hire specifically or adapted from a wider European range."

If they push:

"For a remote role based in Geneva, with the scope you describe, owning architecture across APIs, SDKs and the MCP layer and shaping the roadmap, I'm looking at around CHF 185k base plus equity. I co-founded and sold a platform, and I've worked as a Senior Staff IC since, so I'm pricing it at the scope rather than the title."

Then stop talking and let them answer.

## If the offer comes in low

Suppose they come in around CHF 145k to 150k:

"Thanks, I'm interested and I'd like to make this work. The base is below what I'd expect for this scope in Switzerland. The posting describes influence over architecture, product and roadmap, which in most Swiss companies sits at Staff level and pays in the 170s. Could we get to CHF 170k? If the base really is tight, I'm open to taking part of the gap as equity."

If they say the band is fixed:

"Understood. Then let's look at the other pieces: a larger equity grant, a signing bonus, and a written compensation review at six months with clear criteria, for example the MCP and SDK work shipped. Any of those would help close the gap."

If they question the "Senior+" vs Staff framing:

"Fair enough, titles vary. What I'd point at is the work itself. I've built a platform and its API layer from zero to 50+ enterprise clients and an acquisition, I've made the kind of performance and decomposition calls you're describing, and I've moved two teams onto new developer tooling without any authority to make them. That's the scope in your posting."

## Geographic discount pushback

If they want to pay a European rate:

"The posting asks for someone based in Switzerland, and Geneva is consistently among the most expensive cities in the world. I'd expect the number to be benchmarked against Swiss rates, not a blended European band. Hiring in Lisbon or Warsaw is a different conversation, and a fair one, but it's not this one."

If they want to pay in USD or EUR:

"That works if it's indexed or reviewed every year against CHF, since all my costs are in francs. Otherwise I'd rather have the contract in CHF at today's equivalent."

## Equity

Without a stage, use these rough references for a senior early hire:

- Seed: ~0.3% to 1.0%
- Series A: ~0.15% to 0.4%
- Series B and later: ~0.05% to 0.15%

Find out the stage first, then negotiate in percentage of fully diluted shares, never a raw option count.

Points to push on:

- **Size:** aim for the upper half of the range for their stage, and say why (founding-engineer track record, scope beyond the title).
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask whether the cliff can be shortened, or waived if you're let go without cause.
- **Exercise window:** ask for 5 to 10 years after leaving instead of the default 90 days. This matters more than most people expect.
- **Acceleration:** double-trigger (acquisition plus termination). You've been through an acquisition, so you can explain exactly why it matters.
- **Anti-dilution:** employees almost never get it, so don't spend goodwill asking. Ask instead for a **refresh grant** after the next priced round, committed in writing.
- **Tax:** in Switzerland, taxation of options in unlisted companies depends on the plan and the canton, and is often triggered at exercise. If the plan is a US one and you're on an EOR, have a Geneva tax advisor look at it before you sign.
- **Data:** ask for the last preferred share price and the strike so you can estimate what the grant is worth on paper.

Script:

"I've sat on the founder side of a cap table and through an exit, so I'm comfortable taking part of my pay as risk, but I want the grant to match the scope. For your stage I'd be looking at [X]%, four-year vesting with a one-year cliff, double-trigger acceleration and an extended exercise window. Can we get there?"

## Competing offer template (only if real)

"I want to be upfront. I'm in late-stage conversations with another company for a Staff-level role in Switzerland, and their offer is around CHF [X] base. I'd prefer your role, because building developer infrastructure for agents is closer to what I want to be doing every day. Could we close the gap on base or equity so the decision is easy?"

Only use this if it's true. Getting caught bluffing at a small startup costs trust with the people you'd be working next to.

## After the call

- Ask for the full offer in writing: base, currency, employer entity, benefits, equity terms and plan documents.
- Don't accept on the call. "Thanks, let me go through it properly and come back to you by [day]" is enough.
- Have the equity plan and any EOR contract checked by someone who knows Swiss employment and tax law.
