## Opening Anchor

**Target ask: €230,000 base**

The posted ceiling is €226K. Anchoring at €230K asks them to justify why you shouldn't be above it, which is a stronger position than landing exactly at the top and having no room to move. It also signals that you've done your research.

**Script (after receiving offer letter):**

> "Thanks for the offer, I've had a good look at the package. To be straightforward about it: based on my background and the Geneva cost-of-living context, I was expecting something closer to €230,000 on the base. I know that's above your stated range, but I think the profile justifies it. Is there flexibility on the base, or would it make sense to look at a higher equity grant to close the gap?"

## Equity Negotiation

**What Ashby offers:** Competitive stock options with a 10-year exercise window.

The 10-year exercise window is genuinely unusual. Most early/growth-stage startups use the standard 90-day post-departure window, which forces employees to either buy their options (often expensive) or forfeit them. A 10-year window removes that pressure almost entirely and is worth real money. Acknowledge it, but don't let them use it to justify a lower base.

**What to ask:**
- Confirm the grant size in number of options (not just "competitive")
- Ask for the most recent 409A valuation — gives a baseline for the strike price
- Confirm cliff (standard is 1 year) and total vesting period (standard is 4 years)
- Ask about refresh grants: "Is there a standard refresh cadence at staff level?"

**Equity counter script (if base is firm at ceiling):**

> "If the base is at ceiling, I'd want to revisit the equity grant size. The 10-year window is genuinely good — that's a real differentiator. To make the total package work relative to what I'd earn in a CHF-denominated role, I'd want the initial grant to reflect that I'm coming in ready to contribute independently from day one."
