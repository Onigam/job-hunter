# Negotiation Script: Staff Engineer AI & Dev Tools (Python), French CI/CD SaaS via LITY

## What the posting tells us

- **Recruiter:** LITY (headhunter, Sara Van Der Sluis). The client company is undisclosed.
- **Client:** French, profitable, founded about six years ago, SaaS suite for CI/CD automation in the GitHub ecosystem, 150+ clients, deliberately compact team. Listed location is Toulouse, with full remote possible.
- **Comp in the posting:** "up to €100k" plus equity.
- **Likely identity (unconfirmed):** the profile (Toulouse, profitable, GitHub CI/CD automation, Python-heavy) is consistent with Mergify, but don't name a company to the recruiter until they disclose it. Ask instead.

"Up to €100k" is a ceiling written for a French hire. At roughly 0.94 CHF per EUR, that's about CHF 94k gross, which is below what a mid-level engineer earns in Geneva. The real negotiation here isn't a few thousand euros on base. It's the employment structure, because that decides whether this offer can work from Switzerland at all.

## Switzerland market reference (Staff IC, 2026)

| Employer type | Base, Geneva/Zurich |
|---|---|
| Local scale-ups and fintech, Staff/Lead | CHF 160k to 190k |
| Big tech Zurich, Staff (L6 equivalent) | CHF 200k to 240k base, plus RSUs |
| Foreign remote startups paying into Switzerland | EUR 120k to 160k is common for Staff |
| Swiss senior freelance (software, long engagement) | CHF 900 to 1,200 per day |

French Staff engineers at profitable scale-ups usually land around EUR 80k to 110k, so their €100k cap is fair in France. It isn't fair in Geneva, where rent, mandatory health insurance (paid by the employee, roughly CHF 450 to 650 a month for one adult) and social contributions take a much bigger bite.

## The structure question (raise it on the first call)

A French company can't just put a Geneva resident on a French payroll. The realistic options:

1. **Employment with Swiss social security.** The French employer registers with the Geneva compensation fund (AVS/AI/APG, unemployment, LPP pension), or pays through an employer of record. This costs them admin, and the employer share of Swiss contributions is lower than French employer charges, which is a point in your favour: a gross salary in Switzerland costs them less on top than the same gross in France.
2. **Contractor.** You invoice through a Swiss sole proprietorship or Sàrl. Simpler for them, but you carry your own pension, insurance and gaps, so the day rate has to cover it. Equity is harder: BSPCE are only for employees and officers, so a contractor would typically get BSA warrants, which have to be bought at a price.
3. **Employer of record (Deel, Remote, etc.).** Common middle ground. They pay the EOR fee (often EUR 500 to 700 a month); you get a proper Swiss contract.

Preferred order: option 1 or 3 with a Swiss-market salary, then option 2 at a day rate that matches.

## Targets

- **Walk-away (employee):** EUR 115,000 gross (about CHF 108k). Below that, it's a meaningful pay cut versus the local market and only makes sense if the equity is unusually strong.
- **Target (employee):** EUR 125,000 to 130,000.
- **Opening anchor (employee):** EUR 140,000, justified by Geneva cost of living and the Staff scope (architecture plus owning the AI integration roadmap).
- **Contractor alternative:** open at CHF 1,000 per day; floor around CHF 850 per day at roughly 210 billable days.
- **Equity:** treat as a bonus rather than the core of the package. A profitable, bootstrapped-feeling company may have no planned liquidity event, so ask how anyone has ever turned equity into cash.

## Script: early expectations (recruiter screen)

"Before I give a number, can you tell me how they'd employ someone in Switzerland? An employer of record, Swiss payroll, or a contractor setup? That changes the figure quite a bit. As a rough guide, for a Staff role with this scope and given I'm in Geneva, I'm looking at around 140 thousand euros gross on a Swiss contract, plus equity."

If pushed for one number: "140 on a Swiss contract. If they'd rather work on a contractor basis, it's a day rate, around a thousand francs."

## Script: they say "the budget is €100k max"

"I understand that's the French band and it makes sense for a French hire. The problem is it's about 94 thousand francs, which is below a mid-level salary in Geneva, and the employer cost of Swiss social charges is lower than in France, so a higher gross here doesn't cost them as much as it looks. I'm not asking for a Zurich big-tech number. 125 would work for me, and I'd be flexible on the mix if the equity is meaningful."

## Script: they come in at €105k to €110k

"Thanks, that's moving in the right direction. I'd like to close the gap to 125. If base is really fixed, I'd look at a larger equity grant, a signing bonus to cover the first year's setup costs, or a guaranteed review at six months against agreed outcomes on the AI integration work. Which of those is easiest for them?"

## Geographic discount pushback

If they argue "it's remote, so we pay the French rate":

"Remote means they get someone in their timezone without relocating anyone. It doesn't change what it costs to live where I already live. What I'd point out is that Geneva is the same timezone and a short flight or train from Toulouse and Paris, so in-person weeks are easy. They're getting a local-feeling hire, not a far-away contractor."

## Equity questions (BSPCE or BSA)

Ask for these in writing before valuing anything:

- Instrument type (BSPCE for employees, BSA for contractors) and strike price.
- Number of shares granted and total fully diluted share count, so you can express the grant as a percentage.
- Latest valuation or the price of the last round, if there was one. If they've never raised, ask how the strike price was set.
- Vesting schedule and cliff (standard is four years with a one-year cliff). Push for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- Exercise window after leaving. French BSPCE often lapse quickly after departure; ask for as long as they allow.
- Acceleration on change of control (single or double trigger).
- Any history of buybacks or secondary sales for employees. For a profitable company with no plans to raise, this is the only realistic path to liquidity.

Tax note: as a Geneva resident, employee options are generally taxed in Switzerland as income at exercise, not under the French BSPCE regime. Confirm with a Swiss tax advisor before signing; the French tax advantages of BSPCE probably won't apply.

## Competing offer template

"I want to be transparent: I'm in conversations with a couple of other companies at Staff level, and the Swiss-based ones are landing between 160 and 190 thousand francs. I'd rather do this one, because the problem is closer to what I want to work on. I'm not trying to start a bidding war; I just need the package to be in a range I can say yes to."

Only use this if it's true at the time. Don't name companies.

## Non-salary levers worth asking for

- Home office and equipment budget, plus a coworking allowance in Geneva.
- Travel to Toulouse or team offsites fully covered, with an agreed frequency.
- Conference and training budget (useful for the Python depth gap).
- Clarity on the support rotation: how often, and whether out-of-hours on-call is part of it and paid.
- Title confirmed as Staff in the contract.
