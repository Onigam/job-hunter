# Negotiation Script: Senior/Staff TypeScript Engineer, undisclosed AI-native startup (via Jobgether)

Location: fully remote, Europe preferred; listed under Switzerland. Candidate based in Geneva.
Posted comp: USD 100,000 to 140,000, "flexibility up to approximately $200,000 for exceptional candidates".
Structure: contractor or contract-to-hire.

---

## 1. Reading the posted number

Three things matter more than the headline range.

**It's in USD and it's a contractor number.** At a rough 0.80 to 0.90 CHF per USD (check the rate on the day), USD 140k is about CHF 112k to 126k. That's below a Geneva *employee* salary for a Senior engineer, and a contractor has to pay for everything an employer normally covers.

**The contractor gross-up.** As a self-employed person in Switzerland, Magino would carry the full AHV/IV/EO contributions (roughly 10% of income), no employer pension (BVG/LPP) contribution, own accident and loss-of-earnings insurance, no paid holidays or public holidays, no 13th salary and no notice-period protection. The usual rule of thumb is that a contract rate needs to be about 1.3x to 1.4x the equivalent salary to break even.

**The top of the band is the real band.** "Up to ~$200k for exceptional candidates" is the number to negotiate around. A Senior Staff title, a cofounder exit to a $1B+ company and hands-on AI-assisted development at org level is the profile that sentence was written for.

Also worth finding out early: who the actual company is, whether the contract is direct or through Jobgether/an intermediary, and whether an intermediary margin sits between the client's budget and the rate on offer.

## 2. Market anchors (2026 working estimates, re-check before the call)

Employee base salaries, CHF:

| Level | Geneva / Romandie | Zürich scale-up / big tech |
|---|---|---|
| Senior full-stack / backend | CHF 130k to 160k | CHF 150k to 190k |
| Staff engineer | CHF 160k to 200k | CHF 190k to 240k |

Swiss senior freelance TypeScript/Node rates typically land around CHF 900 to 1,300 per day, with specialist or AI-heavy work at the upper end.

**Contractor equivalent of a Staff salary.** CHF 175k employee × ~1.35 ≈ CHF 235k, roughly USD 270k to 290k. The posted ceiling doesn't reach that, which is the argument for pushing hard to the top of their range and for getting conversion terms in writing.

**Target package (contractor phase):**

- Walk-away: USD 170k per year (about USD 800 per day on ~215 working days)
- Target: USD 190k to 200k (about USD 900 per day)
- Opening anchor: USD 210k, or USD 1,000 per day if they think in day rates

## 3. Opening the conversation

If asked for expectations on the first call:

> "Before a number, can I check the structure? I'd be invoicing from Switzerland as a contractor, which means I'm covering social contributions, pension and unpaid holidays myself. Given that, and given this is Staff-level scope, owning architecture, integrations and production delivery, I'm looking at the top of the range you mentioned. Around USD 210k a year, or USD 1,000 a day if you prefer a day rate."

If they push for a single number:

> "USD 210k annualised as a contractor. If we're talking about an employment contract through an EOR with Swiss benefits, the number changes, and I'm happy to work that out too."

## 4. If they come in low (for example USD 130k to 150k)

> "Thanks, I appreciate it. I'll be direct: USD 140k as a contractor in Switzerland works out to less than a Senior employee salary in Geneva once I've paid the contributions and insurance an employer would normally cover. The posting mentions flexibility up to around 200k for the right profile, and I think the scope here, owning backend, integrations and standards for AI-generated code, is exactly that profile. To make this work I'd need to be at USD 190k or above. Is there room to get there?"

If the rate is genuinely capped:

> "If the rate is fixed for the contract phase, can we agree the conversion terms now? I'd want the conversion date, the salary and the equity grant written into the contract, plus a rate review at three months."

## 5. Contract-to-hire terms (get these in writing)

- **Conversion date.** A fixed date or a clear trigger (e.g. 3 to 6 months), not "when it makes sense".
- **Conversion package.** Salary, equity, level (Staff) and employing entity agreed before signing the contract. In Switzerland this likely means an EOR such as Deel, Remote or Oyster unless the company has a Swiss entity; ask which, because EOR employment brings back AHV, BVG and paid leave.
- **Cliff credit.** Contract months should count toward equity vesting, or at least toward the cliff.
- **Currency.** Ask to invoice in CHF, or add an FX clause if the rate moves more than ~5%. A USD contract means carrying currency risk that a local employee wouldn't.
- **Termination.** Notice period on both sides during the contract phase (30 days is reasonable), and payment terms of 30 days or better.
- **IP clause.** Standard assignment for work done for the company, with a carve-out for prior work and open-source contributions.

## 6. Equity (early-stage, likely options)

An early AI-native startup will most likely offer stock options once converted. Questions before valuing any grant:

- **Size.** Number of options and the fully diluted share count, so the percentage is clear. For an early Staff hire, somewhere around 0.1% to 0.5% is typical depending on stage; a founding-engineer-level hire can be higher.
- **Strike price and last valuation.** What the strike is based on and when the last round closed.
- **Vesting.** Expect 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Push for cliff credit for the contract months (see above) and monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Exercise window.** Standard is 90 days after leaving. Ask for an extended window (5 to 10 years); some startups offer it and it matters a lot if you leave before an exit.
- **Acceleration.** Ask about double-trigger acceleration on change of control. It's a reasonable ask for a senior early hire.
- **Anti-dilution.** Employees don't usually get anti-dilution protection. Ask instead about refresh grants after future rounds.
- **Swiss tax.** Options are generally taxed as income at exercise, and the rules differ by plan type and canton. If the plan is US-based, ask whether anyone has checked Swiss treatment; a Geneva tax advisor is worth an hour before exercising.

## 7. Geographic discount pushback

If they suggest a lower "European" or remote rate:

> "I understand the budget is set for Europe as a whole. Switzerland's cost base is higher than almost anywhere else in Europe, and as a contractor I'm funding my own pension and social contributions on top. The value I'd bring doesn't change with where I sit, and the time zone overlap with a European team is the same. I'd rather talk about the scope and the rate than a location adjustment."

## 8. Competing offer template

Use only if true at the time; otherwise frame it as market context.

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company for a Staff role in Switzerland, and their number is higher than what we've discussed. This role is the one I'd pick on scope, because of the customer side and the AI-native setup. If you can get to USD [X], I can give you an answer by [date]."

Market-context version, no named offer:

> "Staff roles I'm looking at in Switzerland are coming in at CHF 180k to 200k as employees. As a contractor that's well above your current number, so I'd need you closer to the top of the range to make the comparison work."

## 9. Priorities if trade-offs are needed

1. Rate at or above USD 190k (or equivalent day rate)
2. Written conversion terms including level and equity
3. CHF invoicing or FX protection
4. Cliff credit and extended exercise window
5. Rate review at three months
