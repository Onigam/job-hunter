# Negotiation Script: Cohere, Forward Deployed Engineer, Agentic Platform

## Context
This is a customer-facing delivery role at a well-funded foundation model company (Cohere), building and deploying agentic workflows on their North platform for enterprise customers, with 20-40% travel for on-site engagement. The posting lists the role broadly and even tags it "entry level" on LinkedIn, which almost certainly reflects a scraping artifact rather than the actual scope, given the requirements (production RAG and agentic deployment, evaluation framework design, direct enterprise stakeholder ownership, regulated-industry exposure as a plus). Magino is Geneva-based, Senior Staff level, 17+ years, with a founder-to-acquisition track record (Alaya, sold to Benevity) and recent hands-on work building multi-agent systems, RAG pipelines, and evaluation harnesses. Negotiate as a senior, customer-facing technical hire, not a junior forward-deployed engineer, and get the actual level and title confirmed early since the posting's framing doesn't match the requirements.

## Market anchors (Switzerland / senior forward-deployed or FDE-equivalent)
Forward deployed and solutions-engineering-adjacent roles at frontier AI companies (Cohere, OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir's FDE track as the closest comparable) typically pay a premium over standard platform engineering roles because of the travel and direct customer ownership. For a senior IC based in or negotiating from Switzerland, base cash for this kind of role realistically lands CHF 170,000 to CHF 220,000, with total compensation including RSUs or equity pushing the package to CHF 220,000 to CHF 300,000+ depending on level and how Cohere structures grants for this seniority. Palantir-style FDE comparables in Europe often run higher on total comp specifically because of the travel load, and that's a fair reference point to raise even though Cohere isn't Palantir.

Opening anchor: CHF 195,000 base, plus a meaningful equity or RSU component, plus explicit terms on travel-related compensation (per diem, travel days counted as work days, business-class thresholds for long-haul). Justify with Swiss cost of living, the founder-to-acquisition track record, and the fact that this role owns customer relationships and technical delivery simultaneously, which is two jobs in most orgs.

## Opening the conversation
Something like: "Before we talk numbers, can you clarify the actual level this role sits at internally, and how the total package usually splits between base, equity, and any travel-related compensation?" Getting the internal leveling straight matters here because the public posting undersells the seniority the requirements actually describe, and anchoring against the wrong level costs money later.

Once that's clear: "Given the level of ownership here, the customer-facing scope, and Swiss market rates for someone with a founding and exit track record, I'd be looking at a base around CHF 195,000, with equity and total comp aligned to what you're offering senior FDE-track hires. Does that match where you're positioned for this role?"

## If they come in low on base
"I understand the base might be structured conservatively if equity is meant to carry more of the upside, and I'm open to that if the equity terms are concrete. But this role has me owning both the technical build and the customer relationship, which is usually two roles split across two people. Could we land closer to CHF 175,000 to 185,000 on base, with the equity and travel terms we've discussed built in explicitly?"

## Equity / RSU negotiation
Cohere is a later-stage private company, not an early startup, so equity likely comes as RSUs or options with a defined vesting schedule rather than a founder-style grant. Ask directly: RSU count or percentage, current valuation basis, vesting schedule (4 years standard, confirm the cliff), what happens to unvested equity on termination, and whether there's a tender or liquidity event on any near-term horizon. Given the company's fundraising profile, ask whether the strike price or RSU valuation reflects a recent round, since that affects whether the number quoted is real upside or optimistic marketing.

## Travel and geographic terms
Since the role requires 20-40% travel for on-site customer work, get specifics in writing before base is finalized: are travel days counted toward hours, is there a stipend beyond the stated lunch allowance, what's the policy on long-haul flight class, and how is remote-versus-relocation handled if a chunk of engagements are in a specific region. If they push a geographic discount because the base team isn't in Zurich or Geneva: "I'm negotiating from Swiss market rates because that's my base and cost of living, and the travel component doesn't reduce that, it adds to the scope of the role." Have the CHF 195,000 anchor ready rather than debating the principle abstractly.

## Using a competing process
Even without a live competing offer: "I'm in later-stage conversations with a couple of other platform and applied-AI roles at a similar level, so I'd want to move toward a decision within three to four weeks if this is the right fit." Keep it honest and non-specific.

## Closing
"The problem here, getting agentic workflows to actually hold up inside real enterprise environments, is genuinely the interesting part of this space right now, and I want this to work. Let's get the level, base, and equity terms aligned this week so we can move to a decision." Set a soft deadline without sounding like an ultimatum.
