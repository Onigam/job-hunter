# Negotiation Script, Coresystems AG (Software Engineer, Pirate & Architect)

## Context that shapes this negotiation

Coresystems AG is an established Swiss software company in Zürich (Badenerstrasse 808, hybrid), 51 to 200 people, about 20 years old, with products around SAP Business One and service management (InsightLoop, Ticketing AI, Designer AI, Coresuite). It won the Best of Swiss Software Award 2024 (Gold). This isn't a startup raising rounds, so cash and benefits matter far more than equity here.

Two things to keep in mind. First, the posting is written for a mid-to-senior engineer ("multiple years of experience"), not a Staff+ hire, so their budgeted band may sit below where you should be. Your strongest argument is the architecture ownership they describe ("own major architecture decisions", "shape and own the technical architecture as the company scales"), which is Staff/Lead scope, so the level discussion comes before the salary discussion. Second, you live in Geneva and the job is in Zürich: about 2h45 each way by train. Office days, travel costs and whether part of the commute counts as working time are real money and belong in the negotiation.

No salary is listed. One aggregator (StudySmarter) shows an estimate of EUR 63k to 77k, which looks like a generic German-market figure and is far below Zürich rates for this profile; ignore it as an anchor and don't let them use it either.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

These come from general market knowledge, not a cited survey. Check them against levels.fyi (Zürich), Glassdoor CH, swissdevjobs.ch salary data and the SwissICT salary study before you talk numbers.

| Benchmark (Zürich) | Base salary (CHF, annual, incl. 13th month) |
|---|---|
| Senior software engineer, Swiss software company 50 to 200 people | 120k to 145k |
| Lead / Staff engineer, same type of company | 140k to 170k |
| Senior engineer, Big Tech Zürich (Google, etc.) | 170k to 220k base, plus RSUs |
| Head of Engineering / Architect, Swiss SME | 160k to 190k |

Your position: 17 years of experience, a cofounder exit, current hands-on multi-agent work, and a CV that matches both halves of the role. You belong at the top of the Lead/Staff band, not in the Senior band.

## Your numbers

- **Target:** CHF 160k total fixed (13 salaries), plus travel covered.
- **Opening anchor:** CHF 172k.
- **Walk-away floor:** CHF 140k, and only with a commute deal (see below). Below that, the Geneva to Zürich travel turns a decent offer into a pay cut.

Always ask whether a figure is 12 or 13 salaries. A CHF 150k "12 x" offer and a CHF 150k "13 x" offer are not the same number, and Swiss companies quote both ways.

## Before numbers: settle the level

> "Before we get into salary, I want to make sure we agree on scope. The posting talks about owning major architecture decisions and shaping the architecture as you scale. That's the work I've been doing at Staff level for the last several years, so I'd expect the title and band to reflect it, something like Lead Engineer or Staff Engineer rather than Software Engineer. Is there flexibility there?"

If they say the band is fixed, ask what the path to the next level looks like and on what timeline, and get it in writing.

## Opening script

> "Thanks for laying that out. For someone who'd own architecture end to end, with 17 years in and a platform taken from zero to acquisition, Zürich rates at a Swiss software company run roughly CHF 140k to 170k, and above that at larger firms. Given the scope you've described, I'm looking at CHF 172k on 13 salaries. I'm also coming from Geneva, so I'd want to agree the office rhythm and travel at the same time."

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low

If the offer lands around CHF 125k to 140k:

> "I appreciate it, and I'm genuinely interested in the role, so let me be direct about the gap. That number fits a senior engineer delivering features. What you've described, architecture ownership, code reviews, design decisions for the whole product as it grows, is a lead-level job, and that's where I'm benchmarking. If CHF 172k isn't possible, I could get to CHF 160k with the commute arrangement covered. Can you take that back?"

If they say the base is capped:

> "Understood. Then let's look at the rest of the package. Where can you move: a signing bonus, a guaranteed review at six months with a defined increase, the travel costs, or extra remote days?"

A six-month review only counts if the target number and the criteria are written into the contract or offer letter.

## The Geneva to Zürich piece

This is your strongest non-salary lever, because it's a concrete cost they can see.

- **Office rhythm:** aim for one day a week in Zürich, two at most. Ask what "hybrid" means for the team in practice.
- **Travel:** ask them to cover a GA travelcard (roughly CHF 4k a year in 2nd class, check the current SBB price) or reimburse train tickets. Many Swiss employers do this for hybrid staff.
- **Travel time:** with Wi-Fi on the IC trains, ask whether time on the train on office days counts as working time.
- **Geographic discount pushback:** if anyone suggests a lower figure because you're "remote from Geneva", answer that Geneva's cost of living is on par with Zürich's, you're not relocating to a cheaper market, and you'll still be in the office on the agreed days.

> "I'm not asking for a remote premium, just that the commute doesn't come out of my salary. Covering a GA and keeping office days to one a week would make the numbers work for me."

## Equity and variable pay

An established AG this size rarely has a stock option plan, but ask, because the answer tells you something:

> "Is there any profit-sharing, bonus, or employee participation plan, phantom shares or similar?"

- If there's a bonus, ask what share of target was actually paid out over the last two or three years.
- If there's a phantom share or participation plan, ask about vesting, what happens if you leave, and how the value is calculated (a formula tied to EBIT is common in Swiss SMEs).
- If there's nothing, use that to push the base: "Since there's no equity component, the fixed salary has to carry the whole package."

## Benefits worth checking in Switzerland

- **Pension (LPP/BVG):** ask what share the employer pays. Above the legal minimum, for example 60/40 instead of 50/50, is worth several thousand francs a year.
- **Vacation:** 25 days is common in tech; ask for it if they start at 20.
- **Training budget:** relevant for a role built on AI tooling that changes every quarter.
- **Notice period and probation:** the standard is three months' probation; check the notice period afterwards.

## Competing offer template (use only if real)

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late stages with another company, and their offer is around CHF [X]. Coresystems is my preference because of the architecture ownership and the AI-first product direction, so I'd like to find a way to make this work. Is there room to get closer to that figure?"

Don't invent an offer. If you have none, you can still say truthfully that you're talking to other companies and would like to decide within a reasonable time.

## Checklist for the call

1. Agree the level or title before any number.
2. Confirm 12 vs 13 salaries.
3. Anchor at CHF 172k, target CHF 160k, floor CHF 140k with travel covered.
4. Nail down office days and travel costs.
5. Ask about bonus, profit-sharing or phantom shares.
6. Check the LPP split, vacation and training budget.
7. Get everything, including any review promise, in writing.
