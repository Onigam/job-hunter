# Negotiation Script: Prudentia Sciences, Forward Deployed Engineer (Basel)

## The shape of this offer

Per the LinkedIn listing, the role is a senior, client-facing FDE tagged "Management" level, with London, Dublin or Basel strongly preferred to support EU clients. The listing frames it as a chance to "build and lead" as a founding engineering leader and to help grow the forward-deployed team. Posted base is **€110,000 to €132,000 plus equity**. The company is headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts. It raised a $20M Series A in January 2026 led by McKesson Ventures (with SignalFire; GV, Iaso Ventures and Virtue from the $7M seed), about $27M in total, and has said part of that money goes to expanding into Europe. So this is a US Series A startup hiring its first European client-facing engineers, which helps your case on scope.

The euro band is the first thing to deal with. At roughly 0.93 to 0.95 CHF per euro it works out to about **CHF 103k to 125k**, which is a London or Dublin number, not a Basel one. A senior engineer in Basel with 17 years of experience would normally sit well above that, and Basel pharma employers (Roche, Novartis) set a high local floor.

Questions to settle before talking numbers:

1. **Employing entity.** Do they have a Swiss entity, or would this go through an Employer of Record or a contractor setup? It decides whether you get a Swiss contract with BVG/LPP pension, Swiss social insurance and CHF pay. An EOR is fine; a contractor arrangement means pricing in the employer charges yourself (roughly 15 to 20% on top).
2. **Is the band per location?** Ask directly whether the €110k to €132k range is a single EU band or adjusted for Switzerland. Many US startups have a Swiss multiplier but only post one number.
3. **Scope of "lead".** Is this the first FDE with a mandate to hire the team, or one of several? A first hire who builds the playbook and the team should be priced as a lead, not as a senior IC.
4. **Location and travel.** Geneva to Basel is about 2h40 by train. Ask whether Basel means an office or "near Basel clients", and how much travel to London and Dublin clients to expect. Working from Geneva with regular client travel may be what they actually need.

## Market anchors

Approximations. **Check against levels.fyi (Zürich and Basel filters), Glassdoor CH, swissdevjobs.ch and, if possible, someone at a Basel biotech or a US startup with a Swiss team before any call.**

| Forward Deployed / Solutions Engineer, US startup, EU senior | €120k to 170k | sometimes variable tied to accounts | 0.1% to 0.4% | US-HQ FDEs often get the higher equity grants |

The takeaway: a fair Swiss number for this role is a base of around **CHF 160k to 180k**. Expect pushback since the posted band is lower, so most of the movement will come from the location adjustment, the level ("lead") and equity.

If they won't move off a euro band, the fallback is the top of it (€132k) plus a written Swiss cost-of-living adjustment, a larger equity grant and full travel cover.

## Equity terms to check

- **Instrument.** US companies usually grant ISOs or NSOs from a US plan. For a Swiss resident, options are normally taxed at exercise as income (blocked or unlisted rules may vary by canton). Ask whether they've granted to Swiss employees before and whether there's a tax ruling. Geneva tax applies while Magino lives in Geneva.
- **Vesting and cliff.** Four years with a one-year cliff is standard; ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Exercise window.** Ask for an extended post-termination window (five to ten years). It's cheap for them and matters a lot if the company isn't public in four years.
- **Acceleration.** Ask for double-trigger acceleration on change of control. The Alaya to Benevity exit is the justification: you've seen how roles get reshuffled after a deal.
- **Numbers.** Get strike price, last preferred price, post-money valuation and fully diluted share count. A percentage without those can't be valued.
- **Dilution.** Anti-dilution protection isn't granted to employees. Ask about refresher grants after the next round and how large the option pool is.

## Scripts

### When they ask for expectations

> "Before I give a figure, can you tell me how you'd employ someone in Switzerland, and whether the posted band is adjusted for Basel? The euro range in the listing reads like a London or Dublin number to me, and I'd rather we start from the right market."

If they push:

> "For a Swiss contract at this scope, being the first forward-deployed engineer, building the playbook and helping hire the team, I'm thinking around CHF 185k base plus equity in the half-percent range. I'm flexible on the split if the equity terms are good."

### If they come in low (for example €125k and 0.1%)

> "Thanks, I'd like to make this work. The base is fine for London but sits below what senior engineers earn in Basel, where the pharma companies set the local floor. Could we get to around CHF 170k on a Swiss contract? If cash is the constraint, I'm open to taking more of it in equity, something closer to 0.4%."

### Geographic discount pushback

If they argue Geneva or Basel shouldn't cost more than Dublin:

> "Rent and health insurance in Geneva are a good deal higher than in Dublin, and health insurance isn't covered by the employer here. I'm not asking for a premium, just the local market rate, the same way you'd pay a Bay Area rate in San Francisco."

### Competing offer template (only if true)

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late stages with another company at around CHF [X] base. Prudentia is my first choice because of [specific reason], and I'd rather sign with you. Is there room to close the gap on base or equity?"

Never invent a competing offer. If there isn't one, use the market data instead:

> "I'm talking to a few Swiss companies, and the senior bands I'm seeing are in the CHF 160k to 190k range. I'd like Prudentia to be competitive with that."

### Closing

> "If we can agree on CHF [base], [equity]% with an extended exercise window, and Geneva as home base with client travel covered, I'm ready to sign this week."

## Things not to give up cheaply

- Swiss contract with pension, rather than a contractor setup at the same headline number.
- A clear hiring mandate if "build and lead the team" is part of the pitch. Get it in the offer letter.
- Travel policy: who pays, and how many days a month on the road.
