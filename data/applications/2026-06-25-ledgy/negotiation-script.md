# Negotiation Script — Ledgy: Engineering Manager, Feature Team

---

## Role Context

Engineering Manager for a feature team of 4-6 engineers at Ledgy's equity management platform. TypeScript/React/Node.js/MongoDB stack. Role based in Zurich or London (hybrid, 2 days/week). Series B company (USD 36M total raised), backed by NEA and Sequoia. Equity is a real component — Ledgy gives equity to all employees.

---

## Market Context

**London listing benchmark:** £110,000-134,000 base + equity. This is what they've disclosed publicly.

**Zurich/Swiss market rates for Engineering Manager (2026):**
- EM with 5+ years IC + 2+ years management: CHF 150,000-185,000 base
- EM with founding/scale-up track record and Staff+ IC background: CHF 180,000-220,000 base
- Fintech/financial compliance domain premium: +5-8% over standard SaaS EM

**Magino's positioning:**
- 17 years of engineering experience, including co-founding a company acquired by a $1B+ unicorn
- Explicit AI tooling leadership, which Ledgy lists as a key EM expectation
- Exact stack match (TypeScript, Node.js, React, MongoDB)
- Switzerland-based: Geneva and Zurich are the same cost band, no remote discount applies

**Target range (Swiss contract, Zurich-based):**
- Opening anchor: CHF 210,000 base
- Comfortable close: CHF 185,000-195,000 base + equity
- Acceptable floor: CHF 170,000 base + meaningful equity top-up
- Walk-away: below CHF 160,000 base with weak or delayed equity

**If London contract offered:**
- Opening anchor: £140,000 base
- Acceptable floor: £120,000 base + equity
- Note: London contract from Geneva is unusual — clarify employment entity early

---

## First Move: Clarify the Employment Entity

Before any numbers are discussed, ask:

> "One thing I'd like to understand before we go into specifics: would this be a Swiss employment contract through the Zurich entity, or would it be structured differently? That'll affect how I think about the comp discussion."

This prevents being anchored to UK rates when a Swiss contract applies.

---

## Opening Anchor Script

After they ask for your expectations or give a first number:

> "Based on Engineering Manager roles in the Swiss fintech space, and given that my background brings 17 years of engineering plus a founding track record that goes beyond the minimum for this role, I'd be targeting CHF 200,000-210,000 base. I want to be upfront about that rather than have it surface as a surprise. Happy to hear what the full package looks like — including equity — before either of us commits to a number."

**Why this works:** It anchors high without being adversarial. Flagging the founding track record positions you above a standard EM hire. Asking about equity first signals you're thinking about total comp, not just salary, which gives them room to bridge the gap.

---

## If They Come In Below CHF 175,000

> "I appreciate you sharing that. With 17 years of experience and a platform I built from zero to acquisition, I'd be below my target at that number. Is there flexibility on base, or is there a meaningful equity component that's designed to close that gap?"

If equity is offered in response, ask for specifics:

> "Can you walk me through the current valuation basis, the vesting schedule, and what this grant looks like as a percentage of fully diluted? I want to understand the real upside at a realistic exit rather than just the headline number."

If neither base nor equity moves:

> "I want to make this work, but I'd need base closer to CHF 180,000 to move forward confidently. If the band is genuinely capped below that, it's better to know now than after we've both invested more time."

---

## If They Try to Anchor on London Rates

The London public listing is £110,000-134,000. If they reference this for a Zurich/Swiss position:

> "The listed range is for London. Given that I'm Switzerland-based and cost of living in Geneva sits at roughly the same level as Zurich, I'd expect to align on Swiss benchmarks rather than UK rates. Those are meaningfully different numbers."

---

## Equity Negotiation

Ledgy is private, Series B. Key questions before accepting any equity component:

1. **Current FMV or strike price:** Ask for the most recent board-approved 409A or equivalent valuation.
2. **Fully diluted percentage:** Don't accept share counts without context. Insist on the percentage of fully diluted shares.
3. **Vesting schedule:** Standard is 4-year vest with 1-year cliff. Push back on any cliff longer than 12 months or vesting longer than 4 years.
4. **Double-trigger acceleration:** Given that you've lived through an acquisition, ask explicitly whether there's double-trigger acceleration on change of control. Without it, you could be acquired and then let go without accelerating unvested shares.
5. **Preference stack:** VC investors have liquidation preferences above common stock. Ask what "exit" actually returns to common shareholders at a realistic 2-3x exit scenario, not just at a high valuation.

**If equity is offered without detail:**

> "Happy to factor in equity. Can you share the current cap table structure and what this grant represents as a percentage of fully diluted? I've been through an acquisition and I know how much the preference stack matters — I want to model this properly before I respond to the full offer."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they apply a remote discount for Geneva vs. Zurich:

> "Geneva and Zurich are effectively in the same cost band. If anything, Geneva sits slightly higher on housing. I don't think a location discount applies here — if you have a specific benchmark driving that, I'm happy to look at it."

---

## Timeline and Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in play:

> "I'm at a decision point with another process, and I'd prefer Ledgy based on the domain and the team structure. If you can reach CHF 190,000 or above, I can give you an answer within 48 hours."

If you don't have a competing offer, use forward momentum:

> "I'm in active processes and expect things to move in the next two weeks. I'd rather close with Ledgy than have timing force a decision elsewhere. If we can land on a number this week, I'm in."

---

## Non-Cash Components

- **Remote days abroad (40 days/year):** Confirm this starts from day one, not after a probationary period.
- **Professional development budget:** Ask for the annual figure in writing. CHF 3,000-5,000 is typical; CHF 5,000 is reasonable to request given the financial compliance domain.
- **Equipment / home office:** Not always listed. A CHF 1,500-2,000 one-time setup budget is a reasonable add-on if base is at the lower end.
- **Start date:** If salary is at the lower bound of acceptable, an earlier start date or reduced notice period is a concession they can offer without touching the salary band.

---

## Final Close

> "I want to be direct: if you can reach CHF [X], I'm in. The domain is right, the stack is what I've been building on for ten years, and the team size is exactly where I operate best. Let me know what you can do."
