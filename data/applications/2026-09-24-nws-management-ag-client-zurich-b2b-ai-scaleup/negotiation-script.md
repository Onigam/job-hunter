# Negotiation Script: Principal Engineer / Engineering Lead, Agentic AI (Zurich B2B AI scaleup via NWS Management AG)

## Context
- Recruiter-led search (NWS Management AG). The recruiter's fee is usually a percentage of first-year base, so they have a reason to push your base up, not down. Use that.
- CTO-level scope of the technical core, hands-on IC / tech lead. Small team, founder access, "meaningful equity, not a token."
- Location is central Zurich, the highest-paying tech market in Switzerland. You live in Geneva, so commuting cost and time are real and negotiable.
- No salary disclosed. Get their range before giving a number if you can.

## Your target
- **Walk-away base:** CHF 185k (below this, equity has to be clearly exceptional and well documented)
- **Target base:** CHF 215k to 225k
- **Opening anchor:** CHF 240k base plus meaningful equity
- **Equity target:** 0.5% to 1.5% depending on stage and valuation; ask for the fully diluted number, not a share count

## Script: when the recruiter asks for your expectations

"For a role with CTO-level scope over the technical core in Zurich, I'm looking at around CHF 240k base, plus equity that reflects owning the platform. That's in line with Staff+ and Principal roles with production agent experience here. Before we go further, what range has the client budgeted? It'll help us both check the fit quickly."

If they push you to go first again:
"I'd rather not negotiate against myself. If you can share the band, I'll tell you honestly whether it works."

## Script: counter-offer if they come in low

If they offer, for example, CHF 190k:

"Thanks, I appreciate it, and I'm keen on the role. The number is below what I'd expect for this scope, though. You're asking for someone to own architecture, reliability and engineering standards for the whole agent platform, which is closer to a CTO seat than a senior IC role. Market for that in Zurich sits well above 190k. If we can get base to 225k, I'm ready to move forward. If base is genuinely capped, I'd want to talk about equity and a few other pieces to close the gap."

If they say the budget is fixed:
"Understood. Then let's look at the rest of the package: a larger equity grant, a signing bonus, a guaranteed review at six months against agreed goals, and support for the Geneva to Zurich commute. Which of those have room?"

## Equity: questions to ask and what to push for

Ask, in writing:
- What instrument is it? Options (ESOP), phantom shares, or actual shares? Swiss startups use all three and the tax treatment differs a lot.
- What percentage of fully diluted capital does the grant represent?
- Latest valuation or last round price, and the strike price.
- Vesting schedule and cliff. Standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff; push for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- What happens on acquisition? Ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination or role change). You've been through an acquisition at Alaya, so you know exactly why this matters.
- Leaver terms: good leaver vs bad leaver definitions, and the exercise window after departure. Push for a long window or cashless exercise.
- Anti-dilution: employees rarely get it, but ask how the pool is refreshed and whether your grant is protected in size relative to future rounds.

Script:
"Equity matters to me here, and it's a big part of why this role is attractive. To weigh it properly I need the percentage of fully diluted capital, the instrument, the strike and last valuation, and the vesting and change-of-control terms. I've been on the founder side of an acquisition, so I'd like double-trigger acceleration in there."

Tax note: in Switzerland, options are generally taxed as income when exercised (or at grant for some listed or freely tradable plans), and capital gains on privately held shares are usually tax-free. Get a written tax ruling from the company if one exists, and check with a Geneva tax advisor before signing, since you'd pay tax in Geneva as your residence canton.

## Commute and location pushback

This isn't a geographic discount situation, since the role is in Zurich. The risk is the reverse: they may treat your Geneva base as a reason to pay less, or expect five days on site.

"I'm based in Geneva, and I'll be in Zurich as much as the team needs, especially in the first months. Geneva's cost of living is on par with Zurich, so I'd expect Zurich rates. For the commute, I'd ask the company to cover a GA travelcard (around CHF 4k a year) and agree on a hybrid rhythm, something like three days on site once we're past onboarding."

Other things to ask for if base is tight:
- GA travelcard or first-class train subsidy (the train is where you'll do a lot of work)
- Flexible hours on travel days
- Occasional hotel nights for on-site stretches

## Competing offer template (use only if true, even if early stage)

"I want to be transparent: I'm also in late conversations for a founding CTO role and a founding engineer role, both AI-focused, with packages in the CHF 200k+ range including equity. This role is my first choice because of the scope and the Zurich team, and I'd like to make it work. Is there room to get closer to 225k base, or to strengthen the equity side?"

Never invent a specific written offer. If you have nothing concrete, say you're "in process with other companies" and leave the numbers out.

## Final checklist before saying yes
- Base, bonus, equity (percentage, instrument, vesting, acceleration) all in writing
- Title: Principal Engineer or Engineering Lead, and clarity on whether a CTO title is possible later
- Reporting line: directly to the founders
- On-site expectation and commute support agreed
- Notice period and start date
- Pension (2nd pillar) contribution split; some startups pay only the legal minimum
- Vacation days (25 is common in tech; ask)
