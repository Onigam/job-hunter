# Negotiation Script: VP of Engineering, Qdrant

## Context to keep in mind

Qdrant is post-Series B, remote-first, and scaling from an open-source database into an enterprise platform spanning the core engine, Qdrant Cloud, Hybrid Cloud, Cloud Inference and Qdrant Edge. This is a real leadership seat reporting into the CEO, not a founding-stage IC role, so cash should be closer to funded-company market rate than seed-stage token cash. Since Qdrant is remote-first rather than Geneva-based, the Swiss anchors below matter as a floor to protect, not as what a US or EU-payroll startup will default to offering someone based in Switzerland.

## Switzerland market anchors (protect the floor, don't lead with these)

- Senior Staff / Principal Engineer, Geneva or Zurich, established tech company: CHF 170k to 220k base, often plus bonus.
- VP Engineering / Head of Engineering, Swiss scale-up: CHF 190k to 250k base plus meaningful equity.
- CTO, early-stage Swiss startup: CHF 150k to 200k base, offset by a founder-level equity stake, typically 3 to 8%.
- Geneva cost of living runs high enough that a "competitive" US or Berlin-calibrated remote salary often lands 25 to 35% under what the same title pays locally in cash terms.

The real anchor should reference Series B/C VP Engineering comp at comparable infra or dev-tools companies (open-source database, ML infra, developer platform), not a generic Swiss local hire number. Use the Swiss figures only to argue the floor if a lowball shows up.

## Opening anchor

Wait for a real number before naming one. If asked to go first:

"For a VP Engineering seat owning reliability, performance and the hiring plan across the core engine and cloud products, reporting to the CEO, I'd expect a base in the 210 to 240k USD range or CHF equivalent, plus an equity grant sized for a Series B/C VP hire, somewhere in the 0.3 to 0.8% range depending on the current cap table, along with standard benefits. I'd want to structure the split based on how much of the value you're putting into equity versus cash at this stage."

Adjust once their number is on the table. Don't restate the anchor if they open above it, just confirm and move to the details.

## If they come in low on cash

"I get that a lot of the upside here is meant to sit in equity given where the company is post-Series B. If the base stays where it is, I'd want to look at a larger equity grant, a shorter cliff, or a compensation review tied to a concrete milestone, hitting a specific ARR number or headcount target, rather than leaving that to an informal check-in a year from now."

Fallback if they won't move on cash or equity: ask for a signing bonus that bridges the gap for year one, framed as compensating for unvested equity or bonus left on the table at a current role.

## Equity negotiation notes

- Ask for the fully diluted percentage and the current post-money valuation, not just a share count. A share count is meaningless without both.
- Confirm the strike price and the date of the last 409A or Swiss-equivalent valuation. A VP-level grant priced right after a valuation bump is a materially worse deal than one priced before it.
- Push for a standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff, and ask specifically about acceleration on acquisition. Single-trigger acceleration is reasonable to ask for at VP level given the exposure that comes with the title.
- Ask how much of the last round is still in the bank and what the runway looks like. A VP hire is a multi-year bet, worth knowing whether the company is funded to get through the next 24 to 36 months before signing.
- If early-exercise is offered, get clear on the tax treatment for a Swiss tax resident before assuming it's a straightforward win. It sometimes isn't.

## Geographic discount pushback

If there's any pull toward a lower number because Geneva "has a lower cost of living than San Francisco or London":

"Geneva actually runs on par with or above most major US and European tech hubs on cost of living, so I wouldn't expect a discount there. Given the role is remote-first by design, I'd rather anchor comp to the scope of the job, owning reliability and the hiring plan across the whole platform, than to where I happen to be sitting."

## Competing offer template

Use only if a real competing process exists. Never invent a number or company name.

"I want to be upfront that I'm also in a later-stage conversation for a comparable engineering leadership role at a funded company. I'd rather make this work here if the scope and the team are the right fit, so if there's flexibility on base or equity, this is the point where I'd want to know."

## Timezone and remote logistics, worth raising before comp is finalized

Qdrant is remote-first with a distributed team; get explicit about where the rest of engineering leadership sits and what overlap the CEO expects for a VP-level role. A Geneva base overlaps well with a European engineering core and most of a US day, but confirm that's compatible with whatever cadence the CEO has in mind before treating any comp number as final. It's not a comp lever by itself, but it changes whether the number on offer is actually sustainable.
