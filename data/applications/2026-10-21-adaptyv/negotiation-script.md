# Negotiation Script: Software Engineer, Lab Automation @ Adaptyv

Context: YC-backed techbio startup building an automated lab that AI agents can drive, based in Lausanne (Biopole area), roughly 11-50 people. On-site, full-time, start ASAP. LinkedIn tags the role as entry level and no compensation is listed. That label is the main thing to handle: the job is to get paid for 17+ years of experience in a role scoped as an IC engineer.

## Market anchors (Switzerland, CHF, gross annual base)

These are approximate ranges from general Swiss market knowledge, not verified salary data for Adaptyv. Check them against Glassdoor/levels.fyi and any data the recruiter shares.

- Junior / entry-level software engineer, Lausanne startup: roughly CHF 85k-105k.
- Mid-level software engineer, Lausanne/Geneva startup: roughly CHF 105k-130k.
- Senior software engineer, Swiss startup or scale-up: roughly CHF 130k-160k.
- Staff-level IC at Swiss scale-ups (Zurich pays more, Romandie a bit less): roughly CHF 160k-200k.
- Robotics / lab automation / embedded-adjacent engineers sit around general software rates in Switzerland. Being a niche skill helps a little, but early-stage cash limits it.
- Realistic target for this seat: CHF 140k-160k base, with equity to make up for the stage. Walk-away floor: around CHF 125k base unless equity is unusually strong.

## Before talking numbers

Get two things clarified early, ideally in the first recruiter call:

1. "The posting is tagged entry level. Is that the level you're hiring at, or is it flexible for someone with more experience?" If the answer is a fixed junior band, find out the ceiling before investing in more rounds.
2. "Is there a leveling framework, and where would someone with my background land?" This makes the comp discussion about level, which is easier to argue than a raw number.

## Opening anchor

Say it after they've shared a range, or if they insist on a number first:

"For a senior engineer in Romandie with hardware integration and platform experience, I'm looking at around CHF 155k base plus equity. I know the posting was written for a more junior profile, and I'm not asking you to pay staff-level rates for the title. But what I'd bring, owning the orchestration and failure-handling design with little supervision, is senior work, and I'd want the offer to reflect that."

If pushed for a range: CHF 145k-160k. Let equity carry the flexibility.

## If they come in low (e.g. CHF 100k-115k)

"Thanks, that's useful. I think that number fits the role as posted, but I'd be doing more than the posting describes; you'd get someone who's designed systems from scratch and run them in production for years. Could we look at it as a senior hire? If cash is tight at this stage, I'm open to closing part of the gap with equity."

Fallback sequence if base won't move:
1. Larger option grant in place of cash. Ask for the number as a percentage of fully diluted shares.
2. A written comp review at 6 months tied to clear outcomes (for example, a multi-instrument protocol running unattended with automatic error recovery), not a verbal "we'll revisit."
3. A one-off signing bonus to cover the first-year gap.
4. Title: "Senior Software Engineer" or "Lead, Lab Automation" costs them nothing and protects the next negotiation.

## Equity

- Ask: percentage of fully diluted shares, strike price, latest valuation used to set it, size of the remaining pool.
- For an early senior IC hire at a YC-stage company, something like 0.1%-0.5% is a reasonable ask, depending on round and pool. Push toward the top of that if base comes in below CHF 140k.
- Vesting: 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Refuse a longer cliff. Ask whether there's a refresh policy after later rounds.
- Swiss specifics: ask whether the plan uses options or shares, how taxation at grant vs. exercise works under their structure, and how long the post-departure exercise window is. Ask for more than 90 days if possible, because a short window can make the equity worthless if exercise is taxed heavily.
- Anti-dilution: employees rarely get it. The realistic version is a refresh grant commitment, so ask for that.

## Geographic pushback

If they suggest Lausanne justifies a lower number than Zurich:

"Lausanne isn't far off Zurich on rent and day-to-day costs, and I'm commuting on-site from Geneva, which is the most expensive city in Romandie. I'd want this benchmarked against Swiss rates, not adjusted down for the region."

If they mention a candidate pool from cheaper EU countries: "That's fair for remote roles, but this one is on-site in Lausanne and pays a Swiss cost base."

## Competing offer template (only if real)

"I'm also in late-stage conversations for a [Staff/Senior] role at around CHF [X]k plus equity. Adaptyv is the more interesting problem, and I'd rather be here, but I need the offer closer to that before I can commit."

Don't invent one. Adaptyv is a small team and the Romandie startup scene is small; a bluff is easy to discover and expensive if it is.

## Close

Get everything in writing before a verbal yes: base, title and level, equity (percentage, strike, vesting, cliff, exercise window), any signing bonus, the date and criteria for the comp review, start date, and on-site expectations (days per week, lab hours or on-call for overnight runs). Ask the on-call question directly. Instruments running unattended overnight often means someone gets paged, and that should be priced in or at least agreed in advance.
