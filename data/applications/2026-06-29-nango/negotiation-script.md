# Negotiation Script — Nango / Backend Engineer (Americas & Europe)

---

## Market Context

**Role posted:** Backend Engineer (Americas & Europe)
**Stage:** YC W23, $1M+ ARR, ~6-person team, open-source
**Location:** Geneva, Switzerland (remote)

**Comparable YC listings (Nango, 2025-2026):**
- Staff Backend Engineer: $140K–$200K USD + 0.25%–0.75% equity
- Senior Backend Engineer: $110K–$160K USD + 0.1%–0.4% equity
- Backend Engineer title at a company this early-stage = high-impact scope regardless of title

**Swiss market anchors for Senior/Staff Backend (2025-2026):**
- Senior Backend Engineer, Geneva/Zurich: CHF 150,000–190,000 base
- Staff+ / Founding-engineer background: CHF 190,000–230,000
- CHF/USD ~1.10 current rate, so CHF 190K = ~$210K USD

**Key leverage point:** Magino brings founding-engineer experience and 17+ years of depth to a role that is being offered at a small team where ownership will far exceed a typical "Backend Engineer" title. This is a Staff-equivalent scope at a 6-person company.

---

## Opening Position

**Target base:** $175K–$190K USD (or CHF equivalent)
**Equity target:** 0.5%–0.75%
**Fallback floor:** $160K + 0.75% equity before reconsidering

**Rationale to articulate:**
- 17+ years of experience, with founding engineer and cross-team Staff+ track record
- Built and scaled a platform through acquisition by a $1B+ company
- Geneva-based: Swiss market rates apply, cost-of-living differential is significant and real
- At a 6-person team, this role carries Staff-equivalent scope and ownership

---

## First Offer Response

If they come in at $120K–$140K:

> "I appreciate the offer. My background is closer to the Staff level in terms of scope and experience, and given that I'm based in Geneva where market rates for senior backend engineers are meaningfully higher, I was expecting something closer to $175K. Is there flexibility there, either on base or on the equity side?"

If they come in at $145K–$160K:

> "That's in the right range directionally. Given the experience I'm bringing and the Geneva cost-of-living context, I was hoping to land at $175K. Is the base firm, or can we move the equity toward 0.75% to bridge the gap?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Nango's likely range:** 0.1%–0.75% depending on level
**Ask for:** 0.5%–0.75%
**Framing:**

> "Given the stage, I want to be genuinely aligned with the company's upside. At a 6-person team with real ARR and YC backing, 0.5% feels like the right number for someone coming in at this experience level. Would you be open to 0.5% as a floor, with a standard 4-year vest and 1-year cliff?"

**Key terms to confirm:**
- Vesting schedule: 4 years / 1-year cliff (standard; push back if anything longer)
- Option type: ISOs (if US entity) or warrants (if Swiss/European entity); clarify which
- Strike price: ask for current 409A or fair market value in writing before signing
- Exercise window post-departure: ask for 5–10 year window (many startups now offer this; it matters significantly at exit)
- Anti-dilution: not standard at this stage, but worth understanding the dilution history

**If equity is capped below 0.5%:**

> "If the equity ceiling is 0.35%, I'd want the base to land at $185K to reflect the full package."

---

## Geographic Premium Pushback

If they say "our ranges are US-benchmarked" or offer at the low end citing remote parity:

> "I understand your ranges are built for a distributed team. What I can share is that Geneva salaries for senior backend engineering roles are in the CHF 170K–200K range, which at current rates translates to roughly $190K–$220K. I'm not asking for a location premium — just parity with what this scope commands in my market. Can we get closer to that?"

---

## Title Negotiation

If the backend engineer title feels misaligned with the scope:

> "I'd like to discuss how the title maps to what the role actually requires at a 6-person team. At this stage, everyone is operating closer to Staff-level ownership. Is 'Senior Backend Engineer' on the table, or is there flexibility on how the role is framed internally and in the offer letter?"

Getting the right title matters for future job searches and for internal clarity about scope expectations.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in hand:

> "I have another offer at [$X / CHF Y] that I'm evaluating alongside yours. Nango is the one I find more compelling given the technical scope and the stage, but the gap is enough that I need to resolve it before committing. If you can get to $175K, I'm ready to move forward."

If no competing offer, but you want to signal optionality:

> "I'm actively in process with a couple of other companies, and I expect to have decisions to make over the next two weeks. I want to be straightforward: the comp difference matters, and I'm not in a position where I need to take the first number."

---

## Things Not to Negotiate Away

- Equity vesting cliff: 1 year is standard; anything longer is a red flag at this stage
- Employment vs. contractor: if they propose a Swiss contractor arrangement, rates shift significantly to CHF 1,000–1,400/day equivalent — make sure you're comparing like-for-like
- Start date: you control this; don't let urgency pressure you into accepting terms you'd regret

---

## Close Script

Once the numbers are agreed:

> "Great, I'm in. Can you send the updated offer letter with the revised terms in writing? Once I have that confirmed, I'll sign and we can align on a start date."

Never sign on a verbal agreement alone. Always get the updated offer letter in writing first.
