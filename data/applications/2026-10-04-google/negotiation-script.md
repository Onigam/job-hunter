# Negotiation Script: Google, Senior Staff Software Engineer, Full Stack, CodeAI

## What the posting tells us

- **Location:** Munich, Germany. It isn't a Swiss role and it isn't remote; in-person interviews are expected.
- **Level:** "Senior Staff" at Google maps to L7. The "Advanced" tag and the 5 years of tech-lead experience in the preferred qualifications are consistent with that.
- **Published pay (Germany):** EUR 190,000 to 194,000 base, plus a **25% bonus target**, plus equity (Alphabet GSUs), plus benefits.

That base band is unusually narrow, so base is close to fixed once the level is set. At Google the real levers are **level**, **the GSU grant**, and **the sign-on bonus**, in that order.

Back-of-envelope target cash at the published band: EUR 192k base + ~EUR 48k target bonus = **~EUR 240k cash** before equity.

## Market reference (Staff+/L7, 2026, approximate; check levels.fyi before the call)

| Employer / location | Base | Total comp (incl. equity, bonus) |
|---|---|---|
| Google L7, Munich | EUR 190k to 194k (published) | roughly EUR 330k to 450k depending on GSU grant |
| Google L7, Zürich | roughly CHF 250k to 290k | roughly CHF 420k to 550k |
| Big tech Staff (L6 equivalent), Zürich | CHF 200k to 240k | CHF 300k to 400k |
| Geneva scale-ups and fintech, Staff/Lead | CHF 160k to 190k | modest or no liquid equity |

The table matters for one reason: Google L7 in Munich already beats anything Geneva pays locally. The real comparison is **Munich vs Zürich**, since Google pays Zürich noticeably more and it's a much easier move from Geneva.

## The most important conversation: level, not money

The biggest risk in a Google offer at this seniority is **down-leveling to L6 (Staff)** after the hiring committee review. The gap between L6 and L7 in grant size is much bigger than anything you can get by pushing on base.

What to do:
- In the recruiter screen, confirm the req is L7 and ask how the level is calibrated after interviews.
- Bring evidence of L7-scope work into every round: setting direction across teams without authority (AI tooling adoption across two teams), owning a platform end to end (Alaya), architecture decisions that held up through an acquisition.
- If an L6 offer comes back: "I appreciate the offer. The scope we discussed, setting technical strategy for developer-facing agents across the SDLC, is the scope of the posted Senior Staff role, and it's the scope I've been operating at. I'd like to understand what the committee saw as the gap, and whether there's a path to L7 at offer, or a committed re-level review at the first cycle."

## Targets (assuming L7 Munich)

- **Walk-away:** base at the band (EUR 190k+), 25% bonus target, and an initial GSU grant worth at least ~EUR 450k over four years. Below that, the total doesn't justify leaving Geneva.
- **Target:** EUR 194k base, initial GSU grant of ~EUR 600k to 700k over four years, sign-on bonus of EUR 50k to 75k, full relocation.
- **Opening anchor:** total first-year comp of ~EUR 450k, framed around Zürich-level pay for the same level and the cost of relocating from Switzerland.

## Script: when they ask for expectations early (recruiter screen)

"I'd rather anchor on level first. If this is L7, I've seen the published Munich base band, so base is mostly settled. I'm thinking about total compensation, and coming from Geneva I'm comparing against what the same level pays in Zürich. I'd want the equity and sign-on to close most of that gap."

If pushed for one number: "Somewhere around 450 thousand euros in first-year total comp, with equity carrying most of it."

## Script: geographic pushback (Geneva to Munich)

"Moving from Geneva to Munich is a real step down in local pay levels, and I'm taking on relocation cost, a new tax system, and the risk of the team match. I'm not asking to be paid Swiss rates in Germany. But I'd like the GSU grant and sign-on to reflect that I'm coming from a higher-cost market where this level pays more."

Then ask directly: "Is there a CodeAI or Core presence in Zürich this role could sit in? If so, I'd like to explore that, since it changes both the comp and the move."

## Script: counter-offer if the GSU grant comes in low

"Thanks, and I'll be honest, I'm keen on this one. Evaluation loops for coding agents are exactly where I want to spend the next few years. The base and bonus are fine. The equity is where it falls short. For L7 scope, and compared to where this level lands in Zürich, I'd need the initial grant closer to [target] to make the move work. If the grant is constrained, a larger sign-on would cover part of the gap."

Then stop talking.

## Competing offer template

Google's comp team moves much more readily when there's a written competing offer. Even a hypothetical one is worth preparing in advance, so the conversation is ready if it becomes real.

"I want to be transparent. I have another offer at [company] for a Staff-level role at [total comp], with [equity details]. Google is my first choice because of the scope in CodeAI. If you can get the grant to [number], I'm ready to sign this week."

Rules: never invent an offer, never share the document unless asked, and only use it if you'd actually take it.

## Equity notes (Alphabet GSUs)

- These are RSUs in a public company (GOOGL), not options, so the value is liquid on vest. There's no strike price, 409A or liquidation stack to worry about.
- Confirm the **vesting schedule**. Google has used monthly vesting and front-loaded schedules in recent years; ask for the exact per-year split, because a front-loaded grant shrinks your income in years 3 and 4 unless refreshers make up for it.
- Ask about **annual refresh grants** at L7 and how they're tied to performance ratings.
- In Germany, RSUs are taxed as income at vest. Ask whether Google withholds at vest (it normally does by selling shares) so there's no surprise tax bill.
- Grants are usually set as a value and converted to shares at a reference price. Ask which price and date are used, and which currency the grant value is stated in.

## Relocation and Germany-specific items

- **Relocation package:** lump sum or managed move, temporary housing, help with flat search in Munich (it's a tight rental market).
- **Tax:** German top marginal rate is ~42% plus solidarity surcharge, against Geneva cantonal plus federal tax. Run a proper net comparison before deciding, including German statutory pension and health contributions.
- **Pension:** Swiss 2nd pillar (LPP) assets stay in Switzerland on a move to the EU (mandatory portion goes to a vested benefits account). Factor that in.
- **Start date:** realistic availability, accounting for any notice period still running.

## What not to negotiate on

- Base, beyond asking for the top of the published band. It's narrow for a reason.
- Bonus target. 25% is standard at this level and won't move.
