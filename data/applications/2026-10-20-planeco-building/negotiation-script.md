# Negotiation Script, Planeco Building (Founding AI Engineer)

## Context that shapes this negotiation

This one is unusual, and the usual founding-engineer playbook only half applies.

Planeco is **bootstrapped and profitable**: a run rate over €10M, 15 to 20% month-over-month growth, about 40 people, no outside board and no fundraising. That cuts both ways. They can actually pay cash, which most "founding" roles can't. But there's probably no option pool, no VC-standard equity plan, and no next round to tie a salary step-up to. The company is German (likely a GmbH) with its team in Lisbon and remote, so expect them to think in EUR, and possibly at Lisbon rates.

The posting lists seniority as "Associate" and says years, titles and management don't count. They're telling you they'll pay for output, not CV. Don't argue from 17 years of experience; argue from scope (you own the whole agent runtime and its guardrails, and its output goes into legal filings) and from what you can show in the portfolio review.

The posting offers no salary and doesn't mention equity. It got 200+ applicants in its first week, so get your portfolio and one-liner right before you worry about the number.

**Ask early, before any number comes up:**
1. "Does 'remote within Europe' include Switzerland? Do you employ through an EOR, or contract?" (Switzerland isn't in the EU, and some EORs and companies exclude it.)
2. "Is there an equity or profit-share plan for early hires, or would this be the first one?"
3. "What's the salary band you had in mind, and is it the same for Lisbon and remote?"

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

These are working ranges from general market knowledge, not a cited survey. Check them against levels.fyi, Glassdoor CH/DE, Swissdevjobs, the Landing.jobs Portugal tech salary report and Germany's StepStone/Honeypot data before talking numbers.

| Benchmark | Base salary | Equity / variable |
|---|---|---|
| Geneva/Zurich, Staff or senior AI engineer at a scale-up | CHF 160k to 210k | RSUs/options, often small |
| Geneva, founding engineer at a funded startup | CHF 140k to 175k | 0.5% to 1.5% |
| Germany (Berlin/Munich), senior AI/LLM engineer at a scale-up | EUR 85k to 120k | VSOP, 0.05% to 0.5% |
| Lisbon, senior engineer at a well-paying scale-up | EUR 55k to 85k | varies, often none |
| Remote-first EU companies paying location-agnostic | EUR 90k to 140k | varies |

AI agent engineers who've actually shipped production systems are scarce in 2026, and the premium over generalist senior engineers is real. Planeco is profitable and growing fast, so they can afford the top of the German band; the question is whether they'll pay it to someone outside Lisbon.

## Your numbers

- **Target:** EUR 130k base (about CHF 122k, recheck FX on the day) plus a meaningful equity or profit-share component.
- **Opening anchor:** EUR 145k base plus 1% VSOP (virtual shares), or an equivalent profit share.
- **Walk-away floor:** EUR 110k base with some form of upside. Below that, Geneva roles pay more cash for less risk. If they insist on Lisbon pay (EUR 70k to 85k) with no upside, that's a no for a Geneva resident.

**Employment structure changes the real number by 15% or more.** Sort it out before agreeing on a figure:
- *Through an EOR with a Swiss payroll:* Swiss employer social charges (AHV/AVS, LPP, accident insurance, roughly 7% to 15% on top) are paid by them, and you're properly insured. This is what you want.
- *As a contractor:* you pay AVS as self-employed, have no LPP or unemployment cover, and have to handle VAT registration above CHF 100k turnover. Your rate needs to be 25 to 35% above the salaried equivalent. At EUR 130k salaried, that's roughly EUR 165k to 175k a year, or about EUR 750 to 800 a day.

## Opening script

> "Thanks for laying out the role. I'd own the agent runtime end to end, from context management and tool calling to the guardrails, and since what it produces goes into permit filings, the cost of getting it wrong is real. For someone who's shipped agent systems in production, remote-first European companies pay roughly EUR 120k to 140k, and I'd add that you're profitable and growing 15% a month, so you're not a seed startup stretching its runway. I'm looking at EUR 145k base, plus some share in the upside, ideally virtual shares or a profit-share, since I know you don't have outside investors."

Then stop talking.

## Cash/upside menu

Bootstrapped founders often hate diluting. Give them options so they can pick the one they're comfortable with:

> "Three versions I'd be happy to sign:
> A: EUR 145k, no equity, plus an annual bonus tied to agent-handled project volume.
> B: EUR 130k plus 0.75% to 1% virtual shares, 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff.
> C: EUR 120k plus 1.5% virtual shares, with a written salary review after 12 months."

## Equity notes for a bootstrapped German company

- **VSOP, not real shares.** German startups usually offer virtual stock options: a contractual right to a cash payout at an exit, not actual GmbH shares (which need a notary for every transfer). Read the plan carefully.
- **The catch with VSOPs at a company with no plan to sell:** they only pay out on an exit or liquidity event. If the founders mean to stay bootstrapped forever, the VSOP could be worth zero indefinitely. Ask: "What triggers a payout? Is there a dividend-equivalent or buyback clause if there's no exit?"
- **Profit share may be worth more here.** A company with a €10M+ run rate and profits can pay an annual share of profit. Being cash in hand, this is often better than virtual equity in a company that never sells.
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is the norm. Ask for good-leaver terms (you keep vested shares if you leave on good terms), and single-trigger acceleration on change of control.
- **Anti-dilution:** with no outside investors, dilution risk is low today. Ask for it to be written down that a future fundraise wouldn't wipe out the VSOP pool.
- **Swiss tax:** a VSOP payout is taxed as employment income in Switzerland. Ask a Geneva tax adviser before signing if the amounts get serious.

## If they come in low

**If they offer Lisbon-level cash (EUR 65k to 85k):**

> "That makes sense for someone living in Lisbon. My costs are in Geneva, though, and I'm not asking for a Geneva premium; Geneva roles would pay me CHF 160k or more. I'm asking for what remote-first European companies pay for this kind of work, which is the EUR 120k to 140k range. If the cash really can't move that far, I'd need the difference made up in profit share or virtual equity."

**If they say "everyone's on the same band":**

> "Fair enough for later hires. This is the founding role on the AI product, though. The runtime, the guardrails and the evals I set up are what every engineer after me will build on, and what your permit filings will rely on. I'd expect that to show up somewhere, if not in base salary, then in the upside."

**If they say "location-based pay":**

> "I get it, and I'll come to Lisbon for work weeks on my own budget if that helps. But you're paying for the output, the same output whether I'm in Lisbon or Geneva. Your posting says titles and years don't matter, only the work. I'd ask that location be treated the same way."

**If they need time:**

> "Of course. Can we agree on the structure, EOR or contract, at the same time? It changes the real number by 15% or more for both of us."

## Competing offer template

Use this only if it's true, or if you've got real conversations going (Axion Lab, the other offers in today's batch). Never invent an offer.

> "To be transparent, I'm also in late-stage talks with a Geneva-based role at around CHF [X] and a founding role with [Y]% equity. Planeco is the one I'd pick for the problem and the data, 3,000 real projects a year is something nobody else can offer, but I need the package to be in the same range. Is there room to close the gap?"

## Things to get in writing

- Base salary, currency, and who carries FX risk if paid in EUR into a CHF account
- Employment structure (EOR, provider name, Swiss social charges covered)
- VSOP/profit-share plan document, payout triggers, vesting, leaver terms
- Annual salary review date
- Travel budget for Lisbon work weeks
- The API, tools and hardware budget they mention in the posting, with a figure
- Notice period and probation length
