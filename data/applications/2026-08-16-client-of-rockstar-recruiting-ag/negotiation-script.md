# Negotiation Script: Rockstar client, Founding Product Engineer (accounting AI)

## Context

This posting is unusual in that it already publishes a band: $150,000 to $225,000 base plus 0.25% to 1.00% equity, fully remote out of a Los Angeles-anchored, $5M-raised, early-revenue startup. That removes the usual first step of getting a number on the table, so the negotiation is really about where in that band Magino lands and how the Geneva/US pay gap and equity mechanics get handled. A founding-stage seed company negotiating with a non-US remote hire will often anchor near the low end of a published band by default, so the work here is justifying the top third of it with a concrete comp on both sides, not with vague seniority claims.

Magino brings a founding-engineer exit (Alaya, acquired by Benevity), 17+ years including architecture at ten times the scale post-acquisition, and, specifically relevant to this role, active daily use of AI coding agents as a primary implementation tool rather than a novelty. That last point maps directly onto "comfortable using AI coding agents as your main implementation tool and holding their output to a high bar" in the listing, which is a harder qualification to fake than years of experience.

## Market anchor

US seed-stage "founding engineer" roles at this funding stage typically cluster in the $160,000 to $200,000 base range for someone without prior founder equity in the company, with the top of a published band reserved for candidates who've shipped a product from zero before, which Magino has. Converting a Swiss cost base into USD comp isn't a straight discount: Geneva living costs run close to or above LA's, so there's no geographic-arbitrage argument to make against him, and that's worth stating plainly rather than assuming the employer will do the math unprompted. A fair target is $195,000 to $215,000 base, near the top of the published range, with equity in the 0.5% to 0.75% band given the company is still small enough that a founding-level hire's equity should reflect real ownership, not a token grant.

## Opening anchor script

"The band you've published, $150,000 to $225,000 plus a quarter to one percent equity, is a good starting point for this conversation. Given I've taken a product from a blank page through an acquisition before, and I'm already using AI agents as my primary way of shipping code rather than something I'd need to ramp into, I'd expect to land near the top of that range, somewhere around $200,000 to $215,000 base, with equity toward the higher end given the stage the company's at. How do you typically calibrate within the band for someone joining at this point?"

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

"I get that seed-stage cash is tight and that a lot of remote candidates get anchored near the bottom of a band like this. I'd push back a little there: Geneva isn't a lower cost base than LA, so there's no geographic discount that makes sense here, and the scope in the posting, owning core workflows end to end rather than executing a spec, is closer to what I'd expect the top of the range to be paying for. If cash is genuinely capped right now, I'd rather talk about closing the gap with equity or a defined comp review at six months tied to a specific product surface shipping, than just accept a lower number and hope it gets revisited."

## Equity negotiation notes

At 0.25% to 1.00%, get the fully diluted share count before comparing that percentage to anything, since a 1% grant means very different things depending on the cap table's current size. Ask directly about the strike price, the vesting schedule (standard is four years with a one-year cliff, confirm it's not longer), whether there's a post-termination exercise window beyond the standard 90 days, given the tax exposure of exercising and holding, and whether the $5M raised was priced or a SAFE, since that affects how meaningful the percentage actually is right now. For a founding-level hire this early, anti-dilution protection isn't standard, but it's worth asking whether early employee grants get any refresh consideration at the next round.

## Cross-border and remote pushback

"I want to flag early that I'll be working from Geneva, not the US, so there may be considerations around how you handle payroll, benefits, and any local employment requirements for a Swiss-based remote hire. I don't expect that to change the comp conversation, since cost of living here isn't lower than LA, but I'd rather surface it now than have it become a surprise once we're past the offer stage."

## Competing offer template

"I'm currently in a Senior Staff role with cross-team architecture scope, so I'm not moving out of urgency, and I've got a couple of other conversations running in parallel at similar or slightly higher base comp. What would move this one to the top of the list isn't the number alone, it's the chance to own a product surface end to end again the way I did at Alaya, so I'd rather find a number that reflects that scope than negotiate purely on urgency."

Use only if genuinely true at the time of the conversation; don't reference a specific competing offer or figure that doesn't exist.
