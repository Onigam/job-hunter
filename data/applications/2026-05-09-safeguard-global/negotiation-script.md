# Negotiation Script — Principal Engineer, Safeguard Global

## Market Context

**Role level:** Principal Engineer (equivalent to Staff+ in most tech orgs, 8-12+ years required)
**Company type:** Established global SaaS company (workforce management, 170+ countries), not a startup
**Work arrangement:** Fully remote
**Your location:** Geneva, Switzerland (one of the most expensive cities in Europe)

**Switzerland market data for Principal/Staff Engineer, remote-eligible:**
- P50 range: CHF 150,000 - 165,000 base
- P75 range: CHF 165,000 - 185,000 base
- P90 range: CHF 185,000 - 210,000 base (reserved for tech leads at top-tier scale-ups)
- Safeguard Global hires globally; their budget likely reflects a wide band. As a Geneva-based candidate, anchor to Swiss market, not to their lowest band.

**Your profile premium factors:**
- 17 years experience (well above 8-12 requirement)
- Founding engineer at a successfully acquired company (direct, quantified outcome)
- Multi-tenant SaaS background on exact same stack (Node.js/TypeScript)
- AI-augmented engineering at senior level (differentiator in 2026)
- Already working remotely (no ramp-up or relocation cost to them)

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for your range, don't give one. Use this:

> "Before I give a number, I'd want to understand the full package, since base, bonus, and equity all factor in differently. That said, based on my background and the Geneva market, I'm targeting a base in the CHF 175,000 - 190,000 range. I'm flexible on structure if the total comp makes sense."

If pushed for a single number, say **CHF 180,000**.

Why this anchor works: it's above P75 but below P90, so it reads as confident rather than unrealistic. It also opens room to move without conceding too fast.

---

## If They Come In Below CHF 155,000

> "I appreciate the offer. The role itself is a strong fit, which is why I want to work through the numbers. What you've shared is a bit below what I was expecting for a principal-level role in Geneva, and also below where comparable offers have been landing. Is there flexibility in the base, or is the structure more weighted toward variable comp?"

Then wait. Don't fill the silence.

If they say the base is fixed, shift to:
> "Is there room to discuss a sign-on, or to revisit the base at the 6-month mark tied to a clear set of milestones?"

---

## If They Come In at CHF 155,000 - 165,000

Acknowledge, don't celebrate.

> "That's closer to where I was thinking. I want to be honest with you: based on where the Geneva market is right now for someone at this level, I'd need to get to at least CHF 175,000 to make this work. I'm not trying to be difficult, I just want to be transparent so we're not wasting each other's time. Is there room to close that gap?"

---

## Geneva Cost of Living Argument (if needed)

Use this if they cite a "global salary band" or reference lower-cost-of-living markets:

> "I understand that you hire globally and the bands may reflect that. My situation is that I'm based in Geneva, where rent, social contributions, and the general cost of living are significantly higher than most European cities. I'm not asking for a Geneva premium on top of your band, I'm just asking to be at the high end of what's reasonable for someone at this level. The alternative would be for me to take a role locally that prices in Geneva from the start."

---

## Equity / Bonus Negotiation

Safeguard Global is not a startup. Expect:
- Annual corporate bonus (5-15% of base is typical)
- Possibly RSUs if publicly traded or pre-IPO equivalent
- No aggressive equity cliff to negotiate

**Bonus target ask:**
> "What does the variable comp structure look like? And is the target bonus percentage guaranteed, or fully performance-contingent?"

Aim for at least 10% target bonus, tied to clearly defined metrics rather than a discretionary manager assessment.

**If RSUs are offered:**
> "What's the vesting schedule? And is there an accelerated vesting clause on change of control?" (If Safeguard Global gets acquired, you want your unvested shares to vest.)

---

## Competing Offer Template (even if hypothetical)

Only use this if you have a real or near-real competing offer. Don't fabricate.

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations with one other company at a similar level. Their offer is coming in around [X]. I'd prefer this role for [specific reason, e.g., the technical challenge, the distributed systems scope], but the gap makes it hard to walk away from the other opportunity. Is there anything you can do to close it?"

---

## Non-Salary Items Worth Negotiating

If base is truly stuck, these have real value:

- **Equipment budget:** Ask for CHF 3,000-5,000 to set up a proper home office
- **Learning budget:** CHF 2,000-3,000/year for conferences and courses (many companies offer this; ask explicitly)
- **Start date flexibility:** More time = more leverage if you have other conversations ongoing
- **Title:** "Principal Engineer" is already right. Don't trade title for comp.
- **Review cycle:** Ask for a 6-month formal review with a clear compensation discussion built in

---

## Closing the Negotiation

Once you've agreed on terms:

> "I'm ready to move forward. Can you send the updated offer letter by [date]? I'll plan to sign within [2-3 business days] of receiving it."

Don't accept verbally without seeing it in writing. Don't sign before you've had 24 hours to sit with it.
