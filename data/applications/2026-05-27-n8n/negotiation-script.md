# Negotiation Script — n8n Senior Product Engineer

## Context

n8n is a Berlin-based, remote-first company (Series B, $60M raised, ~100+ employees). Compensation is likely benchmarked against German or broader EU market rates. Magino is based in Geneva, Switzerland, where cost of living is significantly higher than Berlin (roughly 50-70% higher). This gap is the central negotiation lever.

---

## Market Rate Research

**Geneva / Switzerland — Senior/Staff Software Engineer:**
- Senior SWE at a scale-up: CHF 140-175k
- Staff SWE / Principal: CHF 165-210k
- Founding engineer profile: CHF 160-190k base depending on equity
- These are base salary figures; bonus structures vary

**Berlin — comparable role at Series B:**
- EUR 90-120k base is typical for a senior product engineer at this stage
- n8n's posting does not list a salary band publicly

**Switzerland vs. Berlin delta:** At purchasing-power parity, a Geneva-based engineer targeting CHF 160k is asking for something that maps to EUR 105-110k in Berlin terms. This is a reasonable, justified request.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for your salary expectation, give a number first. Anchoring high but credibly is more effective than waiting for their offer.

**Script:**

> "Based on the role scope and my background, I'm looking at CHF 165-175k base, which aligns with senior/staff-level compensation in the Swiss market. I'm open to conversation on the equity side depending on where the base lands."

**Why this works:**
- CHF 175k is high but not insulting for a 17-year engineer with a unicorn exit
- Mentioning equity flexibility signals you're pragmatic, not just fixated on base
- Giving a range anchors the top number in their mind

---

## If They Come In Below Expectations

**Scenario: They offer EUR 100-110k (roughly CHF 95-105k after conversion)**

> "I appreciate the offer. The role is genuinely interesting and I want to make this work. That said, I'm based in Geneva and the cost of living differential between here and Berlin is significant. I'd need to land closer to CHF 155-160k to make the numbers work on my end. Is there flexibility there, or would a stronger equity component help bridge that gap?"

**Key moves:**
- Don't say the offer is low, say the math doesn't work given your location
- Offer an alternative path (equity) so they have something to move toward
- Give a specific counter number, not a range — a range signals the low end is acceptable

---

## Equity Negotiation

n8n is Series B, $60M raise. Their valuation is not public but comparable Series B companies in this space are typically valued at 5-10x ARR. With 6x YoY growth, this is a high-growth company where equity could be meaningful.

**What to ask for:**
- Standard for a senior engineer at Series B: 0.05-0.15%
- Given Magino's founding-engineer profile and exit background: aim for 0.1-0.2%
- Vesting: 4-year standard, 1-year cliff — this is industry norm, don't negotiate unless they try to extend the cliff

**Script:**

> "On equity, what's the range you're working with for this level? I'd want to understand the vesting schedule and whether there's an acceleration clause on acquisition. Given my background, I'd like equity to be a meaningful part of the package."

**Acceleration clause:** Push for double-trigger acceleration (full acceleration if there's an acquisition AND you're let go). Single-trigger is unusual for engineers but worth mentioning if the topic comes up.

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

Some remote-first companies apply geographic pay bands and will try to pay Geneva-based employees at lower rates than Swiss market norms (arguing you're still accessing their job at Berlin prices). Push back on this.

**Script:**

> "I understand some companies use location-based pay bands, but I'd want to align on Swiss market rates. My living costs, tax obligations, and retirement contributions are all calculated in CHF at Geneva levels. A discount relative to what this role would pay in Switzerland doesn't really work for me practically."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or want to manufacture urgency):

> "I want to be transparent: I'm at a later stage in another process. I'd prefer n8n given the product focus, but I'll need to move on a timeline. Is there flexibility to accelerate the decision on your end?"

Even without a competing offer, signalling that you're actively in process is often enough to move a timeline.

---

## Checklist Before Signing

- [ ] Base salary confirmed in CHF or EUR with clear conversion agreement
- [ ] Equity percentage, total shares, and current preferred share price documented
- [ ] Vesting schedule: 4-year, 1-year cliff confirmed
- [ ] Any acceleration clause on acquisition
- [ ] Remote working setup confirmed (Geneva, Switzerland)
- [ ] 30 vacation days confirmed (European standard per job posting)
- [ ] Professional development budget (EUR/CHF 1k/year) confirmed
- [ ] Health/pension contributions clarified for Swiss residency
