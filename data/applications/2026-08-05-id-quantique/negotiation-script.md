# Negotiation Script: Staff DevSecOps Engineer, ID Quantique

## Context to keep in mind

ID Quantique is Geneva-based and now part of IonQ, a Nasdaq-listed quantum computing company. That changes the negotiation shape compared to an early-stage startup: there's likely a public-company RSU program instead of founder-style equity, an established leveling and comp band structure to work within, and probably less room to move on cash than a startup would have, but a real, liquid equity component instead of paper that only means something after a future exit. The role is on-site at least three days a week in Geneva, so there's no geographic discount conversation to fight, this is a local hire at local market rate, but the comp band might still be anchored to a US or group-wide structure that undervalues Geneva cost of living if nobody pushes back on it. Supply-chain security ownership for a company whose entire product is trust in cryptographic infrastructure is also a genuine premium skill, not a generic platform role, and that's worth reflecting in the number.

## Switzerland market anchors (protect the floor, don't lead with these)

- Staff / Principal Engineer, Geneva or Zurich, established tech or deep-tech company: CHF 175k to 225k base, often plus bonus in the 10 to 20% range.
- DevSecOps and platform security roles with real supply-chain, IaC and CI/CD ownership tend to sit at the upper half of that band or above it, closer to CHF 195k to 235k when the company has real compliance and audit stakes.
- Geneva cost of living runs high enough that a comp band built around IonQ's broader US or group structure can land 20 to 30% under what the same scope pays locally in cash terms, even before touching equity.
- Sign-on bonuses are common in Switzerland when a candidate is leaving unvested equity or a bonus cycle behind at a current employer, worth raising explicitly rather than assuming it's off the table.

Anchor to the scope, owning the DevSecOps platform, CI/CD and release engineering standards, software supply-chain security across the org, not to a generic Geneva engineer number. Use the Swiss figures to defend the floor if the offer opens low relative to local market.

## Opening anchor

Wait for a real number before naming one. If asked to go first:

"For a Staff role owning the DevSecOps platform, CI/CD standards and software supply-chain security across the organization, I'd expect a base in the 200 to 225k CHF range, plus an annual bonus in the 10 to 20% range and an RSU grant sized for a staff-level hire at IonQ's current stage. I'd want to understand how the equity refresh cycle works before locking in the split between cash and RSUs."

Adjust once their number is on the table. Don't restate the anchor if they open above it, just confirm and move to the details.

## If they come in low on cash

"If the base is fixed by a group-wide band, I'd want to look at the RSU grant size, the vesting cadence, and whether there's room for an accelerated first refresh instead of waiting the standard cycle. A signing bonus that bridges the first year would also work, especially if I'm leaving unvested equity or a bonus cycle behind."

Fallback if they won't move on cash or RSUs: ask for a signing bonus explicitly framed as compensating for value left on the table at a current role, and ask whether the title or level itself has room to move, since a level bump often reopens the comp band without anyone calling it a special exception.

## Equity and RSU notes (public company, not startup paper)

- Ask for the RSU grant in actual share count and current share price, not just a dollar value at time of offer, since IonQ's stock has moved a lot and a dollar-denominated grant can be quietly smaller in shares than it sounds.
- Confirm the vesting schedule, most public-company grants run four years with a one-year cliff, and ask specifically whether there's an annual refresh program tied to performance review, not just the initial grant.
- Ask how RSU vesting is taxed for a Swiss tax resident when it's a US-listed company issuing the shares. This is a real complexity, not a formality, and worth a conversation with a tax advisor before accepting a number that looks good on paper.
- Ask whether there's a blackout window policy around sales given IonQ is publicly traded, since that affects when vested shares can actually be sold.

## Geographic and internal-band pushback

If the offer references a group-wide or US-anchored band as the reason cash can't move:

"I understand the band is set at the group level, but this is a Geneva-based, on-site role with real local cost of living, so I'd want the base benchmarked against Geneva market rate for a staff-level platform and security hire, not a band built around a different location. If the structure genuinely can't flex on base, that's where the bonus or RSU side needs to do the work instead."

## Competing offer template

Use only if a real competing process exists. Never invent a number or company name.

"I'm also further along in a conversation for a comparable staff-level platform engineering role elsewhere. I'd rather make this work here given the scope and the fact that I'm already in Geneva, so if there's flexibility on base, bonus or the RSU grant, this is the point where I'd want to know."

## On-site logistics, worth raising before comp is finalized

The role asks for a minimum of three days a week in the Geneva office, more depending on team needs, which isn't a comp lever by itself but is worth pinning down before treating any number as final. Confirm whether the three-day minimum is fixed or whether it flexes upward during release cycles or an incident response, since that changes what the day-to-day job actually looks like against whatever base and bonus land on the table.
