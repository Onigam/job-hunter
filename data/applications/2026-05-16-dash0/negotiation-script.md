# Negotiation Script — Dash0, VP of Engineering

---

## Market Context

Dash0 is a Netherlands-headquartered startup (Amsterdam area) that reached unicorn status ($1B valuation) with its $110M Series B in 2026, bringing total funding to $155M. The company has 40+ employees, 600+ paying customers, and is targeting accelerated US expansion in 2026. The VP Engineering role reports to CTO Ben Blackmore and owns the full engineering organization including Directors of Engineering and their teams.

This is a leadership hire at a company with real product-market fit, meaningful runway, and a clear growth phase. The equity is not a lottery ticket but it's also not illiquid fiction: $1B valuation with $155M raised at Series B is a credible exit trajectory for a developer tools company, especially one with enterprise customers like Zalando and The Telegraph.

**Amsterdam market benchmarks for VP Engineering (2026):**
- Series A startup: €130,000-160,000 base
- Series B / growth stage: €160,000-200,000 base
- Unicorn / late-stage: €190,000-240,000 base
- Dash0 tier target: €195,000-220,000 base

**Swiss / Geneva market benchmarks for equivalent role (CHF):**
- VP Engineering, scale-up: CHF 220,000-270,000 base
- Geneva cost of living is materially higher than Amsterdam. If you're remote from Geneva, that gap is relevant.

**Equity at Series B, VP Engineering band:**
- Typical range post-Series B: 0.25%-0.6% on a 4-year vest
- At $1B valuation: that's $2.5M-$6M paper value at current. Discount heavily for dilution and exit risk; price the floor conservatively at 3-5x revenue multiple
- Ask for the top of the band (0.5%-0.6%) given that VP Engineering is a senior leadership hire, not a standard IC role

**Your anchor target: €210,000-220,000 base** (or CHF 240,000-250,000 if remote from Switzerland). Justify with scope (full org leadership at a unicorn), seniority (founder-level track record), and the Geneva cost-of-living premium if applicable.

---

## Remote vs. Onsite Situation

The role is listed as Amsterdam area. Clarify early whether remote is an option. If they want onsite or hybrid in Amsterdam:

- Dutch employer, Swiss resident creates a cross-border situation. Most companies at this stage use an employer-of-record (EOR) service for foreign remote hires, which costs them an additional 15-25% on top of gross compensation
- Factor this in: if they're considering you as a remote Geneva hire, that EOR overhead justifies pushing toward the higher end of the range
- If they want you to relocate: that's a separate negotiation item. Ask for a relocation package (CHF 15,000-25,000 one-time, plus first-month temporary housing) and do not let the relocation cost come out of your compensation

---

## Opening Anchor Script

When they ask for your expectations or make a first offer:

> "I've looked at the Amsterdam and Swiss markets for VP Engineering at this stage and I'm targeting €215,000 base. That's calibrated to unicorn-stage leadership scope and to my track record, which includes building and scaling a platform from zero through acquisition. I'm open to discussing the equity structure if that creates flexibility on the base, but I'd want the base to reflect the organizational scope of the role, not just the title."

**Why this works:**
- It references a market, not personal need
- It signals research at the level-appropriate tier (unicorn, not generic startup)
- The equity mention opens that conversation without pre-emptively trading base for it
- "Organizational scope" anchors the ask to the VP level, not a senior IC level

---

## If They Come In Below Your Anchor

If the initial offer is below €180,000 (or CHF 205,000 equivalent):

> "I appreciate the offer and I'm genuinely interested in this role. The number is below where I need to be to make this move, and I want to be straightforward about that rather than string things along. Can you walk me through whether there's flexibility, either on base or on the equity size? I'd rather understand the full picture before saying no to something I find interesting."

This keeps the door open without accepting a below-floor number. Don't counter with a specific number while they're below your floor. Get them to reveal whether room exists first.

**Hard floor: €180,000 base** (or CHF 210,000 if negotiated in CHF). Below that, the equity would need to be exceptional and the vesting structure extremely favorable to justify the move from a Senior Staff role.

---

## Equity Negotiation

At Series B with $155M raised, the equity is meaningful but not free money. Price it conservatively.

**What to ask:**
- Grant size: for VP Engineering at this stage, 0.4%-0.6% is appropriate. Push toward the higher end if the base is toward the lower end of your range
- Vesting: standard 4 years with 1-year cliff. If they propose anything shorter, ask why
- Current strike price and 409A / preferred share spread: the gap tells you the realistic option upside at a conservative exit multiple
- Acceleration: single-trigger acceleration on acquisition or change of control. Given that you've been through an acquisition from the founder side, this is an informed ask, not a negotiating tactic
- Post-termination exercise window: ask for at least 2-3 years after departure. The standard 90-day window is a trap for senior hires who may have significant vested equity

**Equity ask script:**

> "On equity, I'd want to understand the grant size you're considering for this level and what the current valuation looks like from the employee perspective: the strike price versus the preferred. I've been through an acquisition from the founding side, so I pay attention to the vesting and exercise structure, not just the percentage. I'd also want to know whether there's acceleration on a change of control, and what the post-termination exercise window looks like."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they reference a "European rate" or apply any discount relative to a US-based hire:

> "I'd ask that you calibrate to Geneva specifically rather than European averages. Geneva's cost of living index sits alongside Zurich and above London on most measures. A European discount benchmarked to Berlin or Amsterdam costs me significantly more in real terms than the numbers suggest. If you're hiring someone based in Geneva, price it for Geneva."

Supporting data: Geneva's cost of living is 25-35% higher than Amsterdam on housing and 15-20% higher overall. It's the most expensive or second-most-expensive city in Europe depending on the index.

If the role requires onsite Amsterdam and relocation is on the table, this argument inverts: Amsterdam is cheaper than Geneva, but that's a lifestyle choice argument, not a compensation argument. Focus the negotiation on market rate for the role, not cost of living, if you're moving.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have or receive another offer during the process:

> "I want to be transparent with you: I'm in late-stage conversations at another company and I expect an offer in the next two to three weeks. I've found Dash0 more compelling based on the stage and the technical problem, and I'd rather close this process first. Is it possible to move to the offer stage on your end, or accelerate the timeline?"

If no competing offer exists, do not fabricate one. Use time pressure honestly:

> "I'm actively in final rounds elsewhere and I expect things to come to a head in the next few weeks. I'd prefer to close with Dash0 first, so it would help to know your typical timeline from this stage."

---

## Non-Salary Items Worth Negotiating

- **Equity refresh grants**: for a VP-level hire, ask whether there's a policy for performance-based equity refreshes after year two. This matters more than the initial grant at a company growing this fast
- **Start date**: Swiss notice periods are typically 3 months at this level. Set the expectation early. If they push for faster, ask for a financial bridge or a day-one signing bonus to cover the gap
- **Equipment budget**: CHF 4,000-6,000 for hardware setup is standard for remote senior hires. Ask for it upfront if not offered
- **Learning and conference budget**: CHF 2,500-4,000/year is reasonable at this level. Often forgotten in negotiations
- **Title clarity**: confirm that VP Engineering is the actual internal level and will be reflected in all documentation, not just the posting

---

## What to Never Do

- Don't accept the first number without at least one counter, even if it's in your range
- Don't reveal your current compensation before getting their offer range. If pressed: "I'd rather not anchor the conversation to my current package since the scope here is quite different from a Senior Staff IC role"
- Don't negotiate against yourself by giving a range. Give a single anchor number and defend it
- Don't treat equity as a bonus. Model it at a conservative 4-5x revenue multiple exit, not the VC optimistic case. That gives you a floor to reason from
- Don't agree to a 90-day post-termination exercise window on your options. It's negotiable and it matters
