# Negotiation Script: Frontiers, Senior AI Platform Engineer (FrontiersOS)

## What the posting tells us

- **Location:** Lausanne, office-based "with flexibility where appropriate". Commuting from Geneva takes about 35 to 50 minutes door to door by train.
- **Salary:** not published.
- **Seniority:** LinkedIn shows "Associate", which is almost certainly a default field. The content says otherwise: 10+ years required, owning major components end to end, setting architecture and roadmaps. Pin the level down early (Senior, Staff, Lead?), because the band depends on it.
- **Benefits listed:** extra wellbeing days, up to 3 volunteering days, EAP, L&D budget, "competitive local benefits (healthcare, pension)".
- **Company type:** Frontiers is an established, privately held publisher headquartered in Lausanne, not a VC-backed startup. Don't expect stock options. The real levers are base, bonus, pension, flexibility and level.

## Switzerland market reference (2026, estimates)

These ranges are approximate and drawn from general Swiss market knowledge. Check them against levels.fyi, Glassdoor (Frontiers Lausanne), and the Swissdevjobs salary reports before the first call.

| Profile | Base, Vaud / Geneva |
|---|---|
| Senior software engineer, local product company | CHF 120k to 150k |
| Senior / Staff platform or AI engineer, local scale-up or established tech-heavy company | CHF 145k to 180k |
| Staff at big tech in Zurich (Google, etc.) | CHF 200k to 250k base, plus RSUs |

Lausanne generally pays a little below Geneva and noticeably below Zurich. On the other hand, AI platform skills (LLM routing, RAG, evals, agent tooling) are scarce right now and command a premium over generic backend roles.

## Targets

- **Walk-away base:** CHF 145,000. Below this, it's a step down from Senior Staff level and doesn't cover the commute.
- **Target base:** CHF 160,000 to 165,000.
- **Opening anchor:** CHF 175,000, justified by 17 years of experience, Staff-level scope, and hands-on agent and LLM platform work.
- **Total comp extras worth asking about:** annual bonus target, employer pension (LPP) contribution above the legal minimum, a GA travelcard or commute allowance (a 2nd class GA costs about CHF 4k a year), and a fixed number of remote days per week.

## Script: early expectations question (recruiter screen)

"For a senior platform role with this kind of scope, owning major FrontiersOS components and setting their roadmap, I'm looking at around 175 thousand base. I'm flexible on how the package is put together, so it would help to know the band you've set for the role and whether there's a bonus component."

If they push for a range: "Somewhere between 165 and 185, depending on level and the rest of the package."

If they ask for current salary first: "I'd rather talk about what this role is worth than about my last package. It was a remote role at a Canadian company, so it doesn't map one to one. What range have you budgeted?"

## Script: if the offer comes in low (say CHF 135k to 145k)

"Thanks, I appreciate the offer, and I want to make this work. The base is below what I was expecting for the level we discussed. My past work matches the posting closely: I've owned a platform end to end from a blank page through an acquisition, and I ran AI tooling adoption across teams at Benevity. Could we get the base to 165? If the band is fixed, I'd like to look at the level, since the scope described sounds like Staff more than Senior."

If base truly can't move: "Okay. If the base has to stay where it is, I'd look at three things instead: a signing bonus to close part of the gap, a guaranteed salary review at six months tied to FrontiersOS milestones, and two fixed remote days a week given the Geneva commute."

## Equity and long-term incentives

Frontiers isn't a startup, so standard options are unlikely. Still ask, because established private companies sometimes have something:

"Is there any long-term incentive plan, profit share or retention bonus for senior engineering roles?"

If something exists, get the details in writing: vesting schedule, cliff, how payouts are valued, and what happens if you leave or the company changes ownership. If nothing exists, use that to justify a higher base: "Since there's no equity component, I'd expect cash comp to carry more of the weight."

## Location and commute pushback

Frontiers is office-based, and you live in Geneva, not Lausanne. Treat the commute as a negotiation point, not a complaint.

"I'm happy to be in Lausanne regularly, being close to the business users matters for this role. Day to day, though, it's a 40-minute train ride each way from Geneva. Could we agree on a set number of remote days, and is there a travel allowance or GA contribution?"

If they offer a lower number using "Lausanne rates": "I understand Vaud bands sit a little under Geneva's. The skills this role needs are hired against Zurich and remote international offers, not just local ones, and that's the market I'm in."

## Competing offer template (adapt to reality, never invent)

Only use this if there's a real competing process. Saying "I'm in other conversations" is fine and true while the search is active.

"I want to be transparent: I'm at a late stage with another company, and their offer is around [X] base. Frontiers is my preference, because FrontiersOS is the problem I'd actually want to work on, and it's close to home. If you can get closer to [Y], I'm ready to sign this week."

If there's no offer yet: "I'm in a few other conversations at similar levels, mostly Staff-level platform roles. I'd rather decide on the merits of the role, so I want to make sure the package isn't what tips it."

## Before signing, confirm in writing

- Title and level (Senior vs Staff), plus how promotion to the next level works
- Base, bonus target and how the bonus is calculated
- Employer LPP contribution rate
- Remote and flexibility policy, in numbers
- Notice period and probation length (Swiss default is 3 months probation)
- Start date and any signing bonus clawback terms
