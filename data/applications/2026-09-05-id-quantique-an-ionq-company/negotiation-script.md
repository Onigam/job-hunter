# Negotiation Script: Staff DevSecOps Engineer, ID Quantique (an IonQ company)

## Context that shapes the numbers

ID Quantique is a Geneva company that IonQ, a Nasdaq-listed quantum computing firm, acquired. That matters for how this negotiation goes: there's likely a public-company RSU program rather than founder-style paper equity, and probably an established leveling structure rather than startup improvisation. It also means less room on cash than an early-stage company might offer, but real, liquid equity instead of a promise that only pays off after some future exit. The posting asks for 8+ years of production engineering with hands-on CI/CD platform ownership plus supply-chain security depth, SBOMs, SLSA, artifact signing, which is a genuine premium skill set, not a generic platform role, and the number should reflect that. There's no relocation or visa support mentioned, so this reads as a local Geneva hire, which helps here since there's no geographic-discount argument to fight off.

## Switzerland market anchors

- Staff or Principal engineer, Geneva or Zurich, established tech or deep-tech company: roughly CHF 175k to 225k base, often with a bonus around 10 to 20%.
- DevSecOps and platform security roles carrying real supply-chain, IaC and release-engineering ownership tend to sit at the upper half of that range or above, closer to CHF 195k to 235k when the company has compliance and audit exposure the way a cryptography vendor does.
- A comp band inherited from IonQ's broader US or group structure can undershoot Geneva market rate by 20 to 30% in cash terms if nobody pushes back, even before equity enters the conversation.
- Sign-on bonuses are common locally when a candidate is walking away from unvested equity or a bonus cycle elsewhere, worth raising directly rather than assuming it's off the table.

Anchor on the actual scope, owning the software factory roadmap, embedding security through the delivery pipeline, mentoring engineers on standards, not on a generic Geneva engineer benchmark.

## Opening anchor script

Let them name a number first if possible. If pushed to go first:

"For a Staff role owning the CI/CD platform, release engineering and supply-chain security across the org, I'd expect a base around CHF 210k, plus a bonus in the 10 to 20% range and an RSU grant sized for a staff-level hire at IonQ's current stage. I'd want to understand the refresh cadence on the equity side before we settle the split between cash and RSUs."

## If they come in low on cash

"Is that number benchmarked against Geneva specifically, or against a broader group band? A role this close to the company's actual trust guarantees, supply-chain integrity for a cryptography vendor, tends to price at a premium, not a discount, wherever it sits."

If they hold firm on base: "I can work with a base closer to your standard band if the RSU grant moves up to reflect staff-level scope, and if there's a clear point to revisit it at the next refresh cycle rather than waiting the full standard period. A signing bonus would also help bridge the gap, especially since I'd be leaving unvested equity behind."

## Equity and RSU notes, public company specifics

- Ask for the RSU grant in actual share count and the share price used to calculate it, not just a headline dollar figure, since a dollar-denominated number can look larger than the share count actually supports if the stock has moved.
- Confirm the vesting schedule. Most public-company grants run four years with a one-year cliff. Ask specifically whether there's an annual refresh tied to performance review rather than assuming the initial grant is the only one.
- Ask how RSU vesting is taxed for a Swiss resident holding shares in a US-listed company. This is a real conversation to have with a tax advisor before treating any headline number as final, not a formality to skip.
- Ask about blackout windows around trading given IonQ is publicly traded, since that affects when vested shares can actually be sold.

## Geographic and internal-band pushback

If the offer leans on a group-wide or US-anchored band as the reason cash is fixed:

"I understand the band is set at group level, but this is a Geneva-based role with Geneva cost of living, so I'd want the base benchmarked against local market rate for staff-level platform and security work, not a band built around a different location. If base genuinely can't move, the bonus or RSU side needs to make up the difference."

## Competing offer template

Use only if a real process exists elsewhere. Never invent a name or number.

"I'm also further along with another staff-level platform or security role. I'd rather land here given the scope and the fact that I'm already in Geneva, so if there's room on base, bonus or the RSU grant, now's the point to know."

## Close

The constraint at a company like this is rarely cash outright, it's which market and which internal band the offer gets pegged to. Anchor on Geneva reality since that's where the work actually happens, get the RSU grant in share count with a clear vesting and refresh schedule in writing, and treat the equity structure with the same scrutiny as the base number before accepting anything.
