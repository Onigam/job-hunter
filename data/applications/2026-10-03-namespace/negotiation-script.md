# Negotiation Script: Namespace, Product Engineer (Zurich, hybrid)

## Context that shapes the numbers

Namespace (Namespace Labs) builds developer infrastructure: faster GitHub Actions runners, remote Docker builders, Bazel/Turbo/Nix caching, and Devboxes for engineers and AI coding agents. They run their own hardware across several data centers. It's a small, venture-backed startup; check the latest funding round on Crunchbase or Dealroom before the call, because it changes how much equity room they have.

The posting (published 2 Oct 2026) says:

- **Location:** Switzerland, Zurich office, hybrid. Germany and the UK are listed as secondary locations.
- **Published base:** **USD 160k to 225k**, plus stock options.
- **Title:** Product Engineer, with no level attached. The same band covering three countries suggests it spans mid to senior, and they'll place you inside it based on interviews.

What follows from that:

1. **The band is wide, so the fight is over placement, not the ceiling.** With 17 years, a founder exit and five years as Senior Staff, you belong at the top. Their worry will be "overqualified, will he leave?", not "is he good enough?" Answer that early (see the "why Product Engineer" answer in `star-stories.md`), then argue for the top of the band.
2. **Clarify the currency.** A Swiss hire is almost certainly paid in CHF on Swiss payroll. Find out how they convert the USD band. At roughly 0.80 CHF per USD (check the rate on the day), the band is about **CHF 128k to 180k**.
3. **Geneva to Zurich costs real money.** Hybrid means regular train trips. Negotiate travel as a separate line item rather than folding it into salary.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough figures from public surveys and recruiter data. Re-check on levels.fyi (Zurich), Glassdoor, swissdevjobs.ch, and the Michael Page and Hays Switzerland salary guides.

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Senior Software / Product Engineer, Zurich startup | CHF 130k to 165k |
| Staff Engineer, Zurich scale-up or product company | CHF 165k to 210k |
| Senior / Staff at big tech in Zurich (Google, etc.) | CHF 170k to 230k base, plus RSUs |
| Staff Engineer, Geneva product company | CHF 160k to 200k |
| This posting, converted | CHF 128k to 180k |

The top of their band roughly matches the bottom of Staff-level pay in Zurich. That's your main argument: you aren't asking them to go beyond the band, only to put you at the top.

## Opening anchor

If they ask for expectations first (the band is public, so turn it back if you can):

"I've seen the published range, thanks for putting it up. Given my background, I'd expect to be at the top of it, so around USD 225k, or the CHF equivalent on Swiss payroll, about CHF 180k. I'd also want to understand the equity side properly before we settle anything."

Targets:

- **Aim for:** CHF 180k base (top of band), plus a meaningful option grant, plus a GA travelcard or a travel allowance.
- **Acceptable:** CHF 170k base with a larger grant and travel covered.
- **Walk-away floor (decide before the call):** around CHF 160k base. Below that, a hybrid Zurich role from Geneva is a step down from what Staff roles pay locally, however good the product is.

## If they come in low (e.g. USD 175k to 190k, about CHF 140k to 152k)

"Thanks, and I'm keen on this role. Namespace is the kind of product I'd use myself. What I'm weighing is that Staff-level engineers in Zurich usually start around CHF 165k, and I'm bringing 17 years, a startup I built from zero to acquisition, and recent experience getting engineers to adopt new developer tools. I'm not asking you to go above your band, only to place me at the top of it. Can we get closer to CHF 180k?"

If they say the base is fixed by level:

"Understood. Then let's look at the rest. A larger option grant, a sign-on bonus, or a guaranteed compensation review after six months tied to agreed goals would all work for me."

## The "overqualified" pushback

If they hint that the band tops out because it's a Product Engineer role, not Staff:

"That's fair, and I'm not asking for a Staff title. I chose this role because I want to ship product, not run architecture reviews. But the experience still comes with me. I've owned a product end to end, I've hired and onboarded engineers, and I can work on the hard customer conversations from day one. I'd rather that show up in where I sit in the band than in the title."

## Equity (stock options)

Namespace offers stock options. Ask for these numbers in writing; without them the grant can't be valued:

- Number of options and the **total fully diluted share count**, so you can work out the percentage.
- **Strike price** and the price per share at the latest preferred round.
- **Vesting schedule.** The standard is four years with a one-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Post-termination exercise window.** The default is often 90 days, which forces a big cash decision if you leave. Ask for 5 to 10 years; many developer-tools startups now offer this.
- **Acceleration on change of control.** Ask for double-trigger acceleration (vesting speeds up if the company is acquired *and* your role is cut). You've been through an acquisition, so you can explain why it matters.
- **Refresh grants.** Is there a policy for top-ups after year two?

Anti-dilution protection is an investor term. Employees don't get it, so don't spend capital asking. Ask instead how the option pool has been handled in past rounds and what the next round's likely dilution looks like.

Swiss tax note: options in unlisted companies are usually taxed as income when you exercise (sometimes at grant, depending on the plan and canton), on the gap between strike and fair value. Ask whether the plan has a Swiss tax ruling, and check with a Geneva tax advisor before exercising. Also confirm whether the options are issued by a Swiss entity or a US parent (Delaware), which changes the paperwork.

Line to use:

"Could you share the grant as a number of options, plus the fully diluted share count, the strike and the last preferred price? I'd like to value it properly rather than guess. I'd also like to discuss the exercise window. Ninety days is hard to plan around, and I'd like something closer to five years."

## Geography and travel

Geneva is not a cheap place to live, and the Zurich office is a 2h45 train ride each way. Don't let either turn into a discount.

"I'm in Geneva and happy to be in Zurich regularly. What's the expected rhythm, once a week or a few days a month? Either way, I'd ask Namespace to cover a GA travelcard, which is around CHF 4k a year, or reimburse the trips. That's a small line for you and makes the hybrid setup work."

If they suggest Geneva should mean a lower salary than Zurich:

"Geneva's cost of living is very close to Zurich's, and the commute adds cost and time on top. I'd see it as neutral at best."

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

"I want to be transparent. I'm in late-stage conversations with another company, a Staff role at around CHF [X]. Namespace is my first choice because of the product and the size of the team, and I'd rather sign here. If we can get the base to CHF [Y] and sort out the exercise window, I'm ready to decide this week."

Never invent an offer. If there isn't one, use the market data instead: "Comparable Staff roles in Zurich are coming in around CHF 165k to 210k."

## Order of play

1. Answer "why Product Engineer" convincingly before money comes up.
2. Confirm currency, payroll entity and expected office rhythm.
3. Get their number. Don't give yours first; the band is public.
4. Anchor at the top of the band (CHF ~180k), framed as placement, not an exception.
5. Ask for full equity details in writing, then negotiate the exercise window and acceleration.
6. Add travel as a separate line.
7. Get everything in writing before you resign or turn down other processes.
