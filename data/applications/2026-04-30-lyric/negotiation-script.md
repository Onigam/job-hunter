# Negotiation Script — Lyric, Staff Software Engineer

## Context
- Lyric is a US-based company (King of Prussia, PA), late-stage, ~500-1000 employees, $90.9M raised
- Role: Staff Software Engineer, US Remote
- No salary range published in the job posting
- Magino is based in Geneva, Switzerland; role requires US East Coast timezone overlap
- Lyric may attempt a geographic discount given Geneva location

---

## Market Reference Points

### US Market (Staff Software Engineer, Remote, Late-Stage)
- P50: $190K-$220K USD base
- P75: $220K-$260K USD base
- P90: $260K-$300K+ USD base
- Total comp at this level at well-funded companies typically includes 15-25% bonus and meaningful equity

### Switzerland / Geneva Market (equivalent seniority)
- Senior Staff / Principal Engineer in Geneva: CHF 160,000-220,000 base
- At post-Series C / late-stage US companies paying USD remotely: $190K-$240K USD remains reasonable
- Don't accept EUR-equivalent reductions if the company is paying in USD to US teammates

### Geographic Discount Considerations
- Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in the world (CoL index comparable to NYC/SF)
- A geographic discount from San Francisco rates to Geneva rates is not a real discount; it's trading one expensive city for another
- Pushback framing: "Geneva's cost of living is comparable to San Francisco. I'd expect the compensation to reflect that rather than a location-based reduction."

---

## Opening Anchor

When asked about compensation expectations, give a number before they do.

**Script:**
"Based on the seniority and scope of this role, and the US market for Staff engineers at this stage of company, I'm targeting $230,000-$250,000 base, plus equity. I'm open to discussing the full package including bonus and vesting structure."

Why this anchor: It's above the P50 but within the defensible range given 17+ years of experience, founding engineer background, and direct domain adjacency (distributed systems at scale, TypeScript/Node, AWS). Starting here gives room to land at $210K-$225K without leaving value on the table.

---

## If They Come In Low (below $200K)

**Script:**
"I appreciate the offer. The base is below where I need to land given the scope and my background. To make this work, I'd need to get to at least $220,000 base. Is there flexibility there, or would it make sense to look at whether the equity component could bridge some of that gap?"

Then stop talking. Let them respond.

**If they push back on location:**
"I'm based in Geneva, which has a cost of living comparable to New York or San Francisco, so I wouldn't expect a geographic discount to apply here. The time zone overlap with the East Coast is actually an asset for the team."

---

## Equity Negotiation (if startup-style equity offered)

Lyric is late-stage and has raised $90.9M. Equity is likely options or RSUs rather than early-stage grants. Key questions to ask:

1. "What's the current 409A valuation, and when was the last appraisal?"
2. "Are these ISOs or NSOs, and what's the strike price relative to the 409A?"
3. "What's the vesting schedule? Is there a 1-year cliff?"
4. "Is there any acceleration provision on acquisition or termination without cause?"
5. "What was the most recent preferred price in the Series B/C?"

**Equity anchor:** For a Staff role at this level, 0.05%-0.15% fully diluted is a reasonable ask depending on the company's stage. At $90M raised with a mature product, it's likely to be toward the lower end of that range, but you can ask.

**Script if equity feels thin:**
"The equity grant is below what I'd expect for this level. Given the company's trajectory and the scope of the role, would it be possible to get to [X shares / $X value at current 409A] over the vesting period? I want to make sure I'm aligned with the long-term outcomes."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in hand (or can credibly suggest one is in progress):

**Script:**
"I'm in late stages with another company at a higher base number. I'm genuinely more interested in Lyric's mission and technical challenges, so I'd like to find a way to make this work. Is there room to get closer to $240K?"

Note: Only use this if you actually have another process underway. Fabricating a competing offer that gets called is worse than not having one.

---

## Other Terms Worth Negotiating

- **Sign-on bonus:** If base is capped, a $20K-$40K sign-on is often easier to approve than a base increase. Ask for it as a bridge.
- **Vacation / PTO:** Unlimited PTO policies at US companies often mean less time off, not more. Ask for a specific minimum (25 days).
- **Home office stipend:** Standard for remote roles: $1,500-$3,000 one-time setup, plus monthly connectivity allowance.
- **Annual L&D budget:** Ask for at least $2,500/year for conferences, courses, books.
- **Equipment:** Confirm company-provided laptop (Mac preferred); if BYOD, that's a red flag worth probing.

---

## Timing and Process Notes

- Don't accept on the spot. "I'd like to take 48-72 hours to review the full package before responding." This is professional and expected.
- Get the offer in writing before negotiating. Verbal offers are not offers.
- If they ask "do you have any other offers?" before giving you the number: "I'm in conversations with other companies, but I'm focused on finding the right fit, not just the highest number. Can you tell me what the compensation range is for this role?"
