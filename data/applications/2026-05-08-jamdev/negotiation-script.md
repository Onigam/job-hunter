# Negotiation Script — Jam.dev, Staff Fullstack Engineer

---

## Market Context

Jam.dev has raised $10M with strategic backing from operators (Vercel CEO, Cloudflare CEO, GitHub CTO), not generalist VCs. This signals a company that's optimizing for product quality and developer credibility rather than hyper-growth metrics. At this funding stage, compensation typically looks different from a Series B or C: the base may be lower than a well-funded scale-up, but the equity percentage should be meaningfully higher and the role scope is genuinely founding-engineer level.

The team is described as small and senior, mostly ex-Cloudflare engineering directors turned ICs. That's an unusual and expensive team to maintain on $10M, which means either they've raised a follow-on round not yet announced, or the founders took lower salaries to preserve runway. Ask about the current funding state early in the process.

**US market benchmarks for Staff-level Fullstack, early-stage startup (2025-2026, USD):**
- Seed to Series A with $10M-$20M raised: $160K-$220K base + meaningful equity (0.15%-0.5%)
- Premium remote-first dev tools company (comparable: early Linear, early Vercel): $200K-$260K base

**Switzerland / Geneva market benchmarks (CHF):**
- Staff / Principal Engineer: CHF 160K-210K base
- Senior Staff / Founding-scope: CHF 200K-240K base

Jam will almost certainly denominate in USD for a European remote hire. Resist any frame that applies a "European discount" to what is effectively a US staff engineer role. Geneva is not Berlin.

**Your anchor target: USD 230K-250K base**, which sits in the upper half of what an early-stage company at this tier can typically offer. Justify it with the scope and your track record, not with personal need or location.

If they offer CHF denomination: target CHF 210K-230K base, calibrated to Geneva specifically.

---

## Opening Anchor Script

When they ask for your expectations or present a first offer:

> "Based on comparable roles at well-funded dev tools companies at this stage, I'm targeting around $240K base. That's calibrated to the staff engineer scope and to my background — founding engineer track record, the stack you're working in, and experience with exactly the scaling problems you're facing now. I'm genuinely flexible on structure if the equity component is meaningful, but I'd want the base to hold at that level regardless."

**Why this works:**
- It anchors to the role scope and company tier, not personal need.
- It signals you've thought about their stage specifically (not a Series D anchor).
- It opens the equity door without pre-trading base for it.
- "Founding engineer track record" positions you above a "strong senior" frame, which is accurate here.

---

## If They Come In Below Your Anchor

If the initial offer is below $200K base (or CHF equivalent below CHF 165K):

> "I appreciate the offer. The role is genuinely interesting and I'd like to find a way to make it work. That said, the number is below what I'd need to move from where I am, given both the seniority level and the cost of living in Geneva. Can you tell me whether there's flexibility, either on base or on the equity side? I want to understand the full picture before responding."

Don't counter immediately when they're below your floor. Get them to confirm whether room exists first. If they say the base is fixed:

> "If the base is firm, then I'd need the equity to be at the upper end of what you'd offer for this level. Can you walk me through the current cap table, the most recent 409A, and what percentage you're considering? That's the number I need to understand the total picture."

**Hard floor: $180K base** (or CHF 148K equivalent). Below that, the equity would need to be exceptional and well-documented to justify the move.

---

## Equity Negotiation

At Jam's stage, equity is where the real conversation is. $10M raised with strategic operators means the company may have relatively little dilution so far, and a potential exit at 5-15x revenue could make a 0.2%-0.4% grant meaningful.

**What to ask:**
- Grant size: for a staff-level hire at this stage, 0.15%-0.5% is the right range. Push toward 0.3%+ if the base is at the lower end.
- Vesting: standard 4-year with 1-year cliff. Ask what happens to unvested shares in an acquisition (single-trigger acceleration is a reasonable ask, especially given your Benevity experience).
- Current 409A fair market value vs. preferred price. The spread tells you the real option value, not the headline number.
- Option type: ISOs (incentive stock options) preferred for US employees; NSOs (non-qualified) are typical for non-US. Understand the tax treatment in Switzerland before accepting.
- Exercise window: ask for an extended window (5-7 years post-departure), not the standard 90 days. This is increasingly common at founder-friendly startups and protects you from a forced exercise decision when leaving.

**Equity ask script:**

> "On equity, I'd want to understand what percentage you're considering for this level, the current 409A fair market value versus the preferred share price, and what the vesting structure looks like. I've been through an acquisition from the engineering side, so I understand how vesting timing and option windows can work against you. I'd also ask about single-trigger acceleration on acquisition and an extended exercise window. Those are standard asks at this stage and they don't cost the company anything if there's no exit."

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If Jam references a "Europe rate" or proposes an adjustment relative to US-based hires:

> "Geneva is consistently one of the most expensive cities in the world, well above London or Paris in most cost-of-living indices. If you're calibrating to European cost of living, I'd ask that it be Geneva specifically, not a European average. An adjustment based on the wrong city works out to a real-terms difference of CHF 20K-40K annually, and I'd want to address that directly rather than discover it later."

Supporting data: Geneva's cost of living index is 20-30% higher than London on housing, and it's the most expensive city in Switzerland in most years. US staff engineer compensation should not be meaningfully discounted for a Geneva-based hire unless you're being offered a full CHF package with Swiss employment protections (which has its own value).

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have or receive another offer during the process:

> "I want to be straightforward with you: I'm at offer stage with another company and working through the details now. I'd genuinely prefer this conversation given what Jam is building, but I do have a timeline. Can you tell me whether you're in a position to move to the offer stage, or what the next step looks like on your end?"

If no competing offer exists, use timeline pressure honestly:

> "I'm in final-stage conversations elsewhere and expect an offer within the next two to three weeks. I want to give this process proper attention, so it would help to understand your typical timeline from this stage."

Do not fabricate a specific company name. A small team like Jam's will probe, and credibility matters more than the leverage.

---

## Non-Salary Items Worth Negotiating

- **Equipment budget**: CHF 3K-5K for hardware setup is standard for Swiss remote roles. Ask for it if not offered.
- **Co-working stipend**: CHF 300-500/month is reasonable if Jam doesn't have a Geneva office. Frame it as a productivity cost, not a perk.
- **Home office allowance**: a one-time CHF 1K-2K for ergonomic setup is a reasonable ask.
- **Start date**: standard Swiss notice period at this seniority is 3 months. Jam should expect this. If they push for faster, a financial bridge (paying out your notice period obligation) is a legitimate negotiating point.
- **Title**: confirm whether "Staff" is the internal level or just the posting label. If the scope is closer to Senior Staff or founding engineer, get the title that matches.
- **Equity refresh**: for an early-stage company, ask how they handle equity refresh grants after year two. Knowing the policy before joining avoids an awkward conversation later.

---

## What to Never Do

- Don't accept the first number without at least one counter, even if it's close to your target.
- Don't reveal your current salary before seeing their offer range. If pressed: "I'd rather not anchor the conversation to my current package since the scope here is genuinely different."
- Don't propose a range — give a single number and defend it. A range tells them the bottom half is acceptable.
- Don't price equity at the optimistic exit case. Use a conservative 5-10x revenue multiple to estimate floor value. That gives you a real number to defend, not a fantasy.
- Don't negotiate by email for anything substantive. Phone or video call means you can hear hesitation and respond in real time.
