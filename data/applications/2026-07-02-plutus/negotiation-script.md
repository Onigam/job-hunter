# Negotiation Script: Plutus, Founding Engineer

## Context to anchor on

Plutus is a Kirkland, WA-based, SEC-registered investment advisor, launched in January 2026, already managing tens of millions in AUM and targeting a billion by year end, on $1.65M raised across an angel round and a seed round. Under ten employees. The posted role didn't list a salary or equity band, which means the number they open with is a real signal of how they think about founding-stage comp, not a starting point they expect to be accepted. Role is US-based (Kirkland), and I'd be coming in from Geneva, which puts a remote-versus-relocation question on the table before comp even gets discussed. Financial services adds a wrinkle too: RIA-regulated companies often move slower on cash comp because of compliance overhead, but the equity upside on a seed-stage fintech growing this fast can be real if the option pool isn't already squeezed.

## Opening anchor

For a Geneva-based senior staff engineer with founding experience, US market data for a founding engineer at a funded seed-stage fintech typically runs $150K to $190K base plus 0.5% to 2% equity, before factoring in the remote and RIA-compliance angle. Swiss market comp for an equivalent senior staff or CTO-track role in Geneva runs CHF 160K to CHF 200K, which is the number I'd use as my own floor when comparing a US remote offer against staying local.

"Given where you're headed, tens of millions in AUM already and a billion targeted by year end, and what a founding engineer role like this usually carries in equity at this stage, I'd want to be talking in the $170K to $190K base range if this is a US-anchored comp structure, with equity around 1 to 1.5%, standard 4 year vesting with a 1 year cliff. If we're structuring this as a Geneva-based remote arrangement instead, I'd want to talk through it as a CHF-denominated package closer to local senior staff rates, since that's the market I'd otherwise be pricing myself against."

## If they come in low on salary

Early seed-stage RIAs often protect cash for compliance and custody costs before anything else, so a lowball open here is more believable than usual. Redirect to equity and vesting terms rather than pushing hard on cash alone.

"I get that a licensed advisor managing other people's money has real fixed costs before payroll even comes up. If $130K to $145K is where cash needs to sit, I'd want the equity to make up that gap, closer to 2% given the stage, plus a clear commitment on when comp gets revisited, tied to AUM milestones you're already tracking toward rather than a vague annual review."

## Equity negotiation

A sub-ten-person team this early usually still has most of the pool intact, which gives an early hire real room to push.

"Before we land on a number, I'd like to understand the option pool size, what's allocated to the founders versus reserved for hires, and the terms from the angel and seed rounds, valuation cap or price per share. Standard 4 year vesting, 1 year cliff works for me. What I'd push back on is any pool that's already thin enough that 1.5% today ends up diluted below where a smaller stake in a properly reserved pool would land after your next raise."

## Geographic and remote angle

This needs to be on the table in the first real conversation, not after an offer lands.

"I want to be upfront that I'm in Geneva, several hours ahead of Kirkland. I can cover meaningful overlap with US East Coast hours most days, which gets me a few hours of live overlap with a Pacific time team too. If a fully remote founding engineer works for you, I'd want that written into the offer rather than treated as a temporary arrangement. If you need someone on-site, that's a different conversation about relocation support and visa timeline, and I'd want to know which one we're actually discussing before we get further into numbers."

## Competing offer template

Only use this if there's an actual live process; a specific number that can't be backed up does more harm than good.

"I'm in later-stage conversations with a couple of other teams at a similar point, and the comp discussions there have landed in a similar band. I'd rather decide this on fit and what you're building toward than run a bidding process, but if there's room to move once we're both serious, I'd rather hear that now than after I've made a decision elsewhere."

## Closing move

End on the mission and the stage, since a team this small is hiring for conviction as much as for a resume.

"None of this is a blocker. I just want the base, equity, and remote structure to reflect the risk I'd be taking on joining pre-billion-AUM, and I'd rather get that right upfront than renegotiate it later. If the numbers line up, I'm ready to move fast."
