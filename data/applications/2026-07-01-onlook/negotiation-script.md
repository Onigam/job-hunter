# Negotiation Script: Onlook, Founding Engineer (Fullstack / AI)

## Context to anchor on

Posted range is $130K to $200K salary with 1.00% to 4.00% equity. Team of four, YC W25, seed to Series A stage most likely, 22,000+ GitHub stars and #1 on Hacker News at launch. That kind of pre-Series A traction with a tiny team is exactly the setup where equity should do more of the talking than salary, since the founders are still cash constrained but hold real negotiating power on valuation if the next round goes well. Role is US/CA based (office near South Park or remote), and I'd be coming in from Geneva needing either relocation support or a visa sponsorship conversation, which is itself a negotiating variable, not just a hurdle.

## Opening anchor

When comp comes up, aim for the top third of the posted band rather than the midpoint, since a founding engineer joining a four person team with a public GitHub track record is not an average hire.

"Given the traction you've already got, the four person team, and what a founding engineer role like this usually carries in equity at this stage, I'd want to be talking near the top of your range, call it $180K to $200K base, with equity toward the 3 to 4% end given how early this still is. I know there's a visa and relocation question on top of that, so let's figure out how those pieces fit together rather than treating them separately."

## If they come in low on salary

Founders at seed stage often lead with a number closer to the floor of the posted range to protect runway. Don't reject it outright, redirect to the total package.

"I get that runway matters more than anything else right now, so if $130K to $150K is where the salary needs to sit, I'd want the equity to reflect that gap, closer to 4% rather than 1%, plus standard 4 year vesting with a 1 year cliff and acceleration if there's ever a change of control. I'd also want to understand what the plan is for adjusting comp once you close a next round, ideally something in writing rather than a verbal promise."

## Equity negotiation

Given the wide 1% to 4% band, the actual number will depend heavily on how many other early hires exist and what's already been allocated to founders.

"Before we land on a number, I'd like to understand the cap table shape, how much is left in the option pool, what the last valuation or SAFE terms looked like, and whether there's an anti-dilution provision for early hires if you raise a priced round. Standard 4 year vesting, 1 year cliff works for me. What I'd push back on is a pool that's already so diluted that 4% today is worth less than 1% would be after a proper seed round."

## Geographic and relocation angle

Since the role is US/CA only and Magino is in Geneva, this needs to be surfaced early and honestly, not slipped in after an offer is made.

"I want to be upfront that I'm currently in Geneva, so this would mean either a relocation package and visa sponsorship, or working out a remote arrangement if that's something you're open to for the right person. If relocation is the path, I'd want moving costs and a lead time that's realistic for visa processing built into the start date, not squeezed into two weeks."

## Competing offer template

Use only if there's an actual competing process in motion; don't bluff a specific number that can be checked.

"I'm mid-process with a couple of other companies at a similar stage, and comp conversations there have been in a similar range. I'd rather make this decision based on fit and what you're building than a bidding war, but if there's flexibility on your end once we're both serious, I'd want to know before I close things out elsewhere."

## Closing move

End on alignment, not just numbers, since a four person team is hiring for conviction as much as skill.

"None of this is a blocker for me, I just want the structure to be fair given the visa piece and the stage you're at. If the equity and vesting reflect the risk I'd be taking on, I'm ready to move quickly."
