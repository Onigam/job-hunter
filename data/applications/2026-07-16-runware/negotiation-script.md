# Negotiation Script: Runware, Staff DevOps Engineer

## Context
Runware is a Series A AI infrastructure company (~$50M raised from Dawn Capital and Comcast Ventures, on top of an earlier $13M seed), building custom GPU hardware and its own data centers to run a proprietary inference engine across image, video, audio, and LLM workloads. The posting is framed as remote but tied to a UK base, while Magino is in Geneva. This is a real infrastructure role, bare metal, GPUs, networking, HA and low-latency systems, not a generalist platform-engineering title, so leveling and location need to be clarified early rather than assumed. Magino is Senior Staff level with 17+ years, a founder-to-acquisition track record (Alaya, sold to Benevity), and platform-ownership experience rather than deep bare-metal specialization, so the negotiation should lean on seniority and production-ownership breadth, not overstate hands-on GPU hardware depth he doesn't have.

## Market anchors (Switzerland-based, remote Staff DevOps / Infrastructure)
Staff-level DevOps, SRE, or infrastructure engineers negotiating from Switzerland but working for an early-growth-stage, UK-anchored startup typically land base cash in the CHF 160,000 to CHF 200,000 range, with total comp reaching CHF 190,000 to CHF 260,000 once equity is included, depending on how much premium the company puts on the bare-metal and GPU-specific skill set. Companies running their own hardware and data centers, rather than a standard cloud stack, often pay a modest premium for this role because the pool of candidates comfortable at that layer is smaller. Since the role is nominally UK-based, expect some pushback on Swiss-level compensation; the counter is that Runware is hiring for the skill set and the outcome, not the postcode, and remote infrastructure roles at this level are priced globally by companies that actually want to compete for senior talent.

Opening anchor: CHF 175,000 base, plus a meaningful equity grant appropriate for a Series A company at this stage, plus clarity on on-call compensation given the always-on nature of GPU inference infrastructure.

## Opening the conversation
"Before we get into numbers, can you help me understand how this role is leveled internally, and whether the comp band assumes a UK base or is flexible for someone working remotely from Switzerland?" Get the leveling and location question resolved before naming a number, since a UK-anchored band and a Swiss-anchored band can differ by a meaningful margin.

Once that's clear: "Given the scope here, production ownership across bare-metal, GPU capacity, and high-availability systems, and Swiss market rates for a Staff-level hire with a founding and exit background, I'd be looking at a base around CHF 175,000, with equity and on-call terms reflecting the always-on nature of the platform. Where does that land relative to your band?"

## If they come in low on base
"I get that early-stage companies often weight equity heavier than cash, and I'm fine with that if the equity terms are concrete and the vesting is standard. But this role carries real on-call load on infrastructure that can't go down without hurting customers like Wix or Together.ai, so I'd want to land closer to CHF 155,000 to CHF 165,000 on base if equity and on-call terms are where we land instead."

## Equity negotiation
Runware is Series A, so equity is likely options with a standard four-year vesting schedule and a one-year cliff, priced against the most recent round. Ask directly for: option count or percentage, strike price and how it's set relative to the Series A valuation, vesting and cliff terms, what happens to unvested equity on an exit or termination, and whether there's a plan for a future liquidity event or secondary sale as the company grows. Given how recent the Series A is, it's fair to ask whether the option pool has been refreshed post-raise or whether early offers are being made against a stale, pre-raise pool.

## On-call and reliability terms
Since this role sits on infrastructure that's genuinely hard to take down without cost, on-call terms should be explicit before the base is finalized: is there separate on-call pay or comp time, what's the rotation size and frequency, and what's the actual incident load been over the last two quarters. If they frame it as "everyone just pitches in when needed" without structure, push back gently: "I want to make sure on-call expectations are explicit and fairly compensated, since that's usually where infrastructure roles quietly become much bigger than the job description."

## Geographic and remote-status pushback
If they push a UK-anchored number specifically because the role is UK-based on paper: "I understand the posting is anchored to the UK, but I'd be doing the same work with the same on-call responsibility from Geneva, and Swiss cost of living and market rates are the honest basis for what I'd need to make this work long-term." Have the CHF 175,000 anchor ready rather than re-litigating the principle every time it comes up.

## Using a competing process
Even without a live competing offer: "I'm in later-stage conversations with a couple of other infrastructure and platform-engineering roles at a similar level, so I'd want to get to a decision within three to four weeks if the fit is right here." Keep it honest and non-specific rather than inventing details.

## Closing
"Building your own hardware stack instead of renting someone else's cloud is the genuinely interesting part of this role, and I want it to work. Let's get leveling, location basis, and on-call terms aligned this week so we can move toward a decision." A soft deadline, not an ultimatum.
