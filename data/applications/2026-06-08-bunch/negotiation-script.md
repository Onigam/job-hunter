# Negotiation Script — Senior Platform Engineer @ bunch

---

## Market Context

**Company situation**: bunch just closed a €30.1M Series B in May 2026 (led by Portage). ARR grew 300% in 2025, NRR 156%. They have 18-24 months of runway and are in active scaling mode. This is a strong negotiating position for a candidate: they have money, they're hiring fast, and a Senior Platform Engineer is a key hire.

**Role**: Senior Platform Engineer, remote-eligible (Portugal/Europe timezone). Berlin-based company.

**Switzerland market rate for Senior Platform/DevOps Engineer (7+ years):**
- CHF 145,000-185,000 base (Geneva/Zurich)
- Total comp: CHF 160,000-210,000 (base + bonus + equity)
- Source: Glassdoor CH, levels.fyi Europe, local recruiter contacts

**Germany/Berlin market rate for same level:**
- EUR 90,000-120,000 base
- With remote premium for Switzerland-based: aim for EUR 105,000-130,000

**Target anchor**: CHF 165,000 / EUR 130,000 base equivalent. This is defensible given 17 years of experience, founding engineer background, and acquisition validation.

---

## Opening Anchor Script

When they ask about salary expectations, respond:

> "Based on the scope of this role and my experience, I'm targeting CHF 160,000-170,000 base, or the EUR equivalent adjusted for the Swiss cost of living. That reflects both the seniority level and the fact that I'd be working from Geneva. I'm also interested in discussing equity, given the stage of the company."

**Why this works**: It anchors high with a defensible range. The CHF framing forces them to acknowledge the Switzerland cost-of-living premium rather than paying you a Berlin rate.

---

## If They Come In Low (EUR 90,000-100,000 range)

> "I appreciate the offer. The role is compelling, but I need to be honest: at EUR 95,000, the effective purchasing power from Geneva is closer to what EUR 70,000 would be in Berlin once you factor in Swiss cost of living and taxes. My minimum for this role is EUR 115,000, and ideally we'd be at EUR 120,000+. Is there flexibility to get there, or should we look at increasing the equity component to bridge the gap?"

**Notes:**
- Don't apologize for the number.
- Frame it as geography math, not greed.
- Always leave a path open (equity as a bridge) so they have a way to say yes.

---

## Equity Script (Series B Startup)

bunch is post-Series B. At this stage, standard equity grants for senior ICs are typically 0.05%-0.20% of the company on a 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff.

> "I'd like to understand the equity component. At Series B stage with the growth trajectory you've had, I'd expect something in the 0.10%-0.20% range for a senior IC role. Can you walk me through the current option pool, the most recent 409A valuation (or fair market value), and the vesting structure?"

**Questions to ask before accepting any equity offer:**
- What is the current strike price (exercise price) and when was it set?
- Is this ISOs or NSOs? (important for tax treatment)
- What's the post-termination exercise window? (Standard 90 days is punishing; negotiate 2-5 years if you can)
- Does the company have a secondary liquidity program?
- What are the anti-dilution provisions in the cap table for employees vs. investors?

---

## Geographic Premium Pushback

If they push back on paying Switzerland rates for a remote role:

> "I understand the framing. A few things to consider: my network and track record are Swiss and European, which is relevant for a company growing in European private markets. The Alaya acquisition happened in the Geneva ecosystem. More practically, I'd need to structure the role in a way that works legally and financially from Switzerland, which has some setup costs and complexity. I'm willing to work through that together, but the base needs to reflect where I'm actually located."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage Template

If you have or receive another offer (real or expected), use this framing:

> "I want to be upfront with you: I'm in conversations with [company type: another Series B FinTech / a platform team at a larger tech company] and expect to have an offer in the next 10-14 days. I'd rather join bunch, because the problem is more interesting to me. But I need the numbers to be competitive. If we can get to CHF 160,000 / EUR 125,000+, I don't need to see what the other offer looks like."

**Notes:**
- Never fabricate a specific company name unless you actually have the offer.
- The "I'd rather join you" framing is honest and creates goodwill while still applying pressure.
- Give them a real deadline (your expected offer timeline) so they have urgency.

---

## Non-Salary Items to Negotiate

If base salary is stuck, shift to these:

- **Signing bonus**: EUR 10,000-20,000 is reasonable at Series B
- **Remote equipment budget**: CHF 2,500-5,000 one-time
- **Learning & development budget**: CHF 3,000-5,000/year
- **Conference budget**: 1-2 conferences/year covered
- **Accelerated vesting cliff**: 6 months instead of 12 if you're a strong negotiator
- **Review clause**: guaranteed comp review at 6 months instead of 12

---

## Final Decision Framework

Accept if:
- Base >= CHF 150,000 / EUR 118,000
- Equity >= 0.08% on standard 4-year vest
- Role scope matches "senior IC with architectural influence" not just operational

Walk away if:
- Base < CHF 130,000 / EUR 100,000 with no equity bridge
- Equity offer comes with a 90-day post-termination exercise window and no room to negotiate
- The role turns out to be purely operational DevOps with no architectural ownership
