# Negotiation Script: Jua.ai, Senior AI Engineer (Zurich)

## Market anchor

Jua is a Zurich-based, physics-model startup, founded in 2022, with roughly $30M raised across a $16M seed (468 Capital, Green Generation Fund, Promus Ventures, Kadmos Capital, and others) and an $11M Series A closed in June 2025. That funding profile puts it solidly in "early-stage but not scrappy" territory, past the point where cash is the only lever but well before the compensation discipline of a Series C or later company. Generic Zurich "AI engineer" numbers on job boards cluster around CHF 120,000-145,000, but those figures mostly describe individual contributors implementing against a spec, not someone expected to bring platform and distributed-systems judgment to a team that's about to hit the wall between "a few GPUs and a clever training loop" and real infrastructure. With 17-plus years of experience and a founding-engineer track record that ended in an acquisition, anchor at the senior end of what a Series A company in Zurich can realistically pay:

- **Target range:** CHF 145,000-170,000 base
- **Floor (walk-away consideration point):** CHF 135,000 base
- **Stretch anchor (opening ask):** CHF 175,000 base

At this funding stage, equity should carry more real weight than at a later-stage company like Nexthink or Benevity: push for total comp to include a meaningful option grant rather than treating equity as a rounding error on top of salary.

## Opening anchor

If asked for a number first, tie it to scope rather than throwing out a bare figure:

"Given that this role is going to need platform judgment as much as modeling work once you're training at the scale EPT-2.0 implies, and given Zurich rates for someone at my experience level, I'm thinking CHF 170,000-175,000 base plus a meaningful equity grant. That reflects 17-plus years of experience and a founding-engineer background that included a unicorn acquisition, not a generic senior IC hire."

If pushed for one number, say CHF 170,000. Leave room to move down from there rather than negotiating up from a number that undersells the scope.

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

If the offer lands below CHF 140,000 base:

"I appreciate the offer, but it reads more like a mid-level number than what I'd expect for someone bringing platform and infrastructure experience at this stage of the company. Comparable senior roles in Zurich, especially ones touching distributed training and large-scale data systems, are landing closer to CHF 155,000-170,000 base. Is there room to move, either on base or through the option grant?"

If base is genuinely capped by a company-wide band:

"If base is fixed, I'd want to look at the option grant size or a sign-on bonus to close the gap. Something in the CHF 8,000-12,000 range as a sign-on would make the numbers work for me this year while the base catches up over time."

## Equity notes

This is a Swiss AG at Series A, so equity is very likely stock options rather than RSUs, and the terms matter more here than they would at a later-stage company:

- Ask for the strike price and the valuation it's based on. A $30M total raise across seed and Series A gives a rough anchor, but ask directly rather than assuming.
- Confirm the vesting schedule, four years with a one-year cliff is standard, and ask whether there's any acceleration on a future acquisition, which is a realistic scenario given the founders' stated ambitions.
- Ask about the option pool size and whether it's been refreshed since the Series A, a stale pool can mean your grant is worth less than the headline percentage suggests.
- Since this is real Series A risk, not later-stage risk, don't let a large-sounding option grant talk you down on base. Base is what pays rent if the company doesn't get to a Series B.

## Geographic and relocation notes

The role is based in Zurich, which is a real move from Geneva, not a short train ride like some Swiss postings. Address this directly rather than letting it slide:

"I'm currently in Geneva, so this would mean either relocating to Zurich or working out a hybrid arrangement with regular time on-site. If relocation is expected, I'd want to talk about a relocation allowance and whether there's flexibility on the ramp-up timeline while I sort out housing."

Zurich cost of living is on par with or higher than Geneva, so there's no case for a discount off the numbers above just because Zurich has a reputation for lower average software salaries than Zug-adjacent finance roles; AI talent at this level commands its own market.

## Competing offer template

Only use if there's an actual second process running:

"I've got another process moving in parallel at a similar level, so I'm working against a rough timeline. I'd rather prioritize this one if we can get aligned on scope and comp in the next couple of weeks."

## Practical notes

- Negotiate base, sign-on, and options as one package rather than item by item, it's easier to trade across categories that way.
- Ask early whether the role is scoped as a pure ML/training position or genuinely spans into platform and infrastructure work, since that answer should shift where in the range you push.
- Get the strike price, vesting terms, and any relocation support confirmed in writing before signing anything.
- Given the "move fast, break things" culture they describe publicly, expect a fast process; have your range ready before the first call rather than after.
