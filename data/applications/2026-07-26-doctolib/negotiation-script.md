# Negotiation Script: Doctolib, Senior Staff Engineer (x/f/m)

Context: role is based in Berlin, remote-friendly with monthly in-person time. Magino is Geneva-based. This is a cross-border negotiation, so the anchor needs to account for both the Berlin band Doctolib is likely budgeting against and the Swiss cost-of-labor comparison, since Doctolib is a well-funded French scale-up (Series F, General Atlantic/Bpifrance-backed) that can flex on a strong candidate even outside its default band.

## Market anchors

Berlin Staff/Senior Staff engineer base pay at a well-funded scale-up like Doctolib typically lands in the EUR 95,000 to 120,000 range, plus stock options. Geneva-market equivalent for the same seniority (Staff to Principal, platform/architecture scope) runs CHF 150,000 to 185,000 base, which converts to roughly EUR 155,000 to 190,000 at current rates. That gap is the whole negotiation. Doctolib's German entity will anchor to Berlin norms by default unless given a reason not to.

The reason is remote seniority plus scope: this isn't a standard IC slot, it's a role explicitly asked to modularize a core monolith and set API strategy across two product lines. That's a Staff+ mandate, not a mid-Staff one, and the comp conversation should open there.

## Opening anchor

"Given the scope here, modularizing the core booking system and setting API strategy across BMS and EHR, I'd expect this to land as a Staff+ mandate. For a remote setup, I'm looking at EUR 125,000 to 135,000 base, plus a meaningful equity grant given Doctolib's stage. I know that's above a typical Berlin band; I'd rather be upfront about the number than waste time on a mismatch."

## If they come in low (Berlin-anchored offer)

"I hear that the internal band is calibrated to Berlin. Two things make me want to push on that: first, the role as described, modularization plus API-first strategy across two products, sits closer to Staff+ scope than a standard Staff IC role. Second, I'm not asking for Berlin cost-of-living adjustments, I'm asking for the market rate of the work itself, which doesn't change based on where I'm sitting when I do it. Is there room to move the base toward 128, or make up the gap through equity and a faster review cycle, say a compensation check-in at month nine instead of the standard annual one?"

## Geographic discount pushback

If Doctolib tries a location-based discount for being outside Berlin: "I'd push back gently on that. Geneva isn't a low-cost hire, it's roughly on par with or above Berlin for senior technical talent, and remote from here still means monthly travel on my side, which I'm already factoring in. If the concern is time zone or in-person cadence, that's the same for CET either way. I'd rather solve for output and availability than for the city on my passport stamp."

## Equity negotiation

Doctolib is late-stage (post Series F, unicorn valuation), so options will likely be modest in percentage but higher in strike-adjusted value than an early-stage grant. Ask for:
- The current 409A or last-round valuation basis for the option strike price, in writing
- Standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff; push back if they propose anything longer than a 1-year cliff
- Refresh grant eligibility timeline (many late-stage companies only refresh at year 3; ask to confirm this explicitly rather than assume)
- Whether there's an early-exercise provision, since that matters more at this valuation stage than the headline percentage

Don't over-index on the equity number itself at this stage. It's real money, but base and the review cycle matter more given how diluted a single-digit-basis-point grant already is this late.

## Competing offer template

Use only if there is an actual competing process running in parallel; don't fabricate one.

"I want to be transparent that I'm also in a later-stage conversation with another team, and the numbers there are close to what I opened with. I'd genuinely rather join Doctolib given the BMS/EHR work, so if there's flexibility to close that gap, I'd rather spend the next round finalizing details than comparing offers."

## Close

Don't accept verbally on the first call. Ask for the written offer, take 48 hours, and confirm the base, equity grant size and strike basis, review cycle timing, and remote/travel expectations are all in the letter before responding.
