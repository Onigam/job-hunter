# Negotiation Script: WhyBrilliant, Founding Engineer

## Context before talking numbers

WhyBrilliant is a Berlin AI recruiting startup founded in 2026 by Patrick Böert and Aleksander Heimrath. It raised a €1M pre-seed led by Merantix Capital in June 2026 and had 1,500+ tech professionals signed up before public launch. The posting lists **€100k-150k base**, hybrid in Berlin, with no equity figure.

Three things shape this negotiation:

1. **Cash is tight.** €1M doesn't go far. A €150k founding engineer salary plus German employer social charges (roughly 20% on top, less above the contribution ceilings) is a big share of the round. Expect them to push toward the middle of the band and offer equity instead. That's fine, but only if the equity is real and sized for a first engineer.
2. **Equity matters more than base here.** At pre-seed the founding engineer grant is the main source of upside. Get the percentage, the instrument (VSOP or real shares) and the fully diluted share count in writing.
3. **Location isn't settled.** The role is hybrid in Berlin and I'm in Geneva. The outcome (relocate, commute, or remote with on-site weeks) changes which market the numbers should be compared to. Settle it before arguing about base.

Questions for the first call:
- "How much of the week do you need in Berlin, and would you consider a Geneva-based setup with regular on-site weeks?"
- "What's the equity range for this role, as a percentage of fully diluted shares?"
- "Is it a VSOP or real shares or options, and what happens to vested units if I leave or the company is sold?"
- "What's the runway at the proposed salary, and when are you planning to raise the seed?"
- "Would I be the first engineering hire, or is someone already on the codebase?"

## Market reference (estimates, verify before the call)

These are rough 2025-2026 ranges from general market knowledge. Check levels.fyi, Glassdoor, the Swissdevjobs salary report, Honeypot/Landing.jobs Berlin reports and anyone in the Merantix portfolio before quoting any of them.

| Reference | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Founding engineer, Berlin pre-seed/seed | €90k-140k + 0.5-2% |
| Staff Engineer, Berlin scale-up | €110k-150k |
| Senior Staff / Staff, Geneva or Zürich scale-up | CHF 180k-230k |
| Senior Engineer, Geneva startup | CHF 140k-170k |
| Founding engineer, Swiss seed startup | CHF 150k-190k + 0.5-2% |

A €1M-funded Berlin company won't match Swiss base salaries, and pretending otherwise ends the conversation. The Swiss numbers are there to justify the top of *their* band and a larger equity grant, not to get above €150k.

## My numbers

- **Opening anchor:** €150k base (top of their band) plus 1.5-2% fully diluted, four-year vesting, one-year cliff.
- **Target:** €135k-145k base plus at least ~1.25%.
- **Trade-off lever:** each €10k below €140k should buy roughly 0.25% more equity. Write down your own exchange rate before the call so you don't make it up on the spot.
- **Walk-away:** below ~€125k with under 1%, or any structure where vested VSOP units are forfeited when I leave.
- **If they employ me in Geneva (EOR or Swiss entity):** Swiss social charges and LPP are higher, so the company's cost goes up. Ask for base in CHF equivalent to at least €140k, and accept that they may insist on Berlin payroll.

## Opening anchor script

> "Before we talk numbers, I'd like to understand the equity side properly, because at this stage it's the bigger part of the deal for me. Assuming it's a meaningful founding grant, I'm looking at the top of your posted band, €150k, with something in the 1.5 to 2% range. That's based on owning the whole stack from day one: front end, Python services, LLM pipeline, security for candidate data, and hiring the next engineers. It's also well below what the same scope pays in Switzerland, so I'm already meeting you partway."

Then stop talking. Let them respond.

## If they come in low (for example €110k and 0.5%)

> "Thanks, I appreciate the numbers being on the table. That's below where I can make it work, mostly on equity. 0.5% reads like an early employee grant rather than a founding one, and this role carries founding engineer scope. I can be flexible on base if the equity reflects that. Could we look at €135k with 1.5%, or €125k with closer to 2%?"

If they won't move on either:

> "I understand the round is small. What could change it: a salary step-up written into the contract when the seed closes, or a larger grant with the same vesting? I'd rather find a structure that fits your runway than walk away over it."

## Equity: what to check

- **Instrument.** Most German startups use a VSOP (virtual stock options). These are a contractual cash claim, usually paid only at an exit, not real shares. Ask whether real shares or options are possible; since 2024 the updated §19a EStG makes real employee equity easier for German startups. Verify the current rules with an advisor.
- **Percentage on a fully diluted basis**, including the existing pool and any SAFEs or convertibles from the pre-seed.
- **Vesting:** four years with a one-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Good leaver and bad leaver clauses:** vested units must stay with me if I leave as a good leaver. Many VSOPs forfeit everything at departure. That's the walk-away line.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination). Single-trigger is rare at this stage, so don't spend negotiating capital on it.
- **Anti-dilution:** founders and employees don't normally get it, so don't ask. Ask instead for a top-up grant review at the seed round.
- **Taxes if I stay in Geneva:** Swiss taxation of options and VSOP payouts differs from Germany's, and cross-border cases get messy. Get a Swiss tax advisor's view before signing.

## Geographic pushback

If they suggest a lower number because I'd be remote or cross-border:

> "I get the logic, but it cuts the other way here. Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and at a Swiss startup this scope would pay noticeably more than your band. I'm not asking you to match that. I'm asking for the top of what you've published, and I'll be in Berlin whenever the work needs it."

If they want full relocation, account for Berlin's lower cost of living and German income tax together. Run the net numbers for both scenarios before the call.

## Competing offer template

Use only with a real process in play. Never invent an offer.

> "To be transparent, I'm in late-stage conversations with another company for a Staff-level platform role. The cash is higher there, but I'm more interested in what you're building and in owning it from the start. If we can close the gap on equity, I'd rather make this work."

## After the verbal offer

- Ask for everything in writing: base, equity %, instrument, vesting, leaver terms, location arrangement and start date.
- Take at least 48 hours, even if it's a yes.
- Ask to see the VSOP or option plan document itself, not just a summary in the offer letter.
