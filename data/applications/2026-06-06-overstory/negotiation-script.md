# Negotiation Script — Director of Platform Engineering at Overstory

---

## Market Context

Overstory is a ~100-person VC-backed climate-tech company (likely Series B/C), headquartered in the Netherlands, remote-first with European and East Coast North America time zones.

**Director of Platform Engineering benchmarks — European remote (2025-2026):**
- Director-level platform/infrastructure at ~100-person VC-backed startup: EUR 145,000-180,000 base
- Switzerland local equivalent (Geneva cost-of-living premium): CHF 175,000-220,000
- At this stage and scope (managing Platform, MLOps, and SRE), the upper end is defensible
- Performance bonus at Director level: 10-20% of base is reasonable to negotiate

**US Director of Platform Engineering comparable:**
- $180,000-240,000 base at Series B/C
- If they anchor to a US-adjacent rate for a European remote role, that's worth noting

**Equity benchmarks for Director level at this stage:**
- Typical range: 0.05-0.2% fully diluted, depending on company stage and prior grants
- At 100 people with meaningful VC backing, 0.1-0.15% is a reasonable ask
- Standard vesting: 4-year schedule with 1-year cliff
- Watch for: whether it's options or RSUs, the strike price relative to the last 409A or equivalent EU valuation event, and whether double-trigger acceleration exists

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for a salary expectation, use this frame:

> "For a Director role with ownership across Platform, MLOps, and SRE teams at a company at your stage, I'd expect a base in the EUR 165,000-175,000 range, potentially higher depending on how the equity component is structured. That's consistent with Director-level platform engineering rates I've seen for remote-European positions at VC-backed companies of this size. I'm open to discussing how base and equity balance depending on what's available."

This anchors at the high end of the range and signals you're thinking in terms of total comp, not just base salary.

---

## If They Come In Low

If the first offer is below EUR 140,000 base:

> "I appreciate the offer. The base is below what I'd mapped against the market for this scope — Director-level with three teams reporting in, plus the cross-functional mandate across ML, infra, and reliability. I'm genuinely interested in the role, and I'd like to find a path to something workable. Is there flexibility on the base, or is equity the better lever here?"

This keeps the conversation open without accepting or rejecting outright.

**Counter-offer script:**

> "The market rate I've seen for this profile and stage is EUR 155,000-170,000. I'd be comfortable at EUR 165,000 base. That's a number I could commit to without reservations."

---

## Equity Negotiation

If equity is part of the package:

**Questions to ask before negotiating:**
- What's the current fully diluted share count?
- What was the strike price set in the last valuation event?
- What's the last known post-money valuation?
- Are these options or RSUs?
- Does the agreement include double-trigger acceleration on acquisition?

**If they offer below 0.07% fully diluted:**

> "For a Director managing this scope at this stage, I'd expect something closer to 0.1-0.15% fully diluted. I understand there may be constraints based on prior grants, but I'd want to understand the range available. Equity matters to me less as a lottery ticket and more as a signal that we're aligned on long-term thinking."

**Vesting ask:**
- Standard 4-year with 1-year cliff is fine
- If joining mid-funding cycle, ask about accelerated first-year vesting
- Always ask for double-trigger acceleration: if Overstory is acquired and you're asked to stay, vesting shouldn't restart

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they offer a lower rate because you're in Geneva vs. a Western EU city:

> "I'd push back on the geography discount framing. Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe: housing, taxes, cost of living all run higher than Amsterdam, Berlin, or Paris. A discount versus a San Francisco rate is fair. A discount versus the European market rate doesn't reflect what it actually costs to live here."

If they're aligning to a Netherlands/EU benchmark:

> "The European market rate for this scope is EUR 155,000-170,000. That's what I'm targeting, and it's consistent with what I see posted for comparable roles in the European remote market."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or are approaching one):

> "I want to be transparent: I have another process at a final stage. The timeline is roughly two weeks. I'd genuinely prefer to close with Overstory given the mission and scope, but I need to make a decision before that process concludes. Is there a way to move the offer timeline forward here?"

If no other offer exists, use a deadline frame instead:

> "I'm being selective about what I pursue, and Overstory is at the top of my list right now. That said, I need to make a decision within the next two to three weeks. I'd rather not leave things open past that point on either side."

---

## Benefits to Negotiate Beyond Base

Overstory lists these in the JD — push for specifics:
- **Home office stipend**: push for CHF 2,000-3,000 one-time setup + monthly EUR 100-150 allowance
- **Education budget**: confirm amount, target EUR 3,000+/year
- **Coworking budget**: if remote-only, ask for a monthly coworking credit (Geneva has good spaces)
- **Annual leave**: aim for 25+ days in Europe; Swiss minimum is 20
- **Signing bonus**: if equity vesting creates a gap from unvested current options, request a signing bonus to bridge it
- **Confirm in writing**: the remote arrangement, time zone expectations, and in-person gathering frequency

---

## General Principles

- Never accept or reject on the call. Ask for 48-72 hours to review in writing.
- Get all equity terms in writing before signing anything: grant size, type, vesting schedule, strike price.
- If they push back on equity, ask what the equity refresh policy is at year 2 or year 3.
- The goal isn't the maximum number; it's a package you can commit to fully, without the background noise of feeling undervalued.
