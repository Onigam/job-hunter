# Negotiation Script, Akuity, Staff Engineer

## Market Context

**Role level:** Staff Engineer, platform/infrastructure, architectural guidance across distributed systems
**Location:** Fully remote, EMEA/APAC timezones with US overlap; Magino based in Geneva, Switzerland
**Akuity profile:** Founded 2021 by the original Argo CD creators, enterprise customers including Intuit, BlackRock, Tesla; likely USD-denominated comp given the US-timezone-overlap requirement and founder base

**Swiss market rates for equivalent role (used as the anchor, not the fallback):**
- Staff Engineer, infrastructure/platform, Geneva or Zurich: CHF 170,000 to 210,000 base
- Kubernetes/GitOps-focused infra roles at Series A/B, venture-backed companies: CHF 180,000 to 220,000
- Converted at roughly 1.15 USD/CHF: USD 195,000 to 253,000 equivalent

**US market comparison for a Staff/L6-equivalent infra engineer at a Series A/B devtools company:** USD 180,000 to 230,000 base, plus equity. Akuity will likely benchmark against this US range internally rather than a Swiss one, so the negotiation needs to bridge both explicitly.

**Honest read before anchoring:** the posting asks for 4+ years building scalable web apps in Golang and hands-on Kubernetes controller/operator construction. Magino's production Kubernetes and Docker experience is real, but his core languages are TypeScript, Node.js, Java, and Python, not Go. That gap will come up in technical rounds. The opening ask should lean on what's unambiguous: 17+ years including a founding-engineer exit at $1B+ valuation, distributed systems architecture under pressure, cross-team technical leadership, and direct experience running Kubernetes and Docker in production, rather than trying to out-anchor on the Go/operator line item.

---

## Opening Anchor

**Target:** USD 210,000 base (or CHF 180,000 equivalent if paid in CHF), plus 0.08 to 0.15% equity depending on stage/round

When asked for a number:

> "I've looked at this from both a Swiss cost-of-living angle and a US Series A/B infra benchmark, since the role spans both. That puts Staff-level infra work somewhere around 200 to 230k, in whichever currency makes sense for how you run payroll. Given the founding-engineer background and the production Kubernetes work, I'd anchor around 210k base, plus equity in the 0.1 to 0.15% range for a company at your stage."

Let them name their internal band before conceding ground on currency or geography.

## Counter-Offer Scripts

**If they come in below 190k (or the CHF equivalent):**

> "I want to be direct about the gap. That number sits under both the Geneva cost-of-living baseline and what I'd expect for a Series A/B Staff infra hire in the US market, even accounting for the fact that Go isn't the language I've shipped the most production code in. I'd need to see something closer to 205 to 210k to make this work. Is there room to move base, or to close the gap through equity and a signing bonus?"

**If they anchor to a lower "remote-friendly" or non-US band:**

> "I get wanting one consistent band across a distributed team. My situation is that I'm cost-based in Geneva, one of the more expensive cities globally, not in a lower cost-of-living market, and the timezone overlap you're asking for is closer to a US-hours commitment than a typical remote-anywhere role. I'd rather we calibrate to that than to a flatter global average."

**If they raise the Go/operator experience gap as a reason to come in lower:**

> "That's fair to name directly. My Kubernetes and Docker experience is real and production-tested; Go and building controllers specifically is the piece I'd be ramping on fastest, and I'd rather say that upfront than oversell it. If it's useful, we could structure the first few months around a defined ramp, with a comp or equity adjustment once I'm shipping operator code independently."

**If they're firm on base and won't move it:**

Push on other levers, in this order:
1. Equity increase: an additional 0.03 to 0.05% if base is capped
2. Signing bonus: USD 15,000 to 20,000, to bridge the first year while ramping on Go
3. Annual learning/conference budget for Go and Kubernetes-focused training: USD 3,000 to 4,000/year
4. Home office and equipment stipend, beyond whatever is already standard: USD 2,000 to 3,000/year

> "If base stays fixed, I'd want the equity to reflect that gap, something closer to 0.15 to 0.18%, or a signing bonus that covers the ramp period while I get fluent in Go and the operator codebase."

## Equity Negotiation

**Ask for:** 0.08 to 0.15%, depending on how recently Akuity raised and at what valuation (ask directly, this changes the math significantly)
**Structure:** standard 1-year cliff, 4-year vest, monthly vesting after cliff
**Questions to ask before accepting any equity number:**

- What's the current fully diluted cap table, and what share is option pool versus investor preference stack?
- What's the strike price, and is there a recent 409A (or equivalent) valuation?
- ISOs or NSOs, and what's the post-departure exercise window?
- Do the lead investors hold participating preferred, and how does that affect common shareholder outcomes in a modest exit?
- Is there a refresher policy for early non-founder Staff hires?

**If the equity number comes in low (under 0.06%):**

> "For a Staff role that's meant to give architectural guidance across the platform, not just execute inside one team, I'd expect equity closer to what reflects that scope, in the 0.1 to 0.15% range. That's also part of what offsets the base sitting under Swiss market rate. Can we revisit that number?"

**Refresher expectation:** Ask out loud for an 18-month refresher conversation tied to demonstrated scope and Go/Kubernetes ramp, even if it doesn't go into the written offer.

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If Akuity tries to apply a lower-cost-region adjustment because the role is EMEA/APAC-facing:

> "I understand wanting a consistent framework for a distributed team, but Geneva isn't a lower cost-of-living market, it's consistently one of the five most expensive cities globally, closer to San Francisco or Zurich than to most of the EMEA band this kind of adjustment usually assumes. I'd rather we price this against where I actually live and the US-hours overlap you're asking for, not a regional average that doesn't reflect either."

**Data point to cite if needed:** Swiss ICT salary surveys and platforms like salary.ch put Staff-level infrastructure engineering in Geneva or Zurich at CHF 170-220k; that converts to roughly USD 195-253k, in the same range as a US Series A/B Staff infra hire, not below it.

## Using Competing Offers

If other conversations are genuinely active:

> "I've got other infrastructure-focused conversations moving in parallel, in a similar 200 to 230k range. I'd rather land at Akuity, working close to the original Argo CD team on a problem enterprises like Intuit and Tesla actually depend on is more interesting to me than what the others are building. But the comp needs to be in that neighborhood for it to be the clear choice."

If there's no competing offer in hand, don't invent one. Fall back to market data instead:

> "The market data I'm working from, both Swiss and US Series A/B benchmarks, puts this role in the 200 to 230k range. That's the frame I'm anchoring to regardless of other conversations."

---

## Timeline and Process Notes

Don't put a number on the table before understanding how much of the role is genuinely architectural guidance versus hands-on Go/operator development day to day, since that split changes the honest pitch. If pressed early:

> "I'd like a clearer picture of the split between architectural guidance and hands-on Go work before I put a number down, since that changes how I'd frame my own ramp-up. Can we come back to comp once we're further into the process?"

**Offer deadline:** ask for at least 5 business days to review a written offer, more if equity documentation needs independent review.

> "I want to look at the equity terms properly before deciding. Five to seven business days works on my end, unless there's something time-sensitive I should know about."
