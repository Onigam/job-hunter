# Negotiation Script — A.Team / Senior Independent AI Engineer & Architect

---

## Market Context

**Posted range:** $120-170/hr (USD), biweekly Net-15 payout, you keep 100% of rate.

**Switzerland contractor reality:** A.Team does not skim fees, but operating as an independent contractor in Switzerland carries real overhead:
- AVS/AI/APG contributions (mandatory social security): ~10% of gross
- LAA accident insurance: ~2%
- Voluntary pension contributions (2nd pillar substitution): 10-15% recommended
- Total burden: 22-27% of gross before income tax

At $170/hr and 40 hrs/week, 48 billable weeks/year: $326,400/yr gross. After contractor overhead (~25%): ~$245,000 effective before income tax. After Geneva cantonal + federal tax (~35% marginal): net ~$159,000/yr equivalent.

**The right anchor:** $170/hr. That's the top of their posted range and it's the only number that makes the contractor math work in Geneva.

**Geneva senior staff engineer market (employed):** CHF 150,000-185,000/yr all-in. The contractor premium needs to cover missing pension, healthcare gap, no paid leave, and administrative overhead. $170/hr covers this. $120/hr does not.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for your rate expectation:

> "I'm targeting $170 per hour. That's the top of the range you've listed, and it reflects both the seniority level and the Geneva market. As a contractor here, I'm self-funding pension contributions and insurance that an employer would otherwise cover, so the loaded rate needs to be higher than an equivalent employed salary would suggest. Happy to walk through the math if useful."

Key points: don't apologize for the number. State it once, then let them respond.

---

## If They Come In at $120-$140/hr

Don't accept. Counter with a specific number and a reason:

> "I appreciate the offer, and I'm genuinely interested in what A.Team is building. The rate doesn't work for me at that level. At $120-140/hr, once I account for Swiss contractor overhead and pension contributions, I'm effectively at or below what I'd earn in an employed senior role without the security. I'd need $160 as a floor to make this work. Is there flexibility there?"

If they push back on the Geneva angle:

> "I understand that A.Team sets rates independently of geography. The issue isn't just cost of living — it's that contractors in Switzerland carry structural overhead that employees don't. The $160 isn't a premium; it's a floor."

---

## Mission Type and Rate Calibration

A.Team offers both part-time (10-20 hrs/wk) and full-time engagements. Adjust accordingly:

| Scenario | Rate | Weekly commitment | Gross/year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-time mission | $170/hr | 40 hrs | ~$326K |
| Part-time alongside Benevity | $170/hr | 20 hrs | ~$163K |

For a part-time mission alongside a day job, $170/hr is even more defensible. You're not replacing a salary; you're adding capacity at full market rate.

---

## Equity

A.Team's standard model is hourly contract. No equity is standard. However:

- Some A.Team missions with early-stage startups may offer equity on top of rate. If this comes up, treat it as a bonus, not a discount justification.
- Never accept a lower hourly rate in exchange for equity unless the startup is Series B or later with a credible cap table you can read.
- If equity is offered, ask: vesting schedule (standard 4-year with 1-year cliff), option type (ISOs/NSOs), and the last-round valuation. Anything pre-product or seed-stage equity is not worth discounting your rate for.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you're in conversations with other companies (or want to signal optionality):

> "I'm currently in late-stage conversations with two other opportunities, one of which is a founding engineer role at a Series A. I prefer the A.Team model for the mission variety and the peer caliber, but I'll need to make a decision within the next two to three weeks. Is this a timeline that works for the matching process?"

This frames urgency without fabrication. You don't need an actual competing offer; you just need it to be plausible — and for someone with your background, it is.

---

## What Not to Say

- Don't justify the rate by listing your years of experience. They already know your profile. Lead with market math, not biography.
- Don't offer to "negotiate" or signal you have a range. You have a number: $170. Let them move it if they need to.
- Don't accept a lower rate with a promise of "more after the first mission." Rate adjustments at A.Team are mission-by-mission; there is no guaranteed escalation path.
