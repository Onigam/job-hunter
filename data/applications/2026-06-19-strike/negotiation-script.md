# Negotiation Script — Strike / Staff Platform Engineer, AI Systems

---

## Market Context

**Role level:** Staff Platform Engineer, AI Systems (individual contributor, senior-most IC tier)

**Strike's posted range (US-based):** $206,000 – $232,000 base + equity

**Geneva / Switzerland market rates for equivalent seniority (2025-2026):**
- Senior Staff / Principal Engineer, AI/platform focus: CHF 180,000 – 220,000 base
- With Geneva cost-of-living premium: CHF 195,000 – 225,000 is realistic to ask for
- USD/CHF rate is approximately 0.88 (June 2026), so Strike's upper bound ($232K) converts to roughly CHF 204K

**Key positioning:** Strike's US range actually maps well onto the Swiss market at the top end. The argument is that you should be at the top of their range, not subject to a geographic discount, because Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in the world and you bring a narrower skill set that is harder to hire for than generic Staff Engineering.

---

## Opening Anchor

**When to use:** After they've indicated genuine interest, typically when they ask "what are your compensation expectations?"

Script:
> "I've looked at what this role pays in the US market and what comparable seniority commands in the Swiss market, and the numbers are actually converging. I'm targeting CHF 210,000 – 220,000 base, which sits at the top of your posted range when you account for the exchange rate. Given that you're looking for someone who can build secure agent orchestration infrastructure from scratch, not just adopt a vendor tool, I think that's the right anchor."

**Why this works:** You're not asking for more than their range; you're justifying why you should be at the top of it. The specific skill mention (secure agent orchestration) signals you've read the role carefully.

---

## If They Anchor Low (Below CHF 190K / Below $205K Equivalent)

Script:
> "I appreciate the transparency. That's below what I'd need to make the move, and I want to be honest with you rather than waste both our time. I'm working with a number closer to CHF 210,000. Before we close the conversation, is there flexibility there, or is this a hard ceiling?"

**Note:** Ask directly whether it's a hard ceiling. Some companies have rigid bands; others have flexibility they won't volunteer. The question forces a clear answer.

If they confirm the ceiling is firm:
> "Then let's talk about the equity side. If the base is fixed, I'd want to understand what the equity package looks like in more detail, including the current valuation basis for the strike price and whether there's an anti-dilution provision."

---

## Equity Negotiation (Bitcoin Company Specifics)

Strike is a private company with equity in the form of stock options or RSUs. Bitcoin is core to their business model, and there may be a Bitcoin bonus or fee-waiver component (they mention a trading fee waiver in the JD).

**Questions to ask:**
- What is the current 409A valuation / preferred share price?
- What is the standard vesting schedule (4-year with 1-year cliff is typical; push back if it's longer)?
- Is there acceleration on acquisition or change of control?
- What is the total equity pool dilution history?
- Is there a secondary market or liquidity program?

Script if equity is front-and-center in their offer:
> "I want to understand the equity properly before I can evaluate the total package. Can you share the current 409A valuation and the grant size in dollar terms at that valuation? I've seen enough early-stage equity that never liquified to want to understand the realistic upside scenario."

**On the Bitcoin fee waiver:** This has real value if you're going to hold Bitcoin anyway, but don't let them use it to offset a low base. Treat it as a nice-to-have, not a comp component.

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they propose a Geneva-discounted rate below US market:

> "I'd push back on applying a geographic discount here. Geneva is the second-most expensive city in Europe by most measures; I'm not operating from a cost-advantaged location. The cost of living in Geneva is comparable to San Francisco. If anything, the net purchasing power of a Swiss base salary is lower than a US one once you account for the local cost of rent, childcare, and everything else. I'd rather we anchor to the work and the skill level, not where I sit."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in hand, use it. If you don't, don't fabricate one. You can still create urgency:

> "I'm at a late stage with another process that has a decision timeline I need to respect. I want to prioritize Strike if we can get to terms that work, but I need to know whether there's a path to CHF 210K before I let the other process move further."

If you do have a competing offer:
> "I have an offer in hand at [X]. I'm more interested in the Strike role because of [one specific thing: the MCP architecture work, the Bitcoin infrastructure angle, etc.]. If you can match or come within 5% of that number, I'm ready to sign."

---

## Final Package Checklist

Before signing, confirm in writing:
- [ ] Base salary in CHF (or USD with explicit conversion mechanism)
- [ ] Equity grant: number of shares, vesting schedule, cliff, exercise price, expiry on departure
- [ ] Change-of-control acceleration clause
- [ ] Annual review cycle and merit increase policy
- [ ] Remote work terms: is Geneva explicitly covered? Any co-location expectations?
- [ ] Equipment budget and home office stipend (common in Swiss/EU employment)
- [ ] Health insurance: Strike mentions contributions; confirm what "contributions" means (partial vs. full)

---

## Timeline Guidance

Don't accept on the call. Standard language:
> "This looks interesting. I'd like a day or two to review the full written offer before I respond. Can you send it over?"

Acceptable to sign within 2-3 business days of receiving the written offer. Anything faster looks desperate; anything over a week risks them moving to the next candidate.
