# Negotiation Script — Snyk Staff Incubation Engineer (Zürich)

## Market Context

Snyk raised a Series G in 2021 at an $8.5B valuation. As of 2026, they are a mature, well-funded scale-up competing for talent against US tech majors operating in Switzerland. Zürich is the most expensive tech market in Europe: Staff Engineers at comparable companies (Stripe, Google, Two Sigma, local fintech/security scale-ups) command CHF 200,000-270,000 base, with total comp (base + bonus + equity) ranging CHF 260,000-380,000+ depending on equity stage and form.

For an "Incubation" scope at Staff level with AI/ML specialization, apply a premium. The market for engineers who can ship AI systems and build zero-to-one products is tighter than general Staff eng right now.

**Target range:**
- Base: CHF 230,000-260,000
- Bonus: 15-20% target annual
- Equity: RSUs or options (see equity section below)
- Opening anchor: CHF 260,000 base

**Floor (walk-away):** CHF 200,000 base + meaningful equity. Below this, the role doesn't make sense given Geneva commute overhead and market alternatives.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for your number first:

> "Based on the scope of the role and current Zürich market rates for Staff-level engineers with AI systems experience, I'm targeting CHF 260,000 base. I'm flexible on the structure if there's strong equity involved, but that's where I'm anchored."

If they push back immediately:

> "I'm happy to discuss the full package. What's the budgeted range for this position?"

Never reveal your floor first. If they say their range is lower than your anchor, don't panic. Move to counter-offer script.

---

## Counter-Offer Script

**If they come in at CHF 180,000-200,000 (below market):**

> "I appreciate the transparency. That range is meaningfully below what I'm seeing for Staff-level AI/systems roles in Zürich right now, and below my current comp. I'd need to see that bridge either through equity or a clear path to revisit base after six months. What flexibility do you have on either of those dimensions?"

**If they come in at CHF 200,000-220,000:**

> "That's closer. I'm still a bit apart from my target of CHF 260,000. Can we meet at CHF 240,000 base? I'm genuinely interested in the role and willing to make the package work if the equity component is real."

**If they come in at CHF 220,000-240,000:**

> "I appreciate that. I'd like to get to CHF 255,000 base. If the equity is strong, I can be flexible on the base, but I'd want to understand the current strike price and the cap table before I commit to trading base for options."

---

## Geographic / Commute Pushback

Geneva to Zürich is a 3-hour round trip by train. If there's a remote vs. hybrid negotiation:

> "I'm based in Geneva, which is 90 minutes by train from Zürich. I'm absolutely open to being in Zürich regularly, but I'd want to calibrate how often 'hybrid' means in practice. If it's 1-2 days per week, that works. If it's 4-5 days, that changes the calculus significantly, both in time and in travel costs."

If they push for full in-office: use this as a reason to push on base.

> "Given the commute overhead for being in Zürich regularly, I'd need the base to reflect that: commuting from Geneva to Zürich full-time is effectively a significant time and cost commitment on top of the role."

---

## Equity Negotiation

**For private company (likely still private as of 2026):**

Key questions to ask before accepting any equity:

1. "What's the current valuation used for the strike price on options, and when was the last 409A or fair market value assessment?"
2. "What percentage of the company does this grant represent, fully diluted?"
3. "What's the vesting schedule? Is there a one-year cliff?"
4. "Is there acceleration on change of control (single-trigger or double-trigger)?"
5. "What's the post-termination exercise window on options? 90 days is a problem if there's no liquidity event in sight."

**Standard ask if they offer options:**

> "Given the lack of near-term liquidity, I'd like to either see a longer post-termination exercise window (ideally 5-10 years) or a higher base to compensate for the illiquidity. Which is more feasible from your side?"

**If they offer RSUs (suggests they have a clearer IPO path):**

> "RSUs are cleaner. I'd like to understand the vesting schedule and whether there's a liquidity event trigger for the first vest."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or want to create urgency):

> "I'm currently in late-stage conversations with another company. I'd prefer to join Snyk for the incubation scope, but I need to make a decision by [date]. Can we move the process forward or finalize the package this week?"

If pushed on details of the other offer:

> "I'd rather not get into specifics out of respect for the other process, but I can tell you it's at a comparable level and base. I'm sharing this because I want to close with Snyk, not as a pressure tactic."

---

## What to Accept vs. Walk Away From

**Accept if:**
- Base CHF 215,000+ with meaningful equity (>0.1% fully diluted at current valuation stage)
- Or base CHF 240,000+ with standard equity
- Hybrid arrangement (2-3 days Zürich max)
- Clear incubation mandate with real resources behind it (not "you're the incubation team and also doing maintenance")

**Walk away if:**
- Base below CHF 200,000 with no flexibility
- Full in-office requirement in Zürich (impractical from Geneva)
- Equity with <90-day exercise window and no near-term liquidity path
- "Incubation" turns out to mean supporting existing products without a real greenfield mandate
