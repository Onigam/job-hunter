# Negotiation Script — VP Engineering at Insify

---

## Market Context

**Role:** VP Engineering, Amsterdam (in-office)
**Company stage:** Series B, €43M raised (Accel, Munich Re Ventures, Evli Growth Partners, Visionaries Club, Frontline Ventures)
**Funding trajectory:** Founded 2020, Series B in recent round — likely 18-36 months to next milestone (Series C or profitability push)
**Your location:** Geneva, Switzerland — significantly higher cost of living than Amsterdam

**Amsterdam VP Engineering market rates (Series B tech company, 2026):**
- Base salary: €130,000-€165,000
- Total comp with equity: €160,000-€210,000+ depending on grant size
- Equity at Series B VP level: typically 0.3%-0.8% fully diluted, 4-year vest, 1-year cliff

**Geneva equivalent for context:** A comparable role in Geneva would pay CHF 180,000-220,000 base. Amsterdam net purchasing power at €145k is roughly equivalent to Geneva CHF 155-160k — so there's a real lifestyle cost to the move.

---

## Opening Position

**Target package:**
- Base: €160,000
- Equity: 0.5% fully diluted, 4-year vest, 1-year cliff
- Benefits: pension contributions, Alleo allowance, 25 days PTO (as listed)
- Relocation: one-time relocation support (€5,000-10,000 is reasonable to ask for)

**Your anchor justification:**
- 17 years experience, co-founder background, acquired by unicorn
- Current Geneva market comp is CHF 180-200k equivalent
- Amsterdam CoL adjustment means €160k base maintains rough parity
- Series B VP hires typically get 0.4-0.6% at companies this size; 0.5% is reasonable mid-point

---

## Opening Script

When they ask for your number, say:

"Based on comparable VP Engineering roles at Series B companies in the Netherlands and what I currently earn in Geneva, I'd be looking at a base in the range of €155,000-€165,000, plus equity that reflects the VP level at this stage. I know Insify has mentioned meaningful equity; I'd want to understand the package holistically. What does the current comp structure look like for this level?"

This anchors high, signals you've done research, and turns it back to them to reveal their range first.

---

## If They Come In Low (below €135k base)

Don't accept, don't reject. Bridge:

"I appreciate the offer. The base is below where I'd need to land given the Geneva-to-Amsterdam cost comparison. I'm moving from a higher cost-of-living market, and I want to make this work. Is there room to get to €150,000 on base? Or alternatively, if the base is a hard ceiling, I'd want to look at the equity grant and whether it can compensate for the gap."

Key principle: never reject a number outright. Always give them a path to yes.

---

## Equity Negotiation

What to ask about:
- Current cap table: what's the fully diluted share count?
- Last 409A valuation (or most recent preferred price)
- What percentage does the offered grant represent fully diluted?
- Vesting schedule: 4-year with 1-year cliff is standard. Push back if they propose monthly cliff or anything longer than 4 years.
- Acceleration clause: ask for double-trigger acceleration (if acquired AND you're let go within 12 months, unvested shares accelerate). This is especially relevant given Insify could be acquired.

Script for equity conversation:

"You mentioned meaningful equity. I want to understand the grant at the VP level. What's the percentage of fully diluted ownership, and what was the last preferred share price? I want to calibrate what this represents as a value estimate, not just a number of shares."

If they say "we don't disclose that" — it's a yellow flag. Most Series B companies with good governance will share this with VP-level hires. Offer: "Would you share the last 409A valuation range at least?" If still no, you're pricing an unknown. Factor that into your base ask.

---

## Geographic Leverage Script

If they push back on the high base ask because "Amsterdam salaries don't work that way":

"I understand Amsterdam market rates. The challenge is I'm not coming from Amsterdam — I'm coming from Geneva, where the cost of living is meaningfully higher and where the comparable role pays CHF 180-200k. If I land in Amsterdam at €130k, I've taken a significant net pay cut for the same responsibilities. I'm not trying to impose Geneva rates on Amsterdam; I'm trying to find a number that makes the move viable for both sides. €155k seems like a reasonable midpoint."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer or are in process elsewhere, deploy this carefully:

"I want to be transparent with you: I'm in late stages with one other company and expecting an offer this week. I'm more interested in Insify specifically because of [specific reason tied to what you've said in the interview]. But I'd need the packages to be comparable. If you can get to €155k base plus the equity grant we discussed, I'm ready to move forward."

Only use this if it's true or nearly true. If you don't have a competing offer, don't fabricate one — instead use the Geneva market rate as your anchor, which is real.

---

## Relocation Ask

"One additional item: I'd be relocating from Geneva to Amsterdam for this role. Is there a relocation package or support Insify provides? Even a one-time allowance of €5,000-8,000 would help with the practical costs of the move."

This is a reasonable ask for an in-office VP hire from abroad. Most Series B companies will at least consider it.

---

## Acceptable Floor

If you're strongly committed to the role and they genuinely can't move on base:

- **Minimum acceptable:** €145,000 base + 0.45% equity (4-year, 1-year cliff) + €5,000 relocation
- At this level, make sure the equity grant is clearly documented and you understand the last preferred price

Below €140k base is not viable given the relocation context from Geneva unless the equity is exceptional (0.7%+) and you've seen credible valuation data.
