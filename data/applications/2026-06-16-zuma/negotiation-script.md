# Negotiation Script — Zuma Staff Engineer (Agentic AI & Data)

---

## Market Context

**Role level:** Staff Engineer (Agentic AI & Data)
**Company stage:** Series A, ~$17M raised (a16z, YC, King River, Range Ventures)
**Location:** San Francisco / Remote
**Posted range:** $180,000 – $220,000 USD + stock options

**SF market benchmarks for Staff Engineer with AI specialization (2026):**
- Early-stage Series A (10-50 employees): $190K–$230K base
- Series B+ or late-stage growth: $220K–$270K base
- FAANG/large tech: $260K–$320K base + significant equity

At ~$17M raised with Blackstone as an anchor client, Zuma is at the high end of Series A. The $180K–$220K range is honest for this stage, but the lower end of that band undervalues the AI specialization they need.

**Geneva cost-of-living note:** Geneva is consistently ranked in the top 3 most expensive cities in the world (alongside Zurich and Singapore). If they try to apply a geographic discount for a remote hire, push back firmly: your cost of living is equivalent to or higher than San Francisco.

---

## Compensation Targets

| Scenario | Base (USD) | Equity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strong open | $235,000 | 0.4–0.6% | Justify with AI market premium |
| Acceptable | $220,000 | 0.3–0.4% | Top of their stated range |
| Walk-away threshold | < $190,000 | < 0.2% | Not worth the location/timezone complexity |

**Equity context for Series A:**
- 0.3–0.6% is reasonable for a Staff Engineer hire at this stage
- Ask for a 4-year vest with 1-year cliff, standard for US startups
- Ask about double-trigger acceleration on acquisition (relevant given Alaya's acquisition history -- you know exactly what this clause is worth)
- Ask current valuation and last 409A valuation to understand what the options are actually priced at

---

## Opening Anchor Script

When they ask for your number first:

> "Based on what I know about the role, the AI specialization required, and the San Francisco market for Staff Engineers at this stage, I'm thinking in the range of $230,000 to $240,000 base. I'm also very interested in meaningful equity given where Zuma is in its trajectory. What does your comp structure look like at this level?"

This anchors above their stated ceiling, which is correct, and immediately redirects to equity before they have time to react to the number alone.

---

## If They Come In at the Low End (around $180K–$190K)

> "I appreciate the transparency. That's below where I'd need to be. Given the AI agent expertise they're asking for -- production deployment, multi-agent systems, LLM observability -- this isn't a generalist engineering role, and the market for people who've actually shipped this in production reflects that. I was thinking $220,000 as a floor, with the equity making up the difference for the early-stage risk. Is there flexibility on either or both?"

Don't apologize for the number. Don't explain the Geneva cost-of-living unprompted unless they bring up geography first.

---

## If They Push a Geographic Discount

> "I want to flag something on that. Geneva has a higher cost of living than San Francisco on most indices -- housing, tax burden, general expenses. I don't think a geographic discount applies here, and honestly, you're accessing a candidate pool with SF-level skills and experience. The comp should reflect the role, not the timezone."

If they persist:
> "I'm open to the conversation, but a material discount would change the math on the overall package enough that I'd want to see it offset in equity. Can we look at both together?"

---

## Equity Deep-Dive Questions

Ask these before accepting any offer:

1. What is the current fully diluted share count?
2. What was the 409A valuation from the most recent assessment?
3. Is the vesting 4-year with 1-year cliff? Any acceleration provisions?
4. Double-trigger acceleration on acquisition? (Given Alaya's acquisition, you can say: "I've been through an acquisition and I know how this clause plays out -- I'd like to make sure it's in the agreement.")
5. What is the exercise window if I leave? 90 days is standard but 5-10 years is increasingly common at YC-backed companies.
6. Have there been secondary sales or employee liquidity events?

---

## Competing Offer Leverage (if applicable)

If you have another offer or are in late-stage conversations elsewhere:

> "I want to be upfront: I'm at offer stage with another company. The comp is comparable but I'm more interested in what Zuma is building. If you can match or get close to $220,000 base, I'm ready to move forward with Zuma. I don't want to drag this out."

If you don't have a competing offer, don't fabricate one. Instead:

> "I'm in active conversations with a few companies at similar stages. I'd rather close with Zuma given the problem you're working on, but I do need the number to make sense."

---

## Closing Move

Once at a number you'd accept:

> "That works for me. Let's move forward. I'd like to see the written offer before I formally decline the other conversations, just as a matter of process."

This is standard and professional. It buys time to review the equity terms carefully before burning other options.

---

## Non-Salary Items to Negotiate (if base is non-negotiable)

- Home office stipend (common for remote-first US companies): $2,000–$5,000 setup + monthly internet/phone
- Annual L&D budget: $2,000–$5,000 for conferences, courses, tools
- Equipment: MacBook Pro M4 or equivalent, company-provided
- Signing bonus to offset any unvested equity you're leaving: calculate exactly what you'd walk away from
- Flexible PTO (vs. accrual)
- Travel coverage for any in-person on-sites
