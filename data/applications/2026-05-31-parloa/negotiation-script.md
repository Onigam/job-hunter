# Negotiation Script — Parloa / Staff Principal Software Engineer

---

## Market Context

Parloa is headquartered in Berlin with offices in Munich, hiring for remote (Europe). The role is Staff/Principal Software Engineer, which in the German market sits at EUR 120,000-160,000 base at companies of this stage (Series B, well-funded). However, Magino is based in Geneva, one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and the Switzerland-Germany compensation gap is significant.

**Switzerland benchmarks for equivalent seniority (Staff+ / Principal):**
- Geneva / Zurich market: CHF 160,000-210,000 base
- Remote from Switzerland to a European employer: CHF 140,000-180,000 (haircut is reasonable, full Geneva parity is not expected)
- All-in with equity and bonus: CHF 180,000-230,000 target range

**Parloa-specific context:**
- Raised $120M Series B (2024), backed by Altimeter Capital
- Enterprise focus means deal sizes are large; Staff engineers are direct revenue infrastructure
- Competing for talent with Zurich-based hyperscalers (Google, Amazon) and Swiss unicorns

---

## Opening Anchor

When the recruiter asks for compensation expectations, respond with:

> "For a Staff or Principal role at this scope, I'm looking at a base in the CHF 175,000-185,000 range, reflecting my location in Geneva and the seniority of the position. I'm open to discussing how equity factors into the overall package."

**Why this works:** It opens high enough to give room to land at CHF 155,000-165,000 if needed. It signals you've done market research. It invites a conversation rather than a take-it-or-leave-it stance.

---

## If They Push Back on Location Premium

Parloa may try to anchor to German market rates. Counter with:

> "I understand your salary bands may be calibrated to Berlin or Munich. My cost of living in Geneva is materially higher than either city — housing alone runs ~2x. I'm not asking for Geneva-company rates, but I do need compensation that reflects the actual cost of operating here. That's what the range I mentioned accounts for."

If they ask you to justify further:

> "Senior Staff engineers in Zurich are earning CHF 180,000-220,000 at Google or Snowflake. I'm not expecting that, but it gives you a sense of the baseline I'm being compared against when I evaluate offers."

---

## If the Initial Offer Comes In Low (Below CHF 140,000)

Do not accept immediately. Respond on the call with:

> "Thanks for getting back to me with numbers. I want to be straightforward: that's below where I need to land given my location and the scope you described for this role. Can we talk about what flexibility exists, either on base, equity, or both? I'd rather find a structure that works than let this stall on a gap we can probably close."

Then follow up in writing within 24 hours with a clear counter:

> "Following up on our conversation. I'd like to propose CHF [X]. Given the cross-team architecture scope and the 15+ years of directly relevant experience, I think that reflects the value on both sides. I'm also genuinely interested in the equity story at this stage of Parloa's growth — happy to talk through what the package looks like in total."

---

## Equity Negotiation

For a company at Parloa's stage (Series B, $120M raised), equity is meaningful but illiquid.

**Target:** 0.10%-0.25% equity (options or RSUs) with standard 4-year vest and 1-year cliff.

**Ask about:**
- Current valuation and last 409A (for options)
- Strike price vs. current preferred share price (the spread matters)
- Acceleration clause on change of control (single or double trigger)
- Cliff exceptions if the company is acquired in year 1

**Script if they offer stock options:**

> "I'd like to understand the strike price relative to the last 409A and where the preferred is sitting. What does the last round imply for the current per-share value, and how many shares would be in the grant? That helps me put a real number on the equity."

**If they push back on equity size:**

> "I'm not anchoring to a percentage. I'm looking at the total economic value of the grant relative to the base. If the base comes in at the higher end of the range, I'm flexible on equity. If the base is lower, the equity needs to pick up more of the gap."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you receive another offer (or want to create urgency without fabricating one):

> "I want to be transparent with you: I'm in late-stage conversations with two other companies, both at a similar seniority level. Parloa is my first choice for the product problem and the tech stack, but I need to make a decision in the next week or so. Is there a way to accelerate the process on your end?"

This is honest framing if you're actively interviewing elsewhere. Do not fabricate an offer you don't have.

---

## What to Never Do

- Do not accept on the call. Always say: "Let me take 24 hours to look at this properly."
- Do not give a hard yes/no on the spot if the number surprises you in either direction.
- Do not apologize for asking for more. "I want to make sure we're aligned" is the frame, not "I'm sorry, but..."
- Do not share your current salary unless you have to. In Switzerland, it's standard to decline: "I'd rather focus on what the role is worth."

---

## Final Checklist Before Signing

- [ ] Base salary confirmed in CHF (not EUR with a conversion promise)
- [ ] Bonus structure (if any) documented in writing
- [ ] Equity grant: shares, type (options vs. RSUs), vest schedule, cliff, strike price
- [ ] Acceleration on change of control
- [ ] Start date and notice period accommodation confirmed
- [ ] Equipment and home-office allowance (Geneva cost of living)
- [ ] Confirm remote status is permanent, not "for now"
