# Negotiation Script: Abusix, Head of Engineering in Network Security (Remote EU/UK)

## Context before talking numbers

The listing shows a band of €0 to €120k, location-based, plus a "meaningful ESOP" and location-based benefits. Abusix is a 15-year-old, fully remote company registered in the US with its engineering roots in Karlsruhe. This is a Head of Engineering seat on the leadership team, reporting to the CEO, with 10 engineers including SRE.

Two problems to face early, before anyone invests time:

1. **The band is well under Geneva rates.** €120k is about CHF 112k-114k. A Head of Engineering in Geneva or Zurich typically earns CHF 200k+ base. Even at the top of the band this is a large pay cut against Geneva Staff-level roles. Decide before the first call whether the role is worth it, and what the equity would have to look like to close the gap.
2. **Switzerland isn't in the EU.** The listing says "Remote in EU or UK." Ask in the first conversation whether they can hire in Switzerland (Swiss entity, employer-of-record, or contractor agreement). If they can't, nothing else in this document matters.

## Market reference (estimates, check before the call)

Rough ranges from general market knowledge for 2025-2026. Recheck on levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Kununu, and Swiss salary surveys (e.g. swissdevjobs) before any call. Exchange rate assumed around 1 EUR ≈ 0.93-0.95 CHF; check it on the day.

| Reference | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Head of Engineering, German scale-up, team of ~10 | €110k-150k |
| Head of Engineering / VP Eng, Swiss scale-up (Geneva/Zurich) | CHF 200k-260k |

| Senior freelance / fractional engineering lead, Switzerland | CHF 1,100-1,500 per day |

**My numbers**

- **Opening anchor:** €140k base (~CHF 132k) plus a sizeable ESOP grant. That's above their band on purpose; "location-based" cuts both ways, and Geneva is one of the most expensive places in Europe.
- **Target:** €125k-135k base plus equity with real numbers behind it.
- **Walk-away:** the top of their band (€120k) with vague equity. At that level, accept only if the ESOP is clearly substantial and the role is exactly what I want, and go in knowing it's a lifestyle choice, not a financial one.

## Opening anchor script

> "Before I give a number, can you tell me how you'd employ someone in Switzerland, and how the ESOP works: percentage, strike price, last valuation? [Listen.] I saw the band tops out at €120k. For a Head of Engineering role on the leadership team, based in Geneva, I'm looking at around €140k base. I know that's above the listing, and I'm saying it because 'location-based' should work in both directions. Geneva salaries for this level start well above that. I'm flexible on the mix of cash and equity if the equity is real."

Say the number, then stop talking.

## If they come in low (for example €105k-115k)

> "Thanks, I understand that's where your band sits. The difficulty is that the roles I'd be comparing this to here pay a lot more. The top of your band, €120k, is the minimum I could make work, and only with an equity grant that makes up part of the gap. Can we talk about the ESOP size, and a review after six months tied to goals we agree on now?"

If base won't move, trade in this order:
1. A larger ESOP grant, with the numbers written down (see below).
2. A signing bonus to cover part of the first-year gap.
3. A written six-month review with a pre-agreed raise if the goals are hit.
4. Swiss-level benefits: their "location-based benefits" should include things like a pension (2nd pillar) contribution at the Swiss norm, not the German one.
5. A bigger L&D budget and a co-working budget in Geneva.

## Pushing back on the geographic discount

> "I get why a company hiring across Europe uses location-based pay. That's exactly why Geneva should land higher, not lower. Cost of living here is among the highest in Europe. I'm not asking for a Zurich banking salary; I'm asking you to pay for the location you've already said you'll pay for."

If they propose a contractor setup: a Swiss self-employed contractor pays all social charges (AHV/AVS, pension, accident insurance) and gets no paid leave. The day rate has to cover that, which usually means 25-35% above the salary-equivalent. Run the numbers both ways before accepting either.

## Equity (ESOP)

"Meaningful ESOP" is the one lever here that can close the gap, so get specifics:

- **What instrument?** Real options, virtual shares (VSOP, common in German companies), or something else? A VSOP only pays on an exit or a dividend event. Ask which.
- **Which entity?** Abusix, Inc. (US) or a German GmbH? This affects tax treatment for a Swiss resident. Get Swiss tax advice before signing; options are usually taxed in Switzerland at exercise or vesting, depending on the plan.
- **How much?** Ask for the percentage, the fully diluted share count, the strike price and the last valuation or 409A. A percentage without a denominator means nothing. For a Head of Engineering on the leadership team at a company this size, 0.5-1.5% is a reasonable range to aim for; the more mature and profitable the company, the lower that number will be.
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Acceleration:** double-trigger (change of control plus termination) at minimum.
- **Liquidity:** a 15-year-old company without an obvious exit may mean the equity never turns into cash. Ask directly whether there have been secondary sales, buybacks or dividends, and what the founders' plans are.
- **Exit window:** push for a long exercise period after leaving (5-10 years instead of 90 days), or a virtual plan where it doesn't apply.

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

> "I want to be straight with you: I'm also in conversations for a Staff/Principal role in Geneva at around CHF [X]. I'd prefer Abusix because of the scope and the chance to lead the team, but the gap is hard to ignore. If we can get to €[Y] base with a [Z]% grant, I'd be ready to sign."

Don't invent a competing offer. If there isn't one, use the market data instead: "Geneva roles at this level are paying CHF 200k and up."

## Before signing, get in writing

- Title (Head of Engineering), reporting line to the CEO, seat on the leadership team.
- Employment structure for Switzerland and who pays social charges.
- Base, bonus if any, and the six-month review terms.
- ESOP: instrument, amount, fully diluted count, strike, vesting, acceleration, exercise window.
- Notice period and non-compete scope. German-style non-competes usually require compensation during the restricted period; check how theirs is worded.
