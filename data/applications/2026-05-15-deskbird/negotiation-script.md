# Negotiation Script — deskbird Staff Software Engineer

---

## Market Context

**Company:** deskbird, Series B ($23M raised), ~100 employees, founded 2020. Based in St. Gallen with hubs across Europe. Profitable trajectory common for B2B SaaS at this stage, but still in growth mode.

**Role level:** Staff Software Engineer. This is one level above Senior. In European B2B SaaS, Staff IC roles typically carry technical leadership weight equivalent to an engineering manager, without the people management overhead.

**Market ranges (2025-2026):**

Remote Staff/Lead Backend Engineer at a European Series B SaaS:
- Base: €120,000 - €165,000 EUR (depending on country of candidate)
- With equity: total comp target €140,000 - €190,000 EUR equivalent

Switzerland/Geneva market equivalent for the same role (on-site or Geneva-based remote):
- Base: CHF 180,000 - CHF 230,000
- Note: Geneva has one of the highest costs of living in Europe. Swiss market rates are 30-45% higher than Western European averages for equivalent roles.

**Your anchor:** Given Magino is based in Geneva and brings 17+ years of experience including a founding-engineer track record and a successful acquisition, the opening anchor should be at the top of the range. Start at €155,000 - €165,000 EUR base (or CHF equivalent if they offer Swiss payroll), plus equity.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask about compensation expectations, lead with a number, not a range. Ranges anchor to the bottom.

**Script:**

> "Based on the scope of the Staff Engineer role and my experience level, I'm targeting a base in the range of €160,000. I'm open to discussing total comp including equity if the base is structured differently."

If they push back immediately or say that's above their band:

> "That's useful context. Can you share what the band looks like for this role? I want to make sure we're talking about the same scope, because at the Staff level I'd be expected to drive architecture decisions and technical leadership across the team, not just individual contributions."

## Counter-Offer Scripts

**If they come in at €110,000 - €125,000 (common for Series B European startups trying to pay "fair" remote rates):**

> "I appreciate the offer. I want to be transparent: that's meaningfully below what I'd need to make this work, and it's also below the market for Staff-level engineers with a founding track record and an acquisition behind them. I've been offered [X] elsewhere for comparable scope, and I'd need to be closer to €150,000 to take this seriously. Is there flexibility in the band, or is there equity that could close the gap?"

**If they invoke the "we pay the same regardless of location" policy (common at remote-first companies):**

> "I understand the philosophy, and I think it's fair for most cases. The practical reality is that I'm based in Geneva, which has one of the highest costs of living in Europe. A rate that's competitive for a remote engineer in Poland or Portugal translates to a 30-40% haircut in Geneva purchasing power. I'm not asking for a local Swiss rate, but I do need the number to work in my context. Can we find a middle ground?"

**If they say the band tops out at €130,000 but offer equity to compensate:**

> "Let's talk about the equity in detail. What's the current strike price, how much runway does the $23M give you, and what does the vesting schedule look like? I want to understand what the equity is actually worth at a realistic exit scenario, not just the option grant on paper."

---

## Equity Negotiation

deskbird is Series B. Equity is real but the range of outcomes is still wide.

**What to ask for:**

- Option grant in the range of 0.05% - 0.15% for a Staff IC at Series B (this is the realistic range, not the aspirational one)
- 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff (standard; push back if they offer 3-year vesting as it often signals the company expects you to leave)
- Ask for the strike price and the last 409A or most recent valuation for context

**If they offer stock options with a high strike price relative to current 409A:**

> "Can you walk me through the cap table and the last valuation? I want to understand what the dilution path looks like before the equity component factors significantly into my decision. At Series B with additional rounds likely before exit, I want to make sure the options are still meaningful after the next raise."

**Anti-dilution / pro-rata:**

As an employee (not an investor), you won't get anti-dilution protection. What you can negotiate is acceleration: ask for double-trigger acceleration on unvested options in the event of acquisition. This is often granted at the Staff level and protects you if the company is acquired before your vesting completes.

> "One thing I'd like to include is double-trigger acceleration on unvested options in the case of an acquisition. Given my background coming through an acquisition myself, I understand how these situations play out, and I'd like that protection in the offer."

## Geographic Premium Argument

If they push back on the Geneva premium:

> "I'm not asking for a full Geneva-market rate. I know deskbird pays remote rates, and I've calibrated my expectations accordingly. What I'm saying is that the number needs to clear the 'makes sense to leave what I have' threshold, and in Geneva, that threshold is higher than in most European cities. I'd be taking on meaningful opportunity cost relative to the Swiss market. I think we can find a number that works for both sides."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (even pending), use it:

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations with one other company. I'm genuinely more interested in deskbird because of [specific reason: the integrations problem, the product scope, the team]. But I need the economics to be competitive or I'd be leaving real money on the table. If you can get to €150,000 with the equity we discussed, I'm ready to move forward."

If you don't have a concrete competing offer:

> "I've had significant inbound interest recently, and I'm being deliberate about where I go next. I'm not playing games with competing offers, but I do want to be honest that the number needs to be in the range we discussed for me to take myself out of other conversations."
