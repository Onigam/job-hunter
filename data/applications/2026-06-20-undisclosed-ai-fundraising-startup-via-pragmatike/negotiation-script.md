# Negotiation Script — Hands-on CTO, AI Fundraising Startup (via Pragmatike)

---

## Market Context

**Role type:** Hands-on CTO at an early-stage AI startup, fully remote EMEA.

**Switzerland market anchors (2025-2026):**
- Senior full-stack engineer (8-12 years), Geneva: CHF 145K-175K
- Staff/Principal Engineer (12+ years): CHF 165K-200K
- CTO at seed-stage startup (equity-heavy): CHF 110K-160K base + 0.8-2.5% equity
- CTO at Series A startup (more established): CHF 150K-190K base + 0.4-1.0% equity
- Magino's profile (17 years, co-founder background, AI systems): CHF 175K-210K is defensible on merit alone; the CTO title at early stage typically implies a cash-equity trade-off

**Key variable:** Compensation was not disclosed in the posting. Early-stage CTO roles often come with below-market base offset by meaningful equity. The right opening is anchored high on base, explicit about equity expectations, and flexible on structure only once you know what "structure" actually means.

**CTO vs. engineer distinction:** The job description specified 3-5+ years experience, which reads as a minimum floor. Magino brings 17 years including a founding-to-acquisition cycle. That experience gap should translate to a higher base and a larger equity stake than what a candidate meeting the minimum would receive.

---

## Opening Ask

**Anchor:** CHF 180,000 base + meaningful equity stake (target 0.7-1.2% for this stage).

**Script (first comp conversation):**

"I haven't seen comp details in the posting, so let me share where I'm anchored. For a CTO role at this stage, I'm looking at CHF 180K base. That reflects the level, 17 years of experience including building and exiting a company, and Geneva cost of living as a reference point even for a remote role. I also expect equity to be part of the conversation, and I'd want to understand the structure before we go further."

**Why this works:** Anchoring with a clear justification (level + experience + geography) prevents the recruiter from setting the floor first. Naming equity expectations upfront signals you understand early-stage comp and are not naive about cash vs. upside trade-offs.

---

## If They Come In Low (Below CHF 140K Base)

**Script:**

"I appreciate the transparency. CHF [their number] is below where I'm benchmarking for this level, and I want to flag that early so we're not misaligned. The posting said 3-5+ years as a minimum; I bring 17, including building a platform from zero through a $1B+ acquisition. If there's flexibility on the range, I'd like to explore it. If the cash is fixed, I'd want to understand the equity package in detail before making a decision, including the current valuation, option pool size, and vesting terms."

**Key tactic:** Don't reject it outright. Reframe toward total comp. If equity is genuinely substantial (0.8%+ at a reasonable valuation), a lower base might still make sense. You need the numbers before you can evaluate.

---

## If They Use "Remote Discount" Logic

Some startups tier pay by location, arguing that remote candidates should accept lower comp than a San Francisco hire.

**Script:**

"I understand remote companies sometimes apply geographic tiers. My view is that the output and the risk-adjusted contribution don't change based on my timezone, and Geneva cost of living is a real variable for me. I'd rather discuss a structure where the comp reflects the work. I'm flexible on other dimensions, including equity weighting, but not on a significant geographic haircut."

---

## Equity Negotiation (CTO at Early Stage)

**At seed or very early Series A, appropriate CTO equity:**
- 0.7% to 2.0% on a 4-year vest with 1-year cliff is standard for a CTO joining this early
- 1.0% is a reasonable midpoint to target
- Below 0.5% warrants a serious conversation about whether this is really a CTO role or a founding engineer role with a title

**Opening ask:**

"For a CTO at this stage I'd expect something in the 0.8-1.2% range on a four-year vest with a one-year cliff. That reflects both the seniority of the role and the risk of joining early. What does the current option pool look like relative to the cap table?"

**Follow-up questions to ask before signing anything:**
- What's the current post-money valuation or SAFE cap?
- What's the strike price relative to the most recent 409A (or equivalent for a European entity)?
- Are these stock options, BSPCEs (if French entity), or a different instrument?
- Is there any acceleration on change of control? Single-trigger or double-trigger?
- What's the exercise window post-departure? Standard 90 days is punishing; push for 2-5 years.
- What percentage of the option pool is unallocated after this grant?

**If equity details are vague or deferred:**

"I'd want to see the cap table and the option grant documentation before we finalize anything. That's completely standard at this stage, and it doesn't signal distrust; it's just what responsible diligence looks like. I can't evaluate total compensation without knowing what the equity is actually worth."

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (real or in final stages):

"I'm in final conversations with another company in a similar space. It's not identical to what you're building, but the comp discussion is live and I expect an offer this week. I'd prefer to make a decision based on the strength of this role, not just the number, but I'd need to know where you land before I can do that. Can you get me a formal offer with equity details by [specific date]?"

If no competing offer, don't bluff. Use timing leverage instead: "I'm running a tight timeline on a few conversations and want to give you a clear answer by [date]. What do I need to have in hand from your side to do that?"

---

## Role Scope Clarification (Critical Before Accepting)

The title says CTO but the posting reads like a founding/lead engineer with a title bump. Before signing, clarify:

- "What does the engineering team look like today, and what's the hiring plan for the next 12 months?"
- "Is there a co-founder or other technical stakeholder I'd be working alongside, or am I the sole technical decision-maker?"
- "What does success look like in the first 90 days, in concrete deliverables?"
- "How much of my time do you expect to be IC work versus team leadership in year one?"

A CTO at 3 people is a different job than a CTO at 20. Knowing which one this is before negotiating matters, because the equity expectations and the comp structure should reflect the actual scope of the role.

---

## Timeline and Closing

**If they ask when you can start:**
"I can move quickly. Two to four weeks from a signed offer is realistic."

**If the process slows after an offer:**
"I want to make a thoughtful decision and I think you'd want the same. Can you give me until [specific date, 5-7 business days] to review the equity documentation and come back with any final questions? I'll have a clear answer for you by then."

**Red flags that should slow you down:**
- Inability to answer basic cap table questions before signing
- No written equity grant documentation before you start
- Vesting cliff longer than 12 months
- "CTO" title but no meaningful decision-making authority on tech or hiring
- Comp presented as non-negotiable on both base and equity simultaneously
