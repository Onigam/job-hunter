# Negotiation Script: plancraft, Staff Engineer (f/m/d) AI

Location: Hamburg, Germany. The posting offers remote, hybrid or on-site, plus up to two months a year working abroad; other listings for the same role mention relocation to Hamburg and 2 to 3 office days per week. Candidate is based in Geneva.
Posted comp: not disclosed. Benefits listed: 30 days vacation, EUR 1,500 learning budget, JobRad, Deutschlandticket.
Company stage: Series B, EUR 38M led by Headline (2025) with Creandum, HTGF and xdeck; EUR 50M+ raised in total, 100+ employees, 20,000+ customers.

---

## 1. The real question: where is the contract?

Before any number, settle the setup. It changes everything else.

- **Option A: relocate to Hamburg.** German contract, German pay. That's a large drop in net income against Geneva, and it would need a strong reason to make sense.
- **Option B: remote from Geneva.** A German GmbH can't simply put a Swiss resident on a German payroll. Swiss social security applies once someone works mainly from Switzerland, so plancraft would need an Employer of Record (Deel, Remote, etc.) or a contractor setup. Ask early whether they've done this before.
- **Option C: hybrid commute.** A regular Hamburg week each month, Geneva the rest. Probably still a Swiss-resident employment case, so it falls under B.

Question for the recruiter call:

> "Before we talk numbers, can we agree on setup? I'm based in Geneva. Is employing through an EoR in Switzerland an option, with a regular on-site rhythm in Hamburg, or is relocation a hard requirement?"

## 2. Market anchors (base, 2026, working estimates, verify before the call)

Sources to re-check: Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Kununu, recruiter conversations.

**Germany (Hamburg / Berlin)**

| Level | Typical base | Well-funded Series B+ |
|---|---|---|
| Senior engineer | EUR 80k to 100k | EUR 95k to 110k |
| Staff engineer | EUR 105k to 130k | EUR 120k to 145k |

**Switzerland (Geneva / Zürich), for the remote case**

| Level | Typical base | Strong scale-up / big tech |
|---|---|---|
| Senior engineer | CHF 140k to 165k | CHF 170k to 190k |
| Staff engineer | CHF 170k to 200k | CHF 200k to 240k |

German startups almost never match Swiss salaries. Realistically, a Hamburg Series B paying a Swiss resident via EoR will land between its German top band and the lower end of the Swiss range. The goal is to pull that number as far toward Swiss rates as possible.

## 3. Target packages

**Remote from Geneva (EoR):**
- Opening anchor: CHF 185k base plus VSOP
- Target: CHF 165k to 175k
- Walk-away: CHF 150k (below that, Swiss offers win on cash alone)

**Relocation to Hamburg:**
- Opening anchor: EUR 150k base plus VSOP and relocation package
- Target: EUR 135k to 145k
- Walk-away: EUR 125k, and only with a meaningful VSOP grant

## 4. Opening the conversation

If asked for expectations:

> "For a Staff role owning AI architecture across teams, and given my background, 17 years in, a cofounder who built a platform through acquisition and a Senior Staff title at Benevity, I'm targeting CHF 185k base if I stay in Geneva, plus equity. I know that's above German bands. It reflects Swiss cost of living and Swiss social charges, which are part of how an EoR setup works anyway. I'm open on the split between base and VSOP once I see the grant."

If they insist on a single euro figure:

> "If you think in euros, around EUR 150k for Hamburg-based, higher for Geneva-based. The level and the setup matter more to me than the exact figure, so let's pin those down first."

## 5. If they come in low (for example EUR 110k to 120k)

> "Thanks, I appreciate it. I'll be straight with you: that's a solid Hamburg number for Staff, but I live in Geneva, where a comparable role pays around CHF 180k to 200k. I'm genuinely interested in plancraft's voice-first direction, so I want to find a way to make this work. To say yes, I'd need the base at CHF 165k or above through an EoR. Is there room to get there?"

If base is capped:

> "If the base band is fixed, can we look at a larger VSOP grant, a sign-on bonus, and a written comp review at six months once you've seen the work?"

## 6. Geographic discount pushback

If they argue for German pay because the company is in Germany:

> "I get that you've got bands to protect. The thing is, you'd be hiring from the Swiss market, not the German one, and my alternatives are Swiss roles. The two months of work-from-abroad in your posting shows you already think location-flexible. I'm not asking for a Zürich big-tech package, just a number that doesn't cost me money to take the job."

Supporting points:
- Swiss employer social charges and EoR fees come out of plancraft's budget regardless; clarify whether their quoted number is gross cost or gross salary.
- Geneva rent, health insurance (mandatory, individual, roughly CHF 400 to 600 a month) and childcare costs are far above Hamburg's.
- In person time is still on the table: offer a regular Hamburg week, with travel paid by plancraft.

## 7. Equity (German VSOP)

Most German startups, plancraft likely included, grant virtual shares (VSOP) instead of real equity. They pay out in cash at an exit, usually nothing before. Questions to ask:

- **Grant size.** Number of virtual shares, total share count (fully diluted), and the strike or base price. Ask what percentage that represents.
- **Valuation basis.** Which valuation is used, the Series B preferred price or a discount? Ask for the latest post-money.
- **Vesting.** Expect 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Leaver clauses.** The big one in German VSOPs. Check what happens to vested shares if you leave as a good leaver, and whether vested shares decay over time after you leave. Push for vested shares to stay fully vested for good leavers.
- **Payout trigger.** Exit only, or also secondaries? Ask whether any secondary has happened for employees.
- **Liquidation preference.** Real shareholders' preferences (Headline, Creandum) get paid first. Ask how the VSOP ranks.
- **Acceleration.** Ask for double-trigger acceleration on change of control, especially relevant given the Benevity acquisition experience.
- **Tax.** VSOP payouts are taxed as income at payout. If you're Swiss-resident, check how the EoR and Swiss tax authorities treat it.
- **Anti-dilution.** Not realistic for VSOP holders. Ask instead about top-up or refresh grants after future rounds.

I'm in late-stage conversations with a couple of Swiss companies at Staff level, with base offers in the CHF 170k to 190k range. plancraft is my preference because of the product problem, turning messy voice input into correct quotes and invoices is exactly the kind of AI work I want to be doing. If we can close the gap on base or equity, I'm ready to move quickly."

## 9. Non-salary items worth asking for

- Travel budget for Hamburg visits (flights plus hotel), paid by plancraft, not deducted.
- Learning budget: raise EUR 1,500 to EUR 3,000, including one conference a year.
- AI tooling budget (Claude, Cursor, etc.) covered in full.
- Start date flexibility.
- Written title confirmation: Staff Engineer, with a documented path to Principal.
