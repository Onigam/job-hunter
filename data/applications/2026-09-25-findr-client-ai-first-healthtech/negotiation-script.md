# Negotiation Script: Lead Agentic Systems Engineer @ AI-first HealthTech (via Findr, Frankfurt)

## Context
- The employer is a growing AI-first HealthTech company in Frankfurt/Rhein-Main building clinical decision support for clinicians and therapists in children's behavioral, developmental and mental health. The posting comes through Findr, a tech recruitment agency, so the first conversations will be with a recruiter, not the hiring manager.
- Published range: **EUR 175,000 to 200,000/year**, full-time, listed as a management-level role. Remote policy isn't stated. Equity isn't mentioned.
- **The key point:** the range is already at the very top of the German market, so there's little room to push base much beyond EUR 200k. The real negotiation is over (1) landing at the top of the band, (2) the work arrangement from Geneva, which has a real cost, (3) equity or VSOP, which isn't mentioned at all, and (4) title and scope, since "Lead" can mean anything from senior IC to head of AI.
- **How the recruiter fits in:** Findr is usually paid a percentage of first-year salary, so they're motivated to close at a good number, but also to close fast. Use them to learn the real ceiling and whether remote is negotiable before you're in front of the client.

**What that means:** for Germany, this offer is excellent. For a Senior Staff engineer in Switzerland, it's roughly market, not a premium. Don't argue the band down or up on Swiss numbers alone; use them to explain why you need the top of it and why the work arrangement matters.

## Your target

- **Walk-away:** EUR 185k base, a written hybrid arrangement from Geneva (no more than 2 on-site days a week, or one week in two), travel covered or a travel allowance, and a clear answer on equity.
- **Target:** EUR 200k base, plus VSOP or options worth meaningful upside, travel costs covered, and a scope that includes owning AI architecture and evals across the product, not just one feature team.
- **Opening anchor:** EUR 200k base plus equity. Justification: 17 years of production systems, a platform built from zero to an acquisition by a unicorn, hands-on LLM and multi-agent work, plus hiring and team-building experience that fits a management-level title.

## Script: when the recruiter asks about expectations

"I've seen the range in the posting, and it's in the right place. Given the scope, with a production agentic system, clinical safety and building the team around it, I'd expect to be at the top of it, so EUR 200k. Two things matter just as much to me as the number: how the role works with me based in Geneva, and whether there's equity. Can you tell me what the client has in mind on both?"

Then let them answer first. Don't give a number below 200k in the first conversation.

## Counter-offer scripts

**If they offer EUR 175k to 180k:**
"Thanks, I appreciate it. For me the gap is mostly about scope. The posting describes someone who owns guardrails, evals, monitoring and reliability for AI that clinicians rely on, and who works with product and healthcare experts to decide what gets built. That's the top of the band. If base is capped closer to 185k, I'd want to see that made up in equity or a signing bonus."

**If they say the band is fixed at 190k:**
"I can work with 190k if the rest of the package reflects it. Could we add a signing bonus of around EUR 15k to 20k, a first review at six months tied to agreed milestones, and cover the travel between Geneva and Frankfurt?"

**If they insist on full-time presence in Frankfurt:**
"I understand wanting the team together, especially early on. I'd be glad to spend the first month mostly on site to get to know the team and the clinicians. After that, two days a week, or one full week in two, is what I can commit to sustainably from Geneva. If full relocation is non-negotiable, we'd need to talk about a relocation package and a higher base to cover it, and I'd have to think carefully."

**If they want you as a contractor or through an employer-of-record because you live in Switzerland:**
Be careful here. Working from Switzerland for a German employer raises social security (A1 certificate), tax and permanent establishment questions. As a contractor you'd carry your own AVS/AHV, pension and no paid leave, so the day rate needs to be about 25% to 35% above the employee equivalent. "If a contract setup is easier for you, I'm open to it, but the rate would need to reflect that I'm covering social charges, pension and holidays myself."

## Equity notes (German startup specifics)

- German startups often offer a **VSOP** (virtual stock option plan) rather than real shares. It's a cash bonus that pays out on an exit, taxed as regular income when paid, and usually worth nothing if there's no exit. Ask plainly: "Is it real options or a VSOP?"
- Ask for the **number of units, the total fully diluted units, the strike price and the latest valuation**, so you can work out the percentage. "Some equity" isn't an answer.
- Standard is **4 years vesting with a 1-year cliff**. Push back on exit-only vesting or plans where vested units lapse if you leave before an exit.
- **Leaver terms** matter more than the headline number: a good leaver (laid off, role change, mutual agreement) should keep vested units. Watch for clauses that forfeit everything on any departure.
- Ask about **acceleration on change of control** (double trigger is reasonable) and whether there's a pool refresh at the next round.
- For a lead hire at a growing Series A/B company, 0.2% to 0.75% is a reasonable range to ask about. Adjust once you know the stage and valuation.

## Geographic pushback

If they suggest a lower number because you'd be remote: "Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and I'd be commuting to Frankfurt on my own time. I'm not asking for a Geneva premium, just the posted band, without a remote discount on top."

If they suggest a lower number because of Swiss tax or cost differences: "The band was set for the role, not for where I live. I'd prefer to keep it that way."

## Competing offer template

Use only if it's true, or keep it general.

"I want to be transparent with you. I'm in conversations with a couple of Swiss companies for Staff and lead roles in AI infrastructure, with offers in the CHF 180k to 200k range and less travel. This role interests me more because of the clinical problem and the ownership, so I'd like to make it work. Is there room to get to 200k, or to close the gap with equity or a signing bonus?"

## Questions to ask before any number is final

- Stage, funding and runway of the company. Who are the investors?
- Who does this role report to, and is there team-building or hiring in the scope?
- What does "management" level mean here: people management, technical lead, or both?
- What regulatory framework applies to the product today (MDR, GDPR health data), and who owns it?
- On-site expectation in days per week, and whether travel is expensed.
- Notice period and earliest start date expected.
