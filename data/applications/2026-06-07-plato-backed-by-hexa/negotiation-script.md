# Negotiation Script — Plato (Founding Engineer)

## Market Context

**Role type:** Founding Engineer #1 (first hire, joining two founders)
**Location:** Paris, hybrid
**Stage:** Seed/pre-seed backed by Hexa studio (not a traditional VC round)
**Hexa model:** Hexa co-founds and seeds companies, typically providing ~€500k-1M at formation. Equity allocation tends to be generous for early hires because Hexa expects to dilute with future rounds.

**Paris market rates for this profile (2026):**
- Senior/Staff founding engineer, Paris: €90-130k base
- For someone with a co-founding + acquisition exit (Magino's profile): can anchor to €130-150k
- Founding engineer equity at seed stage: 0.5-1.5% is standard, with 0.75-1.0% being the realistic target for #1 engineering hire
- Vesting: 4 years, 1-year cliff is the norm in French startups

**Geneva cost of living premium:** Magino is based in Geneva, which has ~30-40% higher cost of living than Paris. If the role requires significant presence in Paris (vs. primarily remote), this is a legitimate negotiation lever. Travel costs and time are real costs.

---

## Opening Anchor

When salary comes up, aim high and justify with comp data:

> "Based on my profile — co-founding exit, Staff-level at a unicorn, and the AI/LLM work I've been doing — I'm thinking about €140k as a starting point for the base. That's in line with what I've seen for founding engineers with this kind of background. I'm genuinely more interested in the equity story here, so I'd love to understand how you're thinking about that."

**Why this works:**
- Anchors above their likely range, leaving room to negotiate down while landing above their median
- Pivots immediately to equity, which is where the real value is at this stage
- Signals you've done your homework without being adversarial

---

## If They Come In Low (€90-110k range)

> "I appreciate the transparency. That's a bit below what I was expecting given the seniority and the founding engineer scope here, where I'd be owning architecture decisions, not just implementing specs. I'd want to get to at least €120k on the base to make this work financially, especially given that I'm based in Geneva. I'm open on structure — if there's flexibility on equity or a signing bonus, I'm happy to have that conversation."

**Floor:** Don't accept below €115k base for Paris hybrid with Geneva-based living costs.

---

## Equity Negotiation

This is the highest-leverage variable for an early Hexa-backed company. Front (now acquired by Salesforce) and Aircall (raised $100M+) came out of the same studio. The equity can be worth real money.

**Target:** 0.75-1.0% on a 4-year vest, 1-year cliff
**Minimum acceptable:** 0.5% on standard terms

**Ask directly:**
> "What's the equity allocation for this role, and can you walk me through the cap table? Specifically: how many shares are allocated to the employee option pool, what's the current implied valuation, and what's the vesting schedule?"

**Key questions to get answered before signing:**
- What is the option pool size as a percentage of fully diluted shares?
- What price were options last granted at, and what's the strike price for this grant?
- Is there double-trigger acceleration on change of control? (This matters — if Plato is acquired, you don't want your unvested shares to evaporate)
- Are the options ISOs or BSPCEs? (In France, BSPCEs are favorable for employees — lower tax treatment on gains)

**If equity offer is below 0.5%:**
> "Given the timing, I'd expect a founding hire to be in the 0.75-1% range. The risk profile of joining at this stage is real, and the equity should reflect that. Can we revisit the allocation?"

---

## Geographic Lever (Geneva base)

If they push back on salary citing "Paris rates":

> "I take the point on Paris benchmarks. The practical reality is I'm based in Geneva, which has a meaningfully higher cost of living, and I'd be commuting to Paris regularly. That travel time has a real cost — both financially and in terms of life logistics. I'm not looking to extract a Geneva premium on a Paris role, but I do need the number to work for my actual situation, not just a Paris benchmark."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have other offers (or want to create constructive urgency without lying):

> "I'm actively in process with two other companies and expect to have decisions to make in the next two to three weeks. Plato is my top choice based on the problem space and the team, but I want to make sure the package reflects that before I close other conversations."

**If no competing offer:** Don't fabricate one. Instead, use timeline:
> "I'm trying to make a decision by end of [month]. Can we get to a term sheet by [date]?"

---

## Signing Bonus

If base is stuck:

> "If the base is capped at €X, I'd want to bridge the gap with a signing bonus. Something in the €10-15k range would make this work for me. One-time payment, no conditions beyond staying past the cliff."

---

## What Not To Do

- Don't accept the first verbal offer without a written term sheet
- Don't skip asking about the cap table — vague equity is nearly worthless
- Don't anchor to Swiss CHF unless you're negotiating a Geneva-remote contract (this is a Paris role)
- Don't reveal your current Benevity comp unless asked directly; if asked, redirect to your target: "I'm looking at €140k for this type of role"
