# Negotiation Script: Camley, Founding Engineer (Full-Stack AI)

## Context that shapes this negotiation

The listing gives no salary range, lists the role as remote out of India or the UK, and states no visa sponsorship. I'm in Geneva, not relocating, so the sponsorship line is moot and the timezone gap is one hour against UTC, closer to workable than most "remote" postings turn out to be. The real question isn't location, it's that there's no anchor to react to, so the opening move is finding out what band they're actually working from before naming a number.

Camley is early enough to be called a founding-engineer seat and is pitching itself against a $55 trillion asset class with a Harvey-style ambition, which usually means either pre-seed scrappy or freshly funded with real capital behind the comparison. That distinction changes the whole conversation, so it's worth asking directly rather than guessing.

## Opening: find the stage before naming a number

"Before we get into specifics, I'd like a sense of where the company is, funding stage, runway, and roughly what band you're working from for this seat. That tells me whether we're talking cash-heavy or equity-heavy, and I'd rather calibrate to the real number than throw one out blind."

## If they're pre-seed or bootstrapped, cash is genuinely tight

**Opening anchor:**
"Given the stage, I'd expect this to be equity-forward. I'd want a base in the CHF 110,000-130,000 range, roughly $125,000-$145,000, with equity toward the higher end of whatever band you're offering founding engineers, standard four-year vest, one-year cliff. That base covers the floor; the upside is where the real comp lives at this stage."

**If they counter meaningfully lower:**
"I get that runway is the constraint. I can flex on cash if the equity moves to compensate, but I'd want that trade made explicit and revisited at the next funding milestone, not treated as the permanent number."

## If they're seed-to-Series-A with real funding behind the Harvey comparison

**Opening anchor:**
"If there's a funded seed or A behind this, I'd anchor around CHF 150,000-180,000 base, call it $170,000-$200,000, plus equity in the 0.5%-1.5% range depending on how early I'm actually joining relative to the founding team. That reflects Staff-level ownership on the agent architecture and eval layer, not a generalist full-stack hire."

**If they push back on the cash number:**
"Fair, I'm not trying to import a full Swiss-market number onto a startup budget. But this isn't a market where I'd take a discount just because the listing was aimed at India or the UK, since I'm neither relocating nor asking for anything the role wasn't already going to pay a comparable hire elsewhere. Where's the actual room, base or equity?"

## Handling the Python gap honestly

The role wants production Python and FastAPI experience; my daily driver has been Node and React, with Python as part of the toolkit rather than the primary one. That's a real gap worth naming before it becomes a negotiating point against me later.

"I want to flag something myself rather than have it surface in an offer conversation: my production depth is stronger in TypeScript and Node than in Python, even though Python's part of what I use. If that's a hard requirement for day one, it's fair for that to shape the number. If there's room to ramp on the Python side over the first month or two while I bring the orchestration and eval-system experience immediately, I'd rather have that conversation now."

## Equity negotiation notes

- Ask for the fully diluted share count and which round the equity is priced against; a headline percentage means very different things depending on prior dilution.
- Standard four-year vest, one-year cliff is the baseline; push if they propose anything longer.
- Ask about acceleration on acquisition or change of control. Given the Alaya-to-Benevity path, this is a real, informed question: "What happens to vesting if Camley gets acquired in year two or three?"
- Confirm the post-termination exercise window. Under 90 days is worth pushing on; some seed-stage companies now offer extended windows if asked directly.

## Competing offer leverage template

Use only if genuinely true at the time:

"I want to be straightforward that I have another conversation further along that's closer to a standard Swiss-market remote package. Camley is the more interesting problem technically, agent infrastructure against real financial workflows is a harder and more honest bet than most agent startups are making, so I'd rather find a way to make this work than default to the other option on comp alone. Is there room to close the gap?"

## Closing line for any scenario

"I want this to work. The problem is the right kind of hard, and I'd rather start the comp conversation from real numbers on both sides than spend the first three months renegotiating something that should have been settled upfront."
