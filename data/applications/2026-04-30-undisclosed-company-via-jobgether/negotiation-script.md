# Negotiation Script — Staff Software Engineer (Remote EU) via Jobgether

## Market Context

**Role level:** Staff Software Engineer (IC, technical authority)
**Work model:** Fully remote, EU timezone
**Company type:** Undisclosed (via Jobgether matching). Likely growth-stage or scale-up. Company identity revealed after matching.

**Key unknown:** Currency and company base country. Prepare for both CHF and EUR scenarios.

**EU remote Staff SWE market ranges (2025-2026):**
- EU remote, EUR-paying company: €110,000-145,000 base depending on company size and country
- US company with EU remote: $160,000-200,000 USD (or EUR-equivalent at local rate)
- Geneva-based equivalent for this level: CHF 160,000-185,000 base
- Total comp target (base + variable + equity if any): CHF 195,000-220,000 or EUR equivalent

**Geneva cost premium context:**
Geneva's cost of living runs 30-40% above most Western European cities and 15-20% above Zurich. A EUR 120,000 offer from a German or Dutch company is not equivalent to CHF 155,000 in Geneva terms. This gap needs to be named explicitly if it comes up.

---

## Before the First Conversation

Once Jobgether reveals the company, research:
- Company stage (seed, Series A/B, post-IPO, acquired)
- Funding total and recent round date
- Engineering team size (LinkedIn headcount for Engineering)
- Whether they have other Geneva or Swiss employees (check LinkedIn)
- Whether there's a public salary range in any version of the job posting

---

## Opening Anchor

**Use when they make a first offer or ask for your expectations:**

"My target for a remote Staff Software Engineer role is in the CHF 175,000-190,000 base range, or the EUR equivalent adjusted for Geneva cost of living. That's based on current Staff-level benchmarks for platform and architecture roles in the Swiss and EU remote market. I'd also want to understand whether there's an equity component before settling on a specific number."

**Why this works:**
- Sets a credible range at the upper end of market without going above it
- Frames the CHF/EUR conversion as expected, not aggressive
- Separates base from equity, creating two levers

---

## If They Come In Low (EUR 100,000-115,000 or CHF 145,000-160,000)

"Thanks for the offer. The role itself is genuinely interesting, but I have to be honest that this base is below what I'd need given Geneva's cost structure and the seniority level. Remote Staff SWE roles I've been benchmarking in the EU market are coming in at EUR 125,000-140,000, and from my base in Geneva that needs to land closer to CHF 170,000-180,000 to be equivalent. Is there room to move, or does equity or a signing bonus change the picture?"

**If they push back on Geneva costs:**
"I understand you're hiring EU remote, not Geneva-specific. But I'm anchored to Geneva costs and that's not changing. I'm being transparent about the number I need, not inflating a wish list. If we can close the gap on base or total comp, I'm ready to move quickly."

---

## EUR vs CHF Conversion Argument

When an EU company offers EUR and resists a higher number:

"A EUR 115,000 offer from a company paying in euros looks reasonable on paper, but in Geneva terms that's roughly CHF 115,000-118,000 at current rates, which is well below Staff-level compensation for this city. I'm not asking for a Geneva premium on top of market, I'm asking for market rate when adjusted for where I actually live. The delta we're talking about is CHF 20,000-30,000 annually."

---

## Equity Negotiation

For a startup or growth-stage company (likely given undisclosed Jobgether profile):

Ask these questions before factoring equity into any decision:

1. "What does the equity structure look like at Staff level: options, RSUs, or something else?"
2. "What's the current valuation, and when was the last pricing event?"
3. "What's the vesting schedule and cliff?"
4. "Is there any secondary liquidity mechanism for employees before an exit?"
5. "What happens to unvested equity in an acquisition scenario?"

**Framing for the equity ask:**
"I treat equity as long-term upside rather than current compensation unless there's near-term liquidity. I'd want to understand the strike price relative to fair market value and the realistic path to exit before putting a number on it. That said, at Staff level I'd expect a grant in the range of 0.1-0.3% for an early-stage company, or a comparable RSU band for a later-stage one."

---

## Geneva Cost of Living Pushback

If they say "we pay market rate for EU remote":

"I'd push back slightly on that framing. 'EU remote market rate' usually benchmarks against Berlin, Amsterdam, or Lisbon, which are all 25-40% cheaper cities than Geneva. My fixed costs, housing included, are benchmarked against Geneva, not the EU average. I'm not asking for a geographic premium; I'm asking for a rate that makes economic sense from where I'm sitting."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in play:

"I want to be transparent: I'm at a late stage with one other company at a comparable level. I'm more interested in this role for a few reasons I can explain, but the offers need to be competitive for the decision to be clear. Getting to CHF 180,000 base would make this the straightforward choice."

If you don't have a competing offer:

"I've been selective in this search and I'm not entertaining offers below CHF 170,000 at this stage. I'd rather stay patient and find the right fit at the right comp than compromise on either."

---

## On Contractor vs. Employee Status

Jobgether sometimes places candidates as contractors rather than employees, particularly for cross-border EU remote roles. If this comes up:

"If the engagement is a contractor structure rather than employment, the rate needs to reflect the additional overhead: no employer pension contribution, no paid leave, no health insurance. The contractor equivalent of a CHF 175,000 employment package is roughly CHF 200,000-215,000 gross annual, depending on how benefits are structured."

---

## Closing Move

Once you're aligned on the number:

"I think we're at a place where I'd like to move forward. Can you send the offer in writing with the base, equity terms, and start date? I'll review it and come back within 48 hours."

Never accept verbally on the spot. Always get it in writing before committing.
