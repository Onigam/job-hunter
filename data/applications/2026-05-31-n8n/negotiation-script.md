# Negotiation Script — Sr AI Engineer at n8n

---

## Market Context

n8n raised $240M total, with a Series D in 2024 that put the company at a reported ~$2.5B valuation. At that stage, compensation is typically hybrid: competitive base plus equity that still has meaningful upside but isn't early-stage speculative. The role is remote Europe, so there's no automatic Geneva cost-of-living multiplier, but you're a Senior Staff-level candidate with a clear acquisition track record.

**Market anchor data:**
- Published salary ranges for Sr AI Engineer (TypeScript/Node.js, Europe remote) aggregate around €140-175K base, with senior/staff-level roles at €155-180K at well-funded scale-ups
- Swiss Staff+ engineers with AI and platform backgrounds: CHF 155-200K base for in-market roles
- n8n has published ranges in some postings around £135-155K for Senior roles in the UK market, which maps to roughly €155-180K on a Europe-remote basis accounting for purchasing parity

**Your opening anchor: €170K base, or CHF equivalent if the contract is Swiss.**

This is justified, not inflated: 17 years of experience, co-founder with verified exit, Staff+ at a unicorn, AI engineering specialty that's genuinely scarce.

---

## Opening: When They Ask for Your Expectations

> "I've done some research on where the role sits in the market. For someone at this level with the background I'm bringing, I'm targeting around €170K base. That's based on published ranges for Senior AI Engineer roles at Series C/D companies in Europe and what I've seen for Staff-level positions with an acquisition track record. I'm also expecting equity to be part of the conversation."

If they push back immediately:
> "I'm flexible on how that's structured. If there's a range you're working within, I'd rather know it and have a real conversation than anchor on something that doesn't match your band."

---

## Scenario 1: Offer Comes in Above €160K

You're in a good position. Don't accept immediately.

> "That's in the right range. Before I give you a firm yes, I want to make sure I understand the equity component clearly: the strike price, the vesting schedule, the exercise window. Can you walk me through what a typical Senior package looks like on that side?"

Then push on:
- Learning and conference budget (push from €1K to €2,500-3K if their default is low)
- Equipment setup allowance if you're supplying your own hardware
- Start date flexibility (4-5 weeks notice is reasonable to ask for, even if their preference is faster)

---

## Scenario 2: Offer Comes in Between €140K-€160K

This is a negotiation, not a decline.

> "I appreciate the offer. I want to be direct with you: that's a bit below what I was targeting based on the market data I've looked at and the level we've been discussing. I was expecting something closer to €165-170K. Is there flexibility there, or would it make sense to discuss a different structure?"

If they say the base is firm:
> "Understood. If the base is where it is, I'd want to think about whether we can work on the equity allocation and possibly a signing bonus to bridge the gap for year one. What does the equity component look like at this level, and what's the most recent 409A or preferred price?"

Key equity asks at n8n's stage:
- Target 0.04-0.08% fully diluted for a Senior/Staff AI Engineer
- Exercise window: ask for 5-10 years post-termination (the standard 90-day window is punitive at late-stage private companies with high strike prices)
- Double-trigger acceleration clause on acquisition (you know what acquisition looks like from the inside)
- Ask whether there's been a secondary program or tender offer in the last 18 months

---

## Scenario 3: Offer Comes in Below €140K

> "I appreciate you putting together an offer, and I want to be honest with you: that's significantly below what I was expecting for this level. I don't want to waste anyone's time if there's a fundamental misalignment on the range. Is the budget for this role fixed, or is there room to revisit?"

If they say it's fixed:
> "Can I ask what the typical Senior AI Engineer base is at n8n? I want to make sure I'm comparing apples to apples on the level. If this is structured differently from what I understood the role to be, I'd rather surface that now."

If they won't move:
It's likely a misaligned role level (Senior vs. Staff) or a conscious discount. You can decline without burning the relationship:
> "I've really enjoyed the conversations and I think the team is doing genuinely interesting work. The compensation gap is significant enough that I don't think I can make it work, but I'd want to stay in touch as things evolve on both sides."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they suggest a discount for being Geneva-based on a remote role:

> "The role is remote-Europe, which I understand means I'm compensated on the European market band, not a Geneva-specific one. That's actually what makes it work for me. If the intent was to apply a Swiss cost discount, I'd want to revisit that assumption, because the compensation I'm targeting is already benchmarked against European remote roles, not the Geneva local market."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in process:
> "I want to be transparent: I'm at a late stage with one other company. I'm not using that as pressure, but it does affect my timeline. If you're in a position to move to a formal offer within the next week, I can hold off on making a decision there until I have something concrete from n8n."

If you don't have another offer but want to signal demand:
> "I've been in active conversations with a few companies, though nothing at offer stage yet. I'd rather close this quickly with n8n if the numbers work, because it's genuinely the role I'm most interested in."

---

## Swiss Work Authorization

If they ask about right to work:
> "I'm a Swiss and EU citizen, so there's no authorization process needed on either side."

---

## Contract Review Notes

- Request the contract at least 5 business days before the acceptance deadline
- Check: IP assignment clause (scope it to work done during employment, not all inventions)
- Non-compete clause: Swiss non-competes are enforceable but limited to 3 years and must be compensated if they restrict significantly; question any clause that would prevent work at competitors in the workflow automation or AI engineering space
- Expense reimbursement: confirm home office allowance is specified in writing

---

## One-liner to Close

> "If we can get to €165K base with the equity and the exercise window I mentioned, I'm prepared to sign."
