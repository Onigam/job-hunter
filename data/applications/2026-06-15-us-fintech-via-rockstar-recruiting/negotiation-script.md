# Negotiation Script — Principal Engineer Fullstack / US-FinTech via Rockstar Recruiting

---

## Market Context

The job listing advertised CHF 225,000-250,000, which is already at the high end of the Swiss principal engineer market. That's the advertised range, not the ceiling. US FinTech companies operating in Switzerland often have US-style compensation structures, which means equity can be a significant part of the package and is worth negotiating separately.

Market data for Principal Engineer / Staff+ in Zurich and Geneva (2025-2026):

- **Base salary**: CHF 200,000 to CHF 250,000 for Principal level with 15+ years experience
- **Equity**: US FinTech companies typically offer stock options or RSUs. If the company is pre-IPO, options matter significantly. If public, RSUs with 4-year vesting are standard.
- **Sign-on bonus**: Common at US tech companies when there's a base gap; ranges CHF 20,000-40,000 depending on seniority
- **Total compensation**: CHF 260,000 to CHF 320,000+ is achievable at this level when equity is included

Key differentiators that support the high end: 17 years of experience, founding engineer background, acquisition validation from a $1B+ company, and current Senior Staff at a North American tech company. This is not a typical candidate profile and the number should reflect that.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for expectations (and Rockstar Recruiting will ask early, as agencies typically do), anchor at the ceiling of the range or above it:

> "Based on the role description and my research into principal-level compensation in Switzerland, I'm looking at something in the CHF 240,000-260,000 base range, depending on the equity structure. If there's a meaningful equity component, I have some flexibility on base. What does the full package look like?"

Why this works: you acknowledge the equity dimension, which invites them to show their hand on total comp. You anchor above the published range without being aggressive, and you signal you've done research.

---

## If the Recruiter Asks for a Number First

Rockstar Recruiting will likely push for your number before sharing the company's range. Give a range, not a single number:

> "I'd want to understand the full comp structure before anchoring, but I'm in the CHF 230,000-260,000 territory for base, with the expectation of equity on top for a role at this level. What can you tell me about how the company typically structures the package?"

This keeps the recruiter as your ally in pushing the company, rather than letting them become an anchor against you.

---

## If They Come In Low (base under CHF 210,000)

Don't counter immediately. Clarify first:

> "I appreciate the offer. Before I respond on the base, can you walk me through the equity component and any bonus structure? I want to understand the total picture."

Once you have the full picture, counter with specifics:

> "Looking at the total package, the base is lower than what I expected for this seniority level. I'm targeting CHF 230,000-240,000 for base given my background and what comparable roles are paying in Switzerland right now. Is there room to move there, or would a sign-on be an option to bridge year one?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

Since this is a US FinTech company, equity structure matters. Ask upfront:

> "What does the equity package look like? I'd want to understand whether these are options or RSUs, the vesting schedule, the current valuation if private, and how refreshes work."

Key terms to negotiate:
- **Vesting schedule**: 4 years with 1-year cliff is standard and acceptable. Push back on a 2-year cliff, which is unusual.
- **Strike price (options)**: If the company is pre-IPO, get the 409A valuation in writing. You need to know the spread between strike price and current fair market value.
- **Anti-dilution / pro-rata rights**: Rare for employees, but worth asking if the company is still in growth stages.
- **Double-trigger acceleration**: Ask if unvested equity accelerates on change of control plus termination. Single-trigger (acceleration on acquisition alone) is harder to get but worth asking.
- **Exercise window**: Standard is 90 days post-termination. Negotiate for a longer window (2-5 years) if possible, especially on ISOs. Some companies offer this without asking.

Script if they're resistant on equity:

> "I understand the equity structure is what it is for the most part. If there's no room there, I'd like to revisit the base or add a sign-on to get the total year-one comp to where I'd expect."

## Geneva vs. Zurich Premium

Magino is in Geneva. If the role is in Zurich (or hybrid with Zurich presence expected), the commute adds real cost and time.

If they expect 2+ days per week in Zurich:

> "I want to flag that I'm based in Geneva, so regular presence in Zurich means a 3-hour round trip on top of the working day. I'm committed to being present for what the role needs, but if the expectation is 3 or more days in Zurich weekly, that changes the economics of the role for me and is worth factoring into the package."

This is a mild but legitimate lever. Remote-first or one-day-in-office makes it moot; don't raise it if the role is fully remote.

---

## Sign-On Bonus

Use as a bridge if base has no room:

> "If the base is fixed at that level, a sign-on could close the gap. I'm thinking CHF 20,000-30,000 to bridge year one. It's a one-time cost for the company versus a recurring delta on base."

Companies are often more willing to approve a one-time sign-on than a base increase because it doesn't set a permanent comp precedent.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer active (or a process nearing an offer):

> "I want to be straightforward: I'm at final stages with another company and expect something by end of the week. I'd genuinely prefer to be here if the numbers work. Is there a way to accelerate the decision on your end so I don't have to make a choice without full information?"

If no competing offer exists, use timeline pressure instead:

> "I've got several processes moving in parallel and I'm trying to make a decision in the next two weeks. Can you give me a sense of how quickly things move on your side?"

---

## Things to Clarify Before Accepting

- Exact comp breakdown: base, bonus target (% and whether discretionary or performance-based), equity value and vesting
- Equity type: ISOs vs. NSOs vs. RSUs, and whether the company is planning an IPO or secondary
- Remote/hybrid expectations: days per week, which office (Zurich?), flexibility
- Health insurance: LaMal is employee-paid in Switzerland; does the company contribute?
- Pension fund (2nd pillar): employer contribution above the legal minimum is worth CHF 3,000-8,000/year in real value
- Probation period: standard in Switzerland is 3 months; anything longer is worth negotiating
- Paid leave: Swiss legal minimum is 4 weeks; tech companies typically offer 5, some offer 6
- Equipment and tooling budget: not a deal-breaker but tells you something about culture
- Role level title: "Principal" vs. "Senior Staff" vs. "Distinguished" has real comp band implications if the company follows a formal engineering ladder
