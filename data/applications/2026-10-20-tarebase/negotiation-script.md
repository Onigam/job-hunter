# Negotiation Script, Tarebase (Founding Engineer)

## What we know

The posting is unusually transparent: EUR 100k to 120k base, 0.75% to 1% equity, pre-seed just closed with a European VC, first employee. Hubs are Munich and London, with remote allowed from GMT-1 to GMT+3. They'll pay for a coworking space and promise a decision within 48 hours of references, so expect a fast close once you reach the final stage. Prepare your numbers before the intro call, not after the on-site.

Because the band is public, you won't move them far past EUR 120k on base. The levers that matter are where you land inside the band, the equity percentage and its terms, the employment structure from Geneva, and a written step-up after the seed round.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

These are working ranges from general market knowledge, not a cited survey. Check them against levels.fyi, Glassdoor CH, Swissdevjobs, and the Index Ventures OptionPlan and Balderton/Atomico European compensation data before you talk numbers.

| Benchmark | Base salary | Equity |
|---|---|---|
| Geneva/Zurich, Staff engineer at a scale-up | CHF 170k to 210k | 0.05% to 0.2% |
| Geneva/Zurich, founding engineer at a funded seed startup | CHF 140k to 175k | 0.5% to 1.5% |
| Munich, senior/founding engineer at pre-seed or seed | EUR 85k to 115k | 0.5% to 1.5% (usually VSOP) |
| London, senior/founding engineer at pre-seed or seed | GBP 90k to 120k | 0.5% to 2% (often EMI options) |
| First employee, pre-seed, two cofounders | often 60% to 80% of market cash | 1% to 2% is common |

At roughly 0.93 to 0.95 CHF per EUR (recheck on the day), the top of their band, EUR 120k, works out to around CHF 112k to 114k. That's well under a Geneva Staff salary and under the Swiss founding-engineer range, which is the core of your case. Their 0.75% to 1% equity is on the low side for employee number one at pre-seed, which is the second part.

## Your numbers

- **Target:** EUR 120k base, 1.25% equity, salary step-up to about EUR 135k on closing the seed round.
- **Opening anchor:** EUR 120k plus 1.5%, with the step-up written in.
- **Walk-away floor:** EUR 110k with at least 1%, standard vesting, and a Swiss-compliant employment setup paid for by them.

Settle the employment structure before agreeing on a number. A Geneva resident working for a German or UK company with no Swiss entity usually means an Employer of Record (Deel, Remote, Oyster) or a contractor setup. With an EOR, Swiss employer social charges (roughly 7% to 15% on top) and the EOR fee are theirs, and the band they posted is your gross. As a contractor, you'd carry AVS/AHV, LPP and unemployment cover yourself, so the rate has to be 25% to 35% above the salaried equivalent. Ask which one they assume, early.

## Opening script

> "Thanks, and I appreciate that you put the band in the posting, it makes this easier. I'd like to be at the top of it, EUR 120k. Living in Geneva, that's about CHF 113k, which is quite a bit under what a founding engineer earns here, so I'm not asking you to go past your band on cash. Where I'd push is equity. As the first employee, owning the stack end to end, and having taken a platform from zero to acquisition before, I'd be looking at 1.5%. I'd also like a written salary review to around EUR 135k when you close the seed."

Then stop talking.

## Cash/equity menu

If they push back, give them choices instead of a single demand:

> "Any of these would work for me:
> A: EUR 120k and 1.25%.
> B: EUR 110k and 1.5%.
> C: EUR 120k and 1%, with base moving to EUR 135k and a 0.25% top-up grant at the seed close."

Option C costs them nothing today and protects you once money comes in.

## If they come in low

**If they offer the bottom of the band (EUR 100k to 105k):**

> "I understand that's the starting point. My concern is that Geneva costs are in Swiss francs and that figure lands around CHF 95k, which is junior pay here. With 17 years of experience and a prior zero-to-acquisition, I think the top of your band is fair. If cash is the constraint, I'd take EUR 110k with more equity rather than the reverse."

**If they apply a location discount:**

> "Your band covers Munich and London, which aren't cheap either, and Geneva is more expensive than both. I'm not asking for a Swiss premium, just the same band as everyone else, paid at the top."

**If they say the equity range is fixed:**

> "That's fine for later hires. The first engineer sets the architecture, the infra and the hiring bar that everyone after inherits. If 1% really is the ceiling, I'd want the protections to be better: a one-year exercise window instead of 90 days, and acceleration if the company is acquired."

**If they need time:**

> "Of course. I'd ask that we agree on the structure, EOR or contract, at the same time, since it changes the real number by 15% or more."

## Equity notes

Find out what the 0.75% to 1% actually is. Munich-based German companies usually use VSOP (virtual shares), which pays out only at an exit and is taxed as income. UK companies often use EMI options, but EMI generally requires UK tax residency, so as a Swiss resident you'd likely receive unapproved options instead, with less favourable tax treatment. Where the cap table lives (German GmbH, UK Ltd, or a holding) decides this, so ask directly.

Questions to ask once there's an offer:

- Is it options, VSOP or real shares, and in which entity?
- What's the fully diluted share count, and what's the strike price or last-round valuation?
- Vesting: four years with a one-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- Leaver terms: good-leaver vs bad-leaver definitions matter a lot in German VSOPs. Make sure resigning after the cliff counts as a good leaver for vested units.
- Exercise window after leaving: push for 1 year or longer rather than 90 days.
- Acceleration: single trigger is a stretch at this stage, but double-trigger (acquisition plus termination) is a fair ask for employee one.
- Dilution: employees don't get anti-dilution protection, and you shouldn't spend capital asking for it. A refresh grant review at the seed and Series A is the realistic version.
- Swiss tax: options granted to a Swiss resident are usually taxed at exercise (or at grant for some plans) as income. Get a short check with a Geneva tax adviser before signing; it's a one-hour conversation.

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

> "I want to be upfront: I'm in late stages with another early-stage company, and their offer is around CHF [X] base with [Y]% equity. Tarebase is my first choice because of the problem and the team, so I'd rather sort this out with you. If you can get to EUR 120k and 1.25%, I'm ready to sign this week."

If there's no real competing offer, don't invent one. Use the Geneva market numbers above instead; they make the same point without the risk.

## Before you sign

- Get the full package in writing: base, equity type and amount, vesting, leaver terms, step-up clause, employment structure and who pays the EOR.
- Confirm the coworking budget applies in Geneva and how meet-up travel is reimbursed.
- Check notice period and non-compete wording, which German contracts sometimes include for founding roles.
