# Negotiation Script: Forward Deployed Engineer (Founding), Undisclosed AI Integrations Startup (via Jobgether)

## What the posting tells you

- Compensation listed: **USD 120k to 200k** base, "depending on experience".
- Equity listed: **0.05% to 0.15%**.
- Remote-first, hiring across Canada, the US and Europe. The body of the posting says the role is based in Switzerland, while the header shows Moses Lake, WA. Clarify this early, because it changes who employs you and how you're paid.
- Jobgether is an intermediary. It shortlists candidates and the startup runs the interviews and makes the offer. Don't discuss numbers in depth with Jobgether; save that for the company.

## Questions to ask before any number comes up

1. What's the company, what stage is it at, how much has it raised, and how many months of runway does it have?
2. How many people are on the team, and what number hire would I be? "Founding" means something very different at hire #3 than at hire #15.
3. How would I be employed in Switzerland: a Swiss entity, an employer of record (Deel, Remote, Oyster), or a contractor arrangement?
4. Is salary paid in CHF or USD? If it's USD, who carries the currency risk?
5. Do Swiss benefits come with it: BVG/LPP pension contributions, accident insurance, 4+ weeks of holiday?
6. What does the equity look like in detail: options or shares, strike price, the last 409A or equivalent valuation, total fully diluted share count?
7. How much travel is expected, and how many customer calls fall in US time zones?

## Converting the band to Swiss reality

At roughly 0.80 USD/CHF (check the rate on the day), the posted band works out to **about CHF 96k to 160k**. That's below Geneva market for someone with 17 years of experience and a founding exit behind them. The bottom of the band is junior money in Geneva.

Working estimates for Geneva and Vaud, CHF, to verify on levels.fyi, Glassdoor and with recruiters before the call:

| Profile | Base salary (CHF) |
|---|---|
| Senior engineer, Swiss startup | 130k to 160k |
| Staff / founding engineer, funded startup | 150k to 185k |
| Senior FDE / solutions engineer at a US AI company, Swiss hire | 160k to 200k + equity |

If you're employed through an EOR, the employer pays roughly 13 to 16% on top in Swiss social charges plus pension. That's their cost, not a reason to lower your base, but expect them to bring it up.

**Your numbers:**
1. **Anchor:** USD 200k, or CHF 165k if paid in CHF, plus 0.3% equity.
2. **Target:** CHF 155 to 160k base, 0.2 to 0.25% equity, a CHF salary, and proper Swiss benefits.
3. **Walk-away:** below CHF 140k base, or equity stuck at 0.05% with no other give. Settle on this number before the call and don't move it during the call.

## When they ask for your expectations first

Try to get them to commit first:

"Before I give a number, could you tell me where in the posted band you see this role for someone with my background? And is that band set in USD for US hires, or is there a separate one for Switzerland?"

If they push:

"Based in Geneva, for a founding role that covers customer delivery, integrations and product input, I'm looking at the top of your posted band, so USD 200k or the CHF equivalent, and equity of around 0.3%. I co-founded and sold a platform, so I know what early hires actually carry, and I'd want the equity to reflect that."

Then stop talking.

## If the offer comes in low

Suppose they offer around USD 150 to 160k and 0.1%:

"Thanks, I'm interested and I'd like to make this work. Two things are lower than I expected. The base, once converted, sits below Geneva market for this level, and the equity is in the range I'd normally see for a later hire, not a founding one. I'd rather move on both, but if the base is tight I'm open to taking more of the gap in equity. Could we look at CHF 160k and 0.25%?"

If they say the band is fixed:

"Understood. If base can't move, can we talk about the other levers? A bigger equity grant, a signing bonus, and a written compensation review at six months, tied to customer deployments shipped, would all help close the gap."

If they bring up the lack of a customer-facing title:

"Fair point. I've been upfront about that. What I bring is the part that's hard to train: I've built an API platform that enterprise clients integrated against, I've worked inside an acquirer's much larger system, and I've moved two teams onto new tooling through live pairing. Getting comfortable with the call cadence takes a few weeks. Integration and architecture judgement takes years."

## Geographic discount pushback

If they say European salaries are lower, or want to pay a "Europe" rate:

"Geneva consistently ranks among the most expensive cities in the world, and Swiss tech salaries reflect that. The posting itself says the role is based in Switzerland. I'd expect the number to be benchmarked against Swiss market rates, not a blended European band. If you hire in Lisbon or Warsaw, that's a different conversation."

If they want to pay in USD:

"I'm happy with USD if it's indexed or reviewed yearly against CHF, since all my costs are in francs. Otherwise I'd prefer the contract to be in CHF at today's equivalent."

## Equity (this is where to push hardest)

0.05 to 0.15% is low for anything called "founding". Founding engineers at seed stage typically get somewhere around 0.5 to 1.5%; by Series A, early senior hires usually land between 0.2 and 0.5%. The posted range suggests either a later-stage company or that "founding" just means "first FDE". Find out which before you negotiate.

Points to check and negotiate:

- **Size:** aim for at least 0.25%. Use percentage of fully diluted shares, not a raw option count.
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask whether the cliff can be shortened to 6 months, or waived if they're terminating you without cause.
- **Exercise window:** push for 5 to 10 years after leaving instead of the default 90 days. This matters more than people think.
- **Early exercise:** ask whether it's allowed. In Switzerland, the tax treatment of options depends on the plan and the canton; options in unlisted companies are often taxed at exercise. Check with a Geneva tax advisor before signing, especially if you're employed through an EOR and the plan is a US one.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (acquisition plus termination). You've been through an acquisition, so you can explain exactly why it matters.
- **Anti-dilution:** employees rarely get anti-dilution protection, so don't spend goodwill asking for it directly. Instead, ask for a **refresh grant** after the next priced round, written into the offer or committed to in writing.
- **Data:** ask for the last preferred share price and the strike price so you can estimate the value of the grant on paper.

Script:

"The equity range in the posting reads more like a Series B grant than a founding one. I've been on the founder side of a cap table and through an exit, so I'm comfortable taking risk, but I want it to match. I'd be looking at 0.25 to 0.3%, four-year vesting with a one-year cliff, double-trigger acceleration, and an extended exercise window. Can we get there?"

## Competing offer template (use only if real)

"I want to be transparent. I'm in late-stage conversations with another company for a Staff-level role in Geneva, and their offer is around CHF [X] base. I'd prefer your role because forward deployed work on agent integrations is closer to what I want to be doing. Could we close the gap on base or equity so the decision is easy?"

Only say this if it's true. Being caught bluffing at a small startup kills trust with the people you'd be working next to every day.

## After the call

- Ask for the full offer in writing: base, currency, employer entity, benefits, equity terms and plan documents.
- Don't accept on the call. "Thanks, let me review this properly and come back to you by [day]" is enough.
- Have the equity plan and any EOR contract read by someone who knows Swiss employment and tax law.
