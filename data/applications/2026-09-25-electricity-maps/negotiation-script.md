# Negotiation Script: Electricity Maps, Senior / Staff Full-stack Engineer

The posting is **Copenhagen, office-first**, and it doesn't give a salary, only "competitive salary + stock options." That puts the main negotiation somewhere unusual: a Danish startup pay band on one side and a Geneva cost base on the other. Every number below is an estimate, so check it against levels.fyi, the Danish IDA salary statistics and anyone you know in Copenhagen startups before the first comp call. Exchange rates are rough (1 CHF ≈ 8 DKK).

## 1. Market picture (2026 estimates)

| Reference point | Base | CHF equivalent | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copenhagen startup, Senior full-stack | DKK 60k to 75k / month | ~CHF 90k to 112k | Pension usually on top |
| Copenhagen startup, Staff full-stack | DKK 75k to 95k / month | ~CHF 112k to 142k | Top of the market for Series A/B |
| Copenhagen scale-up / big tech, Staff | DKK 95k to 120k / month | ~CHF 142k to 180k | Liquid RSUs at big tech |
| Geneva / Lausanne startup, Senior / Staff | CHF 140k to 175k / year | n/a | Your local alternative |
| Zurich scale-up or big tech, Staff | CHF 180k to 250k+ / year | n/a | Your strongest competing reference |

Danish salaries are quoted **per month** and usually **exclude pension**. Employer pension contributions of around 8% to 12% on top are common, so always ask whether a number includes pension or not.

The honest summary: a Copenhagen startup will almost certainly pay less gross than a Swiss job at the same level. Two things narrow the gap, the Danish researcher tax scheme (section 4) and the six weeks of vacation. Neither closes it completely, so decide your walk-away before the call.

**Your targets (Staff level, relocating to Copenhagen)**
- Walk-away: DKK 85k / month base plus pension
- Target: DKK 95k to 100k / month base plus pension
- Opening anchor: DKK 105k / month base plus pension, a meaningful option grant and relocation support

If they offer the Senior level instead of Staff, push on the level before the money (section 3). Their Senior band will probably cap below your walk-away.

## 2. Opening the conversation

If they ask for expectations first:

> "For a Staff-level role where I'd be relocating from Geneva, I'm looking at around DKK 105k a month base plus pension, and an option grant that reflects coming in early. I'm flexible on how cash and equity split, and I'd like to see the full package before we pin anything down."

If they push for one number, repeat 105k and don't give a range; they'll just take the bottom of it.

Keep the justification ready, in your own words: 17 years shipping production software, five years as cofounder building a platform from zero to 50+ enterprise clients and an acquisition by a $1B+ company, hiring and setting up the first engineering team, and Staff-level cross-team architecture since then. The posting asks for "founder mentality," and you've actually done it.

## 3. Level before salary

If the conversation drifts toward Senior:

> "I'd like to make sure we agree on level first. The Staff scope you described, setting the bar on what good looks like and owning systems end to end with leadership, is what I did as a cofounder for five years and as a Senior Staff Engineer since. I'd rather talk about how you define Staff here than negotiate inside the Senior band."

## 4. Tax and relocation (the Danish specifics)

**Researcher / key-employee tax scheme (forskerordningen).** Foreign hires above a monthly salary threshold can be taxed at a flat rate of roughly 32.8% all-in (27% plus the 8% labour market contribution) for up to seven years, rather than Denmark's progressive rates, which top out above 50%. The threshold was around DKK 75k / month in 2025; check the current figure with SKAT. At your target salary this is worth a lot, so:

> "I'm assuming the salary will clear the forskerordning threshold. Can we confirm that in the offer, and that you'll handle the paperwork?"

If their number is near the threshold, that's your best argument for moving it up: a small raise that crosses the line changes your net pay far more than it costs them.

**Relocation package.** Ask for:
- A lump sum or paid costs for the move from Geneva
- Temporary housing for the first one to two months (Copenhagen rentals are tight)
- Help with CPR registration, bank account and a tax adviser for the first year

> "Moving from Geneva is a real cost, so I'd like relocation covered separately from the salary: the move itself, a month or two of temporary housing, and a tax adviser for the first filing."

## 5. Geographic pushback

This time the pressure runs the other way: they may argue that Danish salaries are simply lower than Swiss ones. Don't pretend otherwise, and reframe it:

> "I know Copenhagen bands are lower than Swiss ones, and I'm not asking you to match Zurich. What I'm asking is for a Staff offer at the top of the Copenhagen market, because that's the level I'd be working at and I'm giving up a Swiss salary to do it."

If they suggest you work remotely from Geneva at a Danish salary, be careful. A Danish employer hiring someone resident in Switzerland raises social security, payroll and permanent-establishment questions, and a Danish-level salary paid against Geneva living costs is a big pay cut. If remote is on the table, ask for Swiss-market pay through an employer of record, or keep the relocation route.

## 6. Equity (Danish VC-backed startup)

Ask all of these in writing:

- **Instrument.** Warrants or options? Danish startups usually grant warrants. Ask whether the grant qualifies under **section 7P of the Danish Tax Assessment Act**, which lets part of the grant be taxed as capital gains at sale rather than as salary. There's a cap relative to annual salary, so check what it is.
- **Size.** Number of warrants and the fully diluted share count, so you can work out your percentage. A "value" quoted without strike price and valuation means nothing.
- **Strike price** and the valuation it's based on. Check when the last round was; a strike set before an upcoming round is worth more.
- **Vesting.** Usually 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff and double-trigger acceleration on change of control.
- **Good leaver / bad leaver** terms. Danish warrant agreements often have strict clauses that forfeit vested warrants if you resign. Read this carefully, because it matters more than the headline number.
- **Exercise window** after leaving, and whether exercise is only allowed at an exit.
- **Refresh grants** and whether early employees have had any secondary liquidity.

For a Staff hire at a startup of roughly this size, around 0.1% to 0.5% fully diluted is a reasonable range to probe. It depends heavily on the stage and when you join, so ask them what earlier engineering hires received.

## 7. If the offer comes in low

> "Thanks, I appreciate it and I want to make this work. The base is below where I'd expected for Staff, especially given the move. If you can get to DKK 98k a month plus pension, with relocation covered, I'm ready to sign."

If they say the band is fixed:

> "Understood. If base is capped, can we look at a larger warrant grant, a signing bonus to cover the move in year one, or a guaranteed review at twelve months?"

Don't accept on the call. "Let me go through the full package and come back to you by Friday" is always fine.

## 8. Competing offer template

Only use this if it's true or genuinely in progress:

> "I want to be transparent: I'm also in late-stage conversations for a Staff role in Switzerland at a higher base. Electricity Maps is my first choice because of the product and the mission, and I'd rather be here. Can we get closer on base or equity so the decision is easier?"

## 9. Non-salary items worth asking for

- A clear written definition of the Staff scope and how promotion or level review works
- A few remote weeks a year (useful for family visits to Geneva)
- A learning and conference budget, especially for energy-data or grid events
- Start date flexibility to handle the move properly
