# Negotiation Script, Lovable (Staff / Principal Software Engineer, Platform)

## Context that shapes this negotiation

Lovable is a Stockholm company and the role is on-site five days a week, so this is a relocation offer paid in SEK under Swedish employment terms, not a Geneva job. The posting gives no salary figures. Lovable is one of the best-funded AI companies in Europe. Public reporting puts its Series A at about $200M in mid-2025 at a ~$1.8B valuation, followed by a larger round at a much higher valuation later that year. Check the latest numbers before the call, since they change fast. That means they can pay at the top of the Stockholm market, and their equity is worth something real, though at a high strike price.

The main tension: Geneva cash compensation is higher than Stockholm's, while Swedish taxes are higher and the cost of living is lower. Use your Geneva market rate as the anchor for what you're giving up, not as a demand that they pay Geneva rates.

Questions to ask early, before any numbers:
- "Is this hired at Staff or Principal level, and what does the band look like for each?"
- "What form does the equity take: Swedish qualified employee stock options, regular options, or something else? What's the strike price and the latest preferred price?"
- "What relocation support do you offer?"

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

These are working ranges from general market knowledge, not a cited survey. Check them against levels.fyi (Stockholm and Zurich), Glassdoor, Swissdevjobs salary data and Lovable's own reported offers before you talk numbers.

| Benchmark | Base salary | Equity / notes |
|---|---|---|
| Geneva/Zurich, Staff engineer at a scale-up | CHF 170k to 210k | RSUs/options, 0.05% to 0.2% at scale-ups |
| Zurich, Staff/Principal at big tech (Google etc.) | CHF 200k to 260k base | plus CHF 100k+ a year in RSUs |
| Stockholm, Staff engineer at a top-tier scale-up | SEK 95k to 125k/month (~SEK 1.15M to 1.5M/yr) | options, usually 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff |
| Stockholm, Principal at a hot, well-funded AI company | SEK 120k to 160k/month | larger option grant, plus refresh grants |

Rough reality check: SEK 130k/month comes to about SEK 1.56M a year, which is roughly CHF 120k to 130k gross at current exchange rates (recheck the rate on the day). Swedish income tax at that level is meaningfully higher than in Geneva, but housing, childcare and health insurance cost less. Do the net, after-housing comparison before the call, not the gross one.

## Your numbers

- **Target:** SEK 135k/month base, an equity grant worth at least SEK 1.5M/yr at the latest preferred price, plus relocation support.
- **Opening anchor:** SEK 150k/month at Principal level, with an equity grant to match.
- **Walk-away floor:** SEK 115k/month with meaningful equity. Below that, relocating from Geneva costs you money in real terms unless the equity is exceptional.

Level matters more than base. Push to be hired at Principal, or at Staff with a written review toward Principal after 6 to 12 months. The band jump is worth more than a few thousand SEK a month.

## Opening script

> "Thanks for this. To be straight about where I'm coming from: I'm in Geneva, and Staff-level cash compensation here runs from about CHF 170k to 210k at scale-ups, higher at big tech. I'm not asking you to pay Geneva rates in Stockholm. I know the market's different and I've done the after-tax, after-rent comparison. But moving my life for a five-day on-site role has to make sense, and given 17 years in, a platform taken from zero to acquisition and the scope of this team, I'm looking at around SEK 150k a month at Principal level, with equity to match."

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low

**If they offer a standard Stockholm Staff number (around SEK 100k/month):**

> "I can see how that fits your Stockholm Staff band. The problem for me is that it's a real step down from what I'd earn staying put, and I'd be relocating to take it. Is there room to bring this in at Principal, or to close the gap with a larger option grant and a sign-on bonus that covers the first year's move?"

**If base is fixed by the band:**

> "Understood. If the base is locked, let's look at the other levers: the level, the size of the option grant, a sign-on bonus, and relocation. I'd rather get the structure right than argue over a small amount on base."

**If they need time:**

> "Of course. Could you send the equity details in writing with the offer: number of options, strike price, latest preferred price and fully diluted share count? I can't value it without those."

## Equity negotiation

- **Get the numbers, not the percentage.** Ask for the number of options, the strike price, the latest preferred price and the fully diluted share count. At a multi-billion valuation, a percentage sounds small and a SEK value is the useful figure.
- **Option type matters a lot in Sweden.** Qualified employee stock options (kvalificerade personaloptioner) can be taxed as capital gains rather than salary when exercised, but the company has to meet size limits on employees and turnover. Lovable may already be over them. Ask directly which scheme applies, and if it's standard options or warrants, ask whether they'll help with the tax treatment.
- **Vesting and cliff.** Four years with a one-year cliff is standard. Ask whether they offer a shorter cliff for senior hires, or monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Exercise window.** Ask how long you'd have to exercise after leaving. Ninety days is common and often punishing. Push for an extended window, since a high strike price makes this a large cash outlay.
- **Refresh grants.** Ask how refreshers work. At a company growing this fast, the first grant can feel small within two years.
- **Liquidity.** Ask whether there have been secondary sales or tender offers for employees, and whether more are planned.
- **Dilution.** Anti-dilution protection is for investors, not employees, so don't ask for it. Instead, ask how previous rounds affected employee grants and whether top-ups were given.

## Relocation and Geneva-to-Stockholm pushback

> "I'm not asking for a Geneva premium. What I am asking for is that the move itself doesn't cost me: flights, temporary housing for the first couple of months, shipping and help with the Swedish paperwork. And Stockholm's rental market is hard to get into, so housing support for the first few months would make a real difference."

Things to ask for specifically: a relocation lump sum or covered costs, 1 to 3 months of temporary housing, help with the personnummer and registration, a tax adviser for the first year (especially for the Swedish expert tax relief, *expertskatt*, which can exempt 25% of income from tax for several years if you qualify, so ask whether they'll file the application), and a clawback clause that's prorated rather than all-or-nothing.

## Competing offer template

Use only if it's true, or with a clearly hypothetical framing. Never invent an offer.

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations with a couple of other companies, including one in Switzerland where the cash is noticeably higher and I wouldn't need to move. Lovable is the one I most want to work for, because the sandbox and runtime problem is the most interesting one on the table. If we can get to [target base / level / equity], I'm ready to sign this week."

## Final checklist before signing

- Level (Staff vs. Principal) written in the offer
- Base in SEK/month, plus pension contributions (ITP-style occupational pension is standard in Sweden, so confirm the percentage)
- Option count, strike, latest preferred price, scheme type, vesting, cliff, exercise window
- Sign-on and relocation amounts, plus clawback terms
- Start date that leaves time to handle the Geneva lease notice and move
- Notice period under Swedish rules
