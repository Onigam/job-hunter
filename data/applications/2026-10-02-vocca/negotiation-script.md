# Negotiation Script: Vocca, Senior Product Engineer (TypeScript), Platform

## Context that shapes the numbers

Vocca is a Paris healthcare voice AI startup founded in 2024. It raised a $5.5M seed in September 2025 led by Speedinvest and firstminute capital, with Kima Ventures, FJ Labs, Sequoia Scout and angels including the founders of Alan, Datadog, Deel and Mistral. The posting says 3,500+ providers already use the product. The role sits in Paris (rue d'Alexandrie, Sentier), hybrid three days a week on site.

**The posting publishes the band: €70k to €90k base, plus BSPCE.** This is the most important fact in this file. It's a published senior IC band at a seed company in Paris, and it's well below where your Swiss Staff-level numbers sit. Be realistic about what moves:

- **Base:** the top of the band is reachable. Going above it usually means a different title or level, not a better pitch.
- **Equity (BSPCE):** this is where there's the most room, especially if the scope is lead-level (the posting asks you to define testing, review and monitoring standards and to mentor engineers).
- **Title and scope:** "Senior Product Engineer" with a mandate to set team processes is a lead role in practice. Asking for Staff or Lead Engineer is the cleanest route to a number above €90k.
- **Location:** three days a week in Paris from Geneva is the real question. Settle it before you talk money, because it changes everything below.

Before getting deep into the process, decide honestly whether a Paris senior IC package works for you. If your floor is above €95k all-in, say so in the Talent call so nobody wastes four rounds.

**Target:** €90k base plus ~0.3% to 0.4% BSPCE, with a written agreement on the on-site pattern and a travel budget if you stay in Geneva.
**Opening anchor:** €100k base framed as a Lead or Staff title, plus 0.5% BSPCE.
**Walk-away floor:** €85k base with at least ~0.25% BSPCE on good terms, and a workable location arrangement. Below that, the gap to Geneva is too large to justify.

## Settle location first

Geneva to Paris is a little over three hours by TGV Lyria. Three days a week is two nights in Paris every week, which costs roughly €150 to 250 a week in trains alone, plus somewhere to sleep. Options, in your order of preference:

1. Two on-site days a week (Tuesday and Wednesday, one night in Paris) with a monthly travel budget.
2. One full on-site week a month plus remote otherwise, during the first year while the scenario engine is being built.
3. Relocate to Paris with a relocation package (around €5k to 10k lump sum or reimbursed costs).

**Script:**
> "Before we talk numbers, I'd like to sort out the on-site part, since I'm in Geneva. Being in the room matters for this role, especially in the first months, so I'm not asking for full remote. What I'd propose is two set days a week in Paris, with travel covered. Does that work for the team?"

**Cross-border admin (check with an advisor before signing).** If you stay resident in Switzerland and work partly from home, social security and tax are not straightforward. Under the EU/Switzerland rules, working 25% or more in your country of residence normally pulls you into that country's social security, though the multilateral framework agreement on cross-border telework (in force since July 2023, signed by both France and Switzerland) lets teleworkers under 50% stay on the employer's system if both sides request it. Tax goes through the France/Switzerland treaty and Geneva's specific cross-border rules. The favourable BSPCE tax regime is also tied to French tax residence. Ask Vocca if they've employed someone living in Switzerland before; if not, budget time for this.

## Opening the money conversation

Let them name a number first if you can. The band is public, so they probably will.

> "I've seen the €70k to €90k range in the posting. Given the scope, owning the scenario engine and setting the testing, review and monitoring standards for the team, I'd be looking at the top of that range on base, and I'd want the equity to reflect a lead-level role. Can you tell me how BSPCE grants are sized for this position?"

## If they come in low (e.g. €78k to 82k)

> "Thanks, I appreciate the offer. I'm keen on the role, and the gap is real. I've co-founded and scaled a platform to acquisition and worked at Senior Staff level for five years since, and the posting asks for team processes and mentoring on top of the build. For that scope, I'd need €90k base. If base is fixed by the band, I'd be open to a larger BSPCE grant or a Lead title with a review at six months."

If they hold on base, trade in this order:
1. More BSPCE (ask for 0.1% to 0.15% more per €5k of base you give up).
2. A written comp review at six months, tied to the Month 3 milestone in the posting (scenario engine v1 in production, standard clinics onboarding without developer tickets).
3. Travel budget or relocation package.
4. A Lead or Staff title.

## If you want to push above the band

> "I know €90k is the top of the published band. The posting describes defining engineering standards, mentoring and owning the platform architecture, which in most Paris scale-ups would be a Staff or Lead role at €95k to 110k. Would you consider pitching this at that level?"

Don't push hard on this. A seed company with a published band will often say no on base and yes on equity.

## Equity (BSPCE): what to ask

- **Percentage, not number of options.** "What percentage of fully diluted capital does this grant represent, and what's the current fully diluted share count?"
- **Strike price.** It's usually set at the last round's price per share, so ask what it is and when the next round is expected.
- **Vesting.** Four years with a one-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Exercise window after leaving.** French BSPCE plans often give 90 days or less, which can make vested options worthless if you can't afford to exercise. Ask for a longer window or confirm what the plan allows.
- **Acceleration.** Ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination) on unvested BSPCE. Seed companies don't always agree, but it's a reasonable ask.
- **Refresh grants.** "Is there a refresh policy after a Series A, or tied to performance?"
- **Anti-dilution.** Employees rarely get anti-dilution protection on BSPCE. Don't spend capital on it; push for a bigger grant and a refresh instead.
- **Tax residence.** If you stay resident in Switzerland, the French BSPCE tax regime may not apply the way they describe it. Get this checked.

## Geographic pushback

This one runs the other way from usual. Vocca won't apply a Geneva discount; the problem is that the Paris band sits well under your local market.

> "I'm not expecting Geneva pay for a Paris role. What I'm doing is taking a meaningful step down from the Swiss market to join, and travelling every week to make it work. That's why the equity and the travel arrangement matter more to me than an extra few thousand on base."

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

> "I want to be transparent: I'm also in late-stage conversations with another company, at a Staff level with a higher base. Vocca is my preference because of the problem, the scenario engine is exactly the work I want to be doing, but I need the package to be closer. If you can get to €90k with 0.4% and the two-day on-site arrangement, I'm ready to sign this week."

If you don't have a competing offer, don't invent one. Use the Geneva market as your reference point instead; it's real and they can check it.

## Non-salary items worth asking for

- Relocation support if you move
- Learning budget (conferences such as dotJS or Node Congress)
- Equipment of your choice
- Clarity on the 90-day milestones in the posting and who signs off on them
- Claude Max is already in the posting, so no need to ask

## Before you sign

- Confirm the on-site pattern in writing, in the contract or an annex
- BSPCE plan terms in writing: percentage, strike price, vesting, cliff, exercise window, acceleration
- Who's the employing entity, and how they'll handle a Swiss-resident employee (social security, payroll, tax withholding)
- Notice period (French cadres usually have three months) and trial period length (often four months, renewable once, so ask for no renewal)
- Six-month comp review date, if you traded for one
