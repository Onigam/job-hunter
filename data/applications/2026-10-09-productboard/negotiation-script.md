# Negotiation Script: Productboard, Staff Full-stack Product Engineer (Prague/Brno/Remote EU)

## Context first

Productboard is a late-stage, VC-backed product management SaaS company headquartered in San Francisco with its engineering center in Prague and Brno. Its last big round was a Series D in 2022 at a valuation reported around $1.7B. The company has had layoffs since then, so expect cost discipline, and check the current headcount and funding news before the first call.

The LinkedIn posting publishes a band: **CZK 2,295,000 to 3,305,000 gross per year**, and it says most hires at this level (L4) land in the lower-to-middle part of it. Stock options are included. Relocation to Prague comes with a one-time bonus of USD 6k to 13k.

Rough conversions (October 2026, check the live rate before any call):

| | Bottom | Midpoint | Top |
|---|---|---|---|
| CZK | 2.30M | 2.80M | 3.31M |
| EUR (~25 CZK) | ~92k | ~112k | ~132k |
| CHF (~26.5 CZK) | ~87k | ~106k | ~125k |

That band is built for the Czech and EU market. Even the top sits below what a Staff engineer earns in Geneva, so the real question isn't the number inside the band. It's whether Productboard will price a Swiss-based hire differently.

## Blocker to settle first: employment from Switzerland

The posting says remote within the EU, and candidates need the existing right to work in the EU. Switzerland isn't in the EU. Even with an EU passport, working from Geneva means Swiss social security and tax rules apply, so Productboard would need a Swiss payroll setup or an employer of record (EOR). Some companies won't do that for one hire.

Ask this in the first recruiter call, before investing in interviews:

"I'm based in Geneva, which is outside the EU. Do you already employ people in Switzerland, either directly or through an employer of record? If so, is compensation set on the Czech band or on a Swiss benchmark?"

Possible answers and what they mean:

1. **Swiss employees exist, paid on a Swiss band.** Best case. Negotiate against the Swiss anchors below.
2. **EOR is possible, but on the Czech band.** Push for the top of the band plus a location adjustment (scripts below). Remember EOR costs the employer roughly 10 to 20% on top of salary, which reduces their room.
3. **EU residence only.** Relocating to Prague or Brno is the only route. Then the CZK band is fair for the local cost of living, and the negotiation is about level, top-of-band and the relocation package. Decide that before going further.

## Market anchors (Switzerland, 2026, CHF gross per year)

| Profile | Typical base range |
|---|---|
| Senior full stack engineer, Geneva or Lausanne | 120k to 150k |
| Staff engineer, Swiss scale-up or established product company | 150k to 185k |
| Staff engineer with AI/LLM scope, Zurich | 160k to 200k |
| Staff+ at big tech in Zurich (reference only) | 220k+ total comp |
| Staff engineer, remote for an EU company paying EU rates | 110k to 140k |

These are approximate bands from Swiss salary surveys and recent postings. Check them against current data (levels.fyi Zurich, Glassdoor Switzerland, swissdevjobs.ch salary pages) before the first call.

**Target if paid as a Swiss hire:** CHF 165k to 180k base, plus options.
**Target if held to the Czech band:** the top, CZK 3.3M (about CHF 125k), with a larger option grant to make up some of the gap.
**Walk-away floor:** around CHF 135k base for a remote role from Geneva. Below that, compared with local Staff roles, the gap is too wide for the equity of a late-stage private company to close.

## Opening anchor

If they ask for expectations before the location question is settled, don't give a CZK number. Anchor on the Swiss market and the scope:

"For a Staff role that owns the agent architecture across the codebase, and given I'm based in Geneva, I'm looking at around CHF 170k to 180k base, plus options. That reflects 17 years of experience, a platform I built from zero through an acquisition, and Staff-level pay in Switzerland. I'm happy to talk about how base and equity are split."

Say the number, then stop talking.

## If they come back with the Czech band

"I understand the band is built for Prague and Brno, and it makes sense there. My cost base is Geneva, though, which is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and Staff roles here start well above your top of range. Is there a location adjustment for Swiss-based hires? If not, I'd need to be at the top of the band, CZK 3.3M, with a larger option grant to get close."

If they stick to the lower-to-middle part of the band ("most L4 hires land there"):

"The posting says most hires land lower in the range, and I get why. I'd ask you to look at what you've seen in the interviews. You're asking for a former founder who's built agentic systems, and I've done both the founding and the platform scaling through an acquisition. That's the profile at the top of the band, not the middle."

And on level:

"Is there a level above L4 for this track? If my interviews point that way, I'd like to be considered for it, because that's where my scope at Benevity was."

## Pushing back on a geographic discount

Productboard's band is already a geographic choice, Czech rates rather than US ones, so they'll be used to the conversation. The argument runs in reverse here: Geneva costs more than Prague, not less.

"I'm not asking for San Francisco pay. I'm asking not to be paid Prague rates while living in Geneva. If a Swiss band isn't possible, let's get to the top of the existing one and close part of the gap with equity."

## Equity (late-stage private company)

Productboard is past the stage where anti-dilution or big percentages matter. The questions are about what the options are actually worth and when you could sell them.

1. **Number of options and the current strike price**, plus the latest 409A or fair market value and the last preferred share price. The difference between strike and preferred is your paper upside.
2. **Percentage of fully diluted shares.** At this stage it'll be small (well under 0.1%), but ask anyway so you can compare grants.
3. **Vesting and cliff:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
4. **Exercise window after leaving.** A 90-day window can force you to pay a lot of cash and tax to keep vested options. Ask for an extended window (5 to 10 years); some late-stage companies offer it.
5. **Liquidity:** have there been tender offers or secondaries for employees, and is one planned? Without liquidity, late-stage options are a long bet.
6. **Refresh grants:** is there an annual or performance-based refresh policy?
7. **Swiss tax treatment:** US-style options held by a Swiss resident are generally taxed on exercise as income in Geneva. Get advice before exercising, and ask whether an EOR setup changes how the plan works for you.
8. **Acceleration:** ask whether there's double-trigger acceleration on a change of control. Having been on the founder side of the Alaya acquisition, you can ask this credibly.

Script:

"Could you share the number of options, the strike price, the latest fair market value and preferred price, and the exercise window after leaving? I'd also like to know whether there have been employee tender offers. I've been through an acquisition from the founder side, so I want to understand how the plan behaves in a liquidity event."

## Competing offer template

Use only if it's true. Never invent an offer.

"I want to be transparent. I'm in late-stage conversations with a company in Switzerland and expect an offer around CHF [X] base. Productboard is high on my list because of [specific reason, for example the agent architecture scope or Spark]. If we can get closer to [target], I can decide quickly."

If there's no competing offer, use market data instead:

"Based on what I'm seeing for Staff roles in Switzerland, base salaries sit around CHF 150k to 185k, and higher with AI scope. I'd like us to land as close to that as your structure allows."

## Other things to put on the table

The posting lists 5 weeks of vacation, a home office stipend, a learning budget, and hardware. Also ask about:

1. Travel budget for regular visits to Prague or Brno (flights from Geneva are short, but it adds up).
2. Pension: through an EOR in Switzerland, confirm the LPP/BVG contribution and whether it's above the legal minimum.
3. A salary review after 12 months, written into the contract.
4. Conference budget, especially for AI engineering events.
5. Notice period and start date, aligned with whatever notice is owed today.

## Before signing

Get the following in writing: employing entity (direct or EOR), currency and gross base, level (L4 or above), option count with strike price, vesting and exercise window, travel support, review date, and start date.
