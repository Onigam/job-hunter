# Negotiation Script: Mistral, Engineering Team Lead, Sandbox (Switzerland)

No salary is disclosed in the posting. The ranges below are estimates from public Zurich comp data for engineering managers at large tech and AI companies; check levels.fyi and any recent Mistral offers you can find before the first comp call.

## 1. Market picture (Switzerland, 2026 estimates)

| Reference point | Base (CHF) | Total comp notes |
|---|---|---|
| Swiss tech scale-up, Eng Team Lead | 170k to 210k | Small equity, bonus rare |
| Mistral Switzerland, Eng Team Lead (estimate) | 200k to 250k | Stock options on top, value depends on the next round |
| Big tech Zurich, EM / Staff level (Google, Meta, etc.) | 220k to 280k | Liquid RSUs push total well past 350k |
| US AI labs, Zurich office | 250k+ | Large equity grants |

Mistral generally pays below US labs on cash and makes it up with options at a fast-rising valuation. Treat the options as upside, not as salary.

**Your targets**
- Walk-away base: CHF 200k
- Target base: CHF 235k to 245k
- Opening anchor: CHF 255k base plus a meaningful option grant

## 2. Opening the conversation

If they ask for expectations first:

> "For a team lead role at this level in Switzerland, I'm looking at a base around CHF 255k, plus equity that reflects leading a core infrastructure team. I'm flexible on how that splits between cash and options, and I'd like to see the full picture before we settle anything."

If they push for a single number: repeat 255k, don't volunteer a range. A range just hands them the bottom of it.

Justification to have ready, in your own words: 17 years in backend and platform systems, five years running engineering as cofounder through to an acquisition by a $1B+ company, hiring and managing the first engineers, and recent Staff-level cross-team work at Benevity. You're being hired as a manager who can also architect, which is two roles' worth of scope.

## 3. If the offer comes in low

> "Thanks, I appreciate it and I'm keen to make this work. The base is below where I'd expected for a team lead in Switzerland, especially against what engineering managers get in Zurich. If you can get the base to CHF 240k, I'm ready to move forward."

If they say the band is capped:

> "Understood. If base is fixed, can we look at the option grant, a signing bonus to cover the gap in year one, or a guaranteed comp review at twelve months tied to the team's delivery?"

Don't accept on the call. "Let me look at the full package and come back to you by Thursday" is always fine.

## 4. Equity questions to ask (private company)

Ask all of these in writing:

- What instrument is it for a Swiss employee? French BSPCE don't apply outside France, so find out whether it's options on the French parent or something else, and how Swiss tax treats it (taxation at grant versus exercise matters a lot in Switzerland).
- Number of options and the total fully diluted share count, so you can work out your percentage. A grant quoted only in euros "value" is meaningless without the strike price and the valuation it's based on.
- Strike price and which valuation it's set at. Mistral's valuation has jumped round after round, so a strike set at an old valuation is worth much more than one at the latest.
- Vesting: standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Ask about monthly vesting after the cliff and any acceleration on change of control (double-trigger is the reasonable ask).
- Post-departure exercise window. 90 days is common and painful; ask for longer.
- Refresh grants: is there a policy, and when do they happen?
- Secondary sales: has the company allowed employee liquidity in past rounds?

## 5. Location pushback

If they suggest a lower number because you're in Geneva rather than Zurich, or because the role could be based in Paris:

> "Geneva's cost of living is on par with Zurich, and the role's listed in Switzerland, so I'd expect Swiss compensation. I'm happy to be in the Zurich office on a regular schedule if that's useful for the team."

If they push to benchmark against Paris salaries, hold the line: Swiss social charges, housing and health insurance make a Paris-level salary a real pay cut, and Swiss big tech is the actual competition for your time.

## 6. Competing offer template

Use only if you actually have another process going; never invent an offer.

> "I want to be transparent: I'm also in late stages with another company, and their offer is around CHF [X] base with [equity summary]. Mistral is where I'd rather be, because the sandbox problem is the more interesting one. If you can get closer to [target], I'll commit quickly."

If nothing is concrete yet:

> "I'm talking to a couple of other teams, but I'd prefer to decide on this one first. What flexibility is there on the base?"

## 7. Other things worth negotiating

- Start date: take 2 to 4 weeks between roles if you can.
- Learning budget, specifically for Go and systems work, since it's a stated ramp-up area. Framing it as part of the plan to close the gap shows self-awareness rather than weakness.
- Title and scope in writing: team size today, headcount you're allowed to hire this year, and who you report to.
- Remote flexibility from Geneva, with the travel budget to Zurich and Paris covered.
