# Negotiation Script — Storylane / Platform Lead AI Engineer

---

## Context and Positioning

Storylane is a YC S21 company at $10M ARR with 55+ employees. The role is Platform Lead (senior IC + technical leadership), listed as India/Remote. This creates an important tension: the company may be benchmarking compensation against India rates, while Magino's market is Geneva/Switzerland.

The role is senior enough that it demands US-or-European-calibrated compensation, regardless of geographic listing. Storylane is a US-incorporated company backed by YC; their top leadership likely earns US market rates. A Platform Lead responsible for core infrastructure architecture at this stage is not an India-outsourced role, it's a strategic hire.

---

## Market Rate Anchors

**Switzerland (Geneva market, Staff/Principal Platform Engineer, AI focus):**
- Base: CHF 180,000 - CHF 240,000
- Total comp with equity: CHF 220,000 - CHF 300,000+
- Benchmark: Benevity (Senior Staff, Geneva) puts Magino's current/recent base comfortably in this range

**US market (equivalent seniority, remote):**
- Base: $200,000 - $260,000 USD
- Total comp: $250,000 - $350,000+ depending on equity

**India market (comparison, not your anchor):**
- Senior/Staff engineer: INR 50-80 lacs (~$60,000 - $96,000 USD)
- This is not your frame of reference. Don't let them anchor you here.

---

## Opening Anchor

When asked for salary expectations, do not give a range first. Use this:

> "I'd want to understand your full comp structure before anchoring on a number. Can you walk me through what the package looks like: base, equity, and any performance components? I can tell you whether that works for my situation."

If they push: "My current expectations are benchmarked against Switzerland/European Staff Engineer market rates, which puts me in the CHF 200k+ range for base, with equity on top. If you're calibrating against a different geography, I'd want to understand how you think about global pay equity before we go further."

---

## Counter-Offer Script (If They Come In Low)

**If the offer is below CHF 150k / $160k USD base:**

> "I appreciate the offer. I want to be straightforward: the number is below what I'd need to make this work. For a Platform Lead role at this scope and seniority, my floor for base compensation is [CHF 190k / $200k USD]. I'm flexible on structure, but I need to get to that level in total cash before I can factor in equity. Can we talk about how to close that gap?"

**Don't apologize for the ask. Don't say "I know that might be a lot." Frame it as a business conversation.**

---

## Equity Negotiation (Startup Stage)

Storylane is likely pre-Series B. This means options, probably ISOs if US-incorporated, at an early strike price.

**Key questions to ask:**

1. "What's the current preferred share price and last 409A valuation?" This tells you the real dilution picture.
2. "What percentage of the company does this grant represent on a fully diluted basis?" Get the denominator, not just the share count.
3. "What's the vesting schedule and cliff?" Standard is 4-year vest, 1-year cliff. Push back on anything worse.
4. "Is there any accelerated vesting provision on acquisition?" At this stage and given Storylane's growth trajectory, an acquisition is a plausible exit. Double-trigger acceleration (company acquired AND you're let go) is worth asking for.
5. "What's the exercise window after leaving?" 3 months is standard but brutal. Ask for extended post-termination exercise (ETPE), ideally 5-10 years.

**Framing for equity ask:**

> "I want to be a genuine stakeholder in what you're building. The equity matters to me as much as the cash component, so I want to understand the full picture before I react to any number. Walk me through the cap table structure and what this grant looks like on a diluted basis."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they try to adjust compensation for Geneva location:

> "I understand there are geographic pay structures at some companies, but the role you're describing is a strategic hire, not a cost-center role. The infrastructure decisions I'll be making affect your whole platform and every customer on it. I'd expect compensation to reflect that scope, not my postal code. The cost of getting this architecture wrong for a year dwarfs any geographic adjustment on my salary."

---

## Competing Offer Template

Even if no competing offer is in hand, this framing helps:

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in conversations with a few other companies at a similar stage and scope. My timeline for making a decision is [2-3 weeks]. I'm genuinely interested in what Storylane is building, which is why I'm having this conversation directly, but I need to make sure the comp package is competitive enough for me to move forward."

---

## Additional Notes

- The India/Remote posting is a flag. If they initially propose India-benchmarked comp, that's a serious dealbreaker, address it directly and early rather than waiting until an offer.
- Storylane's $10M ARR and 4,000+ enterprise customers is a real signal of traction, which makes their equity meaningful. Don't undersell the equity component in negotiations.
- If the role is truly remote and async-first, Geneva is a non-issue for delivery; make sure that's clear early in the process.
- Ask for the offer in writing, including the full equity details, before making any decisions. Never negotiate verbally on equity.
