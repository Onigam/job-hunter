# Negotiation Script: Founding Engineer, Clera

Context going in: this is a pre-seed startup (roughly $3M raised) with a three-person founding team, based in San Francisco, already past $1M ARR and representing 80,000-plus professionals. That traction matters, this isn't a pure idea-stage founding role where cash and equity both get squeezed to nothing, but it's still early enough that a Swiss Staff-level cash package isn't realistic on base alone. The real negotiation is over the equity grant and whether the arrangement is fully in-person in SF, hybrid, or remote from Geneva, since each of those changes the cash number materially.

## Market context

- Founding engineer roles at US pre-seed to seed startups with real traction (post-revenue, not just a deck) typically land base salary in the $140,000-175,000 range in SF, with equity in the 0.5%-2% range depending on how early the hire is and how much the founders are diluting to hit that number.
- If the arrangement is remote from Switzerland rather than relocating to SF, companies at this stage often try to apply a cost-of-living discount to cash, sometimes significant. That discount is more defensible for a first hire at idea stage than for a company already at $1M ARR with a working product, since the value delivered doesn't change based on where the laptop sits.
- A Geneva-based Staff-level engineer would typically command CHF 150,000-190,000 base locally. That's a useful anchor to cite even though this isn't a Swiss role, because it establishes the floor below which the equity grant needs to do real work to compensate.
- Founding engineer equity at this stage is almost always a straight stock option grant, four-year vest with a one-year cliff. There's no RSU liquidity here, so the value is entirely a bet on the company, which argues for negotiating grant size hard rather than treating a slightly higher base as equivalent value.

## Opening position

"I don't see a number in the posting, so let's start there. Given the scope, full-stack ownership across a real production system with paying customers already, I'd be looking at a base around $165,000 if this is in-person in SF, or a blended arrangement if it's remote from Geneva, plus an equity grant that reflects being one of the first engineers on a team that's already proven the model works. I'd rather anchor on the traction you've already got than treat this like a pre-revenue idea-stage hire."

## If they come in low

"I want to understand what the low end assumes, whether it's benchmarked against a first hire at idea stage or against a team that's already past $1M ARR with 80,000 users on the platform. Those are different risk profiles, and I'd expect the comp to reflect that you've already de-risked the product side. If cash is capped by what you raised, I'd rather talk about where the flexibility actually is, equity size, vesting acceleration, or a defined comp review at the next raise, than just accept the number as fixed."

Key move: don't let "we're pre-seed" absorb all the negotiating room by itself. A company at $1M ARR is not the same risk as a company with a deck and no customers, and the comp conversation should reflect which one Clera actually is.

## Equity questions to ask before agreeing to anything

- Total shares granted and what percentage of fully diluted equity that represents today, not just a headline percentage that could mean different things depending on the cap table.
- Strike price and the last 409A valuation, so the actual bet being made is clear.
- Standard four-year vest with a one-year cliff, or something non-standard.
- Whether there's a plan for a refresh grant tied to the next funding round, since early hires often get diluted hard at Series A if nothing's written down now.
- What happens to unvested equity on acquisition, given the founders already have one successful exit pattern in mind for this kind of business.

## Geographic and location framing

"I'm in Geneva, and I want to be upfront that relocating to SF full-time isn't the default plan, though I'm open to discussing it if that's what the role needs. If a remote or hybrid arrangement works on your end, I'd expect the comp conversation to focus on the value of the work rather than a blanket cost-of-living cut, especially given the platform's already live and generating revenue. If SF presence is non-negotiable for a three-person founding team, I understand that constraint, but I'd want to know it upfront rather than discover it after an offer."

## Counter-offer script if the number lands mid-band

"That's closer, and I appreciate you moving. If base is fixed at this stage, I'd want the gap made up on the equity grant, ideally with the vesting clock starting from an agreed date rather than whenever paperwork clears, and a written commitment to revisit both cash and equity at the next funding milestone rather than leaving that informal."

## Competing offer leverage template

"I'm currently in later-stage conversations with another early-stage team in a similar space, and the equity and cash mix there is somewhat more aggressive on the cash side. I'd rather join the team I actually want to build with, but I need the offer here to be close enough that it's a real choice rather than a formality. If there's room to move on the grant size or a signing component, now's the time to use it."

## If they raise the remote-work concern first

"That's fair to name directly. What I bring to a fully in-person, ship-daily culture is real: I built Alaya's first version alone and then onboarded a team into that same rhythm, and I've operated on small, fast teams before. I'd ask what specifically about daily collaboration worries you, so we can test whether a hybrid or trial period addresses it before treating it as a dealbreaker on either side."
