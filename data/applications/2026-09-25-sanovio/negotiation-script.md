# Negotiation Script: SANOVIO, Founding Engineer (Munich, in person)

**Read this first.** The role is in the Munich office, in person, not in Switzerland. SANOVIO has operations in Switzerland (Swiss clinics are among its early customers), but the posting asks for in-person work in Munich at startup intensity. Your pay will be benchmarked against the German market in EUR. Swiss numbers are included below as your reference point and as the basis for the relocation conversation, not as what they'll pay.

Two other things shape this negotiation:

1. **The posting targets 3 to 5 years of experience.** Their band is probably set for a mid-level full-stack engineer. You're 17 years in, with a cofounder exit. Don't negotiate inside their band; negotiate the scope (founding or lead engineer, first technical hire with architecture ownership) and let the number follow from that.
2. **SANOVIO is bootstrapped and profitable** (over €1M ARR in under 8 months according to Munich Startup, aiming for €10M ARR by end of 2026). No investors means no dilution for now, but also no VC cash buffer. Expect them to be careful with fixed salary and more open to bonus and shares.

No salary is disclosed in the posting ("cash, performance bonus, and shares"). The ranges below are estimates from public 2026 compensation data; check levels.fyi, Glassdoor and anyone you know in the Munich startup scene before the first comp call.

## 1. Market picture (2026 estimates)

| Reference point | Base | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Munich early-stage startup, senior full-stack engineer | €80k to €100k | VSOP common, small grants |
| Munich startup, founding / lead engineer | €100k to €130k | 0.5% to 2% equity typical, often virtual |
| Munich scale-up, Staff / Principal engineer | €120k to €150k | Bonus and VSOP on top |
| Big tech Munich (Google, Apple, etc.), Staff level | €140k to €170k | RSUs push total much higher |
| Geneva / Zurich founding engineer, for comparison | CHF 160k to 200k | What you'd expect staying in Switzerland |

**Rough net comparison, check with a calculator before you negotiate.** CHF 180k gross in Geneva nets out to somewhere around CHF 125k to 135k. €130k gross in Munich for a single person, after German income tax and social contributions, nets out to roughly €72k to €78k. Munich rent is lower than Geneva's but not by that much. Moving to Munich on a typical German founding-engineer salary is a real drop in take-home pay, and you should know your own floor before the call.

**Your targets**
- Walk-away base: €115k (below that, the move costs you money, unless shares are exceptional)
- Target base: €130k to €135k plus performance bonus
- Opening anchor: €145k base, a performance bonus, 1.5% in shares, and relocation support

## 2. Opening the conversation

If they ask for your expectations first:

> "I've read the role as founding engineer with ownership of the architecture and the AI side of the product, and that's the role I'm interested in. For that scope in Munich, with the move from Geneva, I'm looking at a base around €145k, plus a performance bonus and a meaningful share grant. I'm open on how cash and shares balance out, and I'd like to see the whole package before settling anything."

If they say the role was budgeted for 3 to 5 years of experience:

> "That makes sense for the ad. What I'd bring is closer to a first technical lead: I've built a B2B platform from nothing to an acquisition, hired the first engineers, and I'm doing LLM and agent work now. If that's worth more to you than a mid-level hire, I'd like the package to reflect it. If it isn't, better we both know that early."

If they push for a single number, repeat €145k. Don't give a range; they'll just hear the bottom of it.

## 3. If the offer comes in low

> "Thank you, I'm glad we're at this point. The base is below what I need to make the move from Geneva work, especially given the scope we've discussed. If you can get to €135k, I'm ready to move forward."

If the cash is capped because they're bootstrapped:

> "Understood, and I respect that you've built this without outside money. If base is fixed, can we make up the gap elsewhere: a larger share grant, a bonus tied to clear targets like ARR or product milestones, a relocation package, or a guaranteed salary review at twelve months?"

A reasonable fallback package: €125k base, a bonus of 10% to 20% of base with written targets, a larger share grant, one-off relocation support of around €10k to €15k, and a written 12-month review.

Never accept on the call. "Let me go through the full package and get back to you by Thursday" is always fine.

## 4. Share questions to ask (German GmbH)

SANOVIO is a GmbH. Real shares in a GmbH need a notary for every transfer, so most German startups use a **VSOP (virtual stock option plan)** instead. Get everything in writing:

- **Real shares or virtual?** With a VSOP you hold a contractual right to a cash payout at an exit, not actual ownership. No voting rights, no dividends, and usually nothing unless there's an exit event. For a profitable company that may never sell, that matters a lot: ask what happens if they stay independent and profitable. Can profits or secondary sales ever pay out?
- **Percentage of what.** Ask for the grant as a percentage of the fully diluted company, not as a number of "units". For a founding engineer at a company this early, 1% to 2% is a fair ask, and 0.5% is the floor.
- **Vesting.** Standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff, and double-trigger acceleration on change of control.
- **Leaver clauses.** This is where German VSOPs often hide the catch. Check what happens to vested units if you leave as a "good leaver" versus a "bad leaver", and make sure resigning voluntarily after the cliff doesn't wipe out your vested units. Some plans also reduce vested units over time after you leave; push back on that.
- **Taxes.** A VSOP payout is normally taxed as salary income when it's paid out, at your full marginal rate. Real shares (or options under the newer §19a EStG rules for startups) can be taxed more favorably. Ask which one applies and get a German tax advisor to look at it before signing.
- **Dilution.** No investors today means no dilution today. Ask whether they plan to raise, and whether the plan has any protection if they do.
- **Performance bonus.** Ask for the targets in writing, how often it pays out, and whether it's capped.

## 5. Location and relocation

You're moving from Geneva to Munich for an in-person role. Put that up front, not at offer stage, and use it in the negotiation:

> "Working in Munich in person means moving from Geneva, and I'm prepared to do that for the right role. Coming from the Swiss market, my current comp is in CHF and well above German levels, so I need the package to make the move make sense. A relocation budget and some help with the first months would go a long way."

Things you can reasonably ask for:
- A relocation allowance (moving costs, a few weeks of temporary housing, a flight home now and then in the first months).
- A start date that gives you time to sort out the move and your Swiss notice period.
- Some flexibility for occasional remote weeks from Geneva, given SANOVIO already has Swiss customers. The posting mentions a "flexible work setup", so test how flexible it actually is.

If they suggest a lower number because "Munich isn't Zurich", agree with them. The point isn't to get Swiss pay in Germany; it's to get a package that justifies giving up Swiss pay.

## 6. Competing offer template

Only use this if it's true, or if you really are in other processes. Don't invent an offer.

> "I want to be open with you: I'm in late stages with another company, and their offer is around [X] in base plus equity. SANOVIO is my first choice because of the problem and because I'd get to shape the product from the start. If you can get closer to [Y], I'll stop the other processes and sign."

Keep it short and factual. Don't name the other company unless they ask and you're comfortable, and don't use it more than once.
