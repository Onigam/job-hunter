# Negotiation Script: Google, Staff Forward Deployed Engineer (GenAI, Google Cloud), Zurich

## Context that shapes everything

This is a Big Tech offer, not a startup negotiation. Google pays in three parts: base salary, an annual bonus with a target percentage, and RSUs (Google stock) vesting over four years. There's no startup equity here, so cliffs, anti-dilution and option strike prices don't apply. What you can move is the **level**, the **RSU grant**, the **sign-on bonus**, and to a lesser extent the base.

"Staff" at Google usually maps to **L6**. The bigger lever is level, not salary: an L6 offer versus an L7 (Senior Staff) offer is a much larger gap than anything you'll win inside a band. With 17+ years and a cofounder exit, it's worth asking whether you were considered for L7. Google has open Senior Staff FDE postings on the same team.

The role is based in **Zurich**, which is Google's top-paying location in Europe. So there's no geographic discount to fight; Zurich pays more than Geneva does for most tech roles. The location question is practical: commuting from Geneva (about 2h45 each way by train) or relocating.

Salary isn't published in the Swiss posting. Recruiters will usually ask for expectations early.

## Market reference points (estimates, verify before the call)

These are working estimates from public compensation data. Check them on Levels.fyi (filter: Google, Zurich, L6 and L7, Software Engineer and Customer Engineer tracks) before any number leaves your mouth. FDE sits in the Cloud go-to-market org, so its bands may differ from core SWE.

| Reference | Base (CHF) | Bonus target | Annual RSU (CHF) | Total comp per year (CHF) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google Zurich, L6 | ~200k to 240k | ~20% | ~120k to 220k | ~360k to 500k |
| Google Zurich, L7 | ~240k to 290k | ~25% | ~220k to 380k | ~520k to 750k |
| Geneva, Senior Staff at a scale-up | ~190k to 240k | varies | small or illiquid | ~200k to 260k |
| Zurich, Staff+ at other Big Tech | ~210k to 260k | varies | ~100k to 200k | ~330k to 460k |

Takeaway: an L6 Google offer in Zurich will likely be well above anything local in Geneva. Most of the difference is RSUs, which is why the grant size is where you negotiate hardest.

## Your numbers

**Walk-away floor:** decide this before the first call and write it down. Given the commute or relocation cost, a reasonable starting point for thinking is around CHF 350k total comp at L6, but it's your call.

**Target at L6:** base around CHF 235k, 20% bonus target, RSUs around CHF 200k a year (CHF 800k over four years), plus a sign-on bonus to cover the move or the first year of commuting. Total around CHF 480k in year one.

**Target at L7:** base around CHF 275k, RSUs of CHF 300k+ a year.

**Opening anchor:** top of the L6 range, and make the L7 question explicit.

## Scripts

### When the recruiter asks for your expectations early

"I'd rather see where the level lands first, since that decides most of it. For context, I've been a Senior Staff Engineer for five years after cofounding a company that was acquired, and I'd want the level to reflect that. I'm assuming Zurich market for Google at Staff or above."

If they insist on a number:

"Based on what I've seen for L6 in Zurich, I'd expect total compensation above CHF 450k a year, with most of the gap between that and base coming from the equity grant. If this is scoped at L7, the numbers move up from there."

### Pushing on level after the interviews

"Before we get into numbers, I'd like to understand how the level was decided. My background covers founding a company through to acquisition and five years as Senior Staff, including cross-team architecture and AI adoption across two teams. Google has Senior Staff FDE roles open on the same team. Was L7 considered, and what would the committee need to see to get there?"

If they won't move the level:

"Understood. In that case I'd like the offer to sit at the top of the L6 band, especially on the equity, and I'd like to talk about what a promotion case would look like in the first 12 to 18 months."

### If the offer comes in low

"Thanks, I appreciate the offer and I want to make this work. The base is close to where I expected, but the equity grant is below what I'm seeing for L6 in Zurich. I'm thinking closer to CHF 800k over four years, and a sign-on bonus to cover the relocation from Geneva. If we can get there, I'm ready to accept."

Then stop talking. Let them come back.

### Sign-on bonus

"There's a real cost to moving from Geneva to Zurich, or commuting every week while I figure that out. A sign-on of around CHF 50k to 80k would cover it. I'd also ask for relocation support if that's something you offer as a separate package."

### Commute and office policy

Google expects most people in the office about three days a week. Before signing, ask the hiring manager directly:

"How much of this role is spent on site at customers, and where are those customers? If a lot of time is at client sites around Switzerland or in Europe, does the Zurich office requirement still apply the same way?"

FDE roles are customer-facing, so a fair share of the week may be at client offices, some possibly in Geneva or Lausanne. That changes whether relocation is needed at all.

### Competing offer template (use only if real)

"I want to be upfront: I have another offer at around CHF [X] total comp, from [company or type of company]. Google is my first choice because of the Gemini work and the FDE model, but the gap matters. Is there room on the equity grant to close it?"

Don't invent a competing offer. Google recruiters sometimes ask for proof, and getting caught would end the process. If there's no offer, say you're "in late-stage conversations elsewhere" only if that's true.

## Equity notes (Google RSUs)

Ask for the vesting schedule in writing. Google has changed its schedule in recent years, and the year-one value depends heavily on it. Also ask:

1. Whether the grant is a fixed CHF or USD value converted to shares at the start date. The share price on your start date sets the number of shares.
2. How annual refresh grants work at L6 and what a typical refresh looks like for a strong rating. Refreshers are a big part of long-term comp at Google.
3. How RSUs are taxed in Switzerland (taxed as income when they vest, and handled through Swiss payroll). Talk to a Swiss tax advisor, since Geneva and Zurich tax rates differ and moving cantons changes your net.

## Swiss-specific things to confirm

1. Whether base is paid in 12 or 13 installments. It doesn't change the total, but it matters when comparing offers.
2. Pension (LPP/BVG) contributions, and how much above the legal minimum Google puts in. This can be worth tens of thousands of francs a year.
3. Whether an SBB GA travelcard or commute allowance is available if you don't relocate right away.
4. Notice period and start date, so they line up with any current commitments.

## Order of priorities

1. Level (L6 vs L7)
2. RSU grant size
3. Sign-on bonus and relocation
4. Base salary
5. Commute terms and on-site expectations

Don't accept on the call. "Thanks, this is a strong offer. Let me take a day or two to go through it properly and I'll come back to you by [day]."
