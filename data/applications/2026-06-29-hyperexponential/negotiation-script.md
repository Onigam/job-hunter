# Negotiation Script — Staff Platform Engineer @ hyperexponential

## Market Context

hyperexponential is a Series B company backed by a16z, Highland Europe, and Battery Ventures, with ~50 major commercial insurer clients and roughly $73M raised. The platform team is small (7 engineers) and London-headquartered. This role is senior IC (Staff level) with significant ownership over infrastructure decisions.

**Switzerland market rate — Staff/Senior Staff Platform Engineer (Geneva):**
- Typical range: CHF 150,000-200,000 base
- Geneva fintech/infra premium: top quartile is CHF 175,000-200,000
- Target anchor: CHF 185,000-195,000 base

**UK equivalent benchmark (London, same level):**
- Market range: £120,000-£160,000 base
- If the offer comes in GBP: target £150,000-£160,000 minimum
- Do not accept a UK-rate offer without pushing back on the geographic delta

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for your compensation expectation, don't anchor low. Go first with a number, not a range, and frame it before they can:

> "For a Staff Platform Engineer role at this level, based in Geneva, I'm looking at CHF 185,000-195,000 base. That reflects both the Geneva market rate and the scope of the role. I'd also want to understand the equity package before finalizing a number, since total comp matters more than base alone at a Series B company."

Give them a specific number. Don't say "competitive" or "market rate" without naming the market.

---

## If They Come In Below CHF 165,000

Do not accept on the spot. Take 24-48 hours to review, then counter:

> "I appreciate the offer. The challenge for me is that CHF [X] doesn't quite match what Staff-level engineers are earning in this market, particularly in Geneva where the cost structure is closer to Zurich than to London. I've seen comparable roles in this range land at CHF 175,000-185,000. Is there flexibility there? And if the base is constrained, I'd want to look more closely at the equity to see if total comp can get there."

Two moves: ask for base increase, or shift the conversation to equity. Never let them think the first number is final.

---

## Equity Negotiation (Series B)

Before evaluating any equity offer, you need the following. Ask directly:

1. Total diluted share count (to compute your % ownership)
2. Most recent 409A / fair market value of common stock (or equivalent for a UK company)
3. Vesting schedule and cliff (standard: 1-year cliff, 4-year vest monthly thereafter)
4. Whether there's a change-of-control acceleration clause (single-trigger preferred; double-trigger is market but weaker)
5. Post-termination exercise window (90 days is minimum; 5-10 years is employee-friendly)

Frame it neutrally: "To evaluate the equity, I need to understand the diluted share count and the most recent valuation of common stock. Can you share those?"

**Target equity range for Staff level at this stage:** 0.08%-0.15% over 4 years. Use that as a benchmark if they haven't framed a number, and ask: "What does equity typically look like for this level at hx?"

**Red flags to watch for:**
- No cliff
- 5-year or longer vest
- 30-day post-termination exercise window (very employee-hostile)
- Very high liquidation preference on preferred (anything above 1x non-participating is worth scrutinizing)

---

## Geographic Premium Argument

hx is a London company. They may default to London rates or apply a geographic adjustment downward for a Geneva-based hire. Push back directly:

> "I want to be straightforward about the geographic piece. Geneva has a cost structure that's roughly 30-35% higher than London. I'm not in a lower-cost location, I'm in one of the most expensive cities in Europe. The local market for Staff engineers in fintech/infrastructure reflects that. I'm not asking for an arbitrary premium, I'm asking to be compensated at a level that's realistic for my actual cost structure."

Supporting data if needed:
- Geneva 3-bedroom apartment: CHF 3,200-4,500/month
- Swiss mandatory health insurance (LAMAL): CHF 400-600/month per adult, not employer-covered
- Swiss income tax (Geneva) at CHF 180k: effective rate ~28-32%
- Swiss pillar 2 (pension) contributions: typically 10-15% of salary split employer/employee

---

## Benefits and Conditions to Clarify

These matter at a UK company hiring remotely in Switzerland:

- **Remote policy:** Fully remote, or expected visits to London? (ask for budget if travel is required)
- **Equipment:** Home office setup budget
- **Health insurance:** LAMAL is mandatory in Switzerland and expensive. Does hx contribute? (many UK companies don't cover Swiss mandatory health insurance, so factor that into total comp)
- **Pension:** UK companies typically don't contribute to Swiss Pillar 2. Clarify whether there's a pension or retirement contribution.
- **Holiday:** UK standard is 25 days. If base doesn't move, push for 30 days.
- **Training / conference budget:** Ask for an explicit commitment, not a vague "we support learning"

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in progress:

> "I have another process that's moving to offer stage. I'd prefer to be at hx based on the technical scope of the platform work, but I'll need to make a decision by [specific date]. Is there a way to accelerate on your end?"

If no competing offer: don't fabricate a number. You can legitimately say: "I'm actively in conversations with a few other companies at a similar level, so my timeline is tightening." That creates urgency without inventing a specific offer.

---

## When to Walk

If the final offer is below CHF 155,000 base with no meaningful equity adjustment, the math doesn't work in Geneva. The combination of mandatory Swiss costs (health insurance, pension gap, higher taxes) makes this a real floor, not a negotiating position. Walk politely, keep the door open, and say: "I hope we can revisit this if circumstances change."
