# Negotiation Script: Staff Software Engineer, Product Engineering, EU (Ashby)

## Context that shapes the numbers

- **Posted band.** The LinkedIn listing (shown for Switzerland) gives **€154,000 to €250,000 base**. An aggregator copy of the same req shows €154k to €188k, so the top of the range may depend on location tier or level. Ask which number applies to Switzerland before quoting anything.
- **Switzerland is on the location list.** The posting names Switzerland next to Portugal, Spain, Germany, Ireland and others. Ashby probably employs outside its main hubs through an employer of record, so confirm who the legal employer would be, whether you'd be paid in CHF or EUR, and how Swiss pension (2nd pillar) and LAMal are handled.
- **Ashby is private and growing fast.** The posting claims tens of millions in ARR, more than 100% year-over-year growth and low churn, with engineering set to roughly double. Equity is stock options, and the 10-year exercise window is unusually good. It's worth real money, so treat it as part of the package, not a footnote.
- **Staff is the level.** You're negotiating against Staff bands. 17+ years, a platform built from zero to acquisition, and Senior Staff at Benevity back that up. Don't let it slide to Senior.
- **Benefits are generous but don't replace base.** Unlimited PTO, 12+ weeks parental leave and covered supplemental health insurance are nice. None of them pay Geneva rent.

## Step 1: recruiter or hiring manager screen

If they ask for expectations first:

"For a Staff role based in Switzerland, I'm looking at around €240k base, plus options in line with your Staff grants. Your posted band goes up to €250k, and that's where the scope here sits: owning a product area from research and specs through to shipping, with no PM layer in between. If we're in that range, I'm very interested."

If you'd rather get their number first:

"I'd like to understand the level and the Swiss band before I name a figure. Which part of the €154k to €250k range applies to Switzerland, and where does Staff usually land?"

## Step 2: if they come in low

"Thanks for the offer. €[their number] sits nearer the bottom of the band than I expected for Staff, especially in Geneva. The role as you describe it is product, design and engineering ownership of a large area, which is how I ran Alaya for five years. Could we get to €220k base? If base is tight, I'd want to close the gap with a larger option grant."

If base really can't move:

"Understood. Then let's look at equity. A bigger initial grant, or a sign-on bonus to cover the gap in year one, would work for me. Could you go to [grant +30 to 40%]?"

## Step 3: pushing back on a geographic discount

If they suggest a lower tier because the band was built around Portugal or Spain:

"I see why the band has tiers. But I'd be employed in Switzerland, paying Geneva rent and Swiss health insurance, and Switzerland is on your own location list for this role. The fair comparison is what Staff engineers earn here, which is at the top of your posted range, not the bottom."

If they say there's one EU band for everyone:

"That's fair if it's applied evenly. In that case I'd ask to be placed higher in the band, since cost of living here is well above the other locations on the list, or to make up the difference in equity."

## Step 4: equity notes (private-company options)

Ask for these in writing before signing.

- **Number of options and fully diluted shares outstanding.** A share count means nothing without the denominator. Ask for your percentage.
- **Strike price and latest preferred price.** The gap between the two is the paper value today. Ask when the last 409A valuation was done.
- **Vesting schedule.** Usually four years with a one-year cliff. Ask whether it's monthly after the cliff and whether there are refresh grants for strong performers.
- **10-year exercise window.** Confirm it applies after you leave, not just while employed. That's the clause that makes these options worth having, since you won't be forced to exercise or lose them within 90 days.
- **Early exercise.** Ask if it's allowed. It can help on tax, depending on Swiss treatment.
- **Change of control.** Ask about double-trigger acceleration (acquisition plus termination). You've lived through an acquisition, so you know why it matters.
- **Anti-dilution.** Employees almost never get it; that protection belongs to preferred investors. Don't spend negotiating capital on it. Ask instead how much dilution the company expects from the next round and whether grants get refreshed afterwards.
- **Swiss tax.** Options on unlisted shares are generally taxed as income at exercise in Switzerland, and an EOR setup can complicate that. Talk to a Swiss tax adviser before you exercise anything.

## Step 5: competing offer template

Use only if it's real, or frame it as a process you're in rather than a signed offer.

"I want to be upfront: I'm in late stages with another company, at a Staff level, with a base around CHF [X] plus equity. Ashby is my first choice because of how the engineering team works, and I'd rather make this work. Is there room to get closer to €[target] base, or to adjust the grant?"

If they ask for details:

"I'd rather not share the company name, but I'm happy to confirm the numbers in broad terms. It's a Staff role, Swiss-based, and the base is in the CHF 200k range."

## Things that aren't salary but are worth asking about

- **Time zone.** You're in CET. Ask how much overlap with US colleagues is expected, given most of Ashby's team and customers are in North America.
- **Equipment and education budgets.** The posting includes both; confirm the amounts.
- **Start date.** Set it with enough room to wrap up anything else cleanly.
- **Title.** Make sure "Staff" is in the offer letter, not just the conversation.

## Last check before signing

Base in the right currency, option count with fully diluted shares, strike, vesting, the post-termination exercise window, the legal employer and how Swiss social contributions are handled. Get every item in writing.
