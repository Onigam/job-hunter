# Negotiation Script: Gusto, Staff Software Engineer, AI Developer Tools

## Context
- Gusto published bands on the Greenhouse posting: $180K-$200K base for Denver/remote, $220K-$245K base for SF/NYC/Seattle, plus RSUs and standard benefits.
- The role is listed as hybrid (2-3 days/week) in SF, with hybrid options in Denver, New York, and Seattle. Geneva isn't on that list, so the first real negotiation isn't comp, it's whether a remote-from-Switzerland arrangement exists at all. That has to get resolved before a number means anything.
- Gusto completed its IPO in 2026 at roughly a $14B valuation, on more than $1B in revenue and years of being cash-flow positive. That changes the equity conversation from private-company options to public RSUs, and it's real leverage: this isn't a pre-revenue startup hoping the tooling investment pays off, it's a profitable public company that can afford to pay for a Staff-level hire properly.
- Leverage: 17+ years of systems experience, a real acquisition exit (Alaya to Benevity), and a directly on-point track record of driving AI tooling adoption across an engineering org without formal authority, which is close to the exact job description here.
- Counter-leverage they'll likely have: my LLM and RAG work has been embedded inside a product engineering role, not a dedicated platform-for-AI-tools team, so expect them to probe how much of it transfers to building the tooling itself versus consuming it.

## Geneva / Switzerland market anchor
Staff-level platform and AI-infrastructure engineers in Geneva typically land in the CHF 170,000-220,000 base range, with total comp including bonus and equity often pushing higher at well-funded tech companies. Converted loosely to USD, that sits above Gusto's published Denver/remote band and roughly in line with or above the SF/NYC/Seattle band. That's the anchor for arguing that a Switzerland-based remote arrangement, if Gusto is open to it at all, shouldn't default to the lower published number just because it's not one of the listed hybrid cities.

## Opening move: establish whether remote-from-Geneva is even on the table
"Before we get into numbers, I want to be upfront that I'm based in Geneva, which isn't one of the hybrid hubs in the posting. I'd rather know now whether a fully remote arrangement from Switzerland is something you'd consider for a Staff-level hire, since that determines whether the rest of this conversation makes sense to have."

## Opening comp anchor, if remote is workable
"Assuming we can make the location work, I'd anchor around $230,000-245,000 base, in line with your SF and Seattle band, given the Staff scope and that Geneva's cost of living and market rate for this level sit close to those hubs rather than the Denver/remote number. On top of that, I'd want the standard RSU grant for this level now that you're public, plus clarity on refresh cadence."

## If they anchor to the lower Denver/remote band ($180K-$200K) because I'm remote
"I get the instinct to default to the remote band, but that band was set with US-based cost-of-living assumptions in mind, and Geneva isn't a lower cost-of-living market, it's one of the more expensive ones globally. I'd rather solve this by comparing to the SF/NYC/Seattle band directly than by treating remote as an automatic discount. If there's a structural reason it has to be the lower band, I'd want to understand it, but I don't think cost of living is that reason here."

## Equity questions now that Gusto is public
- Confirm whether the offer is RSUs granted post-IPO or a mix that includes any legacy pre-IPO options, since the tax and liquidity mechanics differ.
- Ask about the vesting schedule, standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff at newly public companies, and whether Staff-level hires get a different structure or accelerated first-year vesting.
- Ask about refresh grant cadence and how it's tied to performance review, since that's usually where a chunk of long-term comp actually comes from at a public company rather than the initial grant.
- Ask what portion of total comp is weighted toward equity versus base at this level, since post-IPO packages sometimes shift more into equity than the initial band suggests.

## Geographic and cost-of-living pushback, if needed
"Geneva ranks among the most expensive cities globally, ahead of most of the US hubs in your band list on most cost-of-living indices. If the location is workable at all, the comp should reflect that directly rather than defaulting to whichever band is administratively convenient."

## Competing offer leverage template
Use only if genuinely in play: "I want to be transparent that I have another process further along at a comparable level, and their number is close to what I'm proposing here. Gusto is the more interesting problem to me, the developer-tooling and AI-infrastructure work is closer to what I want to be doing, but I do need to move on this within a couple of weeks."

## Close
"I'd like to get the location question and the base number settled this week so I can give you a real answer instead of a maybe. If Geneva doesn't work at all for this role, I'd rather hear that now too."
