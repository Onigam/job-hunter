# Negotiation Script — Fullstack / Product AI Founding Engineer

Company: Undisclosed AI Product Startup via Rockstar Recruiting
Posted range: CHF 140,000 - 150,000
Date: 2026-06-22

---

## Market Context

The posted range of CHF 140,000 - 150,000 is below market for a founding engineer at Magino's seniority level (17 years, Staff Engineer, prior cofounder with unicorn exit). In the Swiss market for 2026:

- Mid-level fullstack (5-8 years): CHF 130,000 - 155,000
- Senior engineer (10+ years): CHF 155,000 - 185,000
- Principal / Staff / Founding Engineer (equivalent experience): CHF 170,000 - 220,000
- Zürich premium over Geneva: roughly 5-10% (cost of living similar; Zürich salaries trend slightly higher)

For an early-stage AI product startup, equity should compensate for any base discount below market. If the base stays below CHF 165,000, equity becomes the negotiating lever.

---

## Opening Position

**Target base:** CHF 175,000

**Justification to use in conversation:**
- 17+ years of hands-on engineering, including a founding-engineer track record at Alaya (acquired by Benevity, $1B+ valuation)
- Senior Staff Engineer at Benevity since 2021, current market equivalent in Zürich: CHF 170,000 - 195,000
- Founding engineer roles command a premium over standard senior IC roles because of the scope and risk
- If Geneva-based and the role is in Zürich: travel / accommodation adds real cost (address this explicitly if needed)

**Opening line:**

"Based on my background and current market rates for senior founding engineers in Switzerland, I'm targeting CHF 175,000 base. I'm also expecting equity to be a meaningful part of the package at this stage of the company. Can you walk me through how you're thinking about both?"

## If They Come in at the Posted Range (CHF 140,000 - 150,000)

**Response:**

"I appreciate the transparency on the range. To be honest, that's below where I'd need to land given my experience level and what the market is paying for founding engineers with a platform exit in Switzerland. I'm targeting CHF 165,000 to 175,000 on base. Is there flexibility to work toward that range, or would it make more sense to look at what we can do on equity to close the gap?"

**If they say the budget is fixed:**

"Could we structure it as CHF 155,000 now with a clear six-month review tied to specific milestones we agree on upfront? I'd rather anchor to a trajectory than negotiate against a fixed ceiling. What metrics would you use to evaluate the first six months anyway?"

This reframes the conversation toward alignment, which is useful for a founding engineer role where the first six months define a lot.

## Equity Negotiation

For a very early-stage AI product startup (pre-seed to seed):

**Ask for:** 0.75% - 1.5% fully diluted
**Accept minimum:** 0.5% (below that, the founding engineer premium isn't there)
**Standard terms:** 4-year vest, 1-year cliff

**Questions to ask before accepting any equity number:**

1. "What's the current fully diluted cap table? I want to understand what my percentage means in context."
2. "What's the current valuation or the last 409A equivalent for Swiss companies?" (Know your strike price before it matters.)
3. "Is there a single-trigger acceleration clause on acquisition?" (Critical for founding engineers. If the company gets acquired in year two, you want your unvested shares to accelerate.)
4. "What are the information rights for option holders?" (Right to see basic financials.)

**Equity ask script:**

"For a founding engineer role at this stage, I'd expect equity to be a real part of the compensation, not a rounding error. I'm thinking in the 0.75% to 1.5% fully diluted range, standard four-year vesting with a one-year cliff. Can you share the current cap table and the pool you're working from so I can understand the context?"

**If the equity offer is low (under 0.5%):**

"That's lower than I'd expect for a founding engineer coming in at this stage. At pre-seed or seed, the first few engineers are taking on meaningful risk in exchange for meaningful upside. If the equity can't move, then the base needs to reflect that risk, which puts me back at CHF 175,000 or above. Which lever has more room?"

## Competing Offer Leverage

If there is a real competing offer: use it directly and specifically.

"I do have another process at [Company], which is offering [CHF X / Y% equity]. I prefer this role for [specific reason], but I need the numbers to be close enough that the decision isn't difficult. Is there anything you can do to close that gap?"

If there is no competing offer: do not fabricate one. Use market anchors instead.

"I don't have a competing offer in hand today, but based on the conversations I've been having, market rate for this profile in Switzerland is CHF 165,000 to 185,000 for senior founding engineers. I'd like to land in that range."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If the role is Zürich-based and requires regular presence:

"I'm based in Geneva. A regular Zürich presence means either a significant commute or accommodation costs, which in practice adds CHF 8,000 to 12,000 per year in out-of-pocket expense. I'd factor that into the base conversation. A fully remote arrangement would remove that variable entirely."

---

## Benefits Checklist (Switzerland context)

Confirm each of these before signing:

- **LPP / BVG (pension):** Ask for the employer contribution percentage. Minimum is legally required; good employers contribute 8-10%+ above the mandatory floor.
- **Equity / ESOP terms:** Get the cap table, strike price, and vesting schedule in writing before starting. "We'll formalize it after you join" is a red flag.
- **Home office:** CHF 50-100/month if partially remote (standard in Switzerland).
- **Hardware:** Full setup covered. Refresh cycle every 2-3 years.
- **Vacation:** 5 weeks minimum for senior roles. 4 weeks is standard but negotiable up.
- **Learning budget:** CHF 2,000 - 5,000/year for conferences, courses, books.
- **Insurance (IJM / daily indemnity):** Confirm the employer covers short-term disability insurance, standard but sometimes omitted at very early startups.

---

## Red Flags to Watch

- Equity offered without a clear strike price or cap table view before signing.
- "We'll formalize equity after you start." Push for written terms before day one.
- No mention of LPP at all. It's legally required in Switzerland; if they don't mention it, ask explicitly.
- Vesting with no cliff, or a cliff longer than 12 months (longer cliff protects them, not you).
- "Competitive salary" without a number. Always get a specific range in writing before moving past the first interview.
