# Negotiation Script — Albert (Principal / Distinguished Engineer)

---

## Market Context

**Albert's known compensation range:** Up to ~$750k total compensation for Principal/Distinguished level, per the June 2026 HN "Who is Hiring" post. This is US-scale comp for a remote-friendly role.

**Role level:** Principal / Distinguished Engineer is typically equivalent to L6-L7 at a US tech company. At that level:
- US market: $300k-$750k+ total comp (base + bonus + equity)
- Switzerland equivalent: CHF 180k-280k base for senior IC roles; total comp varies significantly by equity structure

**Albert specifics:**
- Profitable consumer fintech (no funding pressure / down-round risk)
- 20M+ users, established product
- Remote-first, so comp is likely pegged closer to US rates than to local Swiss rates
- Likely equity in a private company (options or RSUs, not public)

---

## Opening Anchor

**When asked for salary expectations, use this:**

"For a Principal/Distinguished Engineer role at this level, I'm looking at total compensation in the range of $400-500k, including base, bonus, and equity. That's based on the comp data I've seen for comparable roles at remote-first US companies at this stage. I'm open to discussing the equity component in detail since the mix matters a lot at a private company."

**Why this works:**
- It's within the stated range ($750k ceiling) but not at the top, so it doesn't read as uninformed or bluffing
- Naming the equity separately opens a second negotiation track
- It's a range, not a single number, which invites counter rather than a hard stop

---

## If They Come in Below $300k (US)

"I appreciate the offer. The role is genuinely interesting and Albert is the kind of company I want to work with. That said, the number is a bit below what I was expecting for this level, particularly given the scope of the role and the remote-first comp structure you're offering the team. Is there room to move on base, or is there more flexibility on the equity side? I'd rather find a structure that works than walk away over a gap we can probably close."

**Key:** Don't reject the number, invite them to explain their structure. Sometimes the low base is offset by meaningful equity.

---

## If They Ask "What Are You Currently Making?"

"I'm currently in a Geneva-based comp structure which isn't directly comparable to remote US rates, so I don't think it's the most useful reference point for this conversation. I'd rather anchor to what's fair for the role at this level. My range is $400-500k total comp."

**Why:** Geneva salaries are lower than US remote comp for senior engineers. Anchoring to local comp hurts you. Reframe without lying.

---

## Equity Negotiation

Albert is a private company, profitable but not yet public. Options or RSUs at this stage have real but uncertain value.

**Questions to ask about equity:**
- What's the current 409A valuation and strike price?
- What's the share class (common vs. preferred)? What does the liquidation preference stack look like?
- What's the vesting schedule? Standard 4-year / 1-year cliff, or something different?
- Is there any acceleration on acquisition or IPO?
- When was the last round priced and at what valuation?

**Negotiation move on equity:**
"The equity is an important part of how I'm thinking about this. Can you give me the 409A valuation and the number of shares so I can model what this looks like at different exit scenarios? I want to understand the real value, not just the headline number."

**If the equity number seems low:**
"I'm comparing this to offers at companies at a similar stage. The equity tranche here is [X]% below where I'd expect for this level. Is there flexibility to increase the grant, or to add an accelerator clause on a qualifying exit?"

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

Albert may try to apply a "Geneva location adjustment" downward. Push back on this.

"I understand some companies apply location adjustments, but I'd push back gently. You're hiring for remote because you want the best engineers regardless of geography. Applying a downward adjustment for Geneva effectively means you're paying less for the same work product. I don't think that's the intent of the remote-first structure."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or are in process elsewhere), use it:

"I want to be transparent: I'm also in late-stage conversations with [company] at a comparable level. Their total comp offer is [range]. I prefer the Albert opportunity for [specific reason], but I'd need to get reasonably close on comp to make that choice. Can we talk about closing the gap?"

**If you don't have a competing offer yet but want to apply light pressure:**
"I've had several conversations at the Principal level over the past few weeks and the range I'm seeing is consistent with what I mentioned. I don't want to manufacture urgency that doesn't exist, but I do need to make a decision within the next two to three weeks."

---

## Counter-Offer Script (If First Offer Is Made)

Offer received. Take 24-48 hours before responding.

"Thank you for the offer. I've had a chance to look at it carefully. I'm genuinely interested in the role and the team. A few things I'd like to discuss before I sign: [base is below my range / equity grant seems light for the level / vesting schedule]. Specifically, I'd like to see [base of $X / equity of Y shares / cliff reduced to 6 months]. Is that something we can work through?"

---

## Walk-Away Points

- Total comp below CHF 200k equivalent (or ~$220k USD) for a remote role at this level is not worth taking
- No equity disclosure at all is a red flag at a private company at this stage
- Greater than 4-year vest with no acceleration is a below-market structure
- Any geographic penalty that takes the offer below the market range for remote senior ICs is worth pushing back on hard, and walking away from if they don't move
