# Negotiation Script: Staff Full Stack Engineer, Series B energy startup (via Immersum)

## The shape of this offer

- **Posted band:** £110,000 to £120,000+ plus stock options. Fully remote, Europe. The band is in pounds, so the client is very likely UK-based.
- **Recruiter:** Immersum (job poster: Charles Curtis, co-founder). The client is unnamed. Agency fees are usually a percentage of first-year base, so the recruiter's incentive points the same way as yours on base salary, less so on equity.
- **Level mismatch:** LinkedIn tags it "Mid-Senior" while the title says Staff. Clarify early which one the client actually means, because it decides whether the "+" in "£120k+" is real.
- **Location noise:** the page header shows "Des Moines, IA", the body says Europe 100% remote. Confirm remote from Switzerland is genuinely fine.

Rough conversion: £110k to £120k is about CHF 115k to 130k at recent GBP/CHF rates (roughly 1.05 to 1.10). **Check the live rate before any comp call.** That's well below the Geneva market for Staff-level engineers, so the whole negotiation turns on the employment structure, the top of the band, and the equity.

## Three things to settle before talking numbers

1. **Who the client is.** Ask Immersum for the company name, funding round size and date, and headcount once you're past the first screen. You can't value options without knowing the last round.
2. **How they'd employ someone in Switzerland.** Options are an Employer of Record (Deel, Remote, Oyster and similar), a Swiss entity, or a contractor setup. This matters more than it looks:
   - With an EOR, the employer pays Swiss social charges (AVS/AHV, unemployment, accident insurance, LPP/BVG pension) on top of gross salary. Ask whether the £ band is your gross salary or their total employer cost. If it's total cost, your gross is roughly 10 to 15% lower.
   - As a contractor, you carry AVS, pension, holidays, sickness and no notice protection yourself. Any day rate needs to sit **at least 25 to 30% above** the employee-equivalent salary.
3. **Currency.** Ask to be paid in CHF at a fixed amount, or at minimum in EUR. If they insist on GBP, ask for an annual FX review so a weaker pound doesn't quietly cut your pay.

## Scripts

### When the recruiter asks for expectations early

> "I've seen the band. Before I give a number I need two things: the level the client actually has in mind, since the posting says Staff but LinkedIn shows Mid-Senior, and how they'd employ someone based in Switzerland. Once I know those, I can be precise."

If pushed:

> "For a genuine Staff role, fully remote, I'd be looking at the top of the band and above, so around £135k gross, with the employer covering Swiss social charges and a meaningful option grant. I'm based in Geneva and comparing against local Staff roles that pay well over that, so the equity matters a lot here."

### Geographic discount pushback

> "I understand the band is set against London. Geneva is more expensive than London for most things that matter: rent, health insurance, which is paid privately here, and childcare. Local Staff roles are in the CHF 170k to 210k range. I'm not asking you to match Geneva, but I can't go below London rates either."

### If the offer comes in low (e.g. £110k, unspecified options)

> "Thanks for this, I want to make it work. The base is at the bottom of the band you posted for a Staff role, and I'd expect the top end given the scope: technical vision across the system, tendering automation, risk tooling and agentic workflows. Can we get to £128k? If the base really is fixed, I'd want to see that reflected in the option grant, closer to 0.35%."

### If they say the base is capped

> "Understood. Then let's look at the rest. Could we do a guaranteed salary review at six months tied to agreed milestones, a larger grant, and the EOR costs on your side rather than deducted from my gross?"

### Competing offer template (use only what's true)

> "To be transparent, I'm in late stages with [company] for a [Staff/Principal] role in Switzerland, with a base around CHF [X]. This role is my preference because of the scope and the domain, but the gap is large. What flexibility do you have to close part of it, in base or in equity?"

Never invent an offer. If nothing is live, talk about the market range for local roles instead, which is real and easy to check.

## Equity checklist

Ask for all of this in writing before signing:

- **Number of options and the fully diluted share count**, so you can compute your percentage. A share count with no denominator means nothing.
- **Strike price and the preferred price of the last round.** The gap between them is your paper value today.
- **Vesting:** standard is four years with a one-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Post-departure exercise window.** The default is often 90 days, which forces you to pay to exercise when you leave. Ask for 7 to 10 years.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination without cause), at least 50% of unvested.
- **Plan type.** UK EMI options are tax-favoured but usually available only to UK-based employees. From Switzerland you'll most likely get unapproved options. In Switzerland, employee options in private companies are generally taxed as income at exercise; confirm the treatment with a Geneva tax adviser and ask whether the company has a cashless or early-exercise option.
- **Anti-dilution:** employees don't get anti-dilution protection; that's an investor term. The realistic ask is a **refresh grant policy** (e.g. reviewed annually or after each round) so your stake isn't diluted to nothing by Series C and D.
- **Liquidation preferences** on the existing rounds. If preferred shareholders have 1x non-participating, fine. Anything participating or above 1x shrinks common stock in a mid-sized exit, and you should know before you value the grant.

## Questions for the recruiter

1. What's the client's name, and when did the Series B close?
2. Is the band gross salary or total employer cost?
3. Does "£120k+" mean there's room above, and for what profile?
4. Is the level truly Staff, with ownership of architecture across the system?
5. How have they employed people outside the UK so far?
6. How many engineers are there today, and who would this role report to?
