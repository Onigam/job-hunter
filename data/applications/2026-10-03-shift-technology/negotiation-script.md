# Negotiation Script: Shift Technology, Staff AI Engineer (Architecture)

## Context that shapes the numbers

Shift Technology is a Paris-headquartered insurtech, founded in 2014, selling AI for claims, fraud and underwriting to insurers in 25+ countries. Its last big round was a USD 220M Series D in 2021 that valued it above USD 1B, and it renewed a five-year partnership with AXA in March 2026. It's a late-stage private company, not a startup, and it pays like one: structured bands, a variable component, and equity that's real on paper but illiquid.

The posting is listed in **Paris**, offers "flexible remote/hybrid" working and gives no salary. That's the central issue for you. Shift will benchmark against Paris, and Paris pays a lot less than Geneva.

What that means for the negotiation:

- **The employment structure matters more than the number.** As a Geneva resident, you can be hired three ways, and each gives a very different net outcome (see the next section). Settle this before you talk money.
- **The title is Staff, and your background fits it.** 17 years, a founder exit and five years at Senior Staff level put you comfortably at Staff. Python as a second language and AWS rather than Azure are the gaps they'll press on; acknowledge them, don't let them pull you down a level.
- **French equity is BSPCE or free shares (AGA).** Both regimes are designed around French tax residents. As a Swiss resident, the tax treatment will differ, so get it checked before you count it as compensation.

## The structure question: settle this first

| Option | How it works | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| A. Swiss contract via an Employer of Record (Deel, Remote, etc.) | Shift pays an EOR, which employs you in Geneva with Swiss payroll, AVS and LPP | Best outcome. Swiss salary, Swiss social security. Costs Shift more, so expect pushback on the number |
| B. French contract, working mostly from Geneva | French payroll, but under EU/CH social security rules, working 25%+ in Switzerland usually puts you under Swiss social security, which Shift would need to handle | Messy for both sides. Raise it early so it doesn't kill the offer late |
| C. French contract, relocating or spending most of your time in Paris | Standard Paris package | Biggest pay cut against Geneva living costs. Only worth it if equity and role justify it |

Push for **Option A**. Line to use:

"I live in Geneva and I'd be working mostly from there, with regular trips to Paris. The cleanest setup for both of us is probably an EOR contract in Switzerland. It keeps the social security side compliant, which a French contract wouldn't if I'm in Geneva most of the week. Is that something Shift has done before?"

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough figures from public surveys and recruiter data. Re-check on levels.fyi, Glassdoor (Shift Technology, Paris), Welcome to the Jungle, swissdevjobs.ch, and the Michael Page and Hays Switzerland salary guides.

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Staff Engineer, Geneva, product company | CHF 165k to 210k |
| Staff / Principal AI Engineer, Zürich (base only) | CHF 180k to 240k |
| Staff Engineer, Paris scale-up | EUR 105k to 135k |
| Staff AI / ML Engineer, Paris, late-stage AI company | EUR 115k to 150k |

Shift advertises "competitive salary plus a variable performance component". Ask what percentage the variable is, how it's been paid out in practice over the last two years, and whether it's individual or company performance.

On a Swiss contract, confirm whether the figure includes a 13th month and how the LPP pension is split. On a French contract, remember that EUR gross loses roughly 22 to 25% to employee social charges before income tax, while Swiss charges are closer to 13 to 15%. A EUR 130k French gross and a CHF 130k Swiss gross aren't remotely the same money.

**If Option A (Swiss contract):**
- Target: CHF 185k base, plus variable
- Opening anchor: CHF 198k
- Walk-away floor: CHF 165k

**If Option B or C (French contract):**
- Target: EUR 140k base, plus variable
- Opening anchor: EUR 150k
- Walk-away floor: EUR 125k, and only if you'd actually move closer to Paris or the equity grant is meaningful

Why you can anchor high: Staff AI engineers who can design agent orchestration and also take it to production are scarce in Europe, and Shift is competing with Mistral, H Company and the US labs' Paris offices for them. You bring the second half of that, production platforms owned end to end through an acquisition, which is the hard part to hire for.

## Opening anchor script

"For a Staff AI role, with my background in owning a platform from zero through acquisition and five years at Senior Staff level, I'm looking at around CHF 198k base on a Swiss contract, plus the variable. I've factored in that Azure and Python-first would be a ramp-up for me; without that, I'd ask for more. How does that compare with your band for this level?"

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low

**If they offer a Paris package (for example EUR 115k to 125k) for a Geneva-based role:**

"I appreciate the offer, and I want this to work. The difficulty is that it's priced for Paris and I'm in Geneva, where costs are a lot higher, and on a French contract the social charges take a much bigger bite. Paris-based Staff AI engineers at late-stage companies are already in the 130s to 150s. Could we look at either an EOR contract around CHF 185k, or a French package closer to EUR 140k?"

**If they cite the Python or Azure gap:**

"That's fair, and I'd rather see it in a review than in the starting number. Could we agree on the target now, or a slightly lower base with a formal compensation review at six months, tied to something concrete like shipping the first production agent workflow on Azure?"

**If base really is fixed, trade for things that cost them less:**

- A larger BSPCE or AGA grant, or a faster vesting schedule
- A signing bonus (EUR 10k to 20k, or CHF equivalent) to cover the first year's travel to Paris
- A guaranteed variable for year one
- A travel budget for Paris trips, paid on top of salary
- A written six-month review date with a defined salary range
- Extra Focus Fridays or an annual conference and training budget

## Geographic discount pushback

They'll argue the role is a Paris role. The answer isn't that Geneva deserves a premium; it's that you're doing a Staff job wherever you sit.

"I understand the role is listed in Paris. What you're paying for is Staff-level architecture and mentoring, and that's the same whether I'm at a desk in Paris or Geneva. I'm happy to be in Paris regularly. I'd ask that the offer reflects the market where I actually live and pay my bills."

If they hold firm on Paris rates, the honest fallback is to calculate the net figure for each option side by side (use a Swiss and a French salary simulator) and decide on numbers, not on the gross headline.

## Equity notes

Shift is late-stage and was valued above USD 1B in 2021. That cuts both ways.

- **Ask for the current 409A-equivalent or latest internal valuation and the strike price.** A grant priced at the 2021 peak may be underwater if the valuation has since come down, as it has for many late-stage companies.
- **Ask what percentage of fully diluted shares the grant represents,** not just the number of shares. Then ask about the preference stack: how much was raised in total, and are there liquidation preferences above 1x?
- **Liquidity.** There's no IPO announced. Ask whether Shift has ever run a secondary or a buyback, and whether employees could take part.
- **Vesting.** Standard in France is four years with a one-year cliff. Ask for acceleration on change of control (double trigger), which matters at this stage because an acquisition is a plausible exit.
- **Leaver terms.** BSPCE often have to be exercised within a short window after leaving. Ask what happens if you leave after the cliff, and push for a longer exercise period.
- **Tax as a Swiss resident.** The favourable BSPCE and AGA regimes are built for French tax residents. Get a cross-border tax advisor to confirm how a grant would be taxed in Geneva, at grant, vest and sale, before you trade any base salary for equity.

Rule of thumb: value the equity at zero when you compare offers, then treat whatever it pays as upside. Negotiate on base and structure first.

## Competing offer template

Use this only with a real process in progress. You've got several Geneva and remote applications live, so it's likely to apply.

"I want to be transparent with you. I'm in late-stage conversations with another company, Geneva-based, and their range for a comparable Staff role is around CHF [X]. Shift is my first choice because of the agentic claims work and the way you're thinking about agent autonomy with ARISE. If we can close the gap on base and sort out the contract structure, I'd be ready to sign quickly."

Don't name the other company unless asked, and don't inflate the figure. Late-stage companies often check with recruiters.

## Questions to ask before you negotiate

1. Is the role open to someone based in Geneva on a long-term hybrid basis, and how many days in Paris per month do you expect?
2. Have you employed people in Switzerland before, directly or through an EOR?
3. What's the salary band for Staff, and where does the variable sit as a percentage of base?
4. How has the variable actually paid out in the past two years?
5. What's the equity vehicle, the size of a typical Staff grant, and the current strike price?
6. What are the review and promotion cycles, and what separates Staff from Principal at Shift?

## Walk-away checklist

Walk away, or pause, if:

- Shift insists on a French contract while expecting you to work mostly from Geneva, without handling the social security side
- Net income on the best option comes out below the floor, with no equity or review to make up for it
- They push for a Senior title instead of Staff on the same scope
- The remote arrangement is "flexible" only verbally, with no commitment in writing
