# Negotiation Script, EWOR GmbH, Fintech Founding Engineer

## Market Context and a Structural Note Before Anchoring

**Role level:** Founding Engineer, 0-to-1 fintech build, reporting directly to a department founder, full remote (Europe or Americas)
**Location:** Magino based in Geneva, Switzerland; EWOR is a German GmbH (Munich)

**What EWOR actually is:** EWOR is a founder fellowship and early-stage investor built by the people behind Adjust (Paul Müller, 1.2 billion euro exit) and SumUp (Petter Made, roughly 8 billion euro valuation). Their normal model funds outside founders with up to 500k euros of capital plus mentorship. This role is different: it's an internal fintech department EWOR is building itself, with the intent to spin it out later for institutional backing. That distinction matters for negotiation, because the comp and equity structure here is closer to employee-of-a-venture-studio than founder-of-your-own-company, even though the job posting uses founder-style language like "maximum autonomy" and "high-agency builder."

**Before anchoring, get clarity on the entity structure:**
- Is the employment contract with EWOR GmbH directly, or with a to-be-formed subsidiary for the fintech vertical?
- If the department spins out, does equity carry over 1:1, get re-papered, or reset?
- Who are the other fintech-vertical hires (full-stack, AI/ML, cloud) reporting to, the same department founder or different leads?

These questions should come before a number is named, not after, since the honest anchor depends on whether this is "employee equity in an EWOR-controlled entity" or "founding equity in a venture that might not exist as a separate company for another year."

**Swiss market rates for equivalent scope (used as the anchor, not the fallback):**
- Founding/Staff-level engineer, fintech or platform-heavy, Geneva or Zurich: CHF 160,000 to 200,000 base
- Comparable early-stage, founder-adjacent technical hires at Series Seed to A in Switzerland: CHF 150,000 to 190,000 base, plus meaningful early equity
- Converted at roughly 1.05 EUR/CHF: EUR 143,000 to 190,000 equivalent

**Read on EWOR's likely band:** venture studios paying out of an internal fund rather than a funded startup's runway often anchor lower on base and higher on equity than a standard Series A company would. The opening ask should acknowledge that trade explicitly rather than pretending it isn't in play, while still holding a floor on base that reflects Geneva cost of living and 17+ years of experience including a $1B+ exit.

---

## Opening Anchor

**Target:** EUR 150,000 to 165,000 base (or CHF equivalent), plus 1% to 2.5% equity in whatever entity ultimately holds the fintech vertical, with explicit terms on what happens at spin-out

When asked for a number:

> "Given this is closer to a founding role inside a venture studio than a funded startup, I'd expect the mix to lean more on equity than a typical Series A offer would. On base, I'm anchoring around 155 to 165k euros, which reflects both Geneva cost of living and the scope of owning the architecture for a fintech build from zero. On equity, I'd want something in the 1 to 2% range in whatever entity ends up holding this vertical, with clarity on how that converts if and when it spins out."

Let them respond to the entity-structure question before conceding on the split between base and equity.

---

## Counter-Offer Scripts

**If they come in below EUR 130,000 base:**

> "I want to be direct about the gap. That number sits under what I'd expect for founding-level fintech architecture work even accounting for the studio structure, and it's well under Geneva cost of living. I'd need to see something closer to 150k to make the base work on its own, or I'd need the equity number to move meaningfully to close that gap."

**If they lean heavily on "this is a huge opportunity, equity will make up for it":**

> "I've built one of these from zero before, so I take the upside seriously, it's a real part of why this role is interesting to me. But I've also seen how much can change between an internal department and a spun-out company with its own cap table. I'd rather have a base that reflects the work now and equity that reflects the upside later, not one substituting entirely for the other."

**If they push a flat "remote-anywhere" band regardless of geography:**

> "I get wanting one consistent band for a distributed team. I'm based in Geneva, one of the more expensive cities globally, and the role is asking for the kind of ownership and hours commitment that's closer to an in-person founding role than a typical remote-anywhere job. I'd rather we price this against where I live and what the role actually asks for than a flattened global average."

**If they're firm on base and won't move it:**

Push on other levers, in this order:
1. Equity increase, an additional 0.5% to 1% if base is capped below 150k
2. Written terms on spin-out conversion, so equity isn't left to a future verbal promise
3. A defined review point at 6 months tied to shipped milestones, with a base adjustment attached
4. Signing bonus, EUR 8,000 to 12,000, to offset the lower guaranteed comp in year one

> "If base stays where it is, I'd want the equity closer to 2 to 2.5%, and I'd want the spin-out conversion terms written down now, not handled informally later once there's actual value to argue about."

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Ask for:** 1% to 2.5%, depending on how much of the fintech vertical is already funded versus still internal, and whether this is treated as employee number one for that specific venture
**Structure:** ask whether it's standard 1-year cliff, 4-year vest, or a studio-specific schedule tied to spin-out milestones, since venture studios sometimes use non-standard vesting triggers
**Questions to ask before accepting any equity number:**

- Is the equity granted in EWOR GmbH itself, or reserved for a future spun-out entity that doesn't exist yet?
- If the vertical never spins out or gets shut down, what happens to vested equity or its cash equivalent?
- What's the cap table look like for the parent entity, and does the department founder hold outsized control that would affect a future round?
- ISOs, NSOs, or virtual/phantom shares, given the GmbH structure and the German-plus-Swiss cross-border tax question?
- Is there a refresher tied to hitting the "institutional backing" milestone the job posting mentions?

**If the equity offer is vague or undocumented ("we'll figure that out once we spin out"):**

> "I'd want that in writing before I start, not after. If the plan is genuinely to spin this out for institutional backing, that's exactly the moment equity terms get renegotiated in the company's favor rather than mine, so I'd rather set the baseline now while there's nothing to fight over yet."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

> "Geneva isn't a lower cost-of-living market, it's consistently one of the five most expensive cities worldwide, closer to Zurich or San Francisco than to most of the remote-Europe band this kind of role usually assumes. I'd rather we price base against where I actually live than a regional average that doesn't reflect it."

**Data point to cite if needed:** Swiss ICT salary surveys and platforms like salary.ch put founding or Staff-level engineering roles in Geneva or Zurich at CHF 160-200k, which converts to roughly EUR 150-190k, well above a typical flattened EU-remote band.

---

## Using Competing Offers

If other conversations are genuinely active:

> "I've got other founding-engineer conversations moving in parallel, in a similar range on base with more conventional equity structures behind them. What makes EWOR interesting is the mentorship access, Paul Müller and Petter Made have both actually done the exit I'm being asked to help engineer toward, and that's not something the other options have. But the comp and the equity terms need to be clear enough on paper for that to outweigh the extra structural risk of an unspun department."

If there's no competing offer in hand, don't invent one. Fall back to market data and the structural questions instead:

> "The market data I'm working from puts founding-level fintech work in the 150 to 190k euro range on base. Given the added uncertainty of the studio structure here, I'd want that anchored on the higher end, with equity terms clear enough that I'm not negotiating twice."

---

## Timeline and Process Notes

Don't put a final number down before getting a straight answer on the entity-and-spin-out question, that answer changes whether this is a comp negotiation or partly a governance negotiation. If pressed early:

> "Before I give you a number, I want to understand whether I'd be an EWOR GmbH employee or joining a separate entity for this vertical, since that changes how I think about the equity side. Can we get that settled first?"

**Offer deadline:** ask for at least 5 business days to review a written offer, more if the equity or entity-structure documentation isn't finalized yet.

> "I want to look at the entity structure and equity terms properly before deciding, especially since this sounds like it's still being finalized on your end too. A week works on my side unless there's a hard deadline I should know about."
