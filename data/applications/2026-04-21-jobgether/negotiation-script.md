# Negotiation Script — Jobgether AI Platform Engineer

---

## Market Context

**Posted range:** $130,000-$140,000 USD + equity + bonus (fully remote)

**USD to CHF conversion (approx. 0.88):** CHF 114,000-123,000

**Geneva/Swiss market rates for this level (Staff+ AI/Platform Engineer, 2026):**
- Senior Engineer (5-8 yrs): CHF 130K-160K
- Staff/Principal Engineer (10+ yrs): CHF 150K-195K
- AI-specialized Staff Engineer: CHF 160K-210K
- Magino's level (17 yrs, Staff+, ex-founding engineer with acquisition): CHF 170K-220K

**Assessment:** The posted range is meaningfully below Swiss market rates for this experience level. The fully remote framing gives Jobgether flexibility on geography, but it doesn't change Magino's cost of living or local comp expectations. This gap needs to be addressed explicitly, ideally before an offer is made.

---

## Opening Anchor

When asked about expectations (phone screen or first interview):

> "My market rate for a Staff+ engineer with AI platform specialization in Geneva is in the CHF 170K-185K range, or roughly $190K-210K USD. I've seen the posted range and I'd want to understand whether there's flexibility there before we go too far down the process. I'm genuinely interested in the role, so I'd rather have that conversation early."

Rationale: Set the anchor high but justify it with level and geography. Framing it as "I'd rather have this conversation early" signals seriousness rather than friction.

---

## If They Push Back on Range ("Our budget is $130-140K")

**Script:**
> "I hear you. The range you've posted converts to around CHF 115-123K, and for someone at Senior Staff level in Geneva with a track record of shipping AI systems in production, that's below where I'd need to be. Is there flexibility in equity or bonus structure that could bridge the gap? And is the range firm, or is it a starting point?"

**Then listen.** If they say the range is firm:
> "Can you help me understand the comp philosophy? Is this range set because you're benchmarking against European markets, or against a single US/global band? I want to understand the structure before I respond."

---

## Counter-Offer Script (If Offer Comes In at $130-135K)

> "Thank you for the offer. I've thought about it, and I want to be direct: the base comes in meaningfully below my current market rate for this level. I'm at CHF 165K-175K equivalent in my current comp, and I'd need to be at $155K-165K USD to make the move financially rational. Is there room to move on the base, or alternatively to increase the equity component to offset the gap?"

Key principle: give a specific number, not a range. "I need $160K" is stronger than "I'd want somewhere in the $155-165K range."

---

## Equity Negotiation

Jobgether's stage is unclear, but they mention "equity and bonus opportunities."

**Opening ask:** Request the equity breakdown before accepting any offer:
> "Can you share the equity structure? I'd want to understand the grant size, vesting schedule, strike price, and the last 409A or preferred share price so I can model the value."

**If they offer options:**
- Aim for 0.15-0.40% depending on stage (seed/Series A vs. Series C+)
- Standard: 4-year vest with 1-year cliff
- Ask about acceleration clauses: "Is there any single or double-trigger acceleration on change of control?"
- Ask about exercise window: standard is 90 days post-departure; push for 5-10 years if they're pre-IPO

**Script if the equity offer feels thin:**
> "The option grant feels a bit light for a high-ownership, Staff-level role. Most companies at this stage offering this kind of scope are at 0.2-0.4%. Can we revisit the grant size?"

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they try to apply a "location-based" discount because Magino isn't in a high-cost US city:

> "I'm in Geneva, which is consistently ranked among the top five most expensive cities in the world. If anything, a Geneva-based engineer is absorbing the cost premium, not the company. I'd push back on any logic that applies a discount here."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have (or can create) a competing process:

> "I should mention I'm in late stages with another company. I'd prefer Jobgether, the problem is more interesting to me, but I'd need the comp to be in a comparable range to move quickly. If we can get to $160K base and a meaningful equity refresh, I'm ready to close this week."

Use this only if it's true or nearly true. A specific competing offer number is stronger than a vague reference.

---

## Bonus Negotiation

If a signing bonus is available:
> "Given there's a gap between where I am now and your base range, would a signing bonus help bridge that? Even a one-time payment of $15-20K would make the transition more straightforward."

Signing bonuses are often easier to approve than permanent base increases.

---

## Timing

- Don't accept on the call. "I'll need 48-72 hours to review this properly" is always legitimate.
- If they push for a faster answer: "I want to say yes, which is why I want to make sure I'm not missing anything in the offer. Can I have until [specific date]?"
