# Negotiation Script — Supabase, Platform Engineer (Multicloud)

---

## Market Context

**Role level:** Senior/Staff Platform Engineer (8+ years required, infra/multicloud specialization)

**Supabase specifics:**
- Series C-stage company, well-funded, fully remote
- No offices; provides WeWork/co-working allowance globally
- US-headquartered, pays in USD or local currency depending on location
- Fully remote means comp may be location-adjusted; Geneva is expensive — push back on any attempt to apply a "Switzerland discount" (cost of living in Geneva is higher than most US tech hubs)

**Switzerland market rate for this level (2025-2026):**
- Staff Platform/Infrastructure Engineer, Geneva market: CHF 160,000–220,000 base
- Senior SWE with infra specialization at a funded startup: CHF 140,000–180,000
- Remote roles paying USD at Geneva-equivalent: $160,000–$200,000 base (USD, US-adjusted)
- Supabase is likely to offer in USD given US HQ; expect $150,000–$190,000 as their range

---

## Opening Anchor

**When they ask for your number first:**

> "I'm looking at the CHF 190,000 range for base, which is consistent with Staff-level infrastructure roles in the Geneva market. I'm flexible on the exact structure if there's meaningful equity in play, but I'd rather start there and work backwards from what the role actually looks like day to day."

**If they push back immediately:**

> "I've looked at what Staff Platform Engineers are earning in Switzerland and in comparable US remote roles, and the range I gave reflects that research. What's the base you're working with? I'd rather know the real number than negotiate against a range neither of us believes."

---

## If They Come In Low (below CHF 160k / $155k USD)

**Script:**

> "That's below what I was expecting based on the market and the scope of the role. I've been a founding engineer, I've shipped multicloud re-platforming work in production, and I'm coming with eight years of senior platform experience. I'd need to see a path to at least [CHF 170k / $165k] on the base side, or a meaningful equity grant that changes the math."

**Do not accept the first number in the same call.** Ask for 24-48 hours to consider and come back with a counter.

---

## Equity Negotiation (Startup-Specific)

Supabase is well-funded (Series C) but pre-IPO. Options are relevant.

**Questions to ask:**
- What is the current valuation / last round valuation?
- What percentage of the company does my grant represent? (Insist on this number, not just the raw shares)
- What is the vesting schedule? Standard 4-year/1-year cliff?
- Is there a secondary market or any liquidity provision for employees?
- What happens to unvested equity in an acquisition? Is there single-trigger or double-trigger acceleration?

**Framing the equity ask:**

> "I'm treating the equity as real compensation, not lottery tickets. That means I need to understand the dilution history and the current cap table situation well enough to make a judgment. If the option grant represents less than 0.1% of the company at current valuation, I'd want to see a higher base to compensate for the illiquidity risk."

**Target for a role at this level:** 0.05%–0.15% equity, 4-year vest, 1-year cliff. Push for a refresher grant schedule.

---

## Geneva Cost-of-Living Pushback

If they try to apply a location adjustment downward:

> "I understand the idea of location-based pay, but Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in the world — it consistently ranks above San Francisco and New York in cost of living. If the company is using a 'Geneva discount' because Switzerland is not the US, that's the wrong frame. The right comparison is what a Senior Staff engineer costs in a comparable market, and Geneva doesn't come cheap."

**Data points to cite:**
- UBS Cost of Living 2024: Geneva ranks in the top 5 most expensive cities globally
- Housing in Geneva averages CHF 2,500–4,000/month for a 2-bedroom
- Swiss social security contributions (AVS/AI/APG) reduce net compensation significantly vs. US

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or are in process elsewhere):

> "I want to be straightforward with you: I have another process moving quickly with [company/role type]. I'm more interested in Supabase because of the technical scope, but I can't justify taking a significant compensation step down. If you can meet me at [number], I'm ready to move forward."

If you don't have a competing offer but are in early stages elsewhere:

> "I'm actively in process with a few other companies at this stage. Nothing signed, but I don't want to string you along — if the comp is in the right range, Supabase is where I'd rather be."

---

## Benefits to Negotiate Beyond Base

- **Equipment budget**: Supabase typically provides $1,500–$3,000 home office setup
- **Co-working allowance**: Push for a named Geneva co-working space if the WeWork network doesn't cover it well
- **Professional development**: Request CHF 3,000–5,000/year for conferences, courses, or certifications
- **Start date flexibility**: 2–4 weeks is standard; if you're serving notice, 6–8 weeks is reasonable to request

---

## What Not to Say

- Don't accept verbally in the same call where you receive the offer
- Don't give a range when asked for your number — ranges get rounded to the bottom
- Don't mention the word "need" (as in "I need X") — frame everything in terms of market, not personal necessity
- Don't apologize for negotiating
