# Negotiation Script: Founding Engineer, Backend & Platform, Clera

## Context to keep in mind

Clera is pre-seed, reportedly around 3 million dollars raised, building AI-native operations infrastructure for private markets: fund accounting, reconciliation, and treasury automation across dozens of client firms. At this stage, cash is scarce and the founders are almost certainly optimizing for runway, so the real negotiation is less about squeezing base salary and more about equity percentage, vesting terms, and title or scope that reflects near-CTO level ownership. Some of Clera's other listings mention an SF hackerhouse with visa sponsorship; this specific Backend & Platform role doesn't state that explicitly, so confirming location expectations, on-site versus remote, and whether Geneva-based remote work is even on the table needs to happen before comp gets serious.

## Switzerland market anchors (protect the floor, don't lead with these)

Geneva isn't the natural anchor here since Clera is a US pre-seed startup, but it's still worth having Swiss numbers in your back pocket if remote-from-Geneva becomes part of the deal. A Staff or Principal-level backend engineer in Geneva with founding-level scope typically sits in the CHF 160k to 190k range, and cofounder-level platform ownership on a resume tends to push toward the top of that band or above it. If Clera wants someone remote from Switzerland rather than relocated to SF, that's a legitimate point to raise: a founding engineer working from a high cost-of-living market shouldn't be paid a generic remote-anywhere rate, since the alternative for Clera is paying SF-level cash plus SF-level equity for the same seat.

## Opening anchor

Wait for a number before naming one, but if forced to go first:

"For a founding engineer role at pre-seed, doing near-CTO level backend and platform work, I'd expect a base in the 140 to 160k dollar range if I'm remote, or SF market rate for the role plus a founding-team equity grant if relocation is expected, along with enough equity to reflect that I'm effectively building the engineering org's technical foundation, not joining an existing one."

## If they come in low on cash

"I get that pre-seed runway is tight, and I'm not looking to be the reason you burn faster than planned. If base has to stay where it is, I'd want that reflected in the equity grant, in vesting terms, or in a defined comp step-up tied to the next raise, since otherwise I'm the one absorbing the gap through sweat equity without anything written down about when that gets revisited."

Fallback if cash truly cannot move: ask for equity meaningfully above whatever the first offer is, a shorter cliff, acceleration on acquisition or change of control, and a scheduled comp review at six months tied to hitting early design-partner milestones.

## Equity and cap table questions

Ask for the fully diluted share count and the valuation the last raise was priced at, not just a bare percentage, since 1 percent of a 3 million dollar pre-seed round means something completely different once a seed or Series A round dilutes everyone. Confirm the standard one-year cliff and four-year vesting, and ask directly whether there's acceleration on acquisition, given that's exactly how the Alaya story ended and it's a reasonable thing to plan for rather than assume. Ask how many months of runway the 3 million covers and when the next raise is targeted, since that shapes both how much the equity might dilute soon and how real the near-term cash constraints actually are. If there are other engineers already on the team, ask what their equity terms looked like, to gauge whether the offer on the table is a real starting point or already the ceiling.

## Location and remote leverage

Before comp gets finalized, get a straight answer on whether this role requires SF relocation, sponsored or otherwise, or whether remote from Geneva is genuinely viable for a founding engineer position. If relocation is expected and isn't the plan, that's worth naming early rather than letting it surface after an offer is drafted: "I want to be upfront that I'm based in Geneva and my strong preference is to stay here, remote or with periodic travel to SF. If that changes how the role or the comp gets scoped, better to know now than after we're both invested in this."

## Competing offer template

Use only if a real competing process exists. Never invent a number or company name.

"I'm also in later-stage conversations for a comparable founding or staff-level engineering role. I'd rather build this with you given the problem you're solving, so if there's room on base, equity, or vesting, this is the point where I'd want to know before deciding."
