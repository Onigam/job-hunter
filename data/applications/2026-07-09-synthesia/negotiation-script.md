Negotiation Script: Synthesia, ML Platform Engineer

## Context worth naming up front

Synthesia is a London-headquartered company, not a Swiss one, and the posting reads as a global remote-friendly role rather than a Zurich-office-only one, even though a Zurich office is opening this year. That means the anchor should probably reference London/European AI-lab comp rather than generic Swiss ML salary surveys, since Synthesia is competing for platform talent against other well-funded AI companies, not against generic Geneva employers. The role is also explicitly a "systems generalist" seat rather than a research ML seat, which typically prices a notch below research-scientist comp but above a generic backend or SRE role at the same company. Worth confirming early whether the Zurich office changes the comp band at all, or whether it's purely optional and pay stays benchmarked to the existing London/remote structure.

## Market anchors

Public data on Synthesia software engineer total comp in London clusters roughly GBP 75,000 to 200,000 depending on level, with a median around GBP 107,000 to 111,000, which likely undershoots what a senior platform hire commands given the company just closed a $200 million Series E at a $4 billion valuation. Staff-level platform engineers at funded AI companies in this range typically land closer to the top quartile of that band or above it once equity and the seniority of the role, cross-team architecture ownership plus GPU/production ML infra, is factored in. A reasonable anchor for a senior or staff-scoped ML Platform Engineer, adjusted for Swiss cost of living and Magino's Staff-level track record, sits around CHF 190,000 to 210,000 base equivalent, or the GBP equivalent if paid off the UK entity, with equity layered on top given the company's growth stage.

## Opening anchor

"Before I put a number on the table, I want to understand two things: whether this role is scoped closer to senior or to staff given the cross-team architecture ownership implied in the posting, and whether comp is benchmarked against the London structure or something else for the Zurich-adjacent hire. That'll tell me where to anchor."

If pushed for a number regardless: "Given the scope described, platform architecture across research and product, GPU infrastructure ownership, and the seniority that implies, I'd want to start around CHF 195,000 to 210,000 base, with equity discussed separately."

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

If the offer lands meaningfully under that range: "I appreciate the offer, and I want to flag that this reads closer to a mid-level infra band than what the posting describes, cross-team architecture ownership across research and product isn't a mid-level scope. Given where the company is post-Series E, I was expecting something closer to CHF 195,000 or above. Is there room to move on base, or is part of the gap something equity or a sign-on could close?"

If they point to the GPU/serving depth gap to justify a lower number: "That's fair to name directly. My depth is in platform and distributed systems at scale, not GPU scheduling or CUDA-level serving work specifically, and that's ground I'd close fast rather than arrive with already mastered. I'd rather agree on what ownership looks like at 90 days and be evaluated against that than take a permanently discounted number for a gap that's meant to close."

## Equity negotiation

Ask directly what the strike price reflects, the recent $4 billion Series E valuation or an earlier, cheaper round, since a late secondary sale suggests the company takes vested equity liquidity seriously, which is a good sign but doesn't answer the strike price question on its own. Confirm standard four-year vesting with a one-year cliff, and push if it's longer than that. Ask about the post-termination exercise window, since anything meaningfully under ten years can force an expensive early-exercise decision later. Given the company just did a secondary sale, ask whether that precedent extends to future liquidity events or was a one-off tied to the Series E close.

## Geographic and structure pushback

If comp gets anchored down on the assumption that Zurich or Geneva is cheaper than London: "Geneva isn't a discount market, cost of living here runs close to or above London, and the role's scope doesn't shrink because I'm not sitting in the London office. I'd rather anchor on ownership and the platform work than on a location assumption that doesn't hold up."

If the "not a pure ML engineer role" framing gets used to justify a lower band than research hires get: "I understand the role is scoped as systems generalist rather than research, and that's actually the work I want. I'd push back on treating that as a lower-value seat though, since platform reliability is what lets the research and product work ship at all, and comp should reflect that."

## Competing offer template

Use only if a real competing process exists.

"I'm further along in another process at a comparable level, with a base offer in a similar range to what we've discussed here. I'd rather land this one, the mandate of letting research and product actually ship generative models reliably is the more interesting problem, but the offer needs to be close enough that I'm not leaving something real behind."
