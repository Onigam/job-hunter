# Negotiation Script — Scait / Founding Engineer

## Context

- **Posted range:** CHF 105,000 - 125,000 base
- **Location:** Suurstoffi 18b, Rotkreuz (Zug canton), 100% in-office presumably
- **Stage:** Pre-seed, oversubscribed round closed June 2026 (high six-figure CHF). First paying customers. DACH expansion planned.
- **Key factors:** The posted base is significantly below market for someone with 17+ years of experience and a founding-to-acquisition track record. Equity is the main lever at this stage. The salary gap must be addressed directly or compensated through meaningful equity.

---

## Phase 1: Before You Have an Offer

**Goal:** Understand equity expectations and team structure before the offer call.

Ask in the first or second conversation, naturally:

> "Quick question on the structure before we go further: where does the founding engineer fit on the cap table? I want to understand whether this is a title without the equity that normally goes with it, or whether you're genuinely thinking of this as a founding-team role in terms of ownership."

This surfaces whether they've thought seriously about equity or whether it's an afterthought. Their answer tells you more about the role than the job description does.

Also ask:

> "Who's on the engineering team right now? I want to understand what I'd be walking into, technically, on day one."

## Phase 2: The Offer Call

### Baseline read on the posted range

CHF 105,000-125,000 is below market for this profile. For context:

- Senior/Staff Engineer in Switzerland (Zurich/Zug area): CHF 140,000-175,000 base
- Founding Engineer at an early-stage startup: typically 10-20% below top-of-market base in exchange for meaningful equity
- Magino's profile (co-founder, 17+ years, unicorn exit) justifies the top of the market range

The posted ceiling of CHF 125,000 represents roughly a 25-30% discount on fair market rate. That discount only makes sense if the equity is genuinely meaningful.

### Opening anchor (if they ask your number first)

Don't give a number first. Say:

> "I'd rather hear what you have in mind. You know the budget and the equity structure better than I do, and I don't want to anchor without the full picture."

If they push hard for a number before sharing theirs:

> "For a founding engineer seat with this scope, I'd be looking at CHF 145,000-155,000 base plus meaningful equity. I'm genuinely open to adjusting the base downward if the equity reflects the founding-team nature of the role, but I need to understand what that looks like first."

### If they open at CHF 105,000-115,000

That's the low end. Don't counter immediately:

> "I appreciate the offer. Before I respond, can you walk me through the equity component in detail? I want to make sure I'm looking at the full picture before I react to the base."

After hearing the equity terms, counter:

> "Here's my read. The base is below what I'd expect for this scope and seniority, probably by CHF 25,000-30,000 relative to market. I'm willing to accept a discount on the base for the right equity number, because I understand you're pre-series A and cash is constrained. But for that trade to make sense I'd need to see at least 0.8%-1.2% on the cap table with standard 4-year vest and a 1-year cliff. Can you work with that?"

### If they open at CHF 120,000-125,000

Closer to acceptable. Negotiate on equity and possibly a performance-based review:

> "The base is workable if the equity reflects the founding-engineer framing. I'd want to land at 0.8%-1% minimum, with a conversation about a salary review at 12 months once the Series A closes. What can you do on those two points?"

## Phase 3: Equity Deep-Dive

At pre-seed in Switzerland, equity is often structured as options under a ESOP (Employee Stock Option Plan) or via a participation agreement. Swiss startups are increasingly using ESOP structures, but verify the mechanics.

### Questions to ask

1. **What percentage of the company is in the ESOP pool?** Typical pre-seed ESOP pool is 10-15%. If the pool is 10% and you're getting 1%, you hold 1% of that pool, not 1% of the company. Clarify.

2. **What is the current pre-money valuation?** A 1% stake matters very differently at a CHF 2M vs. CHF 8M valuation.

3. **What's the vesting schedule?** Standard is 4-year vest with 1-year cliff. Ask explicitly.

4. **Are these stock options or phantom equity?** Phantom equity pays out like equity on exit but you never actually own shares. It's fine but worth knowing.

5. **Is there an acceleration clause on acquisition?** Single-trigger (you get all unvested shares immediately on acquisition) or double-trigger (you get them only if you're also terminated)? Double-trigger is more common and less favorable for employees.

6. **What is the exercise price?** At pre-seed the strike price should be very low. Get this in writing.

7. **What does the liquidation preference stack look like?** 1x non-participating preferred is standard and employee-friendly. 2x or participating preferred stacks against you in a modest exit.

### Equity counter-script

> "I want to make sure we're aligned on the equity structure. For a founding engineer joining this early, I'd expect 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff, a strike price set at fair market value at grant date (which should be very low at pre-seed), and clarity on whether these are options or phantom equity. Can you walk me through the current ESOP pool size and how my grant is sized relative to it, not just the percentage headline?"

## Phase 4: Geographic and Cost-of-Living Context

Rotkreuz is in Zug canton, one of the most expensive areas in Switzerland. While Zug city has a lower cost of living than Geneva, Rotkreuz specifically sits between Zug and Lucerne in a commuting zone where housing costs are substantial. If commuting from Geneva:

- A daily Rotkreuz commute from Geneva is not realistic (2.5-3 hours each way). This needs a conversation about remote days or relocation.
- If relocation from Geneva to the Rotkreuz/Zug area is required, housing costs increase materially.

Use this as context if they push back on base salary:

> "Worth noting that Rotkreuz/Zug area housing runs comparable to or above Geneva for most of the rental market. The cost-of-living context supports a base in the CHF 135,000-145,000 range for this role level."

## Phase 5: Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in process:

> "I want to be upfront: I'm in final stages with one other company and expect an offer this week. Scait is the more interesting technical problem for me right now, so I'd like to resolve this first if possible. If you can move to CHF 135,000 base and 1% equity, I'm ready to have a serious conversation."

If you don't have a competing offer, use timeline instead:

> "I'm trying to make a decision by end of this week. Is there a way to move quickly on final terms?"

---

## Phase 6: Accept / Walk Away

### Accept at:
- CHF 128,000+ base with 0.8%+ equity (4/1 vest, standard terms)
- CHF 120,000-128,000 base with 1.0%+ equity and a 12-month salary review clause
- Any base with meaningful equity if the pre-money valuation is above CHF 5M (making 1% worth CHF 50,000+ pre-Series A dilution)

### Walk away if:
- Base is below CHF 115,000 with no movement and equity is below 0.5%
- Equity is phantom-only with no path to actual ownership
- No acceleration clause and vesting cliff longer than 12 months
- They are unable or unwilling to explain the cap table clearly

Scait's posted range of CHF 105,000-125,000 is at the bottom of the founding engineer bracket and only justifies itself with equity above 0.75%. The combination of below-market base plus real equity is a reasonable deal; below-market base plus thin equity is not.
