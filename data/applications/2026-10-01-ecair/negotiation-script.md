# Negotiation Script: Ecair, Staff Engineer (first Platform hire)

## Context that shapes the numbers

Ecair is a Paris fintech founded in May 2023 by Maxime Bensadoun (ex-Revolut, ex-Lydia) and Victor Jolly (ex-COO of Lydia). It finances energy renovation and solar work: credit for households through installers, and advances on MaPrimeRénov' subsidies for installers. In late 2025 it raised about €11M (roughly €5M equity from Übermorgen, Planet A, Kima, Bpifrance and others, plus about €6M of debt from Enerfip). Early 2026 press put the team at around 23 people working with ~200 installer companies.

What that means for the negotiation:

- The posting gives no salary range. It's a seed-stage French company, so the cash budget will be set against Paris, not Geneva.
- The role is in Paris and listed as "Cadre". Magino lives in Geneva. Location is the biggest variable in this offer, bigger than the base number, and it should be settled before talking money (see the cross-border section).
- Founders who came from Lydia and Revolut know what fintech engineers cost. Expect a well-prepared counterpart, not a naive one.
- Equity will be BSPCE. At this stage, for the first platform hire and a Staff title, equity is a real part of the package, so it deserves as much time as the base.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

These are approximate figures from public salary surveys and recruiter data, not hard numbers. Re-check on levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Welcome to the Jungle, the Michael Page and Hays France/Switzerland guides, and Figures.hr for French startups before the first conversation.

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Staff Engineer, Paris seed/Series A startup | €95k to 125k |
| Staff Engineer, well-funded Paris fintech scale-up (Qonto, Alan, Swile tier) | €120k to 150k |
| Senior Staff / Principal, Paris scale-up | €140k to 170k |
| Staff Engineer, Geneva | CHF 180k to 230k |
| Staff / Principal Engineer, Zurich | CHF 200k to 260k |
| Senior Staff Engineer, Geneva (current reference point) | CHF 200k to 250k |

Typical BSPCE grant for a Staff-level early hire at a ~25 to 40 person seed company: around 0.25% to 0.75% fully diluted, with the higher end for someone founding a team rather than joining one.

**Target (Paris-based contract):** €125k to 135k base, plus 0.5% to 0.8% in BSPCE.
**Opening anchor:** €140k base.
**Walk-away floor:** €115k base, and only with equity at the top of the range plus a remote arrangement that keeps life in Geneva workable.

If they offer a Swiss-compatible setup instead (Geneva-based, mostly remote), the anchor moves to CHF 190k with a floor of CHF 165k, and the equity expectation stays the same.

## Cross-border and remote: settle this first

Raise it in the first call with Julien, not at the offer stage. Questions to ask:

1. "Is the role open to someone based in Geneva coming to Paris regularly, say one or two weeks a month, or do you need a full Paris presence?"
2. "If it's mostly remote from Geneva, would you contract through a French entity, an employer of record in Switzerland, or something else?"
3. "Who covers travel between Geneva and Paris?"

Things to verify with an advisor before signing anything (don't assume):

- Under the EU/Swiss social security rules, someone living in Switzerland who works a substantial share of their time there (the usual threshold is 25%, with a framework agreement allowing up to 49% for telework) may stay in the Swiss system, and the French employer then has obligations in Switzerland. Small startups often don't know this and may balk once they find out. Bring it up early so it isn't a surprise.
- Income tax treatment of a Geneva resident paid by a French employer depends on where the work is physically done. Get a short written opinion from a cross-border tax advisor.
- BSPCE have favourable French tax treatment that may not carry over in the same way to a Swiss tax resident. Check how gains would be taxed in Geneva.

## Opening anchor script

"Before we get to numbers, I'd like to align on location, because that drives everything else. Assuming we agree on a Paris contract with a hybrid rhythm from Geneva, I'm looking at a base around €140k, plus BSPCE that reflect being the first platform hire rather than a later addition. That's at the top of the Paris Staff range, and I think it's justified: you're asking one person to set up the platform team, define how coding agents ship to production safely and own the partner API, and I've done the zero-to-acquisition journey once already. How does that compare with what you'd budgeted?"

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low

**Script A, base below range (for example €105k to 115k):**
"Thanks for being straightforward. That's a solid number for a senior engineer joining an existing team, but this role founds the team. I'd need the base closer to €130k. If cash is the constraint at your stage, which I understand after a seed round, I'm open to closing part of the gap with more BSPCE, as long as the strike price and the exercise window are in writing."

**Script B, they say the band is fixed:**
"Understood. Then let's work on the other parts of the package. Could we agree a review at six months against concrete milestones, for example the platform team staffed and a shipping standard for agent-authored PRs in place, with a defined step-up in base if they're met? And I'd want the equity to carry more of the weight."

**Script C, they push back on remote:**
"I get why you'd want the first platform engineer close to the product teams, and I'd plan to be in Paris regularly, especially in the first months. Full relocation isn't something I can do right now. If presence matters that much, let's talk about how often and who covers the travel, rather than making it all or nothing."

## Equity: what to ask and what to push for

Questions first, so you can value the grant:

- "How many shares are outstanding on a fully diluted basis, so I can understand the percentage?"
- "What was the price per share in the last round, and what will the BSPCE strike price be?"
- "What's the vesting schedule and cliff?" (expect 4 years with a 1-year cliff)
- "What happens to unvested BSPCE on an acquisition? Is there single or double-trigger acceleration?"
- "How long do I have to exercise if I leave?" (the French default is often short; ask for at least several years, which some Paris startups now offer)
- "Is there a refresh policy for early employees after the next round?"

Points to push on, in this order:

1. **Size.** First platform hire plus Staff title justifies the top of the 0.25% to 0.75% band. Anchor at 0.8% and settle around 0.6%.
2. **Exercise window.** A longer post-departure window costs the company little and protects real value.
3. **Acceleration.** Double-trigger acceleration on change of control is a fair ask. The Alaya acquisition is a concrete reason to care about it.
4. **Cliff.** If the start date gets pushed back because of a notice period or cross-border paperwork, ask for the cliff to run from the signature date.

Anti-dilution protection isn't something employees get in France, and asking for it reads as inexperienced. Ask about refresh grants after the Series A instead.

## Geographic discount pushback

They may say Paris salaries are lower, so a Geneva number doesn't apply.

"That's fair for a Paris-based role, and my anchor is already a Paris number, not a Geneva one. What I'm asking for is the top of the Paris Staff range, because the scope is founding a team, not filling a seat. If you'd rather I work mostly from Geneva, then the cost of living and the Swiss social security obligations both come into it, and we should look at that setup on its own terms."

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

"I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations for a Staff platform role at a company based in Switzerland, and they've indicated a base around CHF [X]. Ecair is my first choice because of the problem and the way your team ships with coding agents, but I need the package to be in a reasonable range. Is there room to move on the base or the equity?"

Never invent a competing offer. If there isn't one, use the market data above instead.

## Final checklist before signing

- Location, remote rhythm and travel costs written into the contract
- Contracting entity and social security setup confirmed, ideally checked by an advisor
- Base, any bonus and the six-month review clause in writing
- BSPCE: number, percentage fully diluted, strike price, vesting, cliff start date, acceleration, exercise window
- Notice period ("préavis") and any non-compete clause; in France a non-compete must be paid to be valid, so check the terms
- Start date that leaves time for the cross-border paperwork
