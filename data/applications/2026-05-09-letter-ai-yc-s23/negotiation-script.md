# Negotiation Script — Letter AI Founding Engineer (Frontend)

## Context

**Listed range:** $100K-$150K USD + 0.25%-1.50% equity
**Company stage:** 12 people, YC S23, Pioneer Fund backing, customers include Lenovo and Redpanda
**Your situation:** Geneva-based, open to remote. 17 years of experience, including a founding engineer role that resulted in a unicorn acquisition.

**Note on visa:** The listing says "US citizen or visa holder only." Clarify this early. If they're open to a fully remote European contractor arrangement, the compensation framing shifts (no benefits overhead for them, but you'd need to negotiate gross accordingly).

---

## Opening Anchor

**Target:** $150K USD base + 1.0%-1.5% equity

You have substantially more experience than the 3+ years they listed. You've been a founding engineer before, the product works, and you know exactly what "owning the frontend codebase" means at a 12-person company. You're not a candidate who needs to grow into the role.

**Opening line when they ask your expectation:**

"Based on the scope and what I know about YC-backed companies at this stage, I'm thinking around $150K base. On equity I'd want to be in the 1% range given I'd be a founding team member. Does that work as a starting point?"

Keep it matter-of-fact. Don't apologize for the number.

---

## If They Come In Below $130K

Don't accept on the spot. Use this:

"I appreciate the offer. The role is genuinely interesting to me, but at $[their number] I'd need to think hard about how this compares to my current situation. Is there room to get to $140K? Or if the base is fixed, could we offset that on the equity side, moving from [their number]% toward 1%?"

Give them one clean path forward. If they say the range is firm, ask for time to think, then decide whether equity makes up the gap.

---

## Equity Negotiation

For a 12-person company post-YC, 0.5%-1.5% is reasonable for a founding engineer. Aim for the top half.

**Key questions to ask before signing:**
- What's the current valuation cap or most recent 409A valuation? (This determines what 1% is actually worth today)
- What's the vesting schedule? Standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff, but ask explicitly.
- Is there double-trigger acceleration on acquisition? (You've been through an acquisition, you know this matters)
- What's the fully diluted share count, and have there been previous option pool increases?

**If they push back on equity %:**

"I've been through an acquisition before on the founding team side, so I have a clear view of what equity means in practice. Given that I'd be taking on the full frontend ownership from day one, I want the stake to reflect that. Could we look at 1%?"

---

## Remote from Geneva: How to Frame It

This is an advantage for them (no benefits overhead, no payroll complexity if structured as a contractor) and a potential discount pressure point for you. Pre-empt it:

"I'm set up to work as an independent contractor through my Swiss entity, which actually simplifies things on your end: no US employment overhead. The tradeoff is I'd need the compensation to reflect that, since I'm covering my own benefits and contributions. $150K gross as a contractor is closer to $120K equivalent in employment terms."

If they try to apply a "remote discount" because you're not in SF or Chicago:

"I understand the argument, but the work is the same regardless of timezone. I've collaborated with US teams for years and it works well. The location shouldn't change the value of the role."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have or can reasonably expect another offer:

"I want to be straightforward: I'm in conversations with a couple of other companies, one of which is also at a similar stage. I'd prefer to close things here because the product problem is interesting, but I need the offer to be competitive. Can you move on [base / equity]?"

Don't invent a competing offer. But if you're actively interviewing elsewhere, this is accurate and worth stating.

---

## Closing

Once you have a number you can accept:

"That works for me. Let's move forward."

No further negotiation after acceptance. Get the offer letter in writing before giving notice anywhere.
