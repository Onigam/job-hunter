# Negotiation Script — Carma / Founding Head of Engineering

## Context

- **Posted range:** $200K-$250K base + 3.00%-5.00% equity
- **Location:** San Francisco, in-person (Magino is currently in Geneva)
- **Stage:** YC W24, $5.5M seed, targeting Series A in 2026
- **Key factors:** This is a founding-level role with meaningful equity; the comp range is respectable for SF but the equity is the main long-term driver. The in-person SF requirement is the main friction point to address.

---

## Phase 1: Before You Have an Offer

**Goal:** Surface the location question early without making it a dealbreaker.

Raise it in the first call, not the offer call. Something like:

> "Quick practical question before we go further: I'm currently based in Geneva. I'm open to discussing what the right arrangement looks like, but I wanted to flag it early rather than waste anyone's time. Is full relocation to SF a hard requirement, or is there flexibility on a hybrid arrangement while the team is still small?"

If they say full relocation is required, you have two options: commit to it (with a relocation package as part of the negotiation) or decline gracefully. Don't pretend you'll relocate if you won't.

If they say there's flexibility, get that in writing before signing anything.

---

## Phase 2: The Offer Call

### Baseline read on the posted range

$200K-$250K is realistic for a Head of Engineering at seed stage in San Francisco but on the lean side for someone who has founded a company that was acquired. SF Staff/VP Engineering roles at this level typically run $220K-$300K base. The equity is what makes or breaks this deal.

### Opening anchor (if they ask your number first)

Don't give a number first if you can avoid it. Say:

> "I'd rather hear what you have in mind for someone at this level. You know the budget better than I do, and I don't want to anchor low by accident."

If they push: anchor at $270K base, equity at the top of the posted range (5%), plus a relocation package of $25K-$40K if relocation is required.

### If they open at $200K-$210K

That's the low end. Respond without countering immediately:

> "I appreciate the offer. Before I respond, can you walk me through the equity component and the vesting structure? I want to make sure I'm looking at the full picture."

After getting equity details, counter:

> "Here's where I land. Given my background as a founding engineer who took a platform from zero to acquisition, and the scope of what you're describing, I think $240K-$250K base is the right number. On equity I'd want to be at 4.5%-5% given the stage and what I'd be taking on. Is there room to move on either of those?"

### If they open at $230K-$250K

Closer to the right number. Negotiate on equity and relocation:

> "The base works. I'd like to lock in 4.5%-5% equity, and if relocation is required, I'd want to discuss a package to cover the move and a reasonable setup period. What can you do on those two points?"

---

## Phase 3: Equity Deep-Dive

For a YC seed stage company, the equity mechanics matter as much as the percentage.

### Questions to ask

1. **What is the current cap table valuation?** A 4% stake in a company valued at $10M is $400K. At a $30M post-Series A valuation that 4% will be diluted, so understand what dilution looks like at Series A.

2. **What's the vesting schedule?** Standard is 4-year vest with 1-year cliff. Anything shorter on the cliff is a red flag; anything longer than 4 years on total vest is unusual.

3. **Are the options ISOs or NSOs?** ISOs (Incentive Stock Options) are more tax-advantaged. Ask for ISOs if they offer NSOs.

4. **What's the exercise price and strike price?** Early exercise capability (83(b) election) can be valuable if the strike price is still low.

5. **Do they have any anti-dilution protection or pro-rata rights for employees?** Unlikely at this stage but worth asking.

6. **What's the liquidation preference on investor shares?** A 1x non-participating preferred is standard and employee-friendly. 2x or participating preferred stacks against you in a modest exit.

### Equity counter-script

> "I want to make sure we're aligned on the equity. At seed stage with a $5.5M raise, I'd expect the strike price is still low, which is great. What I want to confirm is: 4-year vest, 1-year cliff, ISO treatment, and early exercise rights so I can make an 83(b) election. Can you confirm those terms? And what does the dilution model look like at Series A? That helps me think about what 4% means in practice."

---

## Phase 4: Relocation (if required)

This is the biggest variable in this deal. If they require full SF relocation:

**Minimum to ask for:**
- $30K relocation package (flights, temporary housing, moving costs)
- 90-day overlap period (don't start until you have housing sorted)
- Remote work for the first 2-4 weeks while settling in

**Frame it this way:**
> "I'm genuinely open to relocating. To make that work practically, I'd need a relocation package in the $30K-$40K range to cover the transition, and a start date that gives me time to close things out properly in Switzerland. What does your onboarding timeline look like?"

If they push back on relocation support: use it as leverage on base salary.
> "If the relocation package isn't something you can do, I'd need to adjust the base to account for the out-of-pocket transition costs. That puts me closer to $260K to make the math work."

---

## Phase 5: Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or are deep in process elsewhere), use it:

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in final stages with one other company and I expect an offer this week. Carma is my top choice for this type of role, but I want to make sure the numbers reflect the scope before I make a decision. If you can move to $250K base and 5% equity, I'm ready to sign."

If you don't have a competing offer, don't fabricate one. Use timeline pressure instead:

> "I'm trying to make a decision by end of this week. Is there a way to move quickly on the final terms so we don't lose time?"

---

## Phase 6: What to Accept / Walk Away From

### Accept at:
- $240K+ base
- 4% equity minimum (5% preferred)
- Standard 4/1 vesting, ISOs, early exercise
- Relocation package if in-person SF is required

### Walk away if:
- Base is below $220K with no movement
- Equity is below 3% with any ambiguity on vesting
- They insist on SF in-person with no relocation support and won't budge on base
- Vesting cliff is longer than 12 months or vest period is longer than 4 years

---

## Compensation Context: SF Market vs. Geneva

For reference, Geneva market for a VP/Head of Engineering at this level runs CHF 180K-220K base with no equity. SF seed-stage offers typically run $200K-$260K base with 1%-5% equity. This Carma offer sits at the top of the equity range, which is genuinely unusual at seed stage. That equity is the reason this role is interesting even with the relocation requirement, and it should be protected aggressively in negotiations.
