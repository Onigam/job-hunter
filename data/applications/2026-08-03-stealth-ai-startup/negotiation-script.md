# Negotiation Script: Founding Engineer - Product Architect, Stealth AI Startup

## Context to keep in mind

This is a seed-stage, VC-backed US startup (Bellevue, WA), hiring at Principal (L7) or Senior Principal (L8) equivalent for a founding architect seat, fully remote from what the posting describes. They explicitly call out "founding-team equity with meaningful upside" and "cash compensation calibrated for seed stage," which is code for below-Series-B-market cash, made up on the equity side. Since this would be a remote hire based in Geneva rather than a local one, the standard Swiss salary anchors below apply more as a floor for what to protect than as what a Bellevue seed startup would default to offering. Worth deciding upfront: how much cash discount is worth taking for founding equity in an unproven, pre-launch company, versus what a similar seat at a funded, revenue-generating platform startup would pay.

## Switzerland market anchors (for framing, not for the seed-stage employer)

- Senior Staff / Principal Engineer, Geneva or Zurich, established tech company: CHF 170k to 220k base, often plus bonus.
- VP Engineering / Head of Engineering, Swiss scale-up: CHF 190k to 250k base plus meaningful equity.
- CTO, early-stage Swiss startup: CHF 150k to 200k base, often below market, offset by a founder-level equity stake, typically 3 to 8%.
- Geneva cost of living runs high enough that a US-calibrated "competitive" seed salary often lands 30 to 40% under what a comparable Zurich or Geneva-based role would pay in base cash.

These numbers exist to protect a floor, not to anchor the actual ask. The real anchor for a US seed-stage founding role should reference the US market for Principal-level infra engineers, adjusted for the fact this is IC work with founding scope and founding-level risk.

## Opening anchor

Use once there's a real offer on the table, not before. Something close to:

"I want to make sure we're aligned before this goes further. For a Principal or Senior Principal founding architect seat with this scope, end-to-end ownership of the platform architecture, direct customer work, and shaping the engineering culture from scratch, I'd expect a base in the 190 to 210k USD range, along with a founding-engineer equity grant in the 1 to 2% range before any Series A dilution, plus standard benefits. I know seed-stage cash is constrained, so I'm open to structuring this so more of the value sits in equity if the vesting and grant terms reflect the founding-level risk I'm taking on."

Adjust the base number once the actual offer lands. If they open first, let them name a number before restating the anchor.

## If they come in low on cash

"I hear that seed-stage cash is tight, and I'm genuinely fine with that being part of the deal. What I'd want in exchange is for the equity grant to reflect that I'm taking on founding-level risk at a pre-launch company: no product in market yet, no revenue signal, no team beyond the founder. If the cash number stays where it is, I'd want to talk about a larger equity allocation, a shorter cliff, or acceleration terms that protect me if the company's direction changes before I've vested meaningfully."

Fallback move if they won't budge on either lever: ask for a compensation review built into the offer letter itself, tied to a specific milestone like closing the first paying customer or hitting a headcount trigger, rather than leaving it to an informal future conversation.

## Equity negotiation notes

- Ask for the fully diluted percentage, not just a share count. A share count means nothing without knowing the current cap table and last valuation.
- Confirm the strike price and the 409A valuation date. If it's stale, a fresh valuation could mean a worse strike price landing right after start.
- Push for a standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff, and ask directly whether there's single-trigger acceleration on acquisition, common for a founding-level hire, versus double-trigger, which is more standard but less protective.
- Ask what happens to unvested equity if the company doesn't raise a Series A within some window. Seed-stage stealth companies sometimes stall between rounds, and that's a real scenario worth having an answer for before signing, not after.
- If they frame the equity number as a percentage "at close of this round," ask what the post-money valuation actually is, since the same percentage means very different dollar outcomes depending on that number.

## Geographic discount pushback

If there's any pull toward a lower number because Geneva "has a lower cost of living than the Bay Area" or similar:

"Geneva is actually one of the more expensive cities globally, on par with or above most major US tech hubs, so I wouldn't expect a geographic discount here. I'd also point out the role is remote by design per the posting, so the value I'm bringing, architecture ownership and hands-on build, isn't tied to a specific office or cost basis. I'd rather set comp based on the scope of the role than on where I happen to be sitting."

## Competing offer leverage template

Use only if there's a genuine competing process in motion. Never fabricate a specific number or company name.

"I want to be transparent that I'm also further along in another process for a similarly scoped platform architecture role, at a company that's past seed stage with more cash certainty. I'd rather make this work if the scope and the founder relationship are right, so if there's room to move on either the base or the equity side, now's the moment I'd want to know."

## Timezone and remote logistics, worth raising before comp, not after

Bellevue is nine hours behind Geneva. Before finalizing anything, get explicit about expected overlap hours, whether the CEO expects daily sync time given the "report directly to the CEO" language in the posting, and whether that's compatible with a sustainable schedule long term. This isn't a comp lever, but it changes whether the comp number is worth it at all.
