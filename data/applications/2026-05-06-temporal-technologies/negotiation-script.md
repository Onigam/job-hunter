# Negotiation Script — Temporal Technologies, Staff Software Engineer AI SDK

---

## Market Context

Temporal raised a $103M Series B in 2022 at a reported $1.5B valuation, with Madrona, Sequoia, and Index Ventures on the cap table. By 2026 they're likely into Series C territory or approaching profitability — the durable execution space has consolidated, and Temporal is the clear leader. The equity is meaningful: the gap between $1.5B and a credible exit multiple at their trajectory is real money.

The published salary band for this role is **$196,000–$245,000 USD** annually, plus equity. That's an honest band for a US-based remote Staff engineer. The question for a Geneva-based candidate is how they handle geography, employment structure, and currency.

**Employment structure note:** US companies rarely employ directly in Switzerland without a local entity. Temporal likely either (a) employs through an EOR (Employer of Record) like Deel or Remote.com, or (b) contracts through a Swiss Sàrl/SARL arrangement. Both are common. EOR employment is cleaner on benefits; contracting often allows higher nominal rates to offset the lack of employer social contributions. Clarify this early — it affects how you frame your number.

**Benchmarks (USD, Staff Software Engineer, 2025-2026):**
- US remote staff-level at a Series B/C dev tools company: $220K–$300K base
- Temporal's published range top: $245K — below market for exceptional candidates at this tier

**Switzerland / Geneva benchmarks (CHF):**
- Staff / Principal Engineer (Geneva market): CHF 175K–220K base
- Senior Staff scope (Alaya-to-Benevity trajectory): CHF 210K–250K

**Your anchor target: $265K–$275K USD base**, above the published band. Justification: 17 years of experience, founding engineer track record validated by a $1B+ acquisition, direct AI tooling expertise, and TypeScript/Python depth that maps exactly to the role. A candidate who built what Temporal customers are building is worth more than the midpoint of a generic posting.

If they denominate in CHF: target **CHF 230K–245K**, which is consistent with Geneva's cost of living and the senior staff scope.

## Opening Anchor Script

When they ask for your expectations or make a first offer:

> "I've looked at the posted band and at comparable roles at dev tools companies at Temporal's stage. Based on my background, specifically building and shipping a platform from zero through a billion-dollar acquisition, and the direct relevance to what Temporal's AI SDK team needs, I'm targeting around $270K base. I understand that's above the published range, and I'm happy to discuss how equity factors in if there's flexibility there. But I'd want the base to reflect the scope."

**Why this works:**
- It's above their range without being absurd — you can justify it with specifics.
- It references the founding engineer credential, which is above a typical staff candidate.
- It opens the equity conversation without trading base for it pre-emptively.

## If They Come In at the Top of the Published Range ($245K)

> "I appreciate the offer and the role is genuinely the right fit. That said, I'd want to push a bit on the base. I'm bringing a specific background that maps directly to this team: I've built in Python and TypeScript for years, I've designed public APIs with documentation and community adoption as first-class concerns, and I've driven AI tooling workflows in engineering orgs. I'd want to get to $260K–$265K on base to move forward. If that's not possible on base, I'm open to discussing equity structure — but I'd rather resolve the base first."

**Hard floor: $235K base.** Below that, equity needs to be exceptional (top of the staff-level grant range with a clear path to Series C pricing).

---

## If They Come In Below $220K

> "I appreciate the offer. The role is the right kind of problem and I'd like to find a way to make it work. That said, that number is below what I'd need to move, given both my seniority level and the Geneva cost of living. Can you help me understand whether there's flexibility on base, or whether we should look at the equity component to close the gap? I want to understand the full picture before I respond."

Don't counter with a specific number until you know whether room exists. Get them to reveal the ceiling first.

## Equity Negotiation

Temporal is a late-stage private company, which means the equity math is different from a seed-stage startup. Strike prices will be higher, but so is the floor on exit multiples.

**What to ask:**
- Grant size: at staff level, a credible range is 0.015%–0.06% depending on current valuation. If the base is at the low end, push toward the higher end of the equity band.
- Vesting: standard 4-year with 1-year cliff. Confirm this explicitly.
- Current 409A fair market value vs. most recent preferred share price: the spread tells you the option upside at a reasonable exit.
- Post-termination exercise window: ask whether it's 90 days (standard, punishing) or extended. Given that you've been through an acquisition, this is an informed question, not a negotiating tactic.
- Acceleration: ask whether there's single-trigger or double-trigger acceleration on acquisition. Given Temporal's trajectory and your Benevity experience, this matters.

**Equity ask script:**

> "On equity, I'd want to understand the grant size you're considering for this level and what the 409A looks like relative to the preferred. I've been through an acquisition from the founder side, so I have a realistic view of how vesting timing interacts with exit scenarios. I'd also want to confirm the exercise window after departure — 90 days is a meaningful constraint at this valuation level."

## Geographic and Employment Structure Considerations

If they raise a geographic discount or EOR arrangement:

> "Geneva is consistently one of the most expensive cities in Europe: comparable to Zurich on housing and significantly above London on a cost-of-living basis. If you're applying a geographic adjustment, I'd ask that it be calibrated to Geneva specifically, not to a European average. A few percent off looks small on paper and translates to a meaningful real-terms difference at this income level."

**If employment is via EOR (e.g., Deel):**
- The effective cost to Temporal will be 20–30% above the salary due to employer-side social contributions in Switzerland. Frame your ask relative to what they'd pay a US-based hire all-in, not relative to the nominal salary.
- Swiss employer AVS/AI contributions are roughly 12–13% on top of salary. That's already baked into what they pay the EOR.

**If employment is via Swiss contract:**
- You'll be responsible for your own AVS/2nd pillar contributions as a freelancer or Sàrl, which runs 15–25% of gross. Price this into your rate, not separately.

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have or receive another offer during the process:

> "I want to be direct: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company and I expect an offer in the next two weeks. I'd prefer Temporal because the AI SDK scope is the closer fit, but I do have a timeline now. Is there a way to accelerate the process on your end?"

If no competing offer exists, don't fabricate one. Use timeline pressure honestly:

> "I'm actively interviewing and I'd expect things to come to a head in the next three to four weeks. I'd rather give this process the attention it deserves, so it would help to know where you are in the timeline."

## Non-Salary Items Worth Negotiating

- **Home office setup**: the published benefit is $1,000, which is low for Geneva hardware prices. Ask for CHF 3K–5K if you need to negotiate equipment.
- **Internet reimbursement**: their published $74/month is below Swiss ISP rates for a business-grade connection. Ask for CHF 100–120/month.
- **Professional enrichment / conference budget**: the published $1,800/year is reasonable. Confirm it applies to European conferences (travel costs more from Geneva).
- **Start date**: Swiss notice periods at this level are typically 3 months. Temporal should expect this. If they push for faster, propose a part-time bridge or a financial offset for the notice period.
- **Title**: confirm whether "Staff" is the actual internal level. If the scope is Senior Staff (cross-team architecture, domain expert responsibilities), get that in the contract.

---

## What to Never Do

- Don't accept the first number without at least one counter.
- Don't reveal your current salary before getting their offer range. If pressed: "I'd rather not anchor on my current package since the scope here is quite different."
- Don't propose a range. Give a single number and defend it.
- Don't price equity at the optimistic exit case. Use a conservative 5–8x revenue multiple — that gives you a floor, not a fantasy.
- Don't let "we normally don't go above the band" end the conversation. Bands are guidelines, not ceilings, and you have a specific case to make.
