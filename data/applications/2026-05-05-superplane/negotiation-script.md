# Negotiation Script — SuperPlane Product Engineer

---

## Market Context

**What SuperPlane posted:** $6,000–$10,000 USD/month ($72K–$120K/year)

This is a US-dollar range, likely benchmarked against US or Eastern European remote candidates. The upper end ($120K USD) converts to approximately CHF 104,000 at current exchange rates. That's below Geneva market for the profile and experience level being brought to this role.

**Geneva/Switzerland benchmarks (Senior Staff, 17+ years, founding-engineer profile):**
- Senior Staff Engineer, Geneva: CHF 150,000–190,000 base
- VP/Head of Engineering, Geneva: CHF 170,000–230,000 base
- Platform/Infra specialist with AI background: premium of 10–15% on base

**Comparable recent data points (2025–2026):**
- Benevity Senior Staff Geneva equivalent: CHF 160,000–180,000 base
- PostHog / Linear / similar dev-tools remote roles targeting EU: $140K–$180K USD
- Geneva cost of living index: ~30% higher than Zurich, ~70% higher than median European city

**Bottom line:** The right anchor for this role, given Geneva location, 17+ years, and a founding-engineer background, is CHF 160,000 (~$185K USD) or equivalent in USD at CHF parity.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask about compensation expectations, respond before they name a number if possible:

> "Based on the seniority of the role and my location in Geneva, I'm targeting CHF 160,000 base, or the USD equivalent. Geneva runs expensive, and that range reflects Senior Staff market rates here rather than a US or Eastern European benchmark. I'm open to discussing how equity figures into the total picture."

If they've already floated their $6K–$10K/month range:

> "I saw the range in the listing. The upper end would be around CHF 104,000 at current exchange rates, which is below Geneva market for this level. I'd need to land at CHF 160,000 or the USD equivalent to be at the right benchmark. What flexibility exists there?"

---

## If They Push Back on Location Premium

> "Geneva is consistently in the top three most expensive cities globally. I'm not asking for a location premium on top of market rate, I'm asking for market rate. A Senior Staff Engineer with a founding background in a US hub would be priced at $180K–$220K; CHF 160K is the Geneva equivalent of the lower end of that band."

---

## Counter-Offer Script (If They Come In Low)

If they offer at or below CHF 130,000 / $110K USD:

> "I appreciate the offer. It's below where I need to land to make this work long term given my situation in Geneva. I'd need CHF 155,000–165,000 to accept. Is there room to move there, or would it help to talk through how equity could bridge the gap?"

If they offer CHF 130,000–145,000:

> "That's closer. The gap is CHF 15,000–20,000. If you can meet me at CHF 155,000, I'd be ready to move forward. Alternatively, if there's flexibility on equity, let's have that conversation now."

---

## Equity Negotiation

SuperPlane is early-stage with meaningful equity on offer. Key questions to ask:

1. "What percentage of the company does the equity grant represent on a fully diluted basis?"
2. "What was the most recent 409A valuation, and when was it done?"
3. "What's the current outstanding share count?"
4. "Is the vesting schedule standard four-year with a one-year cliff?"
5. "What happens to unvested shares if the company is acquired before my cliff?"

**Minimum acceptable equity for this role/stage:** 0.25%–0.75% on a fully diluted basis, depending on company stage and recent valuation.

**If they resist on base and offer equity instead:**

> "I'm willing to structure a deal that includes more equity if the base gap is real. To do that meaningfully I need the fully diluted percentage and the 409A. Can you share those before we put numbers on paper?"

**On acceleration/cliff in an acquisition scenario:**

> "Given that you're early-stage, I'd want single-trigger acceleration on acquisition, or at minimum double-trigger acceleration with a generous catch-up window. This is standard protection in a pre-Series B environment."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they say remote employees outside the US get a geographic discount:

> "I understand some companies apply location-based adjustments. For context, Geneva sits at the top of global cost-of-living indices, above San Francisco and New York. Applying a discount from a US benchmark to Geneva would actually result in a meaningful pay cut in purchasing power terms. I'd want to stay at or above CHF 150,000 to avoid that inversion."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have an active competing offer (or want to create time pressure without lying):

> "I'm at a decision point on a couple of other conversations that are moving quickly. I'd like to move forward with SuperPlane if we can close the compensation piece this week. Can we get on a call today or tomorrow to sort it out?"

If you don't have a competing offer but are in late-stage conversations elsewhere:

> "I have a few conversations in late stages. I'm prioritizing SuperPlane because the technical problem is the most interesting to me, but I need to be practical about the comp. If we can land somewhere in the CHF 155,000–165,000 range, I'd be comfortable deprioritizing the others."
