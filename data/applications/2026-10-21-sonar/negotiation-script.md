# Negotiation Script: Sonar, Staff Software Engineer (Java), Billing, Geneva

## Context worth knowing before the first call

Sonar (SonarSource) was founded in Geneva and still runs a hub there. The posting says the company is profitable with over $400M in revenue, and it's privately held, so this isn't an early-stage startup where equity is most of the package. Expect a structured salary grid, an annual bonus, and possibly a long-term incentive plan. Ask about every piece explicitly rather than assuming.

The role is three office days a week in Geneva (Mon, Tue, Thu). No relocation needed, which is a point in your favour since it removes cost and risk for them.

## Market range (Geneva, Staff level, CHF, to verify before the call)

These are working estimates from Swiss market patterns. Check levels.fyi, Glassdoor (SonarSource Geneva) and any recruiter conversations before the first comp discussion.

| Component | Low | Mid | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base salary | 165k | 185k | 210k |
| Annual bonus | 10% | 10-15% | 15%+ |
| Total cash | ~180k | ~210k | ~240k |

Zurich big tech pays Staff engineers well above this (often 280k+ total), which is a useful reference point but not a fair comparison. Use it to show the ceiling, not as a demand.

**Your numbers:**
1. Anchor: CHF 205k base plus bonus.
2. Target: CHF 190k base, bonus at 15%, with any long-term incentive on top.
3. Walk-away: below CHF 170k base without a meaningful LTI or bonus. Decide this number before the call and don't move it during the call.

## When they ask for your expectations first

Try to get their range first:

"I'd rather understand the band you've set for this level before I put a number down. What range did you have in mind for the Staff role on the Billing squad?"

If they insist:

"For a Staff role in Geneva with ownership of billing architecture, I'm looking at around CHF 205k base, plus bonus. That's based on what Staff-level roles pay locally and on the scope in the posting, which covers architecture, delivery and mentoring across a squad that sits on every product launch."

Then stop talking. Let them respond.

## If the offer comes in low

Offer around CHF 165-175k base:

"Thanks, I appreciate the offer and I'm keen on the role. The base is below where I expected for Staff scope in Geneva, though. I've got 17 years of experience, I've built and sold a SaaS platform, and I've worked as a Senior Staff IC in a Java microservices org. Is there room to get the base closer to CHF 195k?"

If they say the band is fixed:

"Understood. If base is capped, can we look at the other levers? A higher bonus target, a signing bonus, or a guaranteed compensation review at six months tied to clear objectives would all help close the gap."

If they push back on the billing gap ("you don't have Stripe experience"):

"That's fair, and I said so upfront. The posting lists it as desirable, not required, and the hard parts of usage-based billing, like idempotent metering, reconciliation and migrating customers between pricing models, are distributed systems problems I've dealt with for years. I'd rather be paid for the scope I'll own than discounted for a domain I'll learn in the first quarter."

## Geneva cost pushback

If they reference a company-wide or EU grid (Bochum, London), or suggest Geneva is overpaid:

"Geneva is consistently one of the most expensive cities in the world, and that's reflected in local Staff salaries. The role is posted in Geneva and requires three office days, so I'd expect the comp to be benchmarked against Geneva, not against the Bochum or London grids."

## Long-term incentives and equity

Sonar is private and profitable. Ask, don't assume:

1. Is there an LTI, stock option or phantom share plan for Staff-level hires?
2. If options: strike price, vesting schedule (four years with a one-year cliff is standard), and the post-departure exercise window.
3. How are grants valued, and what happens in a liquidity event or acquisition (single or double trigger acceleration)?
4. Are there refresh grants, and on what cadence?

If there's no equity at all:

"That's fine, but it means the cash component has to carry more of the package. In that case I'd want the base closer to the top of the range."

## Other things to put on the table

1. Signing bonus to offset any bonus forfeited at the current employer.
2. Training budget, conference attendance (for billing/payments or Java ecosystem events).
3. Pillar 2 / LPP pension contribution split, since employers in Switzerland often go above the legal minimum and it's worth real money over time.
4. Vacation: aim for 25 days minimum.
5. Start date that works for any notice period.

## Competing offer template

Only use this if there's a real competing process. Never invent an offer.

"I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company, and their offer is around CHF [X] base. Sonar is my first choice because of the billing problem and the fact that it's here in Geneva. If you can get to CHF [Y], I'm ready to sign this week."

If there's no offer but other processes are active:

"I'm in a few other processes at Staff level, and I expect to have something concrete within [two/three] weeks. I'd prefer to make a decision with Sonar first, so it'd help to know whether there's flexibility on base."

## Final checklist before the call

1. Walk-away number written down.
2. Their likely band researched (levels.fyi, Glassdoor, recruiter).
3. Bonus structure and LTI questions ready.
4. Clear sentence on the billing gap that doesn't sound apologetic.
5. Ask for the full offer in writing before accepting anything verbally.
