# Negotiation Script — Solace Health / Staff Software Engineer

---

## Market Context

**Solace Health:** Series B, founded 2022, backed by Inspired Capital, Craft Ventures, Torch Capital, Menlo Ventures, Signalfire. ~84 employees, fully remote, Redwood City CA.

**Publicly posted range:** $200K–$225K USD (from job board scrapes; not confirmed in the official posting).

**U.S. Staff SWE market (2026):**
- Series B, SF Bay Area remote: $220K–$270K base
- 10th percentile Staff: $195K, 50th: $230K, 90th: $265K
- Total comp (base + equity refresh + bonus): $280K–$380K

**Geneva context:** Solace pays in USD. For a Geneva-based employee, the company likely either pays via a contractor arrangement or explores an EOR (Employer of Record). USD-to-CHF: $230K ~ CHF 200K. Competitive but not exceptional for a senior engineer in Geneva.

**Your leverage:**
- Exact-match stack (TypeScript, NestJS, PostgreSQL, Redis, React)
- Founded and scaled a platform to acquisition, i.e., directly comparable trajectory to Solace
- European timezone is actually useful for a fully-remote U.S. team (async coverage)
- Already at Staff+ level with 17 years experience

---

## Opening Anchor

If asked "What are your compensation expectations?":

> "I'm looking at $235K–$250K base, plus equity on top. My sense of the market for Staff engineers in this space puts that in a reasonable range, and given the technical scope of the role and my background, I think that's a fair starting point. Happy to talk through it."

Do not start lower. The posted range tops at $225K; anchoring at $235K–$250K puts pressure upward and gives room to land at $225K–$235K.

---

## If They Come in at $200K–$210K

> "I appreciate the offer. I want to be straightforward: $200K is below what I was expecting based on the market and the scope of the role. I've seen Staff-level compensation at comparable Series B companies sitting closer to $230K. Is there room to move the base, or would additional equity be on the table to close the gap?"

Wait for their response. Do not fill the silence.

If they say base is fixed:
> "What does the equity component look like? If the base is constrained, I'd want to understand the total picture before making a decision."

---

## Equity Negotiation (Series B)

Ask for:
- Grant size in dollars at last valuation (not just shares, since share count without valuation is meaningless)
- Vesting schedule (standard is 4 years / 1-year cliff — flag anything less favorable)
- Acceleration clause (double-trigger is standard; single-trigger is better for you, rarely offered)
- Whether they've taken a 409A recently and what the current FMV is

Target equity for Staff at Series B: $150K–$300K at current valuation (4-year vest). If the grant is below $100K at valuation, push back:

> "The equity range I've been seeing for Staff engineers at companies at this stage is higher than that. Is there flexibility on the grant size, or is this a fixed band for the level?"

---

## Geographic Adjustment Pushback

If they try to apply a geographic discount because you're in Geneva:

> "I'd push back on a location-based discount. The value I bring is the same regardless of where I'm working, and the role is fully remote. If anything, European timezone coverage adds async breadth to the team at no cost. I'd want to keep the conversation anchored to the role, not my location."

If they raise EOR fees as a reason to discount:
> "The EOR cost is real, but it's fixed overhead that doesn't scale with my salary. It doesn't make sense to reduce my comp permanently because of a flat cost on your side."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or want to create urgency without lying):

> "I'm in late stages with another company and would like to wrap this up if we can find an agreement. I don't want to play games, so I'll tell you directly: the other opportunity is at a higher base, but Solace is where I'd rather put my energy if we can get close on compensation."

If you don't have another offer, don't fabricate one. Instead:

> "I want to be honest with you: I'm being selective at this stage and I have a few conversations in progress. I'd rather close this quickly than drag it out, so I want to be direct about where I need to land."

---

## Contractor vs. Employee (Geneva)

If Solace cannot hire directly in Switzerland, they'll likely propose either:
1. Independent contractor arrangement (you invoice via your own entity)
2. EOR through a company like Deel, Remote.com, or Oyster

**Contractor:** Add ~25–30% to the base equivalent to account for employer contributions (AVS, LPP, etc.) you'll cover yourself, plus accounting costs.

> If offered $225K as a contractor: push to $280K–$300K to net the same as a salaried employee in Geneva.

**EOR:** Ask who pays the EOR fee. If you pay, negotiate it out of base. If they pay, it should not reduce your comp.

---

## Things to Always Get in Writing

- Base salary (in USD or CHF, explicit currency)
- Equity grant: number of options, exercise price, strike price, vesting schedule, cliff
- Whether equity accelerates on acquisition
- Notice period / at-will vs. fixed term
- Any geographic pay adjustment policy
