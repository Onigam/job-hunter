# Negotiation Script — Founding AI Engineer @Panora (Hexa)

---

## Context

- **Role type:** Permanent employment contract (CDI in France)
- **Company:** Panora, backed by Hexa/eFounders (Paris-based studio, portfolio includes Aircall, Spendesk, Front)
- **Role location:** Paris (with possible remote flexibility)
- **Candidate location:** Geneva, Switzerland
- **Stage:** Early-stage startup, product already in production with brokerage clients

---

## Market Rate Reference

**Paris market — Founding AI Engineer (senior profile):**

| Level | Annual salary (Paris) |
|---|---|
| Senior AI Engineer (3-6 yrs) | EUR 80,000-100,000 |
| Staff AI Engineer (7-10 yrs) | EUR 100,000-125,000 |
| Founding Engineer, senior profile | EUR 110,000-140,000 |

Hexa portfolio companies tend to pay above median for early hires given the studio's brand and the equity ask. Expect an opening offer around EUR 90,000-110,000 for this role.

**Geneva market equivalent for reference:**

| Level | Annual salary (CHF) |
|---|---|
| Senior Engineer | CHF 130,000-160,000 |
| Staff/Principal Engineer | CHF 155,000-200,000 |

Geneva rates are meaningfully higher. If the role allows remote from Geneva, that differential is worth surfacing.

**Target anchor:** EUR 130,000-140,000. Justified by 17+ years experience (well above the 3-6 yr requirement), prior founding engineer track record with acquisition outcome, and AI/LLM production depth.

---

## Opening the Conversation

**When they ask "what are your salary expectations?" — don't answer first.**

> "Before I give you a number, can you tell me the structure you have in mind? I want to understand the base salary range, the equity component, and whether there's flexibility on the location arrangement. Those three things are connected for me."

This surfaces the equity and remote setup before you anchor on salary.

---

## Salary Script

**If they ask for your number first:**

> "For a founding engineer role with the scope described, I'm looking at EUR 130,000 to EUR 140,000. I'm above the experience range you listed, and I bring a full founding cycle: built a platform from zero, scaled it to 50+ enterprise clients, and went through an acquisition. That's not a common profile. I'm also very senior on the AI production side, which is the core of what you need. I'm happy to have a different conversation if there's equity that changes the math."

**If they come in at EUR 90,000-100,000:**

> "I appreciate the offer, and I want to make this work. The base is lower than where I'd need to be to step back from my current engagement. If you can get to EUR 120,000 I can be flexible on the equity side, and if the base stays lower then I'd need the equity to compensate more meaningfully. Can you tell me more about the cap table and what founding engineer allocation looks like at this stage?"

**Floor:** EUR 105,000 base. Do not accept below this without a concrete equity offer of at least 0.75% vested over four years.

---

## Remote / Geneva Arrangement

If the role requires Paris presence but Magino wants to negotiate hybrid or full remote:

> "I want to flag that I'm based in Geneva. I'm genuinely open to regular Paris presence, and I've done Paris-Geneva arrangements before. But if we're talking about a full relocation, that changes my thinking on the total package because the cost differential is significant. Can you tell me what flexibility exists on the location side?"

If they push for full relocation to Paris:

> "I can consider it for the right opportunity, but then the compensation needs to reflect what I'd be giving up. Geneva senior engineers earn CHF 160,000-200,000 for this profile. If I move to Paris at EUR 110,000 that's a meaningful step down in real terms. I'd need EUR 130,000 minimum to make that work."

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Opening ask:**

> "For a founding engineer role at this stage, I'd expect somewhere between 0.75% and 1.5%. What does the current allocation look like, and what's already been issued to the founding team and early investors?"

**On vesting:**

> "I'm comfortable with a standard four-year vest and a one-year cliff. If there's an acceleration clause on acquisition I'd like to see it included, given that the company is backed by Hexa and exits are part of the model."

**If it's a French BSPCE structure (likely for a French startup):**

> "Can you share the current BSPCE strike price relative to the last valuation? I want to model the actual value of the option grant, not just the percentage. If the company was just created the strike should be nominal, but I want to confirm that before signing."

**If they offer equity without cap table disclosure:**

> "I can't evaluate an equity offer without knowing the total diluted share count and the strike price. I don't think that's an unreasonable ask at this stage of a conversation. Can you share that, or at least a summary?"

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have (or want to signal) competing options:

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in final stages with two other companies right now. One is a remote AI engineering role, one is a Geneva-based scale-up. I'm genuinely more interested in what Panora is building, but I'm on a timeline. If you're moving forward, it would help me to have a concrete written offer in the next ten days."

Only use this if you can back it up. Hexa moves quickly on candidates they want; this tends to accelerate their process without damaging the conversation.

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

- No equity discussion for a founding engineer hire (non-negotiable to ask about)
- Equity offered as "we'll figure it out" with no written term
- Salary anchored to a junior 3-year profile when you're being hired as a founding engineer
- Paris-only stance with no acknowledgement of the Geneva cost differential
- No 1-year cliff on equity (suggests cap table thinking hasn't matured)
- Role framed as "senior developer" rather than "founding engineer" in the contract
