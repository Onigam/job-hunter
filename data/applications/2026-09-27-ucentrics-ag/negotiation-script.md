# Negotiation Script: Ucentrics AG, Senior Software Engineer (Full-Stack), Zürich

## Context before talking numbers

Ucentrics is an ETH Zürich spin-off with roughly ten people, backed by Venture Kick, Innosuisse and the Google and Microsoft startup programmes. Named customers include SBB, Accelleron, EAM and P-Wave. The funding stage looks early (seed or pre-Series A), and nothing public suggests a large round. That shapes everything below:

1. **Cash will be tighter than at a scale-up.** Early Swiss deep-tech startups often pay below the Zürich big-tech market and make up the gap with equity. Expect a band below what Nexthink or Google Zürich would offer.
2. **Equity is the real lever.** At this size, a senior engineer is close to a founding-team hire. The percentage, vesting terms and leaver clauses matter more than CHF 10k of base.
3. **Level mismatch.** The posting asks for 5+ years; I have 17+, a cofounder exit and Senior Staff experience. Either they pay for that (a "lead" or "founding engineer" framing) or I accept a lower base in exchange for meaningful equity. Decide which before the call.
4. **Location.** The role is in the Zürich area and the work model isn't stated. Geneva to Zürich is about 2h45 each way by train, so the number of on-site days changes what the offer is worth.

Questions for the first call:
- "What's the salary band for this role, and is there room for a lead or founding-engineer level?"
- "What's the ESOP structure: options or shares, what pool size, and what vesting and leaver terms?"
- "When was the last round, and how much runway does the company have?"
- "How many days a week do you expect people in Zürich, and is part-remote from Geneva workable?"

## Market reference (estimates, verify before the call)

Ranges from general market knowledge for 2025-2026. Cross-check with swissdevjobs.ch salary data, levels.fyi (Zürich), the Swiss ICT salary survey, and ideally someone from the ETH spin-off ecosystem.

| Reference | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Senior full-stack engineer, early-stage Zürich startup | CHF 120k-145k + ESOP |
| Senior/lead engineer, funded Zürich scale-up (Series B+) | CHF 140k-170k |
| Staff engineer, Zürich scale-up | CHF 165k-200k |
| Senior engineer, Zürich big tech (base only) | CHF 170k-220k |

Typical early-stage equity for a senior hire in Switzerland: roughly 0.2% to 1%, more if the role is framed as founding or lead engineer. Treat these as assumptions until they share their numbers.

**My numbers**

- **Opening anchor:** CHF 160k base, justified by pitching lead scope (architecture ownership, hiring, DevOps setup), not just senior IC.
- **Target:** CHF 140k-150k base plus 0.5-1% equity on a standard 4-year vest, and a Zürich travel or remote arrangement.
- **Walk-away:** below about CHF 130k base with small equity (under ~0.3%), or full-time on-site in Zürich without relocation support. At that point the cash is a real step down and the equity doesn't compensate.

## Opening anchor script

> "Based on what I've seen for lead-level engineers at Zürich startups, and given that I'd come in owning architecture and the delivery pipeline, not just features, I'm looking at around CHF 160k base. I'm genuinely flexible on the split between cash and equity, because at your stage I'd rather have a real stake in the outcome. What does your band look like?"

If they ask for a number first and I can't deflect:

> "I'd rather understand the whole package first, especially the equity. But to give you a sense, I've been at Senior Staff level, and I'd expect something in the 150s to 160s for cash, adjusted depending on what the equity looks like."

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

If they offer, say, CHF 125k-135k:

> "Thanks, I appreciate the offer, and I like the product a lot. The base is lower than I'd expected for what I'd bring, which is 17 years of experience, including building a platform from zero to acquisition. I understand cash is tight at your stage. Could we close the gap on the equity side? If the base stays at 135, I'd want to see closer to 1%, with a 4-year vest and a sensible leaver policy. Alternatively, a base of 145 with the equity you've proposed would work for me."

If they can't move on either:

> "Would you consider a salary review after six months, tied to specific milestones we agree on now? For example, the move to infrastructure-as-code on Azure or a first enterprise customer on the new architecture. I'd want that in writing."

## Equity notes (Swiss startup specifics)

- **Instrument.** Ask whether it's options (ESOP), restricted shares, or phantom shares. Swiss spin-offs sometimes let early employees buy shares at nominal value, which can be very tax-efficient, while options can be taxed as income at exercise. Get the tax treatment confirmed, ideally with a Swiss tax ruling if the company has one.
- **Percentage, not a count.** Ask for the fully diluted share count so the percentage is clear, and ask about the size of the pool relative to the next expected round.
- **Vesting.** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly or quarterly vesting after the cliff.
- **Leaver clauses.** These matter more in Swiss ESOPs than people expect. Good-leaver terms should keep vested equity at fair value; bad-leaver should be narrowly defined (cause, not just resignation).
- **Acceleration.** Given my own acquisition history, I'd ask for double-trigger acceleration: vesting speeds up if the company is acquired and my role is then terminated or materially changed.
- **Dilution.** Pure anti-dilution protection is rare for employees. A reasonable ask is a top-up review at the next priced round.

## Location and cost pushback

This isn't a geographic-discount situation, since Zürich and Geneva are both expensive. The issue is commute cost and time.

> "I'm in Geneva, so if the role needs regular presence in Zürich, I'd want to understand the rhythm. Two days a week on-site works well for me. For that, I'd ask the company to cover a GA travelcard (about CHF 4k a year), or add the equivalent to base. If you'd prefer me full-time in Zürich, we'd need to talk about relocation support."

If they push for full-time on-site from day one:

> "I understand, especially with hardware like AR glasses in the loop. Could we do the first two or three months mostly on-site to get up to speed with the team and the devices, then settle into a hybrid rhythm?"

## Competing offer template

Only use real facts. If there's an active process elsewhere (for example the Nexthink AI engineer role in Lausanne), it's fair to mention it without inflating it.

> "I want to be transparent: I'm also in late-stage conversations with a larger company in Romandie, where the cash component is noticeably higher. Ucentrics is where I'd rather be, because of the product and the stage. If we can get the base to CHF [X] or the equity to [Y]%, I'd be ready to sign this week and withdraw from the other process."

If there's no competing offer, don't invent one. Use the timeline instead:

> "I'm talking with a few other companies and expect things to move over the next couple of weeks. I'd prefer to decide on Ucentrics first, so it'd help to have the full package in writing soon."

## Before signing, get in writing

- Base, and any agreed salary review date with its milestones
- Equity: instrument, percentage on a fully diluted basis, vesting schedule, cliff, leaver terms, acceleration
- Work model: on-site days per week, travel cost coverage
- Pension (BVG/LPP) contribution split. Some startups only do the legal minimum; ask whether they go beyond 50/50
- Notice period and probation length (3 months is standard)
- Holidays (5 weeks is the norm in Switzerland; 25 days minimum to accept)
