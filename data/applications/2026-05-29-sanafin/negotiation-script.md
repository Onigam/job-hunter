# Negotiation Script — SanaFin Founding Engineer

## Market Context

SanaFin is pre-seed and Swiss-based. Compensation will likely be a blend of below-market base plus meaningful equity. The key lever here is the founding engineer designation: this is not a hire joining an existing team, it's a co-architect of the product. That framing matters in the equity conversation.

**Switzerland market anchors (2026):**
- Senior Full-Stack / Staff Engineer in Geneva: CHF 130k-170k at funded startups
- Founding Engineer at seed stage (funded): CHF 110k-140k base with equity offset
- Pre-seed founding engineers often accept CHF 90k-120k in exchange for 0.5-2.0% equity
- Geneva cost-of-living is the highest in Switzerland; Zurich is comparable. Do not accept a remote discount for a Geneva-based role.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for salary expectations, do not give a range first. Use:

"I'm looking at CHF 130k base as a starting point, given the founding engineer scope and the Geneva market. I'm very open to structuring part of that as equity if the vesting terms make sense, but I'd want to understand the cap table and the equity package before adjusting the base."

If they push for a range before sharing their budget:

"My range starts at CHF 120k and goes up depending on equity. If you can share what you've budgeted for this role, I can be more concrete."

---

## If They Come In Low (below CHF 100k base)

Do not accept or decline immediately. Respond with:

"I appreciate you sharing that. That's meaningfully below what I was expecting given my background and the founding engineer scope. I'd need to see equity that compensates for the gap. What does the equity package look like, and what's the current valuation you're working from?"

Then evaluate whether the equity math closes the gap:
- Pre-seed valuation of CHF 1-3M: 1% is worth CHF 10k-30k at entry, potentially much more at exit
- If the equity is less than 0.5% for a founding engineer, push back directly: "For a founding engineer role where I'm defining the architecture and building the first version of the product, less than 0.5% doesn't reflect the risk and scope. What's the team's thinking on equity for this level of contribution?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Target range for pre-seed founding engineer:** 0.5% to 2.0%, depending on how early you're joining and what the founding team already holds.

Ask these questions before accepting any equity offer:
1. "What's the current cap table structure, and how many shares are in the option pool?"
2. "What vesting schedule are you offering?" (Standard is 4-year with 1-year cliff and monthly vesting thereafter. Anything shorter on the cliff is a red flag.)
3. "Is this options or restricted stock? And what's the strike price?" (Pre-seed strike should be very close to par value.)
4. "Do you have an anti-dilution clause for early employees?" (Not common at pre-seed, but worth asking.)
5. "What happens to unvested shares if the company is acquired before my cliff?" (Push for single-trigger acceleration on acquisition, or at minimum double-trigger.)

**Counter if equity is too low:**

"0.3% doesn't feel right for founding engineer scope. I've been through an acquisition before and I know what that number looks like on the other side. Can we get to 0.75% at minimum, with the option to revisit after six months if the product is tracking well?"

---

## Geneva Discount Pushback

If they reference remote pricing or try to benchmark against Zurich instead of Geneva:

"I'm based in Geneva, which is consistently the most expensive city in Switzerland. I'd want to make sure the compensation reflects that rather than being benchmarked against a lower cost-of-living location. If the role is remote-friendly, we can discuss, but the baseline should be Geneva market."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have or expect another offer, use it cleanly:

"I want to be transparent: I'm in conversations with other companies at a similar stage. I'm more interested in SanaFin's problem space, which is why I'm prioritizing this conversation. But I do need to resolve this quickly. Can you give me a sense of your timeline for the offer, and whether you have flexibility on the equity side?"

If the competing offer is higher in base but lower in equity upside, frame it as:

"The other offer is higher in base, but I think the equity story at SanaFin is more compelling if the round closes at the expected valuation. I'd be willing to bridge that gap if you can get to [target equity %]."

---

## Final Notes

- Get the equity offer in writing before signing anything. A verbal commitment on percentage means nothing until it appears in the option grant.
- Confirm what "founding engineer" means structurally: is there a title path, a seat at the table for product decisions, formal co-founder designation later if the company grows?
- If they can't move on base or equity, push for other terms: signing bonus (one-time), accelerated first review in three months, a professional development budget (CHF 3k-5k/year is standard at Swiss startups), or remote flexibility.
