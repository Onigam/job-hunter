# Negotiation Script: TechShack, Fractional CTO / Technical Advisor

## The starting point

The posting lists 500 to 700 GBP per day for one to two days a week, which converts to roughly CHF 570 to 800 at current rates. That's a UK-market fractional rate, not a Switzerland-based one, and it sits well under what a Geneva-based Staff+ engineer with a founder exit commands even remotely. Fractional CTOs in the Swiss and wider EU market with an acquisition on their record typically land somewhere between CHF 900 and 1400 per day, sometimes more once equity or a retainer structure is on the table. That gap is the whole negotiation.

## Opening anchor

Don't counter the day rate in isolation, anchor on scope and value instead. Something like: "I looked at the range in the posting, and I want to flag that it's roughly half of what fractional CTO engagements with a comparable track record go for in my market. Given the platform and fundraising-readiness work you're describing, I'd want to start the conversation at CHF 1100 per day, with room to talk about how that scales if the time commitment grows past two days a week." Say the number first, then justify it, not the other way around.

## If they push back on rate

If they come back with "that's above our range," don't retreat immediately. Try: "I get that the posted range was set before this conversation. A couple of options: I can hold closer to your range at CHF 800 to 850 per day if there's meaningful equity attached, or I can stay at my anchor if this stays a pure cash engagement. Which direction fits your cap table better right now?" This puts the tradeoff back on them instead of just splitting the difference.

## Equity notes

Early-stage AI startup, fractional role, so equity is a reasonable ask even without full-time hours. Ballpark for a part-time technical advisor at this stage is somewhere between 0.25% and 1%, vesting over three to four years with a one-year cliff, and it's worth asking explicitly whether there's an existing advisor equity pool so you're not the one setting precedent. Watch for anti-dilution language, or the lack of it, in whatever agreement they draft, and ask who else has taken fractional or advisor equity at this company so far. If they offer equity instead of a rate bump, get the current valuation and share count in writing before agreeing to a percentage, a small percentage of a company that raises at a low valuation isn't worth much.

## Geographic discount pushback

If they try to anchor to "UK contractor rates" because the posting is priced that way, the response is straightforward: "This is a remote role and the deliverable is the same regardless of where I'm sitting. Geneva isn't a discount market, and my rate reflects the track record, not my postal code." Keep it short, don't over-explain it, and don't apologize for the number.

## Competing offer as a lever

Even without a live competing offer, it's fair to say: "I'm currently evaluating a couple of other fractional and advisory engagements at a similar stage, so I'd want to get to a decision within the next week or two." This is true in spirit if there's any other active job search happening, and it sets a natural deadline without inventing a fake company name or number.

## Walk-away point

Below CHF 650 per day with no equity and no path to more days per week, it's not worth the context-switching cost against other advisory work. Better to pass than to anchor future fractional rates too low.
