# Negotiation Script: CTO and Head of IT (AI), GF-ACCORD AI

## Context to keep in mind
GF-ACCORD AI is a 1-10 person, early-stage Geneva startup. The posting lists two open CTO slots, requires an agile certification and a top-ten MSc/PhD (credential-heavy for a team this size), and quotes 1.0-4.0% equity with no base salary figure. That combination usually means one of two things: the base is genuinely low and equity is doing the heavy lifting, or the founders haven't settled comp yet and are testing the market. Either way, get the base number in writing before spending real time on equity math.

## Switzerland market anchors
- Senior/Staff engineer, Geneva: CHF 130,000-160,000 base.
- Engineering lead / VP Engineering at a funded startup, Geneva: CHF 160,000-200,000 base.
- CTO at a funded seed-to-Series-A startup, Geneva: CHF 170,000-220,000 base, often with 1-5% equity depending on how early.
- CTO at a 1-10 person, likely self-funded or angel-funded startup: base is frequently below market, sometimes CHF 100,000-140,000, with equity meant to close the gap. This is the bracket GF-ACCORD probably sits in given headcount and the "two CTOs" detail.
- Geneva cost of living runs 15-20% above Zurich and well above Paris or Lisbon, so a Zurich-anchored offer needs adjusting upward, not treated as a ceiling.

## Opening anchor
Lead with the range, not a single number, and tie it to what the role actually covers.

"Given the scope here, technical leadership plus the org and hiring side of a Head of IT role, I'd expect a base in the CHF 160,000-190,000 range for Geneva, plus a founder-adjacent equity grant given how early this is. That's roughly where a CTO role sits locally once you account for the two hats you're asking one person to wear."

If they open with a number below CHF 130,000 base, don't counter immediately with a number, ask first:

"Before I respond with a figure, can you walk me through how you're thinking about the split between base and equity at this stage? That'll tell me whether we're negotiating the same thing."

## Counter-offer script if they come in low
"I hear that cash is tight this early, that's normal for a team your size. What I'd propose instead: a base closer to CHF 140,000, with the difference from my target made up in equity, plus a six-month compensation review tied to funding milestones or revenue targets we agree on now. I'd rather have that written down than hope it happens informally."

If they push back on the review clause: "I'm not asking for a guarantee of more money, just a scheduled conversation with clear triggers, so neither of us is negotiating from memory in six months."

## Equity negotiation
The posting's 1.0-4.0% range is wide enough to mean almost anything depending on valuation, dilution assumptions, and how many people already hold equity.

- Ask for the fully diluted cap table position before agreeing to a percentage: "Is that 1-4% on a fully diluted basis, and where does that land me relative to the founders and any other CTO hire?"
- Standard vesting: 4 years, 1-year cliff. Push back on anything longer than a 1-year cliff for a CTO-level hire, that's a red flag for how they treat senior hires generally.
- Ask about anti-dilution protection or at least a refresh mechanism tied to future rounds, since 1-10 person companies often raise again within 12-18 months and early equity gets diluted fast without one.
- If there are genuinely two CTO hires, ask directly how equity and scope split between the two roles. Overlapping mandates at this size usually mean unclear org design, which is worth surfacing before signing anything.
- Get the strike price and 409A-equivalent valuation (or Swiss equivalent) in writing, not just the percentage.

## Geographic discount pushback
If they try to anchor to a lower-cost location given the fully remote, multi-city hiring: "I've seen the posting covers Boston, London, Paris, and Zurich alongside Geneva. I'm already based here, so there's no relocation cost or ramp-up time, and Geneva's cost of living is higher than most of those cities. I'd expect that to work in my favor on base, not against it."

## Competing offer template
Use only if there's an actual competing process in motion, real or in late stages, not a bluff with nothing behind it.

"I'm in later-stage conversations with another team for a similar scope of role, so I do have a timeline to work with. I'd rather move forward here if we can land on terms that make sense, but I wanted to be upfront that I'm not evaluating this in isolation."

## Bottom line
Walk away point: base below CHF 110,000 with equity under 1% and no cliff protection. That combination signals the company either can't afford a CTO yet or hasn't thought through what the role costs, and no equity story fixes that on its own.
