# Negotiation Script — Crystal Intelligence VP Engineering

## Market Context

Crystal Intelligence is a private company (~100 employees, founded 2016) with headquarters in London and Amsterdam. The role is fully remote; Magino is based in Geneva. No salary data was published in the job posting.

**Relevant benchmarks:**
- VP Engineering, Switzerland (Geneva market): CHF 220,000-280,000 base
- VP Engineering, UK (London remote): £150,000-190,000 base (~CHF 170,000-215,000)
- VP Engineering, Netherlands (Amsterdam remote): EUR 160,000-200,000 base (~CHF 160,000-200,000)
- Blockchain/fintech VP Eng premium: +10-15% above standard software market

**The Geneva anchor:** Crystal may try to pay UK or NL rates since that's where they're headquartered. Geneva's cost of living is materially higher (housing alone is 40-60% above Amsterdam, 30-40% above London in comparable zones). Push back on UK/NL anchoring directly and early.

**Target range:** CHF 240,000-265,000 base, plus equity.

---

## Opening Move

Do not volunteer a number first. If asked "what are your expectations?":

> "I've been benchmarking VP Engineering roles in the Swiss market where I'm based. The range I'm working from is CHF 240,000-265,000 base, which is in line with what similar roles at scale-ups of this size pay in Geneva. What's the budget for this role?"

If they push back with UK or NL benchmarks:

> "That makes sense as your reference point given where you're headquartered. The challenge from my side is that I'm based in Geneva, and the cost differential is significant enough that UK rates create a real gap. The Swiss market for this level is CHF 220,000-270,000. I'd need to be somewhere in that range to make the economics work."

---

## If They Come In Low (under CHF 200,000)

If the first offer is below CHF 200,000:

> "I appreciate the offer. I want to make this work, but I'd need to come up meaningfully to get this done. I'm looking at CHF 240,000 as a base, which is grounded in the Geneva market for VP Engineering. Is there flexibility to move in that direction, or is there other structure (equity, performance bonus) that could close the gap?"

Do not accept the first number. A counter at 20-25% above their opening is normal and expected at this level.

---

## If They Offer CHF 200,000-220,000

This is a "close but not there" scenario. Counter at CHF 240,000 with a clear rationale:

> "CHF [their number] is closer to where I'd need to be, but I'm still CHF 20,000-40,000 short of the range I've been working from. Could we meet at CHF 240,000? At that number I'm ready to move forward."

Keep the counter specific. Vague "I was hoping for more" invites them to split the difference without moving toward your number.

---

## Equity

Crystal Intelligence is private, so compensation likely includes stock options (EMI options if UK-granted, or phantom stock/SAR depending on structure). Key questions to ask:

**What to ask:**
- "What's the equity package for this role, and what's the current valuation cap table basis?"
- "What's the vesting schedule and cliff?"
- "What's the exercise window after departure?"
- "Is there a secondary liquidity mechanism or has the company had any secondary transactions?"

**Target at this level:**
- 0.25%-0.5% for VP Engineering at a 100-person private company with no recent public funding announcement
- Standard 4-year vest, 1-year cliff
- Exercise window of at least 5 years post-departure (standard UK EMI is 90 days; push for extended window if possible)

**If equity feels thin:**
> "The base and equity together need to make sense as a package. If the equity is structured conservatively, I'd need to weight the base a bit higher to offset the liquidity risk."

---

## Remote Premium Argument

Crystal saves on London and Amsterdam office costs by hiring remotely. That saving is real:
- London desk cost: £8,000-15,000/year per person
- Employer NI (UK): 13.8% on salary over £9,100, so on £170k that's ~£22,000/year
- If hiring through a Swiss employer-of-record, social charges are different but the point stands

> "One thing worth noting: hiring remotely from Geneva versus adding a senior hire in London or Amsterdam saves you a meaningful amount in employer costs and office overhead. That saving gives you room to meet market rates in my location."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have (or receive) a competing offer, use it directly:

> "I want to be transparent: I have another process at [company] that's moving to an offer stage. Their range is CHF [X]. I'd prefer to be at Crystal Intelligence because of [specific reason, e.g., the migration challenge, the blockchain infrastructure problem], but I need you to be close on compensation. Can you get to CHF 240,000?"

If you don't have a competing offer, don't fabricate one. The alternative framing:

> "I'm running a focused search and have a few processes at similar stages. My floor for this kind of role is CHF 240,000, and I'd rather close this one cleanly than drag out a back-and-forth. Can you get there?"

---

## Other Levers (if base is stuck)

If they genuinely can't move the base:
- Sign-on bonus: ask for CHF 20,000-30,000 one-time to compensate for forfeited unvested equity or annual bonus at current role
- Performance bonus: 15-20% annual target bonus tied to migration milestones (SLA maintenance, latency targets)
- Accelerated vesting: ask for 25% cliff vest after 6 months instead of 12 if they're truly remote-first and want commitment signal
- Extra PTO or flexible arrangement: not CHF, but real value in Switzerland's labor market

---

## What Not to Do

- Don't apologize for your number. State it, then wait.
- Don't negotiate against yourself ("I know that might be high, but..."). They have a budget; find out what it is.
- Don't accept verbally on the call. Ask for the written offer, then confirm.
- Don't sign same-day unless the terms are already fully negotiated and you've slept on it.
