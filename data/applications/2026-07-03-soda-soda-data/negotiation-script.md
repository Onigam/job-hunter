# Negotiation Script: Soda, VP of Engineering

## Context
Posting lists roughly $195k for a fully remote, Europe-based VP of Engineering, reporting to the CPO, ~50% hands-on. Soda is VC-backed, HQ in Brussels, small team, data observability and testing product. No stated equity range in the posting, so equity needs to be raised explicitly.

## Switzerland market anchors
- Geneva-based Staff/Principal Engineer base salary typically runs CHF 150k-190k.
- VP Engineering at a Series A/B European startup, Geneva or Zurich cost basis, typically lands CHF 190k-230k base when the company is paying Swiss-adjusted comp.
- $195k (~CHF 173k at current rates) is below Geneva market for a VP-level title with a hands-on IC load, though it's a reasonable Brussels/remote-EU anchor. The gap is the opening lever: either the base moves up, or the title and equity compensate for a lower cash number.
- Total comp benchmark to cite: Staff+/VP roles at comparable-stage data infra startups (Series A-B, 30-80 employees) in Western Europe cluster around CHF 200k-260k total comp when equity is included at a reasonable strike.

## Opening anchor
"The range you've shared is around $195k. For a VP of Engineering role that's still 50% hands-on and owns the architecture-to-roadmap seam, Geneva-adjusted comp for that scope typically sits closer to CHF 210k-230k base, plus a meaningful equity grant given the stage. I'd want to start the conversation there and understand how the equity and any location adjustment factor in before we land on a number."

## Counter-offer script if they come in low
"I appreciate the offer, and I'm genuinely interested in the role and what Soda's building. The base is below where I'd expect for this scope and level, though, especially given I'd be splitting time between hands-on delivery and owning the engineering-to-product seam with the CPO. Could we look at closing that gap, either through base or a larger equity grant? If CHF 210k base isn't workable given where the company is at, I'd want to understand the equity math well enough to see how the two together get me to a number that reflects the role."

## Equity negotiation
- Ask for the fully diluted share count and the strike price before evaluating any percentage, a 0.5% grant means very different things depending on the cap table.
- Standard early-stage terms to expect and confirm: 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff. Push back if the cliff is longer or vesting is back-loaded.
- Ask whether there's an early-exercise option and what the post-termination exercise window is; a 90-day window on stock options can strip most of the value if there's a gap before the next role. A 5-to-10-year window is worth asking for explicitly.
- If the equity grant feels light for a VP-level hire, it's fair to ask directly: "What would the range look like for someone joining at this level who's expected to be one of the more senior technical voices in the company?"

## Geographic discount pushback
If Soda tries to anchor pay to a lower-cost-of-living country on the team: "I understand comp sometimes flexes by location, but Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and I'd want comp that reflects that rather than a blended EU average. I'm open to a conversation about how you handle geographic bands, but the number needs to work for where I actually live."

## Competing offer leverage template
"I want to be straightforward that I'm in later-stage conversations with another company for a similar scope of role, so I'd like to move at a reasonable pace here. I'd rather make this work if the fit is right, and having a clearer picture of where Soda can land on base and equity would help me prioritize accordingly."

## Close
Whatever the final number, confirm in writing: base, equity percentage and strike, vesting schedule, start date, and remote work terms including any required travel to Brussels. Get the offer letter before giving notice anywhere else.
