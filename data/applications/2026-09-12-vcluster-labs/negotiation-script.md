# Negotiation Script, vCluster Labs, Senior/Staff Backend Engineer

## Market Context

**Role:** Senior Software Engineer (Backend), posted with Staff-level scope (Kubernetes internals, tenancy model design, end-to-end feature ownership)
**Location:** Geneva, Switzerland (role is remote-first, distributed team)
**Posted bands, by region:**
- US: $135,000 to $175,000 base + equity + bonus
- Canada: CA$140,000 to CA$200,000 base + equity + bonus
- Germany: €80,000 to €115,000 base + equity + bonus

There's no published Swiss band. Germany is the closer regional comparator than the US number, but Geneva runs well above German comp for the same level.

**Swiss market rates for equivalent role:**
- Senior/Staff backend engineer, infrastructure or platform focus, Geneva: CHF 165,000 to 200,000
- Kubernetes-heavy roles at venture-backed, growth-stage companies: CHF 175,000 to 210,000
- Converting the top of vCluster's Germany band (€115k, roughly CHF 108k at ~0.94) lands meaningfully under that Geneva range

**Honest read before anchoring:** the core requirement is deep Kubernetes internals plus strong Go, ideally with authored controllers or upstream contributions. Magino runs Kubernetes and Docker in production but his primary languages are TypeScript, Java, and Python, not Go. That gap is real and will likely come up in technical screens. The anchor should lean on what's unambiguous, founding-engineer ownership, a live multi-tenant architecture built from scratch, a defensible build-versus-rewrite call under investor pressure, rather than trying to argue equivalence on the Go/controller line item.

**Justification for a high anchor:**
- 17+ years of experience, including founding and scaling Alaya to acquisition by Benevity
- Alaya's core architecture was multi-tenant from day one, the exact problem this role is hiring to own
- Production Kubernetes and Docker experience across two companies
- Geneva is consistently ranked among the most expensive cities globally for cost of living
- The posted regional bands, converted to CHF, already sit under Geneva market rate

## Opening Anchor

**Target:** CHF 170,000 base, 0.08 to 0.12% equity

> "I'm working off Geneva market rate for a senior backend infrastructure role at a venture-backed company, which runs somewhere around CHF 170-200k. Given the founding-engineer background and hands-on Kubernetes work in production, I'd anchor around CHF 170k base. On equity, at your stage I'd expect something in the 0.08-0.12% range."

Let them introduce the posted regional band first rather than opening with it.

## Counter-Offer Scripts

**If they open near the German band converted to CHF (roughly CHF 105-110k):**

> "I want to be direct: that's under Geneva market rate for this level, even setting aside the Go ramp-up. To make the move work living here, I'd need to see something closer to CHF 150-160k as a starting point. Is there flexibility on base, or room to close the gap through equity and a signing bonus?"

**If they point to the posted band as fixed company policy:**

> "That makes sense as a general policy. My situation is that I'm cost-based in Geneva, not in a lower-cost European market, and a flat regional band doesn't capture that. A senior infra hire in Geneva costs noticeably more than the same role in most of the rest of Europe. Is there a location adjustment, or flexibility elsewhere in the package?"

**If they raise the Go/Kubernetes-internals gap as a reason to come in lower:**

> "Fair to name directly. The Kubernetes and Docker experience is real and production-tested, Go is genuinely what I'd be ramping on fastest. I'd rather say that upfront than oversell it. If it helps, we could structure the first few months with a defined ramp period and a comp step-up once I'm shipping independently in Go."

**If they won't move base:**

Push other levers in order:
1. Equity bump of an additional 0.02 to 0.04% if base is capped
2. One-time signing bonus, CHF 12,000 to 18,000, to bridge the first year
3. Learning budget for Go and Kubernetes-focused training, CHF 2,500 to 3,500/year
4. Home office and equipment budget, CHF 1,500 to 2,500/year

## Equity Negotiation

**Ask for:** 0.08 to 0.12%, 1-year cliff, 4-year vest, monthly after cliff
**Questions to ask before accepting a number:**
- Current fully diluted cap table, and how much is option pool versus investor preference stack
- Strike price and most recent 409A-equivalent valuation
- ISOs or NSOs, and post-departure exercise window
- Whether Khosla or other leads hold participating preferred, and how that affects common outcomes
- Any refresh policy for early non-founder hires

**If the number comes in low (under 0.05%):**

> "For a role this close to shaping the tenancy model, I'd expect equity that reflects real influence on the roadmap, not a standard IC grant. The gap between 0.05% and 0.10% matters a lot in a reasonable outcome, and it's part of what would offset a base under Geneva market. Can we revisit that?"

## Geographic Discount Pushback

> "I get that the band is built to work across a distributed team, that's a reasonable default. My pushback isn't about a premium for its own sake, it's that Geneva rent, healthcare, and taxes sit closer to Zurich or a major US city than to what a Germany-calibrated band assumes. I'd rather we price to where I actually live than to a broader European average."

**Data point if needed:** Swiss ICT salary surveys and platforms like salary.ch put senior infrastructure engineers in Geneva at CHF 165-210k, well above the CHF-converted top of vCluster's posted Germany band.

## Using a Competing Offer

> "I've got other infrastructure-focused conversations moving, in a similar CHF 165-190k range. I'd rather land at vCluster, the multi-tenancy problem is more interesting to me than what the others are building, but the comp needs to be in that neighborhood for it to be the clear choice."

Without a real competing offer, don't invent one. Fall back to the market data line instead.

## Timeline and Process Notes

Don't put a number on the table before understanding how much of the role is genuinely internals/controller work versus API and platform glue, since that shapes the anchor.

> "I'd like a clearer picture of the split between controller-level work and the rest of the platform before I put a number down. Can we come back to comp once we're further into the process?"

**Offer review window:** ask for five to seven business days, more if equity documentation needs outside review.
