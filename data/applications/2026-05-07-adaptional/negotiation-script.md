# Negotiation Script — Founding Engineer at Adaptional

Role: Founding Engineer
Company: Adaptional (YC S25, SF Presidio)
Posted range: $180,000–$280,000 + 1.00%–2.00% equity

---

## Market Context

Adaptional posted a wide band ($180K–$280K). For a founding engineer with 17+ years of experience, a prior founding-engineer exit, and hands-on AI/agentic systems work, the correct target is the top of or above their posted range.

**SF market anchors (2025/2026, founding engineer with exit history):**
- Median founding engineer TC at seed/post-seed: $200K–$240K cash + 0.5%–1.5% equity
- Senior founding engineer / first engineering hire at YC company with prior exit: $260K–$320K base + 1.5%–2.5% equity
- Staff-equivalent IC at Series A SF startup: $280K–$340K total cash

**Your anchor: $300,000 base + 2.0% equity**

That's above their posted range, which is intentional. You have a defensible reason: a prior acquisition-exit as founding engineer is a meaningful data point they're pricing for, and their own range suggests they know the role is senior.

---

## Compensation Priorities (rank order)

1. Equity percentage (this is likely a breakout company or a useful learning; equity has more expected value than incremental cash at this stage)
2. Base salary (cost of SF living is real; don't anchor yourself too low)
3. Relocation package (if you're moving from Geneva, negotiate this separately)
4. Signing bonus (one-time offset for any unvested equity or benefits left behind)

---

## Opening Anchor Script

Use this when they ask "what are your compensation expectations?" — say it before they give you a number if possible.

> "Based on my background and the market for founding engineers with prior exits, I'm targeting around $300,000 base. On equity, given that I'd be coming in early and taking meaningful risk, I'd want to be at 2%. I'm open to discussing the structure if there's flexibility. What does your comp model look like?"

Why this works: you've anchored high, given a rationale (prior exit, early risk), and immediately signaled flexibility without caving. Asking about their model keeps the conversation moving.

---

## If They Come In Low (under $240K base)

> "I appreciate the offer. The range is a bit below where I'm targeting for this level of role, especially given the founding-engineer context and my prior track record. I'm not walking away, but I'd want to close that gap before we move forward. Is there room to come up on base, or would you prefer to structure more of it through equity?"

If they push back and say the range is fixed:

> "Understood. If the cash is capped, then I'd want to make sure the equity reflects the early-stage risk appropriately. At 2%, I'm a real stakeholder. At 1%, I'm more like a senior employee. Those are different conversations."

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Target: 2.0% minimum, 4-year vest, 1-year cliff, double-trigger acceleration**

Key terms to negotiate:

- **Cliff**: Standard is 1 year. Accept it, but confirm the cliff resets if the company is acquired.
- **Acceleration**: Push for double-trigger (acquisition + termination without cause). Single-trigger is rare and makes you look nervous; double-trigger is standard and reasonable.
- **Exercise window**: Ask for a 5–10 year post-termination exercise window (PTEW). The standard 90-day window is a trap for early employees with illiquid equity. YC-backed companies are increasingly offering extended windows; it costs them nothing if you stay.
- **Anti-dilution**: You're unlikely to get full ratchet. Ask for pro-rata rights on future rounds (the right to participate to maintain your percentage). Rarely granted to employees but worth asking at founding-engineer level.

Script for equity conversation:

> "On equity, two things I'd want to confirm: the post-termination exercise window and double-trigger acceleration. The 90-day window is a real risk for early employees if the company stays private for a decade. And double-trigger acceleration on acquisition is pretty standard at this stage. Are those negotiable?"

---

## Relocation Package (if relocating from Geneva)

If you're moving from Geneva to San Francisco, this is a separate line item. Don't let them fold it into your signing bonus.

> "One thing I'd want to address separately: relocation. Moving from Geneva to SF involves real costs — shipping, temporary housing, flights, visa processing if needed. That's typically a $15,000–$25,000 item. Can we treat that as a separate reimbursement, not part of the signing bonus?"

**Note:** The listing says "US citizen/visa holders only." If you'll need a visa (O-1 or H-1B), confirm early whether they sponsor, and negotiate legal fees ($5K–$15K) as a company expense.

---

## Signing Bonus

If they can't move on base salary, ask for a signing bonus to bridge the gap.

> "If the base is firm at $X, I'd want to see a signing bonus that closes the gap on year-one compensation. I'm not asking for something unreasonable, but I left some unvested equity on the table and I'd want the first year to reflect the full picture."

Target: $25,000–$50,000 one-time, not subject to clawback if you stay 12+ months (push for no clawback if they terminate you; only clawback if you leave voluntarily before 12 months).

---

## If They Make a Final Offer Below Your Floor

Don't reject it on the call. Buy time:

> "I want to give this serious thought. Can I have 48 hours to review everything?"

Then come back with one specific ask:

> "I've thought it through and the role is genuinely compelling. The one thing that's holding me back is [base / equity %]. If you can move on that, I'm ready to sign."

Pick one thing. Multiple counter-asks signal desperation. One targeted ask signals confidence.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or want to create urgency without fabricating one):

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late stages with one other company. I'd prefer to come here, but I need the offer to be competitive or I can't make the case to myself. Where can you flex?"

Only use this if it's true or you're willing to follow through. If you're bluffing and they call it, you've lost credibility and probably the offer.

---

## Floor / Walk-Away Number

Don't go into the final round without a private floor.

Suggested floor for this role (SF, founding engineer, 17+ years, prior exit):
- Base: $240,000
- Equity: 1.5%
- Relocation: covered as separate item

Below that, the risk/reward math doesn't hold up relative to Geneva-based opportunities at similar seniority.
