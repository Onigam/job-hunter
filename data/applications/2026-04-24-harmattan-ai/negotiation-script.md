# Negotiation Script — Harmattan AI / Chief Engineer

## Market Context

**Company stage:** Post-Series B ($200M, Jan 2026), $1.4B valuation, Dassault Aviation as lead investor. Scaling aggressively across France, Switzerland, UK, UAE, US.

**Role seniority:** Chief Engineer in R&D, Lausanne. This is a C-level adjacent or VP-equivalent technical leadership role. Not an IC Staff role — this carries architectural ownership and likely team leadership at the engineering organization level.

**Location premium:** Lausanne is one of Switzerland's most expensive cities. Geneva and Lausanne are comparable in cost of living. Swiss defense sector tends to pay above standard tech SaaS ranges because of security clearance requirements and limited talent pool.

**Switzerland market anchors (2026, CHF):**
- Senior Staff IC at a Series B tech company: CHF 180k-220k base
- VP Engineering / Head of Engineering: CHF 220k-270k base
- CTO / Chief Engineer at a post-Series B defense startup: CHF 250k-320k base + meaningful equity
- Total cash comp target for this role: CHF 260k-290k

**Defense sector premium:** Defense companies often pay a 10-15% premium over equivalent SaaS roles because of the security/compliance complexity and narrower candidate pool with relevant experience.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask about compensation expectations, anchor high and justify it:

> "For a Chief Engineer role at this stage and location, I'm looking at CHF 270k-290k base, plus equity that reflects the seniority of the position. That's consistent with where the Swiss market sits for this kind of technical leadership post-Series B. I'm open to discussing how that breaks down between cash and equity depending on what the package looks like."

**Why anchor here:** CHF 270k is at the top end but defensible for a Lausanne-based Chief Engineer at a $1.4B company. It leaves room to negotiate down to CHF 240k-250k and still land above your floor. If they come in higher, good — don't leave money on the table.

**Floor:** CHF 220k base. Below that, push back firmly.

---

## If They Come In Low (under CHF 200k)

> "That's meaningfully below what I'd expect for this scope. Chief Engineer at a Series B defense company, Lausanne cost of living, with the architecture ownership and team leadership this role implies — the Swiss market puts this at CHF 240k-270k for equivalent roles. Is there flexibility on the base, or is the equity package designed to bridge that gap? I'd want to understand the full picture before reacting to a number."

Follow-up if they say equity compensates:
> "I'd need to see the equity terms to evaluate that. What does the vesting schedule look like, and at what price was the last round? The $1.4B valuation is public but I want to understand the strike price on the options."

---

## Equity Negotiation

**What to ask for:**
- At a $1.4B Series B company, meaningful equity for a Chief Engineer is 0.08-0.25% on a 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff.
- Below 0.05% is not worth the illiquidity risk at this stage.

**Standard vesting script:**
> "On equity, I'd want a 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff, which is standard. What's the current option pool strike price? And is there an acceleration clause on a change of control? Given Dassault is already a strategic investor, that's worth discussing upfront."

**If they push back on the equity ask:**
> "I understand the pool may be tighter at this stage. What I'd want to avoid is taking a below-market cash comp with below-market equity. One or the other has to be at market. Where do you have more flexibility?"

**Anti-dilution / protection:**
- Ask whether future rounds include weighted average anti-dilution for employees or just investors.
- Realistic expectation: you won't get investor-grade protections, but you should understand how dilution works in the cap table before signing.

---

## Geographic / Cost of Living Pushback

If they reference a Paris-level comp (France is cheaper than Switzerland):

> "I'm based in Geneva and the role is in Lausanne, so we're squarely in Swiss compensation territory. Geneva and Lausanne are among the most expensive cities in Europe. A CHF 180k package in Paris is not equivalent to CHF 180k in Lausanne — after tax and cost of living, it's a meaningful difference. The Swiss market data supports the range I quoted."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer or want to create urgency (use only if true or if you're actively interviewing elsewhere):

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company and expecting to receive an offer in the next week or so. I'd prefer to make a decision based on fit and scope rather than just timing, but I want to be realistic about my decision window. If Harmattan AI is moving quickly, that's helpful to know."

---

## Benefits to Clarify Before Signing

- **Pension (2nd pillar / LPP):** Confirm employer contribution rate. Swiss law requires minimum contributions but top employers add above the minimum.
- **Health insurance subsidy:** Not mandatory in Switzerland but increasingly offered by tech companies.
- **Remote flexibility:** Lausanne-based but ask what the expectation is for in-person days.
- **Annual bonus:** Confirm whether this is a fixed bonus structure or discretionary.
- **Equity refresh:** At this stage, ask whether there's a policy for refresh grants after year 2.
- **Notice period:** Swiss standard is 1-3 months. Negotiate down to 1 month if possible.
