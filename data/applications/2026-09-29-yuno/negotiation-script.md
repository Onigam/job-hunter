# Negotiation Script: Yuno, Staff Engineer (Client Experience)

Posted location: Madrid, Spain, listed as remote. Full-time IC role.
Posted comp: no figure. "Competitive salary", stock options, global health insurance, home office bonus, flexible time off.
Company: Yuno, founded in Colombia, payments orchestration ("AI-native operating system of global commerce"). $45M Series B announced 12 August 2026 (led by Global PayTech Ventures, with a16z and Tiger Global participating). Clients include McDonald's, NetEase Games, GoFundMe, inDrive, Rappi.

---

## 1. Read the situation first

**The band is probably set for Spain, not Switzerland.** The role is posted in Madrid, and Spanish Staff Engineer pay is well below Geneva's. Yuno's first number will likely be built on a Madrid or "EU remote" benchmark. The negotiation is mostly about whether they'll pay a location-agnostic Staff rate or a Spanish one, and how they'd employ someone in Switzerland at all.

**Find out how they'd hire in Switzerland.** Yuno offers global health insurance, which suggests they already employ people outside their entity countries, probably through an EOR (Deel, Remote, Oyster) or as contractors. That shapes the whole package:
- EOR employment in Switzerland means Swiss social charges on top of salary. Some companies quietly lower the offer to absorb that cost, so ask directly.
- As a contractor, Magino carries AHV/AVS, pension (2nd pillar), accident insurance and unpaid leave personally. A contractor rate needs to be roughly 20 to 30% above the equivalent salary to break even.

**The gaps are real.** No Go, no Kotlin, no payments background. They weaken the case for a high base, so don't pretend they don't exist. Magino's strength is Staff-level scope already held (founder-built platform to acquisition, Senior Staff at a unicorn, cross-team architecture, mentoring). Don't negotiate before the technical rounds are done; bargaining power peaks once they've decided they want Magino.

**Questions for the first recruiter call:**
- What's the band for this role, and is it set by location or company-wide?
- How do you employ people based in Switzerland: EOR, contractor, or a local entity?
- How many stock options, at what strike price, and what's the vesting schedule and post-termination exercise window?
- Is there a bonus, and is the "home office bonus" a one-time amount?
- Is the Client Experience team in Madrid, Bogotá or spread out, and what are the expected overlap hours?

## 2. Market anchors (2026, working estimates)

Re-check against Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Salarium (Swiss salary calculator) and recruiter feedback before any call. Rough conversion used here: CHF 1 ≈ EUR 1.05 to 1.07, check the current rate on the day.

| Benchmark | Typical base |
|---|---|
| Staff engineer, Madrid, local scale-up | EUR 85k to 115k |
| Staff engineer, well-funded fintech, EU remote | EUR 110k to 150k |
| Staff engineer, Geneva / Zurich scale-up | CHF 170k to 210k |
| Staff engineer, Zurich big tech | CHF 200k to 250k+ (plus RSUs) |

Realistic outcome: a Series B from Latin America hiring into Madrid will most likely land between EUR 110k and 140k base unless they agree to a Swiss or location-agnostic band. Getting CHF 170k+ requires them to treat this as a Staff hire priced for where the person lives.

**Targets (base, gross, employee-equivalent)**

- Opening anchor: CHF 195k.
- Target: CHF 170k to 180k.
- Walk-away: CHF 150k, unless the option grant is unusually strong and well documented. Below that, Geneva living costs (rent, LAMal premiums, childcare) make it a clear step back from a Senior Staff role.
- If contractor: add 25% to all of the above (opening around CHF 240k/year, i.e. roughly CHF 1,050 to 1,100 per day on ~220 days).

## 3. Opening the conversation

If asked for expectations early:

> "I'd rather hear your band first, mostly because I'm based in Geneva and I'd like to know how you handle Switzerland: EOR, contractor, or something else. For context, I'm coming from a Senior Staff role at Benevity, and for Staff scope from Switzerland I'm targeting around CHF 195k base. I'm flexible on how that splits between salary and options."

If they insist on one number:

> "Around CHF 195k base as an employee, and more if it's a contractor setup since I'd be covering social charges myself."

## 4. If they come in low

**Scenario A: a Madrid number (for example EUR 105k).**

> "Thanks for being clear. I understand that's your Madrid band. The issue is that I'd be doing the job from Geneva, where that number is roughly what a mid-level engineer earns, not a Staff one. The role is fully remote, so where I sit doesn't change the work or the scope. I'd need to be at CHF 170k base or above to make this work. Is there a location-agnostic band for Staff, or room to price this against Switzerland?"

**Scenario B: close but under target (for example CHF 155k).**

> "That's in range, and I appreciate the movement. If we can get base to CHF 170k, I'm ready to move forward. If base is capped, I'd look at a larger option grant or a sign-on bonus to close the gap, and I'd want a compensation review after six months, tied to specific milestones on the Client Experience roadmap."

**Scenario C: they cite the Go/Kotlin gap to justify a lower level.**

> "Fair point, and I raised it myself. The language is a few weeks of ramp; the Staff part of the job, designing systems, leading across teams and mentoring, is what I've been doing for the last decade. If you'd like a check, I'm happy to agree on a six-month review with a level and comp adjustment written into the offer."

## 5. Geographic discount pushback

They may say "we pay by location" or "our Madrid engineers earn X". Keep it factual:

> "I understand paying by location, and in this case the location is Geneva, one of the most expensive cities in Europe. Health insurance alone is several hundred francs a month per person and isn't employer-paid here. I'm not asking for a Madrid band plus a premium. I'm asking for a Staff salary at the rate of the place I actually live, which is what a local Staff role would pay me."

If they won't move on base, push the other levers: options, sign-on, equipment and co-working budget, travel costs to Madrid covered by Yuno, and a written review date.

## 6. Equity (stock options, Series B)

Options only have value if you know what you're getting. Ask for all of this in writing:

- **Number of options and fully diluted share count**, so you can work out the ownership percentage. A grant expressed only as "X options" means nothing on its own.
- **Strike price and latest preferred price** (the Series B price per share). The gap between the two is the rough paper value today.
- **Vesting:** standard is four years with a one-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Post-termination exercise window:** 90 days is common and painful. Ask for 5 to 10 years, or at least 1 year.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination). Having been through the Benevity acquisition, this is a reasonable and informed ask.
- **Refresh grants:** is there a refresh policy after year two?
- **Tax in Switzerland:** employee options are usually taxed at exercise (sometimes at grant, depending on canton and plan terms). Ask whether the plan has been reviewed for Swiss employees, and check with a Geneva tax adviser before signing. If it's an EOR setup, confirm that options can actually be granted to someone employed through the EOR.
- **Anti-dilution:** that protects preferred shareholders (investors), not employee options. Don't spend negotiating capital on it. What protects you is the ownership percentage and a refresh policy.

Suggested ask if base is capped:

> "If base is fixed at that level, I'd want the option grant to reflect Staff scope. Could you share the fully diluted share count and the Series B price per share so I can compare the grant properly? I'd also ask for a longer exercise window than 90 days, since that decides whether the options are worth anything if I ever leave."

## 7. Competing offer template

Use only if true, or if another process is genuinely advanced. Don't invent a number.

> "I want to be open with you: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company for a Staff role, at a base above CHF 180k. Yuno is my preference, because of the product and the stage you're at, and I'd rather decide on the work than on the money. If you can get to CHF 175k base with the option grant we discussed, I'm ready to sign this week."

## 8. Before signing

- Employment setup (EOR, contractor, entity) confirmed in writing, including who pays Swiss social charges.
- Salary currency: push for CHF. If paid in EUR, ask for an annual FX review or a currency adjustment clause.
- Working hours: Madrid and Geneva share a timezone, but the company started in Bogotá. Clarify how much evening overlap with Latin America is expected.
- Option grant letter with share count, strike, vesting, exercise window and acceleration terms.
- Written six-month review date and criteria, if agreed.
