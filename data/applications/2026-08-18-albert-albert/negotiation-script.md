# Negotiation Script: Albert, Principal / Distinguished Engineer

## Market anchors

Albert's own HN posting states compensation up to roughly $750k cash plus meaningful equity for principal/distinguished hires, which is well above typical US bands for the level (Distinguished Engineer market is usually $280k-450k+ base+bonus, with total comp climbing higher at companies that pay top of market). That posted ceiling is the anchor to negotiate against, not a generic Switzerland salary survey, since the role is remote and Albert has already signaled it pays US tech comp regardless of where the hire sits.

For context if a Swiss-based band comes up instead: Staff/Principal engineers in Geneva at fintech or scale-up companies typically land CHF 180k-260k base, and Geneva-based comp discussions should reference this only as a floor, not a ceiling, given Albert's stated range is well above local market.

## Opening anchor

Ask for the top of Albert's stated range rather than the middle. Framing:

"Given the scope of a principal/distinguished build and the range you've posted publicly, I'd want to start the conversation around $650k-750k total cash comp, with equity sized for someone owning a net-new product line rather than a feature team. I've taken something from zero to acquisition before, so I know what the first six months of this actually requires."

Don't open with a number below the midpoint of their own stated range. Anchoring low signals you didn't read the posting closely, and the posting is public.

## If they come in low

If the offer lands meaningfully under $650k total or treats "principal" as closer to a senior staff band:

"I want to make sure we're calibrated on scope before we talk numbers again. Is this role closer to principal-track ownership of a net-new product, or is it staff-level execution within an existing roadmap? The comp range you posted was pretty specific, so I'd like to understand where in that range this offer sits and why."

This forces them to either justify the gap against their own public number or move. Avoid ultimatums this early; ask a scope question first, renegotiate second.

## Equity notes

- Confirm whether Albert is still private and get the last 409A valuation date and strike price before evaluating any options grant.
- Ask for the fully diluted share count, not just the number of options offered, so the percentage is real.
- Standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff is the baseline; for a principal/distinguished hire building a net-new line, it's reasonable to ask for a slightly front-loaded schedule or a shorter cliff given the seniority.
- Given profitability for 4+ years, ask directly whether there's any near-term liquidity path (tender offer, secondary window) since profitable late-stage companies sometimes run these annually. If they do, that changes how much weight to put on equity vs. cash.
- Push for anti-dilution protection language or at minimum a commitment to standard refresh grants tied to performance, not just initial hire.

## Geographic discount pushback

If anyone raises a Geneva-based discount despite the role being remote-first:

"I noticed the posting is remote almost anywhere, so I'd assume comp is tied to the role and scope rather than location. Geneva's cost of living is among the highest in Europe, and the CHF has stayed strong against the dollar, so a location adjustment would actually move the wrong direction here if one were applied."

If they insist on a location-based band regardless, ask for the specific methodology (which cities, which multiplier) rather than accepting a flat percentage cut. Vague "cost of living adjustment" language with no stated methodology is a signal to hold firm on the US-anchored number.

## Competing offer template

Use only if there's an actual competing process in flight, don't fabricate one:

"I'm mid-process with another company at a similar level, and I'd rather make a decision based on the full picture than rush either side. If you can get me a formal offer within [X days], I can give you a clean answer instead of dragging this out."

Even without a named competitor, timeline pressure framed as "I want to decide, not stall" reads as confident rather than bluffing, as long as it's not paired with a fake company name or number.
