# Negotiation Script — Director of Engineering, Tucuvi

## Market Context

**Tucuvi profile:** Spanish company, Series A ($20M raised), clinical AI SaaS, ~20-50 person company. Remote role. Compensation in EUR.

**Spanish Director of Engineering market (2025-2026):**
- Mid-market / Series A range: €90,000 - €120,000
- Top-of-market for specialized AI/healthtech: €120,000 - €140,000
- Directors with founding-engineer profiles and acquisitions behind them: upper band, €130,000 - €145,000

**Geneva cost-of-living adjustment (for context):**
- Equivalent role in Geneva: CHF 180,000 - CHF 220,000+
- That's roughly €190,000 - €230,000 at current exchange
- Use this as background context, not as an aggressive anchor in the first conversation

**Target range:** €130,000 - €145,000 base + equity
**Walk-away floor:** €115,000 base if equity is meaningful

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask about compensation expectations, anchor at the top of the Spanish market and reference the profile:

> "Based on the scope of the role and the market for this level in Spain, I'm thinking somewhere in the €135,000 - €145,000 range for base. That's consistent with Director-level compensation at well-funded Series A companies in the healthtech space. I'm also expecting equity to be part of the discussion at this stage."

If they push back that this is above their range:

> "I understand that may be above the initial budget. I'm open to discussing how equity and the variable component factor in — I'd rather get the full picture before deciding if there's a gap. What does the total comp package look like?"

---

## Counter-Offer Script (If They Come In Low)

**Scenario: They offer €100,000 - €110,000 base**

> "I appreciate the offer and the transparency. I'll be honest: the base is below where I expected to land. At €[X], it's difficult to make the math work from Geneva, where the cost of living is significantly higher than Spain. I'd need to get closer to €130,000 on the base, or find a way to bridge that gap through equity or performance components. Is there flexibility to move the base, or is there room to discuss a larger equity grant?"

**If they can't move the base but offer more equity:**

> "I can work with that if the equity is real. Can we talk through the current cap table, valuation, vesting schedule, and what a realistic exit scenario looks like? I want to understand what that equity is actually worth, not just the face value."

---

## Geneva Premium Pushback

Use this if they try to anchor to Spanish market rates:

> "I take the point on the Spanish market. Worth noting that I'm based in Geneva, where rent alone runs CHF 2,500 - 3,500/month for a two-bedroom, and overall cost of living is 40-50% higher than Barcelona or Madrid. I'm not asking for the Geneva market rate, but I do need the compensation to reflect that reality. €130,000 is already a meaningful discount to what I'd expect in a local Swiss role."

---

## Equity Negotiation

For a $20M Series A company, target:

- **Director of Engineering:** 0.25% - 0.75% depending on current team size and stage
- Ask explicitly: current fully diluted cap table percentage, last 409A valuation (or equivalent), anti-dilution provisions
- Vesting: standard 4-year with 1-year cliff is fine; ask about acceleration on change of control (double-trigger preferred)
- If they offer options below 0.25%: "Given the stage and the scope of this role, I'd expect equity in the 0.4-0.6% range. Can you walk me through what's been granted to the existing engineering leadership?"

**Phantom shares / warrants note:** Some European companies use phantom shares instead of real equity. Ask upfront:

> "Are these real options, or phantom shares? I want to understand the structure before we discuss the percentage."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in play (or want to create urgency):

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in conversations with two other companies at a similar stage. Both are moving toward offers. My preference is Tucuvi based on the product mission and the engineering culture signals I've seen, but I need to give them an answer within the next 10 days. Is there a timeline that works on your end to get to an offer?"

If you don't have a competing offer but want to compress their timeline:

> "I'm actively in process with a few other companies and expect to have decisions to make within two to three weeks. If Tucuvi is moving toward an offer, I'd appreciate knowing roughly when I might hear back so I can manage my timeline responsibly."

---

## Language / Spanish Skill Note

The job description requires fluency in Spanish and English. If this comes up in negotiation or at offer stage and Spanish is limited, address it directly rather than letting it fester:

> "My Spanish is [conversational / basic / functional in technical contexts]. I'm aware the listing specifies fluency. I want to be honest about where I am rather than oversell it. Is this a hard requirement for the role as currently scoped, or is there flexibility depending on the team composition?"

This is better than discovering it's a dealbreaker after an offer is extended.

---

## Variables to Negotiate Beyond Base

- Performance bonus: ask for the structure and historical payout rate
- Professional development budget: confirm the amount and what it covers
- Hardware: MacBook confirmed in the listing
- Flexible hours / async: confirmed in the listing, but get it in writing
- Team-building travel: who pays for the 2+ annual events (flights, accommodation)
- Notice period: Geneva-based employment may have different notice period norms than Spain
