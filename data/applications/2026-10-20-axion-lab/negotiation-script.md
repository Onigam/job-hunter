# Negotiation Script, Axion Lab (Founding Product Engineer)

## Context that shapes this negotiation

Axion Lab has two cofounders and no engineers yet. The posting gives no salary, only "high-equity founding role, 4-year vesting, 6-month cliff". I found no public funding information, so assume pre-seed or early seed and a cash budget that's tight. The company operates out of Luxembourg/Paris/Amsterdam and will likely think in EUR at French or Benelux startup rates. You live in Geneva and your costs are in CHF. Most of this negotiation is about closing that gap, and about the equity making up for whatever cash they can't pay.

Ask early, before any number comes up: "What round are you at, how much runway do you have, and what does the cap table look like today?" Every answer below depends on it.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

These are working ranges built from general market knowledge, not a cited survey. Check them against levels.fyi, Glassdoor CH, Swissdevjobs salary data and the Choose France/Welcome to the Jungle startup salary reports before you talk numbers.

| Benchmark | Base salary | Equity |
|---|---|---|
| Geneva/Zurich, Staff engineer at a scale-up | CHF 170k to 210k | small, often RSUs or options at 0.05% to 0.2% |
| Geneva, founding engineer at a funded seed startup | CHF 140k to 175k | 0.5% to 1.5% |
| Paris, senior/founding engineer at seed | EUR 75k to 105k | 0.5% to 1.5% (BSPCE) |
| Luxembourg, senior engineer at a startup | EUR 90k to 120k | varies widely |
| First engineer, pre-seed, two founders | often 60% to 80% of market cash | 1% to 3%, sometimes more |

Your position: 17 years of experience, a cofounder exit, and a background that fits the job description closely. You should be at the top of whichever band applies.

## Your numbers

- **Target:** CHF 165k (about EUR 175k at current rates, recheck the FX on the day) plus 1.5% to 2% equity.
- **Opening anchor:** CHF 180k base plus 2.5% equity, or a cash/equity menu (see below).
- **Walk-away floor:** CHF 135k base with at least 1.5%, or a lower cash figure only if equity goes clearly above 2.5% and the runway is 18+ months.

Settle the employment structure before agreeing on a number. A Swiss resident working for an EU company with no Swiss entity usually means an Employer of Record or a contractor arrangement. With an EOR, Swiss employer social charges (roughly 7% to 15% on top, depending on the scheme) land on them. As a contractor you pay those charges yourself and have no LPP or unemployment cover, so the day rate needs to be 25% to 35% above the salaried equivalent.

## Opening script

> "Thanks for sending this over. To be straight about where I am: I live in Geneva and my costs are in Swiss francs, and for a Staff-level engineer here, cash at a funded early startup runs from about CHF 140k to 175k. Given I'd own the whole technical side from day one, and I've already taken a platform from zero to acquisition once, I'm looking at CHF 180k base with around 2.5% equity. I know cash is tight at this stage, so I'm happy to look at a mix that trades some salary for more ownership."

Then stop talking.

## Cash/equity menu

Give them choices rather than a single demand. It lets them pick based on runway, and either way you come out ahead:

> "Here are three versions I'd be glad to sign:
> A: CHF 175k and 1.5%.
> B: CHF 155k and 2.25%.
> C: CHF 135k and 3%, with salary moving to CHF 170k when you close the next round."

The step-up clause in C costs them nothing today and protects you once the money comes in.

## If they come in low

**If they offer Paris-level cash (EUR 80k to 95k):**

> "I understand that's what a senior hire costs in Paris. The trouble is that it isn't the market I live in, and I'm not asking for a Geneva premium, just a number that works in Geneva. If the cash really can't move, the equity has to make up for it, and the 0.5% to 1% that's typical for a seed-stage hire wouldn't. Can we look at 2.5% or more, plus a written salary review when you raise?"

**If they say "everyone gets the same band":**

> "That makes sense for later hires. The first engineer is a different job though: I'd be setting the architecture, infra and hiring bar that everyone after me inherits. I'd expect that to show up in the equity at least."

**If they need time:**

> "Of course. I'd only ask that we agree on the structure, EOR or contract, at the same time, since that changes the real number by 15% or more."

## Geographic discount pushback

> "I hear the remote argument, but it cuts both ways. You'd get someone who's done this exact job before, in your timezone, two to four hours by train or plane from your work weeks, without relocation costs. Geneva's cost of living is among the highest in Europe, so a discount to Paris rates is a real pay cut for me, not a fair adjustment."

Travel for the Paris/Luxembourg/Amsterdam work weeks should be paid on top of salary. Get that in writing.

## Equity, what to check and ask for

- **Percentage on a fully diluted basis**, and the current valuation or strike price. A share count without the total is meaningless.
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 6-month cliff is better than the usual 12 months. Keep it.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination) on at least 50% of unvested shares. As first engineer this matters, since acquirers often replace early technical leads.
- **Exercise window:** ask for 5 to 10 years after leaving instead of 90 days. Founders can usually grant this.
- **Instrument and tax:** BSPCE (France) and Luxembourg plans behave differently. As a Geneva resident, options are generally taxed as income at exercise, and the taxable amount may be pro-rated across countries if you've worked from several. Have a Swiss tax advisor look at the plan before signing.
- **Anti-dilution:** employees almost never get it, so don't spend capital asking. Ask instead for a **refresh grant review** after each priced round, or a pro-rata top-up if your stake drops below an agreed floor.
- **Early exercise:** if the plan allows it, exercising while the strike is still near zero can reduce tax later. Check this with the advisor too.

## Competing offer template

Use only if real. If there's no actual offer, use the "market data" version instead and don't invent one.

**Real offer:**

> "I want to be transparent: I have an offer from another company at CHF [X] base. I'd rather join Axion because of [the AI-plus-experts model / owning the platform from zero], so I'm not trying to start a bidding war. I'd just need us to get closer on [cash or equity] for this to be an easy yes."

**No offer, market data only:**

> "I'm talking to a few other teams at a similar stage, and the founding roles I've seen in Switzerland sit around CHF 150k to 175k with 1% to 2%. I'd like Axion to be competitive with that, especially on equity, since it's where you've got room."

## Non-salary items worth asking for

- Hiring budget and timeline, so you're not the only engineer for 18 months.
- Learning/conference budget (EUR 2k to 3k per year is reasonable).
- Hardware and AI tooling budget (models, API spend for your own development) on the company card.
- Title: "Founding Engineer" or "Head of Engineering", with a written path to CTO as the team grows.

## Closing

> "If we can land on [chosen option] with the EOR setup and the acceleration clause, I'm ready to sign this week."
