# Negotiation Script: Staff Engineer, Platform & Infrastructure, Nango

Context that shapes the numbers here: Nango is YC-backed (W23), around fourteen people, multi-million ARR with 400+ paying customers, and has disclosed only a small early round, so this reads as an early-stage cap table rather than a company sitting on a large war chest. Third-party salary aggregation for this Staff Platform Engineer opening shows a base range of roughly $140,000 to $220,000, which is a geographically adjusted band rather than one flat global number, and it's wider than most senior roles because it spans North America, LATAM, and Europe. This is a staff-level IC seat with no direct reports, explicitly the first hire whose main job is the platform itself, so comp should track that scope, not headcount.

## Market anchor

Staff-level platform or infrastructure engineer, Geneva-based and remote, at a well-funded early-stage startup: roughly CHF 155,000 to 190,000 base is realistic, reflecting Swiss cost of living and 17+ years of experience that includes founder-level infrastructure ownership. The disclosed $220,000 ceiling for this role, converted at current exchange rates, lands near the top of that Swiss range, so there's genuine room to anchor high without asking for anything unusual. Being the first hire dedicated to the platform is worth a premium over a standard staff slot; someone setting the operating model for infrastructure that a dozen future hires will inherit should be priced for that, not for maintaining something that already exists. Opening anchor: CHF 172,000 base plus an equity grant sized for a foundational infrastructure hire.

## Opening anchor script

"Given the scope here, being the first person whose primary focus is the platform itself on a fourteen-person team, I'd expect a base around CHF 172,000 if it's priced against Geneva, plus an equity grant that reflects setting the operating model other infrastructure hires will inherit. I saw your ranges vary by region. Can you tell me which band Switzerland or a comparable high-cost European market falls under, and whether that's base only or total comp?"

## If they come in low

"I hear the number. Is that the Swiss-adjusted band, or closer to where a lower-cost region in your range would land? I'm anchoring to the scope of the role, owning the cloud platform and leading BYOC into a real product, not just the base geography."

If they hold the line: "I can flex on base if the equity grant is sized for someone setting up the platform function from scratch rather than joining an existing one, or if there's a defined comp review tied to BYOC hitting a specific milestone, first paying customer on it, say, within six months. What I'd rather avoid is landing below CHF 155,000 equivalent, that's under market for this level and this scope where I'm based."

## Equity notes

Ask for the current valuation basis, whether that's the last priced round or a 409A-equivalent, plus the strike price and vesting schedule; four years with a one-year cliff is standard, worth confirming Nango follows it. With only a small amount disclosed raised against multi-million ARR, the company looks capital-efficient and probably hasn't diluted heavily, so it's worth asking directly what percentage or share count a staff-level, platform-founding grant represents rather than accepting a dollar figure alone. Ask about the post-termination exercise window too; early-stage grants sometimes carry short windows that matter more in practice than the headline grant size. Since this role owns compliance-adjacent infrastructure work, SOC 2, GDPR, HIPAA reviews, it's also worth asking whether there's a defined path to a title or comp bump once those programs are live, since that's usually when a first platform hire's scope visibly expands.

## Geographic discount pushback

"Your listings show ranges spanning roughly $140,000 to $220,000 depending on region. Geneva is one of the more expensive cities globally, on par with or above most US tech hubs for cost of living, so I'd want the band here treated as high-cost rather than discounted the way some remote-first companies default to for Europe. Happy to share comparable cost-of-living data if that's useful."

## Competing offer, if genuinely in play

"I'm in later-stage conversations with another company at a comparable scope and a similar range to what I mentioned. This role is the one I'm most drawn to given the BYOC problem specifically, but I wanted to be upfront about timing so we can move at a pace that works for both of us."

## Close

Being early-stage and YC-backed is a reason to push on equity, not to accept a discounted base; a team running production infrastructure for 400+ paying customers has already proven the product works, so the risk premium on base salary should be lower than at a true pre-revenue startup, even while the equity upside stays real. Get the base, equity percentage or share count, vesting schedule, and exercise window terms in writing before accepting. Early-stage offers move fast, and verbal numbers from a recruiter call have a way of shifting by the time the written offer shows up.
