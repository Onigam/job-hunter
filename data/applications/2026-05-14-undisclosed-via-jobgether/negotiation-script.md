# Negotiation Script — Lead Applied AI Engineer (Undisclosed via Jobgether)

## Market Context

**Role level:** Lead / Principal AI Engineer (IC, cross-team influence)
**Location:** Geneva, Switzerland / Europe Remote
**Currency:** CHF preferred for Switzerland-based employment; EUR if contracted through a European entity

### Switzerland Market Rates (2026, Senior/Lead AI Engineer)

- Senior AI Engineer (5-8 yrs): CHF 140,000-170,000 base
- Lead / Staff AI Engineer (8+ yrs, cross-team): CHF 165,000-210,000 base
- Founding/Principal AI Engineer at funded startup: CHF 150,000-185,000 + meaningful equity

Magino's profile (17+ years, cofounder, acquisition exit, Staff IC at Benevity) places him firmly in the upper half of the Lead range. The AI premium is real in 2026: engineers with production LLM deployment experience command 15-25% above general software engineering rates.

**Target range:** CHF 185,000-205,000 base, depending on company stage
**Walk-away floor:** CHF 160,000 base (with meaningful equity) or CHF 175,000 (cash-only, no equity)

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask for your salary expectation, anchor high before they give a number:

> "Based on my level and the Geneva / Swiss remote market for Lead AI engineers with production deployment experience, I'm looking at CHF 195,000-205,000 base. I'm open to discussing the full package including equity, but that's where I'd want to land on the cash side."

If they ask for a single number:

> "Let's call it CHF 200,000 as a starting point. That reflects the seniority level and the current market for applied AI work at Lead or Principal level in Switzerland."

## If Their Offer Comes In Low

**Scenario A: Offer is CHF 155,000-170,000**

> "I appreciate the offer. To be honest, it's lower than where I'd need to be given the scope of the role and the market I'm benchmarking against. Geneva-based Senior Staff and Lead AI engineers with production GenAI experience are coming in between CHF 175,000 and 205,000. Can we get closer to CHF 185,000 on the base? I'm flexible on the structure, but I need the base to move."

**Scenario B: Offer is CHF 170,000-180,000**

> "That's closer. The base is a bit below what I had in mind based on market data. Would there be room to move to CHF 190,000? If the base is fixed at 175,000, I'd want to revisit the equity or signing bonus to get the total closer to where I need to be in year one."

**Scenario C: Offer is CHF 180,000+**

> "That's in range. Before I say yes, I want to make sure I understand the equity component. Can you walk me through the option grant, vesting schedule, and current valuation?"

## Equity Negotiation (Startup or Scaleup)

If the company is venture-backed or pre-IPO, equity becomes a significant lever.

**Questions to ask before negotiating equity:**

- "What's the current valuation and when was the last round priced?"
- "How many shares outstanding? What percentage does the grant represent?"
- "What's the vesting schedule and cliff?"
- "Is there any accelerated vesting on acquisition or change of control?"
- "What's the strike price relative to the last 409A or FMV?"

**Standard vesting to expect:** 4 years with 1-year cliff. Push for double-trigger acceleration on acquisition if possible.

**Equity counter if the grant feels light:**

> "The option grant you've described represents less than 0.1% at the current valuation. For a Lead-level hire with founding engineer experience who's expected to shape AI strategy across the org, I'd expect something in the 0.15-0.25% range. Is there room to revisit the grant size?"

## Geneva / Switzerland Geographic Premium

Do not accept a geographic discount framing. Magino is already in Geneva and doesn't require relocation support. The correct framing:

> "I'm already in Geneva, so there's no relocation cost to factor in. What you're getting is full European timezone coverage with no setup friction. I'd expect the comp to reflect Swiss market rates, not a discounted 'remote' rate."

If they push back citing "European remote" rates (i.e., Eastern Europe benchmarks):

> "For context, my current benchmark is Geneva-market rates and Benevity's Senior Staff compensation, which is a Swiss-incorporated entity. A pan-European remote rate would be a step down from where I am now, which doesn't work for me."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another process moving, use it. If not, use a soft version:

> "I should be transparent: I'm in active conversations with a few other companies at a similar stage and level. I'm not trying to create artificial pressure, but if this role is the right fit I'd want to get there quickly. Can we move on a decision by end of next week?"

**If they ask what the competing offer is:**

> "I'd rather not share specifics until I have a written offer in hand, but I can say the comp discussions are in the CHF 185,000-200,000 range. I'm genuinely interested in this role on the merits, which is why I'm being direct about where I need to land."

---

## Signing Bonus

If base is capped, a signing bonus is a clean one-time lever:

> "If the base is firm at 175,000, would there be room for a CHF 20,000-25,000 signing bonus to bridge the gap in year one? It doesn't affect your run-rate comp after that, but it makes the total comp work for me right away."

---

## Non-Negotiables to Confirm

Before signing anything, confirm in writing:

- Base salary in CHF (or EUR equivalent with exchange rate clause)
- Equity grant: number of options, strike price, vesting schedule
- Notice period (Switzerland default is 3 months; negotiate down to 1 month if possible for startups)
- Remote work policy formalized in contract
- Equipment allowance or home office setup
- Whether Swiss social contributions (AHV/ALV) are employer-paid on top of gross, or included
