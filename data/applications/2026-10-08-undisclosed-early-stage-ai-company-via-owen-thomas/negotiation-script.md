# Negotiation Script: Lead Product Engineer → CTO (AI & Data Infrastructure)

_Undisclosed early-stage AI company, via Owen Thomas (recruiter). Posted range: EUR 80-100K base + equity. EU remote / flexible London._

The posted base is well under what a Staff+ or CTO-track engineer earns in Geneva. Equity and the written path to CTO have to carry this deal, so most of the negotiation effort goes there, not into squeezing the last EUR 10K of salary.

All figures below are working estimates from public salary surveys and typical seed-stage terms. Check them against current sources before the first comp conversation.

---

## 1. Market reference points

| Benchmark | Approximate base | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Staff / Principal Engineer, Geneva or Zurich, scale-up | CHF 170-210K | Plus bonus or RSUs at larger firms |
| CTO, seed to Series A startup, Switzerland | CHF 160-220K | Usually with 1-5% equity depending on timing |
| Lead / Staff engineer, London AI startup | GBP 100-140K | Roughly EUR 115-165K |
| Senior engineer, EU remote (non-CH) | EUR 80-110K | The pool this posting is priced against |

The posted EUR 80-100K is priced for the last row. The role is the first two rows: hands-on lead today, CTO tomorrow, owning the core platform at a company that already has paying customers.

**Walk-away base.** Decide it before the call. A reasonable floor, given Geneva living costs, is around EUR 105K, and only if the equity is strong. Below that, the equity would need to be exceptional (close to cofounder-level) to make sense.

---

## 2. Opening anchor

Let them name a number first; the range is already public, so don't volunteer one before the second conversation. When asked directly:

> "The range in the posting is built for a senior engineer role. What you're describing is the person who owns the platform now and runs technology later. For that, I'm looking at a base around EUR 125K, with equity sized for someone who's on track to be CTO. I've built a platform from zero to acquisition before, so I know what the first engineering hire is worth to a company in your position. Where's the flexibility on your side, base or equity?"

**Why EUR 125K:** it sits at the low end of London lead-engineer comp, clearly below Swiss Staff+ rates (which shows you've already discounted), and leaves room to land at EUR 110-115K.

---

## 3. If they come in low (at or under EUR 100K)

> "I understand the seed-stage budget, and I'm not asking you to pay Zurich rates. But EUR 100K in Geneva is roughly what a mid-level engineer makes here, for a role that's meant to grow into CTO. If base is fixed, I'd want that gap made up in equity, and I'd want the CTO path in writing. Can we look at both together?"

If they can't move base at all, trade it for, in order of priority:

1. More equity (see section 4)
2. A written base review tied to the next funding round, e.g. base goes to EUR 120K+ on closing a Series A
3. The CTO title, or a dated review for it, in the offer letter
4. A signing bonus or a guaranteed annual budget for travel to London

---

## 4. Equity

This is where the deal is won or lost.

**Size.** A senior first hire who's expected to become CTO at a seed company with paying customers would typically see somewhere around 1-3%. Below 1% the role is being priced as an employee, not a future executive. Ask for the fully diluted number, not just a share count.

> "Can you give me the grant as a percentage of fully diluted shares, including the option pool? And what's the last round's valuation and preference stack, so I can work out what the shares are actually worth?"

**Vesting and cliff.** Four years with a one-year cliff is standard. Ask for:

- Double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination) on at least 50% of unvested shares. You've been through an acquisition, so you can say from experience why this matters.
- A post-termination exercise window of several years instead of the usual 90 days.

**Promotion grant.** Ask for a top-up grant on stepping into the CTO role, and get the trigger written down.

> "If the plan is for me to become CTO, I'd like the offer to say what triggers that, roughly when we'd review it, and that a top-up grant comes with it. Otherwise the equity I'm signing for now is priced for a different job."

**Anti-dilution.** Employees almost never get anti-dilution protection, so don't spend capital on it. A refresh grant on promotion or after a priced round gets the same effect and is far easier to agree to.

**Swiss tax.** If you live in Geneva and hold options in a UK or EU entity, how the options are taxed depends on Swiss rules, and options in unlisted companies are generally taxed when they're exercised. Ask whether the company has granted options to a Swiss resident before, and get advice before signing.

---

## 5. Geographic discount pushback

They may argue that EU remote means EU pay, whoever the hire is.

> "I get that the range is EU-wide. But you're not hiring for location, you're hiring for someone who's done this before. Geneva costs more than most of the EU and I'm not asking to be paid like I'm in Zurich. I'm asking to be paid like the person who'll own your platform. London lead roles at AI companies pay above EUR 110K, and I'm on the same timezone and a short flight away."

**Employment setup.** A Swiss resident is likely to be hired through an EOR or as a contractor. Find out which before agreeing on a number:

- **EOR:** Swiss employer social charges add cost on their side. Agree on the gross salary, and make sure they aren't quietly cutting it to cover the EOR fee.
- **Contractor:** you pay your own social contributions (AVS/AHV and pension) and get no paid leave. A day rate or contractor fee needs to sit roughly 20-30% above the equivalent employee base to come out even.

---

## 6. Competing offer template

Only use this if there's a real process running. Keep it factual and don't bluff about numbers.

> "To be straight with you, I'm also in late-stage conversations with [company] for a [Staff / Principal] role based in Switzerland. Their package is higher on base. I'd still rather build something here, because owning the data platform and the path to CTO is what I'm after. Can you get closer on [base / equity] so that the decision is about the role and not the money?"

If there isn't a competing offer, use the market instead:

> "I'm talking to a few companies at the moment, and Staff-level roles in Switzerland are coming in around CHF 170K and up. I'm not trying to match that here, but I need the equity to close the gap."

---

## 7. Through the recruiter

Owen Thomas runs the process, so the first comp conversation will probably be with them.

> "Before we go further, can you tell me how fixed the 80-100K range is, and how the client thinks about equity for this role? I'm based in Geneva, so I want to make sure we're not wasting each other's time on base."

Recruiters on contingency usually get paid as a percentage of base, so they have a reason to push base up. Use that, but save the equity conversation for the founders, since they're the only ones who can actually size it.

---

## Summary

| Item | Target | Acceptable | Walk away |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | EUR 125K | EUR 110-115K | Under ~EUR 105K without exceptional equity |
| Equity | 2-3% fully diluted | ~1.5% plus promotion grant | Under 1% |
| CTO path | Dated, written trigger | Written review at Series A | Verbal only |
| Acceleration | Double-trigger, 100% | Double-trigger, 50% | None |
| Exercise window | 5-10 years | 2+ years | 90 days, unless the grant is shares rather than options |
