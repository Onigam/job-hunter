# Negotiation Script — VP Engineering / CTO
## Well-Funded AI Infrastructure Startup (via Jack & Jill)

---

## Market Context

**Published range:** $300,000–$500,000 USD + equity  
**Role level:** VP Engineering / CTO, early-stage stealth startup  
**Location:** Remote (US-anchored offer; Geneva-based candidate)

### US market benchmarks for VP Eng / CTO at funded AI startups (2026)
- Seed / Series A: $250k–$400k base + 0.5–2.5% equity
- Series B+: $300k–$500k base + 0.25–1% equity
- Published range at top end matches or exceeds Series B+ norms, suggesting strong funding and competitive recruitment

### Geneva / Switzerland context
- CHF equivalent at current rates (~0.88 USD/CHF): $450k USD = ~CHF 396k, $500k USD = ~CHF 440k
- Swiss VP Eng market rate: CHF 230k–330k at established tech companies, higher at US-listed or US-funded firms
- This offer, if paid in USD, is well above Swiss domestic market — frame as a premium role, not a concession
- If company proposes CHF localization: reject or negotiate an exchange rate floor guarantee

---

## Opening Anchor

**Target:** $460,000–$480,000 USD base + 1.5–2% equity

**If they ask your number first:**
> "Based on the scope you've described — building and leading across four engineering layers, managing a global team, and being the first engineering executive at this stage — I'd expect to be in the $460k–$480k range for base. The equity conversation is just as important to me, and I'd want to understand the current cap table and what percentage you're budgeting for this role."

**If they give a number first (bottom of range, e.g. $320k):**
> "I appreciate the transparency on the range. Given what the role actually requires — full-stack technical ownership from hardware to AI orchestration, plus building and managing a global team from a relatively early stage — the bottom of the range doesn't reflect the seniority of the hire you're making. I'd need to be closer to $450k to move forward seriously. Is there flexibility toward the top of what you published?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

### Target parameters
- **Percentage:** 1.5–2% for a founding VP/CTO role at seed or Series A
- **Vesting:** 4 years, 1-year cliff (standard; push back if they propose longer cliff)
- **Acceleration:** Double-trigger acceleration on acquisition (critical given this candidate has been through an acquisition — you know exactly how this plays out)
- **Anti-dilution:** Pro-rata participation rights in future rounds if possible; at minimum ask for information rights

### Script
> "On equity, I want to make sure we're aligned on what a VP/CTO role at this stage is worth in terms of ownership. For a company at seed or early Series A, I'd expect to be in the 1.5–2% range, vesting over four years with a one-year cliff. I'd also want to make sure there's double-trigger acceleration in case of acquisition — I've been through an acquisition and I know how important that clause is for someone at my level. Can you tell me what percentage you've reserved for this hire and how the pool is structured?"

### If they push back on percentage
> "I understand if the pool is constrained. In that case I'd want to talk about how we close the gap on total comp — whether that's a signing bonus, higher base, or accelerated vesting on a smaller grant. What's the flexibility there?"

---

## Remote from Geneva: Logistics and Risk Mitigation

The role lists Austria, Canada, California, and Oregon as locations — Geneva isn't listed. Raise this proactively rather than letting it become a late-stage blocker.

**Script:**
> "One thing I want to flag early: I'm based in Geneva, Switzerland, which isn't in the listed locations. I want to make sure that's workable for your legal and payroll setup before we go deep into the process. I'm open to a contractor arrangement, an Employer of Record structure, or exploring what setup makes sense on your end. I've worked remote across US/Europe time zones for the last four years and it hasn't been an issue operationally."

### If they propose a cost-of-living adjustment downward for Geneva
> "Geneva is actually one of the most expensive cities in the world — higher cost-of-living index than San Francisco or New York. I'd push back on a downward adjustment. If anything, the Geneva cost baseline supports the higher end of the range."

---

## Monthly Travel Requirement

The role mentions monthly international travel. Factor this into negotiation.

- Request: company covers business class for intercontinental travel (8h+)
- Request: clear travel policy in writing before signing
- Request: travel weeks count toward any remote/in-person ratio requirements

**Script if it comes up:**
> "I'm fine with the monthly travel — I want to make sure we're aligned on what that looks like logistically. Business class for flights over six hours, clear reimbursement policy, and those travel weeks don't count against any minimum in-person days if that's ever formalized. Is that standard for the team?"

## Competing Offer Template

If you receive another offer (or want to use one as leverage):

> "I want to be transparent with you: I'm in late stages with another company for a similar role, and they've come in at [X]. You're my first choice because of the technical depth of what you're building. If you can get to [target number], I can sign this week."

Even a hypothetical framing works if you have genuine alternatives in process:
> "I have a couple of processes that are moving quickly. I'd rather close this one than manage multiple competing timelines — if we can align on [number], I'd be comfortable committing."

---

## Signals to Watch

- If they move fast and don't negotiate: you may have anchored too low
- If they push back hard on equity: ask about the total pool size and last round valuation to understand actual value
- If they're vague on funding stage: ask directly ("Can you share the round size and current valuation cap?") — you need this to evaluate equity meaningfully
- Clinical / regulated environment: ask about FDA/CE regulatory timeline, it affects how fast you can actually ship
