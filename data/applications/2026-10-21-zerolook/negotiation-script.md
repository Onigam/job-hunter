**Your numbers:**

If they push for a single number:

"Thanks, I want to make this work. At the bottom of the band, the equity has to do a lot more of the work. Could we go to CHF 150k, or keep the base and increase the grant? Either way I'd like to see the percentage fully diluted rather than a number of options."

If base really is fixed at CHF 140k:

"Okay. Then I'd like to talk about the other levers: a larger grant, a compensation review tied to the next round closing, and covering my Geneva-Zurich travel. Any of those would close the gap."

If they use the gaps against you (C++, travel tech, no 1K+ QPS numbers):

"That's fair, and I told you about them in my letter. What you're hiring is someone to design the system, set the standards and build the team, and I've done all three from a blank page and through an acquisition. I'd hire someone strong in C++ for the hot path. The domain I'll learn from you."

## Equity: what to ask for

Ask for everything in writing before signing. Swiss startup ESOPs vary a lot.

- **Percentage, fully diluted.** Number of options means nothing without the total share count, including the pool and any convertible notes or SAFEs from the pre-seed.
- **Strike price and instrument.** Options, or shares through a purchase or a phantom plan? Ask what the strike is relative to the last round's price.
- **Vesting.** Four years with a one-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff. Since you'd be in near the founding moment, a shorter cliff (6 months) is a reasonable ask.
- **Acceleration.** Double-trigger acceleration on change of control (acquisition plus termination) for at least 50% of unvested equity. You've been through an acquisition and know how integration goes; this is worth pushing on.
- **Leaver clauses.** Swiss plans often have good-leaver and bad-leaver rules where a bad leaver loses vested options or must sell them back at nominal value. Get the definitions, and make sure resigning after vesting doesn't count as bad leaver.
- **Exercise window.** Ask for a long post-departure exercise window (years, not 90 days).
- **Anti-dilution and refresh.** You won't get anti-dilution protection on an employee grant, but you can ask for a refresh grant tied to the seed or Series A, since your percentage will shrink at each round.
- **Tax.** In Switzerland, options in unlisted companies are usually taxed as income when you exercise, on the gap between value and strike. Ask whether the company has (or will get) a tax ruling from the cantonal tax authority (Zurich or Zug) to fix the valuation method. It makes a real difference at exit.

## Geneva-Zurich commute

You're in Geneva. Zurich is roughly 2h45 each way by train, and the sibling posting says hybrid 2-3 days. That's a real cost in money and time.

"I'm based in Geneva and happy to be in Zurich on the office days. Given the distance, I'd like the company to cover a GA travelcard (roughly CHF 4k a year) or equivalent travel costs, and to agree on two office days rather than three."

If they suggest you move:

"Not in the short term. Geneva is home. I'd rather we agree on a working pattern that keeps me in Zurich when it matters, like design sessions, hiring loops and founder meetings, and remote the rest of the time."

Note: since the company is in Zurich (or Zug), there's no Geneva cost-of-living argument to make on salary. The argument is about travel cost and time, not location premium.
