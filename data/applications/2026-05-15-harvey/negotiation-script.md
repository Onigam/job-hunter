# Negotiation Script — Harvey, Staff Software Engineer Platform

---

## Market Context

Harvey is a well-funded US legal AI company backed by Sequoia, Kleiner Perkins, OpenAI Startup Fund, and a16z. As of May 2026 they have 1,000+ customers across 60+ countries. The company is operating at a scale where Staff Software Engineer, Platform is a meaningful senior IC role expected to drive platform-wide technical decisions.

Harvey has publicly posted Staff backend roles in the $238,000–$290,000 USD base range. For a Staff-level hire with a founding background and 17 years of experience, the target should be at or above the top of that band.

For Magino in Geneva, the negotiation plays out differently depending on the arrangement:

**Scenario A — Remote from Geneva (most likely)**
US companies hiring remotely in Switzerland typically use an EOR (Employer of Record). Geneva is consistently one of the top 3 most expensive cities in the world. Push back hard on any geographic discount framing.

**Scenario B — Relocation to SF or NY**
Full US compensation applies. SF cost of living is comparable to Geneva on housing. No discount is justified.

## Opening Anchor Script

When they ask for your number first:

> "For a Staff-level platform role at Harvey's stage, I'd anchor around $320,000 base. That's consistent with what Staff engineers with 15+ years of experience and a founding background are seeing at AI companies at this scale. What's the range you're working with for this position?"

If they've shared a range below your floor:

> "The range you've shared is below where I'd need to land to make this work. I have other conversations at the $290,000+ level, and I'm not in a position to discount significantly from that. Is there flexibility on base, or room on the equity side to bridge the gap?"

---

## Counter-Offer Script (if initial offer is low)

If they come in at $250,000 base or below:

> "I appreciate the offer. The base is lower than what I was expecting at this level, about $40,000–50,000 below where I'm anchoring. I want to work through this, so let me be direct: I'd need to see either the base come up to $295,000 or above, or a meaningful increase in the equity grant to compensate for the delta. I'm not looking to drag this out, but I also don't want to start by taking a step back on total comp. What flexibility does the team have?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

Harvey is private, so equity is options or RSUs. Ask these questions before negotiating the number:

- What is the current 409A valuation? (Establishes strike price for options)
- What was the last preferred share price from the most recent round?
- What percentage of the fully-diluted cap table does the grant represent?
- What's the vesting schedule? Standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Push back on anything longer.
- Is there acceleration on change of control? Harvey is a credible acquisition target given its position in legal AI.

Script for equity conversation:

> "I'm treating equity as real compensation, not a lottery ticket, so I want to understand the numbers properly. Can you share the 409A and the last preferred price, and tell me what percentage of the fully diluted cap table my grant represents? That helps me value it against the cash comp."

If the equity seems thin for the level:

> "For a Staff Platform Engineer with org-wide infrastructure scope, I'd expect to be in the 0.15–0.25% range. If the base is also below market, those two things compound. I'd rather have a clear conversation about total comp now than leave it vague."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they try to apply a Geneva discount to a US-level role:

> "Geneva is consistently ranked in the top 3 most expensive cities in the world, alongside Zurich and Singapore. It's more expensive than San Francisco on most measures, not less. A geographic discount in this direction would actually be backwards. I'm open to discussing total comp structure, but I won't accept a discount based on location."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

Even without a hard competing offer in hand, you can frame your positioning:

> "I'm in conversations with a few other companies at similar levels, including at least one other infrastructure-focused role at an AI company. I'm not using that as a pressure tactic, but it does mean I have a reasonable sense of market rates right now, and I want to land somewhere that reflects that."

If you have a specific competing offer:

> "I have an offer on the table at [company] for [base + equity summary]. I'd prefer to join Harvey if we can close the gap, but I need to make a decision by [date]. Is there anything you can do on base or equity?"

---

## Other Terms to Negotiate

- **Remote work policy**: Get it in writing. Confirm explicitly whether "remote" means async-friendly or requires SF/NY overlap hours.
- **Home office setup**: CHF 2,000–5,000 is standard in Switzerland for a senior remote hire.
- **Learning and conference budget**: $3,000–5,000/year is reasonable at this level.
- **Notice period**: Swiss employment law requires a notice period; confirm Harvey's EOR can accommodate the standard 1–3 months.
- **Post-termination exercise window**: If options, push for a 5-year window rather than the standard 90 days. For a senior hire, 90 days is too short given the cost of exercise at a high-valuation company.
