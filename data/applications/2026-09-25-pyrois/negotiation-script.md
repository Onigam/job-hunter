# Negotiation Script: PYROIS, Founding Engineer, Orchestration (Zurich / remote)

The posting gives a band: **CHF 120k to 140k base**, plus options from the employee pool, 25 days off, equipment and AI tools budget. The LinkedIn listing tags it "entry level", which is almost certainly a default setting; the requirements (production orchestration, on-call, capacity planning) describe a senior hire. You're coming in well above the profile they probably pictured, so the conversation is about where in the band you land, how much equity makes up the difference, and whether the band stretches.

## 1. Market picture (Switzerland, 2026 estimates)

| Reference point | Base (CHF) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed / seed founding engineer, Zurich | 110k to 150k | Equity is the main lever |
| Series A/B scale-up, Senior/Staff platform engineer | 150k to 190k | Small option grant |
| Big tech Zurich, Staff level | 220k to 280k | Plus liquid RSUs |
| Pyrois posted band | 120k to 140k | Options on top, amount not disclosed |

Pre-seed companies rarely have room on cash, and pushing hard on base usually costs you goodwill for little money. Aim for the top of the band and put most of your effort into equity and terms.

**Your targets**
- Walk-away: CHF 130k base only if the equity is strong (see section 4), otherwise 140k
- Target: CHF 140k to 145k base
- Opening anchor: CHF 150k base plus a founding-level option grant

## 2. Opening the conversation

If they ask for expectations first:

> "I've seen the 120 to 140 range in the posting. Given I'd be bringing 17 years of platform work and experience taking a company from zero to acquisition, I'd expect to be at the top of it or a bit above, around CHF 150k. That said, at pre-seed I care more about the equity being right than squeezing the base, so I'd like to talk about both together."

Justification, in your own words: you've already done the founding engineer job once, end to end, through an acquisition by a $1B+ company. You've hired and onboarded engineers, which matters for a company building its first ten. You'd be one of the few people on the team who has been through due diligence from the technical side.

Don't give a range of your own. Repeat 150k if pushed.

## 3. If the offer comes in low

> "Thanks, I'm keen on this. The base is lower than I'd hoped for the experience I'm bringing. If you can do CHF 140k and we can talk about the option grant, I think we'll get there."

If they say 140k is a hard ceiling:

> "Understood, I know the cash constraints at this stage. Then I'd like the equity to reflect that I'm taking a discount against what I'd get at a later-stage company. Can we look at the grant size, and a base review once you close the seed?"

A written commitment to revisit base at the next priced round is realistic and cheap for them to give. Ask for it in the offer letter, not as a verbal promise.

Don't accept on the call. "Let me look at the whole package and come back to you by Friday" is always fine.

## 4. Equity

Among the first ten hires at pre-seed, a senior founding engineer typically gets somewhere around **0.5% to 1.5%**, depending on how early and how senior. Ask these directly:

- **What percentage of the fully diluted shares does the grant represent?** Share counts alone mean nothing without this.
- **Strike price and the last round's valuation.** Low strike now is the whole point of joining early.
- **Vesting.** Four years with a one-year cliff is standard. If they're flexible, ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Exercise window after leaving.** The usual 90 days forces you to pay cash and tax on an illiquid asset. Ask for several years; some startups now offer up to ten.
- **Acceleration.** Ask for double-trigger acceleration (a change of control plus termination without cause). Given your history with an acquisition, you can explain exactly why it matters.
- **Anti-dilution.** Employees don't get anti-dilution protection, and asking for it signals you don't know how rounds work. Ask instead whether there's a refresh grant policy after future rounds.

**Swiss tax note.** For unlisted Swiss companies, options are generally taxed as income when you exercise them, on the spread between strike and fair value, and social security applies. Ask whether they've obtained a tax ruling from the cantonal authorities for the plan, and get advice from a Swiss tax adviser before exercising anything.

## 5. Location and commute (Geneva to Zurich)

The posting allows three days a week in Zurich, or fully remote with four hours of daily overlap. Geneva to Zurich is roughly two hours forty by train each way, so three office days a week isn't realistic long term.

> "I'm in Geneva, so I'd propose remote as the default and coming to Zurich for one or two days most weeks, plus off-sites and anything important in person. I'm happy to make the trips on my own time, but I'd ask you to cover a GA travelcard or the equivalent in train costs."

If they try a remote discount:

> "Geneva's cost of living is on par with Zurich, and I'd be working your hours with the full overlap. The work and the output are the same, so I'd expect the same pay."

## 6. Competing offer template

Only use this if you actually have a competing process going. Never invent one.

> "I want to be open with you: I'm in late stages with another company, and they've come in at [CHF X base / equity summary]. Pyrois is my first choice because of the orchestration problem and the stage. If we can close the gap on [base / grant size / exercise window], I'd be ready to sign this week."

Name the one or two things that would get you to yes. A long list reads as a bidding war.

## 7. Checklist before signing

- Base, grant size as a % of fully diluted shares, strike price, and vesting all written down
- Post-termination exercise window and acceleration terms in the option agreement
- Base review at seed close in writing
- Remote arrangement and travel cost coverage in the contract or offer letter
- Notice period (three months is common in Switzerland) and IP assignment clause read carefully
