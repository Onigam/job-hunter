# Negotiation Script — TRM Labs, Staff Software Engineer, Agent Engineering

---

## Context worth naming up front

The posting says "US Remote" in the title but the fuller listing describes an SF office expectation of at least three days a week. Those two things don't reconcile cleanly, and it's the first thing to clarify before any comp conversation goes further: is this genuinely remote-eligible for someone outside the US, hybrid-only for Bay Area residents, or a "remote" req that's really written for domestic candidates and will need an exception made? TRM Labs is a US company; hiring a Geneva-based engineer full time almost certainly means an Employer of Record (EOR) or a relocation conversation, not a standard US W-2 offer. That distinction changes the entire negotiation, so get it answered in the first call, before naming a number.

Posted range for the role is $200,000 to $275,000 USD base, tied to a domestic SF hire. For a Staff-level engineer with founding-CTO scope and a real acquisition outcome, the target should sit at or above the top of that band regardless of which structure ends up applying.

## Market anchors

- **If hired as a Swiss-based remote employee (EOR):** US AI/crime-intelligence companies hiring remotely into Switzerland typically benchmark closer to their own domestic senior bands than to generic Swiss ML salary data, since they're competing for the same scarce agent-engineering skill set globally. A realistic target is CHF 220,000 to 250,000 base, equivalent to roughly $250,000 to $280,000 at current rates, which lines up with the top of TRM's posted band once currency and Geneva cost of living are accounted for.
- **If relocation to SF is required:** Full US comp applies directly. SF and Geneva housing costs are broadly comparable, so there's no case for a discount either direction. Anchor at $275,000 to $300,000 base given Staff scope and founding background, with the posted band treated as a floor, not a target.
- **Generic Swiss "software engineer" salary data is close to irrelevant here.** TRM Labs is competing on a US AI-lab pay scale for agent engineering talent, not a local Geneva market rate, and the negotiation should be anchored accordingly from the first conversation.

## Opening anchor

"Before I put a number on the table, I want to understand the structure: is this role genuinely open to a Geneva-based remote hire through an EOR, or does it assume SF residency with hybrid attendance? That changes what a fair number looks like on both sides."

Once structure is clear, if pushed for a number:

"Given Staff-level scope, the acquisition track record, and how the market for people who've actually shipped production agent systems is right now, I'd want to open around $275,000 base if we're talking a remote or EOR arrangement, with equity and any sign-on discussed on top."

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

If the offer lands near the bottom of the posted band, under roughly $220,000:

"I appreciate the offer, and I want to be direct about where it sits: this reads closer to a mid-Staff or senior-IC number than what I'd expect for the scope described, tool use, memory, planning, and observability across a full agent framework, plus the founding and platform background I'd bring. Is there room to move closer to the top of the range you posted, or is part of the gap something equity or a signing bonus could close instead?"

If they lean on the remote-from-Switzerland angle to justify a lower number:

"I'd push back gently on that. Geneva isn't a discount market, it's one of the most expensive cities in the world, and if the EOR structure is adding cost on your side, I'd rather understand that cost directly than have it show up as a lower base. What does the EOR arrangement actually change about how comp gets structured here?"

## Equity notes

Ask directly what stage TRM Labs is at for this grant cycle and whether the offer includes options or RSUs, since that changes vesting math meaningfully. Standard ask: 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff, and clarity on strike price and the last 409A valuation if options are being offered. Given the role's Staff scope and the company's apparent scale (posted range in the $200K-275K band suggests a well-capitalized later-stage company, not an early seed), push for the equity number to be quoted as a percentage or share count up front rather than left as "we'll figure that out after you accept," since dollar-value framing on illiquid equity is the easiest place for an offer to look better than it is.

## Geographic discount pushback

If any variant of "Swiss salaries are lower than US ones, so we'll adjust" comes up, the answer is the same regardless of phrasing: Geneva is consistently ranked among the three most expensive cities globally, and remote hiring into Switzerland doesn't save TRM Labs office costs the way hiring into a lower-cost region would. If the EOR fee itself is the real constraint, ask to see how much of it is being passed through as a comp reduction versus absorbed as a cost of doing business, and negotiate from that number rather than an assumed discount.

## Competing offer leverage template

Use only if genuinely in play: "I'm also in later-stage conversations for a Staff platform role with a comparable band, so I'd like to move on this relatively quickly if the structure and comp line up. I'd rather work from your best offer than run a bidding process." This creates urgency without requiring a number that can't be substantiated.

## Close

End the comp conversation with concrete next steps rather than leaving it open: confirm whether the remote-from-Geneva path is real or whether relocation is a hard requirement, get the equity terms in writing including vesting and strike details, and propose a specific date for a follow-up call once TRM Labs has checked what's actually possible on the EOR or relocation question.
