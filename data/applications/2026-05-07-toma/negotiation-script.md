# Negotiation Script — Toma, Founding Engineer (AI Products)

---

## Market Context

Toma is a US-based (San Francisco) Series A startup, $17M raised from a16z. The role is "Founding Engineer" which at a 20-person startup typically commands equity in the 0.25%–1.0% range (post-Series A dilution) depending on seniority and timing. Base salary for this level at a well-funded SF startup typically falls in the $180K–$230K USD range. Magino is based in Geneva, which creates a geographic negotiation angle — but the right frame is to position Geneva as a feature (European timezone coverage, no SF cost-of-living subsidy needed), not as a liability.

---

## Opening Anchor

If asked for a number first, deflect once:

> "I'd rather understand the full package first — base, equity, and any benefits. I've seen enough variance across offers that I don't want to anchor before I have the full picture."

If pressed for a number:

> "For a founding engineer role at a well-funded Series A, I'd expect to be in the $200K–$220K USD base range, with meaningful equity that reflects the early-stage risk and ownership. What does the band look like?"

Do not go below $185K base without a compelling equity offset.

---

## If They Come In Low (below $185K base)

> "I appreciate the offer. The base is a bit below what I'd expect for this level and stage. I'm genuinely interested in this role, and I want to make it work — but I'd need to see either the base move to at least $200K, or the equity compensate for the gap. Which of those is more flexible on your side?"

Never accept a lowball without a counter. A counter is expected; it doesn't hurt the offer.

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Understand the grant before evaluating it:**

Key questions to ask:
- What percentage of the fully diluted cap table does the grant represent? (Get the number, not just the option count.)
- What is the current 409A valuation? What was the Series A post-money valuation?
- What's the vesting schedule? (Standard is 4-year with 1-year cliff — push back on anything longer.)
- Is there any acceleration clause on acquisition? (Single-trigger preferred; double-trigger is common but worth asking about.)
- What's the option exercise window after leaving? (10-year window is the gold standard; 90 days is punitive — flag it.)

**Equity counter script:**

> "The option count looks like X. Can you tell me what percentage of the fully diluted cap table that represents? I want to make sure I'm reading this correctly before we finalize."

If the equity is below 0.5% for a founding engineer role:

> "For a founding engineer hire at this stage, I'd expect to be closer to 0.5–0.75% on a fully diluted basis. Is there flexibility to move the grant up, especially given the timing and the ownership scope you've described?"

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

Toma may try to apply a "remote from Geneva" discount. The counter:

> "I understand some companies apply location-based adjustments, but I'd push back on that framing here. Geneva is expensive by any measure — cost of living is on par with San Francisco. And from a business standpoint, European timezone coverage from day one is a feature, not a concession. I'd prefer to evaluate this on the same basis as a US-based candidate."

If they insist on a discount, require a higher equity grant to offset:

> "If the base has to reflect a location adjustment, I'd want to see that gap made up in equity. What's the cap on what you can offer at the founding engineer level?"

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have other offers (or want to create urgency without fabricating):

> "I want to be transparent: I'm at the offer stage with a couple of other companies. Nothing I'd prefer to Toma, but it does mean my timeline is compressed. If we can move quickly on finalizing the terms, I can commit this week."

Even if the competing offers are early-stage, this is honest framing that creates legitimate urgency without lying.

---

## Benefits and Extras to Negotiate

- Home office setup budget (standard at US remote startups: $1K–$2K)
- Co-working stipend (Geneva has good options: CHF 300–500/month is reasonable to ask for)
- Health insurance: US startups typically don't cover Swiss private insurance. Ask for a stipend of CHF 500–700/month to offset Lamal or supplemental coverage.
- Equity refresh: Ask about the cadence for refresh grants — typically annual for high performers.

---

## Final Close

> "I'm genuinely in on this. The role is exactly what I'm looking for and I think I can contribute quickly. If we can land on [X base] and [Y% equity], I'm ready to sign."

Keep the close short and confident. No hedging.
