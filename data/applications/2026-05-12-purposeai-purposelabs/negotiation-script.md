# Negotiation Script — Purpose:Labs Co-Founder / CTO

---

## Context and Role Structure

This is a co-founder role, not a standard employment position. That changes the entire compensation frame. The conversation is not "what salary do they offer" but "how do we structure a co-founder deal that reflects the risk and the contribution." Come in knowing the difference between a co-founder equity grant and an employee option package — they are not the same thing.

Purpose:Labs appears to be pre-seed or seed stage. The founding team is likely small. That means equity should be meaningful.

---

## Market Anchors — Switzerland

**CTO at an established Swiss tech company (Series B+):** CHF 180,000–240,000 base
**VP Engineering / Head of Engineering, Series A:** CHF 150,000–180,000 base
**Technical Co-Founder, seed stage:** CHF 90,000–130,000 base + significant equity stake (2–8%)

**Why this matters for your conversation:** If they come in with a below-market salary framing, you acknowledge it's a co-founder role and reframe the conversation around total value: salary + equity + founding team economics.

Geneva-specific note: your cost of living is higher than most European cities and on par with Zurich. If they try to apply a discount for "remote from Geneva," push back. You're not a remote contractor; you're a co-founder.

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask about compensation expectations, lead with the co-founder framing, not a salary number.

Script:
> "I think of this less as a salary discussion and more as a co-founding conversation. What I want to understand first is: how is the founding equity structured? How many co-founders are there currently, and how is the cap table set up? Those answers matter more to me at this stage than the base number."

If they push for a salary figure early:
> "For something that's truly a co-founding engagement, I'd expect a base in the range of CHF 120,000–140,000, with equity that reflects a technical co-founder contribution — somewhere between 3% and 6% depending on where we are in the conversation about role scope and vesting structure."

---

## Equity Framework

**What to ask for:**
- Co-founder equity: 3–6% is a reasonable starting point for a technical co-founder joining at pre-seed/seed with an existing non-technical founder
- If the company is further along (has funding, has customers), anchor at 3–4%
- If this is truly day-one co-founding, 5–8% is defensible

**Standard vesting terms (non-negotiable baseline):**
- 4-year vesting schedule
- 1-year cliff
- Single-trigger acceleration on acquisition (at minimum)
- Double-trigger preferred

**What to watch for:**
- Phantom shares vs. real equity: some Swiss startups offer phantom shares (virtual stock appreciation rights). These are not the same as actual equity. They don't convert if the company sells in a non-cash deal and have different tax treatment. Push for real equity where possible.
- Dilution: ask for anti-dilution protection (at least weighted average, not just standard dilution). As a co-founder, you want to understand how future funding rounds affect your stake.
- Good leaver / bad leaver clauses: make sure the definitions are fair and that unvested shares are treated reasonably on departure.

---

## If They Come in Low on Salary

If the base offer is below CHF 100,000:

> "I understand that early-stage co-founder economics often mean taking below-market salary in exchange for meaningful equity upside. I'm open to that trade, but the salary floor needs to reflect cost of living in Geneva and the seniority I'm bringing. If we're talking below CHF 110k, the equity stake needs to move up to compensate for the gap. What I want to avoid is a structure where I'm taking both the salary risk and the equity risk without appropriate upside."

---

## If They Push Back on Equity

If they offer employee-level options (0.5–1%) framed as "competitive":

> "That package makes sense for a senior engineering hire. For a co-founding engagement where I'm taking the same founding risk and making the same architectural bets from day one, the equity should reflect a co-founder stake, not an employee grant. The difference isn't just financial — it's about how we're structuring the founding relationship. Can you walk me through how the current cap table is structured and where a technical co-founder would typically land?"

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have other offers (or want to use hypothetical leverage):

> "I'm in conversations with a few other opportunities — one is a VP Engineering role at a funded company in the CHF 170k range, and another is a similar co-founding situation with different equity economics. I'm genuinely more interested in what you're building here, but I want to make sure the structure reflects that this is a co-founding commitment, not a senior hire."

Note: only use competing offer leverage if it's real or you can defend the framing under follow-up questions. Hypothetical leverage can backfire with technical founders who probe.

---

## Benefits to Negotiate Beyond Cash

- Health insurance supplement (mandatory in Switzerland, but quality varies)
- Professional development budget (conferences, courses)
- Home office setup stipend if working remotely from Geneva
- Travel reimbursement for Zurich presence
- Board observer or board seat as technical co-founder (common, worth asking)

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

- They refuse to discuss the cap table before you sign
- The vesting schedule has no cliff (means equity can be taken immediately then left)
- "We'll figure out equity after you join" — this is not how co-founding works
- The total equity pool is too small for a meaningful co-founder grant (check: if total ESOP pool is 10–15%, a co-founder grant of 3–5% is realistic; if the pool is 5%, something is off)
