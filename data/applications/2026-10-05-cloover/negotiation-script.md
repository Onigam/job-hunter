# Negotiation Script: Cloover, Staff Software Engineer (Berlin)

**Context.** Cloover is a Berlin company building software and financing for solar, battery and heat pump installers in Germany and Austria. It has raised about $1.22B in total, but roughly $1.2B of that is committed debt for financing customer loans and only about $22M is a Series A equity round. The most recent recorded round is a $100M debt facility in September 2026. The role is office-first in Berlin and requires relocation from Geneva.

The posting promises a "strong base, outcome-tied bonus and meaningful equity" and publishes no range. All figures below are estimates from public sources (Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Swiss and German salary surveys, recruiter-reported ranges). None has been verified with Cloover. Refresh them before the first recruiter call.

The core tension: Berlin pays far less than Geneva, so the move means a real pay cut. The negotiation is about narrowing that cut with equity, bonus and relocation support, and about deciding in advance where the walk-away line sits.

---

## 1. Market anchors

### Berlin, Staff Engineer at a funded scale-up (estimates)

| Component | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Base | ~EUR 110k to 140k | Top Berlin scale-ups occasionally reach ~150k for Staff |
| Bonus | ~10% to 15% | "Outcome-tied" per the posting, ask how it's measured |
| Equity (VSOP) | ~0.1% to 0.3% | Series A stage, 4-year vesting typical |
| Total cash | ~EUR 120k to 160k | |

### Switzerland (the walk-away baseline)

| Setting | Base (CHF) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Staff / Senior Staff, Geneva or Zürich scale-up | ~CHF 180k to 230k | Bonus and sometimes equity |
| Principal / Head of Engineering, Swiss Series A/B | ~CHF 190k to 240k | 0.3% to 1% equity |
| Big tech Staff, Zürich | ~CHF 350k to 500k total | The ceiling, not a realistic comparable here |

At current rates CHF 200k is roughly EUR 210k gross. Swiss taxes and social charges are lower than Germany's, so the take-home gap is wider than the headline numbers suggest, though Berlin rent is meaningfully lower than Geneva's. Run a net-income comparison with real figures before any call.

---

## 2. Opening anchor

Try to get their range first.

> "Before I give a number, can you share the band you've budgeted for this role? It'd help me understand how Cloover thinks about Staff level."

If they insist on a number first:

> "I'm currently benchmarking against Staff roles in Switzerland, where base sits around CHF 200k. I understand Berlin is a different market and I'm not expecting a Geneva salary. For this role I'm looking at a base around EUR 150k, plus the bonus and an equity grant that reflects Staff scope at Series A stage. Relocation support would matter too, since I'd be moving a household from Geneva."

Why EUR 150k: it sits at the top of the Berlin Staff range and leaves room to land at ~140k. Justify it with seventeen years of experience, technical cofounder of a platform through acquisition by a $1B+ company, and Staff-level cross-team scope at Benevity, which maps directly to "set technical direction" in the posting.

---

## 3. If they come in low

### Scenario A: base around EUR 115k to 125k

> "Thanks, I can tell you've thought about this. I'm still keen on the role. The base is below where I'd need to be, though, especially since moving from Geneva means a real step down in income for my household. Is there room to get closer to 140k? If base is fixed, I'd want to talk about the equity grant and a signing bonus to bridge the first year."

### Scenario B: they can't move base at all

Shift the conversation to:

- A larger VSOP grant, or a refresh after 12 months tied to performance.
- A one-off signing or relocation bonus (EUR 15k to 25k is a reasonable ask for an international move).
- Clear bonus criteria in writing, with a guaranteed minimum in year one.
- A six-month compensation review written into the offer.

> "I understand the base is set. If we can't move it, could we look at the equity grant and a relocation bonus? Those would close most of the gap for me, and I'd be glad to tie part of it to the six-month review."

### Scenario C: they try to level as Senior instead of Staff

> "The role you posted is Staff, and the work you described, technical direction across teams and architecture reviews, is what I've been doing at Benevity. I'd rather start at the right level and be held to it than come in a level down. What would you need to see from me to be confident about Staff?"

---

## 4. Equity notes (German Series A)

Berlin startups almost always use a **VSOP** (virtual stock option plan), not real shares. Ask about it directly.

- **What you actually get:** a cash claim at an exit event, not shareholder rights. Ask for the number of virtual shares, the fully diluted share count, and the strike price, so you can compute a percentage.
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 12-month cliff is standard. Ask whether vesting accelerates on acquisition (single or double trigger). Magino has been through an acquisition and knows how much this matters.
- **Leaver clauses:** check good-leaver and bad-leaver terms carefully. Some VSOPs forfeit vested units if you leave before an exit. Push for vested units to survive a good-leaver departure.
- **Tax:** VSOP payouts are taxed as employment income in Germany when paid out. If Magino later moves back to Switzerland, tax allocation across countries becomes complicated; ask for a tax adviser's view before signing.
- **Dilution:** the round history is unusual, little equity and lots of debt. Debt doesn't dilute, which is good for the equity's percentage, but ask how the debt facilities rank at exit and how much debt the company carries relative to equity. A lender-heavy capital structure can shrink what's left for common or virtual holders.
- **Ask:** "What did the last Series A round value the company at, and what does my grant represent as a percentage of fully diluted shares?"

---

## 5. Geography pushback

Here the usual geographic discount argument runs the other way. Cloover isn't discounting Magino for living somewhere cheap; Magino is taking a pay cut by moving somewhere cheaper. Use that.

> "I'm moving from one of the most expensive labour markets in Europe, and I'm not asking you to match it. What I'm asking is that the package reflects that you're hiring someone who's choosing Berlin over Swiss offers, and that the move itself has a real cost."

Concrete relocation asks:

- Relocation package covering the move, temporary housing (1 to 3 months) and a flat-rate allowance.
- Help with German registration (Anmeldung), tax setup and a work permit if needed. With EU citizenship no visa is required; confirm which applies before the call.
- A short-term remote phase during the move, if the office-first policy allows any flexibility at all.

---

## 6. Competing offer template

Use only if real, or frame as a process in progress rather than an offer.

> "I want to be transparent. I'm in late-stage conversations with a couple of Swiss companies for Staff and Principal roles, where base is around CHF 200k. Cloover is my preference because of the problem you're working on, and I'm willing to take a lower number for that. I'd just need the package to make the move work for my family. Is there anything you can do on base or equity to help me decide?"

---

## 7. Final checklist before accepting

- [ ] Level confirmed as Staff in writing.
- [ ] Base, bonus target and bonus criteria in writing.
- [ ] VSOP terms reviewed: grant size, fully diluted %, strike, vesting, cliff, acceleration, leaver clauses.
- [ ] Relocation package and start date agreed.
- [ ] Net income comparison (Geneva vs Berlin) done with real tax figures.
- [ ] Clarity on what "office-first" means in practice (days per week, travel flexibility).
- [ ] Probation period (Probezeit, usually 6 months) and notice period understood.
