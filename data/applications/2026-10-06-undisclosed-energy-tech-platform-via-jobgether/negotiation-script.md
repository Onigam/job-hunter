# Negotiation Script: Staff Full Stack Engineer, Sell Side Engine (undisclosed energy tech platform via Jobgether)

**Context.** Fully remote, listed for Switzerland, hands-on Staff IC in a small squad rebuilding renewable-energy origination, contracting and onboarding. Unlike most Jobgether listings, this one publishes a number: **£104,000 base "or equivalent in local currency"**, plus stock options, 25 days' holiday, a £1,200/€1,200 equipment allowance and £150/€150 a month for wellness. Pricing in pounds first suggests a UK-headquartered company with an EU presence, and a UK band.

Two risks shape this negotiation:

1. **Currency and market.** £104k is roughly CHF 110k to 115k depending on the day's rate. That's well under the Swiss market for Staff level, and the company will likely read "equivalent in local currency" as an FX conversion, not as a Swiss market rate.
2. **Contract type.** LinkedIn lists the role as "Contract". If that means a B2B contractor arrangement rather than employment through an Employer of Record, Magino would carry all Swiss social charges (AVS/AI/APG as self-employed, no employer LPP contribution, no paid holiday or sick pay), and the same headline number is worth far less.

Figures below are estimates from public comp data (Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Swiss salary surveys, UK scale-up benchmarks), not verified offers. Check current GBP/CHF before the call.

---

## 1. Market anchors

### Switzerland (where Magino lives and would work)

| Role / setting | Base (CHF) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Staff Engineer, Swiss scale-up (Zurich/Geneva) | 170k to 210k | Options or small bonus |
| Senior Staff / Principal, Swiss scale-up | 200k to 250k | Magino's most recent level |
| Senior/Staff engineer, Swiss energy trading or utility | 150k to 200k + bonus | Trading-adjacent tech pays less bonus than the desk |
| Big tech Staff, Zurich | 230k to 300k+ total | Ceiling, not a target here |

### UK (what the published band is built on)

| Role / setting | Base (GBP) |
|---|---|
| Staff Engineer, London energy-tech or fintech scale-up | 100k to 135k |
| Staff Engineer, remote UK, Series B/C | 95k to 120k |

So £104k is a fair UK Staff number and a low Swiss one. The argument isn't that they're underpaying by their own standards; it's that the role is being hired in Switzerland.

### Contractor equivalent

If it really is B2B, an employee earning CHF 190k costs an employer roughly CHF 220k to 230k once social charges and pension are added, and a contractor also loses about 35 paid days a year (holiday plus public holidays). A day rate of **CHF 1,000 to 1,150** across ~220 billable days is the rough equivalent. Anything below about CHF 850/day is a meaningful step down.

### Target for this role (employment via EOR or Swiss entity)

- **Walk-away:** CHF 160k base, or £104k only if options are genuinely large and the contract is proper employment.
- **Realistic target:** CHF 170k to 185k base (roughly £160k to £175k at current rates, which they'll balk at in pounds, so keep the conversation in francs).
- **Opening anchor:** CHF 195k base.

The gap between their number and the realistic target is large. Be ready for the answer to be a hard no on base, and have the equity and title levers ready.

---

## 2. Before naming any number

Ask the Jobgether recruiter three questions, in this order:

> "Is this role employment, through an EOR or a local entity, or a contractor agreement?"
>
> "The posting says £104k or equivalent in local currency. Is that a fixed band, or is there room for the Swiss market?"
>
> "Is the £104k the midpoint or the top of the band?"

The first answer changes every number in this file. Don't negotiate base until it's clear.

---

## 3. Opening anchor

> "Thanks for publishing the range, it saves us both time. I'll be honest about the gap: I'm in Geneva, and Staff-level engineers here are generally paid in the CHF 180k to 210k range at scale-ups. A straight currency conversion of £104k lands around CHF 110k, which is closer to a mid-level salary in this market. To make this work I'd be looking at around CHF 195k base, with the options package on top. I understand that's above the UK band, so I'd like to know how much flexibility exists before we go further."

Say the number, then stop talking.

---

## 4. Counter-offer scripts

### If they hold at £104k (or CHF ~110k)

> "I understand the band is set. At that number, I'd need the rest of the package to carry more weight. Could we look at a larger option grant, a guaranteed review at six months against agreed outcomes, and a sign-on to bridge the first year? Without some of that, I don't think I can make the numbers work from Geneva."

### If they come up partway (e.g. £120k or CHF 130k to 145k)

> "That's a real move and I appreciate it. If base can reach CHF 165k, I'm comfortable on the rest. If that's the ceiling, I'd want the option grant sized to reflect the difference, and I'd like the strike price and the latest preferred share price in writing so I can value it properly."

### If they say "everyone is paid on the UK band, regardless of location"

> "That's a fair policy for people living in the UK. The trouble is the role is listed for Switzerland and my costs, including mandatory health insurance and pension contributions, are Swiss. A UK band applied to Geneva is effectively a 40% to 50% discount on the local market. I'm not asking for a premium, just to be paid at the market I'm hired in."

### If it turns out to be a contractor agreement

> "As a contractor in Switzerland I'd carry the full social charges myself and lose paid holiday, so I'd need to price it as a day rate. For this scope I'd be at CHF 1,100 a day. If you'd rather keep a salary structure, I'd prefer employment through an EOR, which also protects you on misclassification risk."

## 5. Equity notes

The posting says "stock options" with no size. UK companies often use EMI options, which aren't available to people outside UK employment, so Magino would probably get unapproved options. In Switzerland, options are generally taxed as income at exercise (or at grant in some cantonal practices for listed or valued options), so confirm with a Geneva tax advisor before relying on any figure.

Ask for, in writing:

- **Number of options and percentage of fully diluted shares.** For a hands-on Staff hire at Series B/C, 0.05% to 0.2% is a typical range; earlier stage, more.
- **Strike price and latest preferred price**, plus the last valuation round date.
- **Vesting and cliff.** Four years with a one-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Post-departure exercise window.** 90 days is common and painful; ask for five to ten years, or at least two.
- **Acceleration.** Double-trigger acceleration on acquisition (change of control plus termination) is worth asking for at Staff level. Given the Alaya exit, Magino can speak to why it matters.
- **Liquidation preferences.** Ask whether any round has more than 1x or participating preferences, since that decides what common options are worth in a modest exit.
- **Anti-dilution.** Employees almost never get anti-dilution protection. Ask instead for a refresh grant policy so the stake isn't diluted to nothing by later rounds.

Script:

> "Could you share the option count, the fully diluted share count and the current strike? I'd also like to understand the exercise window after leaving, and whether there's any acceleration on change of control. I've been through an acquisition as a founder, so I know how much those terms matter at the end."

---

## 6. Geographic discount pushback

> "I understand remote roles often get priced to a cheaper market, but Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe. Health insurance alone is around CHF 400 to 500 a month per adult, paid personally, and that's before rent. Paying me at the UK rate isn't neutral, it's a large cut relative to where I live. What I can offer in return is a CET-aligned timezone for your European teams and someone who's already done a platform rebuild through an acquisition."

---

## 7. Competing offer template (use only if true, or keep it general)

> "To be transparent, I'm in late-stage conversations with a couple of other teams, including Staff roles with Swiss-market bands. This one interests me most because of the sell-side rebuild and the deal-gating work, and I'd rather make it work here. If you can get closer to CHF 175k, I'm ready to move quickly."

If there's no competing offer, drop the first sentence and keep the rest. Never invent a specific number from another company.

---

## 8. Non-salary levers, in order of value

1. Employment through an EOR instead of a contractor agreement (worth more than most raises).
2. Larger option grant with a longer exercise window.
3. Guaranteed comp review at six months, tied to written outcomes.
4. Sign-on bonus to bridge the first year.
5. Title clarity: confirm "Staff" is reflected in the contract and the levelling framework.
6. Learning budget, conference travel, and the equipment allowance converted to CHF at a fair rate.
