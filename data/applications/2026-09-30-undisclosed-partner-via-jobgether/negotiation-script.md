# Negotiation Script: AI Native Founding Product and Platform Engineer (via Jobgether)

## Who's actually hiring

Jobgether lists the employer as undisclosed, but the posting text matches, nearly word for word, an SGS opening for the same title on SmartRecruiters, based at 3455 Thorndyke Ave W, Seattle. SGS is the Geneva-headquartered testing, inspection and certification group, listed on the SIX Swiss Exchange, with 100,000+ employees. Confirm this with the Jobgether recruiter or on the first call before relying on it.

If it is SGS, that changes the whole negotiation. This isn't a cash-poor startup trading salary for equity. It's a large, profitable listed company running a new AI-native product initiative with a founding team. Cash, bonus and long-term incentives all come from corporate pay bands, so the main levers are level (which band you land in), location (which country's band applies) and whatever incentive exists for the new venture.

## The location question comes first

The Jobgether version says fully remote, US only. SGS is headquartered in Geneva. Before any number comes up, settle which of these you're negotiating:

1. **US role, US payroll.** Requires US work authorization. If you don't have it, ask directly whether they sponsor. For a founding team in a new unit, the answer is often no.
2. **Geneva-based, employed by SGS SA or a Swiss entity, working with the Seattle team.** The most natural fit given where you live and where SGS sits. Expect Swiss corporate bands, and a time-zone overlap requirement (Geneva afternoons to Seattle mornings; the overlap is thin, about 17:00 to 19:00 your time).
3. **Remote from Switzerland via an employer of record.** Possible, but less common for a large group that already has a Swiss legal entity.

Script for raising it early:

"Before we go further, I want to be upfront about location. I'm based in Geneva, a short distance from SGS headquarters, and I don't currently hold US work authorization. Is this role open to someone employed in Switzerland working with the Seattle team, and if so, how would the time-zone overlap work in practice?"

## Your numbers

**Walk-away floor:** write it down before the first call. For a Geneva-based arrangement, your current Senior Staff pay at Benevity is the natural reference; leaving a stable role for a new unit inside a corporate shouldn't mean taking less cash.

**Target (Geneva-based):** CHF 210k to 230k base, 15% to 20% target bonus, plus a long-term incentive grant.

**Opening anchor (Geneva-based):** CHF 240k base plus bonus and LTI, justified by founding scope: you'd own product architecture and the platform the rest of the team builds on.

## Scripts

### When they ask for your expectations first

"I'd like to understand the level and band you have in mind for this role first. Founding roles inside a large group can be scoped very differently. For context, I'm currently a Senior Staff Engineer at Benevity, and before that I cofounded and led engineering at Alaya through its acquisition. The scope here, owning product architecture and the internal platform from day one, reads to me as principal level."

If they insist on a number:

"Based in Geneva, I'm looking at around CHF 240k base, plus the standard bonus and long-term incentive for the level. That reflects the founding scope and what comparable principal roles pay here."

### If they come in low (say CHF 175k, or a Senior rather than Staff/Principal title)

"Thanks for putting a number on it. I want to make this work, and I think the gap is really about level rather than money. The posting asks someone to set the architecture, build the platform, and mentor the engineers who come after, which is principal-level work in most frameworks I know. Could we look at the level first? If it moves, I expect the numbers follow. If the band is fixed, I'd want to close the gap through a sign-on bonus and a larger first LTI grant."

Then stop talking.

### Geographic discount pushback

If they suggest a lower number because the role is "really" a US role being done remotely, or pegged to a cheaper European location:

"Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and it's also where SGS is headquartered, so I'm not asking for an exception, just the local band for the level. Next to a Seattle hire at the same level, I'd expect to come in at roughly comparable cost once you include US benefits and payroll overhead."

### Competing offer template (only use if true)

"I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations for a principal-level role elsewhere, with a package around [X]. SGS is my preference because of the founding scope and because it's here in Geneva, but I need the offer to be in the same range. Is there room to move on base or the LTI grant?"

Don't invent an offer. If there isn't one, use the current Benevity package as the anchor instead: "I'd be leaving a stable Senior Staff role, so the offer needs to at least match that and reflect the extra risk of a new unit."

## Equity and long-term incentives

A listed company won't give startup-style options, but founding roles in new corporate ventures sometimes carry something extra. Ask, and get the answers in writing.

- **LTI eligibility:** Is this level eligible for SGS's long-term incentive plan (performance shares or restricted stock units)? What's the target grant as a percentage of base, and what's the vesting schedule?
- **Venture-specific incentive:** Is the AI-native initiative set up as a separate entity or business unit with its own incentive, such as phantom equity or a value-creation bonus tied to milestones? If it's pitched as a startup inside SGS, it's fair to ask for some startup-style upside.
- **Sign-on:** A sign-on bonus or initial RSU grant is the usual way to make up for unvested compensation you'd leave behind at Benevity. Know that number before the call.
- **Performance conditions:** If the LTI is performance shares, ask what metrics drive vesting (group EPS, TSR) and how often they've paid out in recent years. A group-level metric you can't influence is worth less than it looks.

## Other terms worth negotiating

- **Title and level:** Principal, or Staff at minimum. It affects this band and the next job after this one.
- **Travel:** If you're in Geneva and the team is in Seattle, agree on a travel cadence (e.g. a week per quarter) with the company paying.
- **Working hours:** Put the overlap expectation in writing so late-evening calls don't quietly become a five-day habit.
- **Holidays:** Swiss standard is 25 days; ask for 25 at minimum, 30 if it's negotiable at this level.
- **Pension (LPP/BVG):** Swiss corporate pension plans vary a lot. Ask for the employer contribution rate, since it can be worth several percent of salary.
- **Start date:** Use it as a trade if they can't move further on cash.
