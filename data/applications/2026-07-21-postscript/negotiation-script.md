# Negotiation Script: Postscript, Staff Platform Engineer (Infrastructure)

## Context

Posted range: $190,000 to $230,000 USD base, explicitly stated as having no geographic variation, plus "significant equity." Fully remote since 2018, Series-stage backed by Greylock and YC. No Switzerland office, so the Swiss market comparison is a personal anchor for evaluating the offer, not something to bring up with Postscript directly. Since there's no geo adjustment, the thing to push on is level and scope, not location.

## Switzerland market anchor (for personal reference)

Staff-level platform and infrastructure engineers in the Geneva and Zurich market typically land in the CHF 170,000 to CHF 220,000 total comp range at established tech companies, often with a bonus component, and Swiss income tax is materially lower than most US states. A $230,000 USD base with no state tax withholding, converted at typical rates, lands comfortably above the top of that local band even before equity. That means the top of Postscript's posted range is already a strong outcome relative to staying local. It also means there's real room to push for that top end without the offer feeling unreasonable to accept.

## Opening anchor

Don't open in the middle of the range. Open near or slightly above the top, and justify it with scope, not need.

Script: "I've looked at the range you posted, $190K to $230K. Given the scope here, agent safety guardrails, incident leadership across a 500K events per second system, and IAM and secret rotation ownership, that reads like principal-adjacent scope to me, not the low end of staff. I'd want to talk about landing at $230K base, plus whatever the standard equity band looks like for this level."

## If they come in low (below $210K)

Don't get defensive. Ask what the number is anchored to, then redirect to scope.

Script: "Thanks for sending that over. Can I ask what level that number reflects? Looking at the responsibilities listed, drift detection, validation gates for AI agent operations, incident diagnosis converted into automated runbooks, that's a broader mandate than a typical staff infra role, closer to what I'd expect at the top of your band or slightly above it. I'd like to understand if there's flexibility there before we talk equity."

If they hold firm on base: shift the ask to equity refresh cadence, sign-on, or a defined comp review at 6 months tied to concrete deliverables (for example, shipping the first automated drift-detection runbook).

## Equity negotiation

Since Postscript is YC and Greylock backed and presumably still private, standard checklist:

- Ask for the strike price and the 409A valuation date, not just the share count.
- Confirm vesting schedule: standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Push back if theirs is longer.
- Ask about early exercise eligibility and whether there's a post-termination exercise window longer than the standard 90 days. A 5 or 10 year window is increasingly common and worth asking for directly even if they don't offer it upfront.
- Ask how refresh grants work after year one, since staff-level offers should include a refresh conversation, not just an initial grant.
- If equity feels light relative to base, it's fair to ask for the percentage ownership the grant represents, not just a raw number, since raw share counts are meaningless without knowing the cap table size.

Script: "Could you share the strike price, the vesting schedule, and whether there's an extended post-termination exercise window? I'd also like to understand what percentage of the company the grant represents, just so I can evaluate it properly."

## Geographic discount pushback

Not needed here since the posting explicitly states no geographic variation. If a recruiter tries to introduce one verbally anyway, this is the response:

Script: "The listing states the range doesn't vary by location, so I'd want to keep the conversation anchored to that same range regardless of where I'm based."

## Competing offer angle (use only if real, or reference market data if not)

Script, if there's an actual competing process: "I'm further along in a parallel process with a comparable scope and a base in a similar range. I'd rather move forward here if we can land close to what I outlined, since the role itself is the better fit."

Script, if no competing offer exists yet: don't fabricate one. Lean on market data and scope instead. "Staff-level infra roles with this kind of safety-engineering mandate are commanding the top of posted ranges right now, so I want to make sure we're pricing this at that level."

## Close

Once base and equity are aligned, confirm start date flexibility and ask whether the 6-month comp review can be put in writing, tied to specific milestones rather than a vague "we'll revisit it."
