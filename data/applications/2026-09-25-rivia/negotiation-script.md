# Negotiation Script: Rivia, Senior Frontend (Full-stack) Engineer (Zurich, on-site)

The posting doesn't disclose salary. Rivia is a Zurich AG, founded 2022, that raised a $15M (~€13M) Series A in March 2026 led by Earlybird, with Speedinvest, Defiant, Amino Collective and Nina Capital also in. They report 4x ARR growth in 2025 and ~40 trials running. So: funded, growing, hiring, but still a startup budget. The ranges below are estimates from public Zurich comp data; check levels.fyi, Glassdoor and anyone you know at Earlybird portfolio companies before the first comp call.

## 1. Market picture (Zurich, 2026 estimates)

| Reference point | Base (CHF) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Zurich seed / Series A startup, Senior engineer | 130k to 160k | Options usually 0.05% to 0.3% |
| Zurich Series A/B startup, Lead / Staff engineer | 155k to 185k | Options usually 0.2% to 0.6% |
| Swiss scale-up or healthtech/pharma-adjacent, Senior full-stack | 140k to 170k | Bonus sometimes 5% to 10% |
| Big tech Zurich, Senior / Staff | 170k to 240k | Liquid RSUs on top, not a like-for-like comparison |

The title says "Senior", but you'd bring 17 years, a cofounder exit and Staff-level scope. The main risk here isn't the number, it's being levelled as a mid-career senior and paid at the middle of their band. Fight the level first, then the number.

**Your targets**
- Walk-away base: CHF 150k (below this, the commute cost and level mismatch make it hard to justify)
- Target base: CHF 165k to 175k
- Opening anchor: CHF 185k base plus options in the 0.3% to 0.5% range

## 2. Opening the conversation

If they ask for your expectations first:

> "Given what I'd be taking on, owning the frontend architecture and mentoring the team, and given my background as a cofounder who took a platform through to acquisition, I'm looking at a base around CHF 185k, plus a meaningful option grant. I'm open on how cash and equity split, and I'd like to understand the levelling before we lock anything in."

If they push for a single number, repeat 185k. Don't give a range; they'll hear the bottom of it.

Points to have ready, in your own words: 17 years of engineering, five of them running tech as a cofounder through an acquisition by a $1B+ company, hiring and onboarding the first engineers, and recent Staff-level cross-team architecture at Benevity. You've built exactly the kind of multi-tenant, configuration-heavy product they're scaling.

## 3. If the offer comes in low

Say they come back at CHF 145k to 150k.

> "Thanks, I appreciate it, and I want to make this work. The number is below where I expected, mostly because it looks levelled as a standard senior role. The scope we discussed, owning frontend architecture, leading technically and mentoring, is closer to lead or staff. Can we look at the level first? If the level moves, I think the number follows."

If they say the band is fixed:

> "Understood. Then I'd like to close the gap another way. Could we get to CHF 165k on base, or look at a larger option grant plus a salary review at six months tied to agreed outcomes?"

Silence works. After you state your ask, stop talking.

## 4. Equity (Swiss startup options)

Questions to ask, in writing if possible:
- How many options, and what percentage of fully diluted shares is that today? A number of options alone means nothing.
- Strike price, and the last preferred share price from the Series A.
- Vesting: standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- Exercise window after leaving: Swiss plans often give 90 days or less. Ask for 2+ years, or at least for good-leaver treatment.
- Good leaver / bad leaver definitions. Make sure being let go without cause counts as good leaver.
- Acceleration on change of control. Double-trigger (acquisition plus termination) is a fair ask.
- Anti-dilution: employees rarely get it, but ask how refreshes work after the Series B.

Swiss tax note: depending on how the plan is structured, options can be taxed as income at grant or at exercise, and cantonal rules differ (Zurich vs Geneva if you keep residence there). Ask whether they have a tax ruling for their ESOP, and get a quick opinion from a Swiss tax advisor before signing. This can change the real value of the grant a lot.

## 5. On-site in Zurich from Geneva

The role is on-site in Zurich, which is about 2h45 each way by train from Geneva. This is a real cost and a legitimate thing to negotiate, not a "geographic discount" in their favour. Zurich pay is the reference, whatever your address.

Options to put on the table, in order of preference:
1. Hybrid: 3 days on-site, 2 remote. Frame it around focus work for architecture and code.
2. A GA travelcard paid by the company (roughly CHF 4k a year for 2nd class), plus time on the train counted as working time on travel days.
3. Relocation support if you decide to move, typically one month's salary or a fixed CHF 5k to 10k.

Script:

> "I'm fully up for being in the office regularly; the team visit and working with sales and customer success in person matter. Coming from Geneva, I'd like to agree on a rhythm that works long term. Would three days a week in Zurich work, with the company covering a GA?"

If they push back on anything but full on-site, don't concede on salary to buy remote days. Keep the two conversations separate.

## 6. Competing offer template

Use only if it's true, or as a general reference to other processes without inventing details.

> "I want to be open with you: I'm in late stages with another company, and their offer is at CHF [X] with [equity]. Rivia is my first choice because of the product and the frontend ownership, so I'd rather find a way to make this work. Is there flexibility on base or equity to get closer?"

Softer version if nothing concrete exists yet:

> "I'm talking to a few other teams in Switzerland at a similar level, so timing matters to me. I'd like to give you a clear answer within the next week or so if we can align on the package."

## 7. Other things to ask about

- Annual leave (push for 25+ days)
- Learning budget and conference budget
- Pension (BVG) contribution above the legal minimum; many startups only pay the minimum
- Start date flexibility and notice period at Benevity
- Salary review cycle, and whether there's a refresh on options after the next round

## 8. Before you sign

Get the level, the base, the option details (count, % of fully diluted, strike, vesting, exercise window, leaver terms) and the on-site arrangement in writing. Verbal promises about "we'll revisit after the Series B" don't count unless they're in the offer letter.
