# Negotiation Script: Staff Backend Engineer, AI Tooling (undisclosed company via Jobgether)

## What we know from the posting

- Title: (Senior or Staff) Backend Engineer, AI tooling. The company isn't named; Jobgether is posting for a partner company based in Switzerland.
- Stated pay: a **starting** base salary of €130,000 / £130,000 / CHF 130,000 depending on location, plus **RSUs**.
- 25 days of annual leave plus local public holidays. Full-time employment only, no contractors.
- Remote is possible from Romania. No relocation support.

What that means:

- The same number in three currencies points to one global band that wasn't built around Swiss salaries. CHF 130k is a fair figure in Bucharest and a decent one in most of the EU. For a Staff engineer in Geneva or Zurich, it's low. The word "starting" matters: it's a floor, not a cap, so there's room to negotiate.
- RSUs (rather than options) usually mean a later-stage private company or a listed one. Find out which before talking equity, because a private-company RSU with no liquidity path is worth much less than it looks.
- The posting says "Senior or Staff". Settle the level first. The band, equity and scope will all depend on it, and Magino's background (17+ years, Senior Staff at Benevity, cofounder through an acquisition) clearly points to Staff.

## Step 0: questions for the Jobgether recruiter before any interview

1. "Can you share the company name, or at least the stage and size? I'd like to know whether the RSUs are in a public or a private company."
2. "Is the role levelled at Senior or at Staff, and is the CHF 130k the floor for both levels?"
3. "Where's the Swiss office, and is remote work from Geneva possible, or is it hybrid in a specific city?"
4. "Is the band adjusted for Switzerland, or is it the same figure in every country?"

Don't give a number in this call. If pushed: "I'd like to understand the level and the company first. I'm comfortable that we'll land somewhere that works if the scope is Staff."

**Target:** CHF 190k to 205k base, plus RSUs.

## Opening anchor script

"Thanks for sharing the CHF 130k starting point. I understand it's the same number across several countries. For a Staff role based in Switzerland, though, it's below where the market is. Staff engineers in Geneva typically land between CHF 180k and 230k, and Zurich runs higher. Given that the role asks for someone who's already rolled out AI developer tooling across teams and owned a platform end to end, which is exactly what I've done at Benevity and Alaya, I'm looking at a base around CHF 215k, plus an RSU grant that fits the Staff level. Is there room in the band for that?"

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low

**Script A: they offer close to CHF 130k to 150k.**
"I appreciate the offer, and I'm keen on the role. That number works for a Senior engineer, but the scope you've described, multi-quarter platform initiatives and setting technical direction, is Staff work. To make this work I'd need the base at CHF 190k or more. If the base band is truly limited, I'm open to talking about a larger initial RSU grant to close part of the gap, but the base has to reflect the Swiss cost of living."

**Script B: they say the band is fixed globally.**
"I understand having one band simplifies things. The issue is that the same CHF number buys very different things in Geneva and in Bucharest; rent alone in Geneva is likely two or three times higher. Is there a location adjustment for Switzerland, or a different level we should be looking at? If not, I'd want to see the gap closed through equity and a signing bonus, and a written commitment to a compensation review at six months."

**Script C: they question the Staff level.**
"Happy to go through it. At Benevity I've been at Senior Staff level for several years, working across teams without direct reports, and I led the AI tooling adoption there. Before that I built Alaya's platform from zero through to the acquisition. If you'd like, we can treat the next interview as a level calibration, and I'll go deep on how I'd run the first two quarters of your platform roadmap."

## Geographic pushback (Geneva is expensive)

- Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe for housing, and Swiss health insurance is paid by the employee, not deducted at source the way social contributions are elsewhere.
- Swiss social charges and the employer's second-pillar (LPP) pension contribution are part of the real cost to the company. Ask what the employer contributes to LPP; some companies go well above the legal minimum, which is worth real money.
- Ask whether there's a 13th month salary. It's common in Switzerland and changes the comparison: 13 × CHF 15k is CHF 195k, not CHF 180k.

## Equity (RSUs)

Questions to ask once the company is named:

1. **Public or private?** If public, value the grant at the current share price and look at volatility. If private, ask for the last 409A or equivalent valuation, the last funding round price, and whether there are tender offers or secondary sales.
2. **Grant size and vesting:** how many units, over how many years (four is standard), with or without a one-year cliff. Ask whether vesting is front-loaded or even.
3. **Refreshers:** "Are there annual refresh grants for Staff engineers, and how are they decided?" Without refreshers, total compensation falls sharply after year four.
4. **Double-trigger RSUs (private companies):** shares vest on time but only settle on a liquidity event. Ask what happens if you leave before that and whether vested units expire.
5. **Acceleration:** ask about double-trigger acceleration on change of control. Magino has lived through an acquisition, so this is a natural question to raise.
6. **Tax:** in Switzerland, RSUs are generally taxed as income when they vest (when they deliver), and later gains are usually tax-free for private investors. Check the details with a Geneva tax advisor, especially for a private company where the vesting value is hard to pin down.

**Equity ask script:**
"On equity, the number of RSUs matters less to me than what they're likely to be worth. Can you share the current valuation or share price, the vesting schedule and how refreshers work? For a Staff hire I'd expect an initial grant worth at least CHF 40k to 60k a year at today's value, plus refreshers."

## Other levers if base is stuck

- Signing bonus (CHF 20k to 40k) to bridge the gap in year one.
- Written six-month compensation review tied to agreed platform metrics.
- Learning budget and conference travel.
- Home office setup and remote stipend if mostly remote from Geneva.
- Extra holidays: 25 days is the Swiss legal minimum plus a bit; asking for 30 is reasonable at Staff level.

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

"I want to be upfront: I'm in late stages with another company for a Staff platform role in Switzerland, with a base in the CHF 190k to 210k range. This role is my first choice because AI developer tooling is exactly where I want to spend the next few years. If you can get closer to that number, I'm ready to decide quickly."

Never invent a competing offer. If there isn't one, use the market data instead: "Based on what I'm seeing in the Swiss market for Staff platform roles..."

## Before signing, check

- Employing entity is Swiss, with a Swiss contract, LPP pension and accident insurance.
- Remote arrangement from Geneva is written into the contract, not just agreed verbally.
- Level (Staff) is stated in the offer letter.
- RSU grant details, vesting schedule and what happens on leaving are in writing.
- Notice period and any non-compete clause (Swiss non-competes must be limited in scope, duration and geography).
