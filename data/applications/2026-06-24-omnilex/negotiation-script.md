# Negotiation Script — Omnilex AI Engineer – Legal Search

## Market Context

**Posted range:** CHF 7,000–11,000/month + ESOP
**Annual at top of range (13-month Swiss convention):** CHF 143,000/year
**Annual without 13th month:** CHF 132,000/year

Omnilex is seed-stage (~14 people, $4.5M raised from Founderful, Plug and Play, TSIC, Angel Invest). Cash is constrained relative to a Series B+, but the equity window is genuinely open: seed-stage entry with Swiss Federal Chancellery and Bayer Pharma already in production is a meaningful signal for where the company could go.

**Zurich market for senior AI engineers with search/retrieval expertise (2026):**
- Strong IC with LLM production experience and IR background: CHF 120,000–160,000/year total cash
- At a seed startup with real equity: lower cash is expected, but CHF 11,000/month (top of their range) is the floor, not the ceiling, given 17 years of experience and a founding engineer exit

**Your target:** CHF 11,000/month cash + ESOP negotiated separately.

---

## Opening Anchor

If they ask for your number before making an offer:

> "Based on my background and the Zurich market for engineers with this profile, I'm looking at CHF 11,000/month as the starting point for cash, with equity as a separate conversation. I'm not maximizing on base here, the ESOP matters to me at a company at this stage, but I want to make sure we're aligned on the cash floor before getting into structure. What does the equity look like?"

This anchors at the top of their posted range, signals that you understand startup comp logic, and pivots immediately to equity so the cash number doesn't become the only sticking point.

---

## If They Open Below CHF 9,500/month

Don't accept the first number. Use this:

> "I appreciate the offer. The role is genuinely interesting, which is part of why I'm being direct: CHF [X] is below what I'd need to step away from my current position. I could get to yes at 10,000 if there's room, and if cash flexibility is limited, I'd want to offset that with a higher ESOP allocation. What's the range on the equity side?"

This avoids a flat rejection, introduces equity as a real lever, and keeps the conversation moving without conceding the anchor.

---

## Commute Consideration (Geneva to Zurich)

Two on-site days per week from Geneva is a real operational cost. Factor it in, don't absorb it silently.

If they push back on the cash ask:

> "Part of what I'm reflecting in the number is the commute. Two days a week from Geneva, accounting for travel time and actual costs, adds up over a year. That's not a dealbreaker, it's just context for why the base matters more here than it might for someone already in Zurich. If we can hit 11,000, the logistics become much easier."

If they offer partial remote flexibility (3 remote, 2 on-site):

> "That helps a lot. If the on-site requirement is genuinely two days and there's flexibility on which days, I can make that work. That changes the commute math and makes me more open to discussing the full package."

---

## Equity Negotiation

Omnilex is at seed stage with ~14 people and ~$4.5M raised. Target ESOP for an early senior hire:

**Target grant:** 0.3–0.5% fully diluted, 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff.

**Questions to ask before signing:**
- What percentage of the company does this grant represent on a fully diluted basis?
- What was the share price at the last round (the $4.5M seed)?
- Is there a standard 4-year vest / 1-year cliff, or does Omnilex use a different structure?
- What happens to unvested options on acquisition? Is there a single-trigger or double-trigger acceleration?
- What's the Swiss tax treatment? Are options taxed at grant or at exercise?

**Script for the equity conversation:**

> "Before we finalize, I want to understand the equity in enough detail to value it properly. What percentage of the fully diluted cap table does this represent? And what was the per-share price at the last round? I want to be able to put a floor on the value, not just treat it as speculative."

If they say "standard grant" without specifics:

> "I understand every company structures it differently. Even a rough sense of where the grant sits relative to the total pool would help me make the right decision. I'm not trying to negotiate against you; I'm trying to make sure I understand what I'm agreeing to."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you're running other processes and want to create a decision timeline:

> "I'm in late stages with a couple of other companies and I'd like to close one of them by end of [month]. The Omnilex role is the one I'm most interested in from a problem standpoint, but I want to make sure the comp structure works before I step back from the others. Can we get to a final number this week?"

---

## Closing Move

If the offer is close but not there:

> "I'm very close to yes. The one thing I'd need is [specific ask: CHF 500/month more, or an additional 0.1% on the ESOP, or travel reimbursement for Zurich days]. If that's doable, I'd sign today."

Pick one lever. Negotiating two things at once at the close signals that you're not actually ready to close.

---

## Walk-Away Thresholds

Given the Zurich commute and Magino's profile (17+ years, founding engineer, $1B+ acquisition exit):

- **Strong yes:** CHF 11,000/month + 0.4%+ ESOP
- **Acceptable:** CHF 9,500/month + 0.5%+ ESOP with clear acceleration clause
- **Worth a hard conversation:** CHF 9,000/month + strong equity + travel reimbursement confirmed in writing
- **Walk away:** Below CHF 8,500/month with no meaningful equity concession. The commute cost and opportunity cost relative to other Geneva/remote options make it not viable.
