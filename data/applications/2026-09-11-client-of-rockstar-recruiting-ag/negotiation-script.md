# Negotiation Script: Applied AI Founding Engineer (Agentic Workflows)

Client of Rockstar Recruiting AG, Zurich. Posted range: CHF 140,000-150,000 base.

## Market read

That posted range is low for what the role actually asks for. The requirements list (agent orchestration frameworks, model-serving infrastructure, evaluation pipelines, ML observability) plus "founding engineer" scope reads like a Staff-level hire, not a mid-level one. In Zurich, a Staff or Senior engineer with AI/ML platform ownership typically lands CHF 150,000-190,000 base, and founding-title roles at funded startups often push toward 170,000-200,000 once equity is priced in. Geneva-based candidates commuting or relocating for Zurich roles shouldn't accept a discount just because Rockstar's posting undersells the seniority. Since this is a recruiting-agency listing, the posted band may also just be a placeholder the client hasn't updated. Worth confirming directly rather than anchoring off it.

## Opening anchor

Once there's a real offer conversation, not before: "Based on the founding-engineer scope and the platform ownership described, and what similar agentic AI roles are paying in Zurich right now, I'm looking at a base in the 175,000-190,000 range, plus meaningful equity given the founding title. Where's there room to work with that?"

Justify with specifics, not just a number: 17+ years including one exit (Alaya to Benevity), hands-on experience with the exact category of system this role is building, and prior founding-engineer ownership, not just IC experience under someone else's architecture.

## If they come in low

Script: "I appreciate the offer, and I'm genuinely interested in the role. The base is below where I'd expect for a founding engineer position with this scope, especially given the model-serving and evaluation infrastructure ownership described. Can we look at closing that gap, either on base or through equity, before I give you a final answer?"

If they cite the posted range: "I understand that's what was posted, but a founding-engineer title with this level of infrastructure responsibility usually sits above a standard range like that. I'd rather solve for the right level now than renegotiate in six months."

## Equity, if this is an early-stage client

Ask directly: stage of the company, total round raised, current headcount, and where this hire sits in the cap table conversation. For an early founding engineer seat, target 0.5%-1.5% depending on stage, with standard 4-year vesting and a 1-year cliff. Push back on anything without a cliff waiver for a founding-level hire, since that's usually a sign the paperwork was templated for a much more junior role. Ask whether there's a double-trigger acceleration clause; if not, raise it, it protects both sides if there's an acquisition, which is exactly the kind of outcome I have direct experience with.

## Geographic discount pushback

If they try to anchor lower because I'm not currently Zurich-based: Geneva to Zurich is a normal commute pattern for senior roles here, and remote-friendly arrangements are already standard for this kind of AI engineering work. Geneva's cost of living isn't meaningfully lower than Zurich's, so there's no real basis for a location discount, and I'd say so plainly if it comes up.

## Using a competing offer

Even without a live competing offer, it's fair to say: "I'm in later-stage conversations with a couple of other platform and AI engineering roles at similar seniority, so I'd like to move relatively quickly on this if the comp lines up." Use this to create urgency without fabricating specifics, no fake company names or numbers.

## Walk-away line

If the final number stays meaningfully below 160,000 base with no equity upside to compensate: "I don't think this gets me to where I need to be given the scope of the role, but I'd like to stay in touch. If the comp structure changes as the role or company matures, I'm still interested."
