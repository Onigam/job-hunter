# Negotiation Script — Axe, Founding Engineer

## Market Context

Axe is an early-stage UK-based AI startup (7 months old, EU VC-backed). The role is remote, though the listings suggest a UK/Ireland timezone preference. Magino is based in Geneva, Switzerland.

Key reference points:
- UK founding engineer at seed/pre-Series A: £80k-£130k base depending on stage and traction
- Axe has ARR doubling month-on-month — this is past the "prove it" phase, which changes the risk calculus
- Swiss Senior Staff / founding engineer market: CHF 150k-200k base (Benevity range, Geneva market)
- At this stage Axe likely has limited cash runway per hire — equity is the real upside

**Target package:**
- Base: £110k-£130k (or CHF equivalent ~140k-165k), anchoring at £120k
- Equity: 1.0-2.0% on a 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff, targeting 1.5%
- If base is constrained: accept lower cash (£90k-£100k) only if equity goes to 2.0%+

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask about expectations:

> "I'm targeting £120k base. Given that I'm coming in as a founding engineer, not just an engineer, and given the traction you're showing, I'd want that to be paired with a meaningful equity position, something in the 1 to 1.5 percent range. That said, I'm flexible on how the package splits between cash and equity depending on what stage you're at."

If they push back immediately on base:

> "I'm based in Geneva, which is a relatively expensive market. I'm not anchoring to Swiss rates specifically, but £90k doesn't reflect the scope of what a founding engineer actually does here. What does your current range look like?"

---

## If They Come In Low (£70k-£90k base)

Do not accept or reject immediately. Instead:

> "That's a bit below where I was expecting to land. I want to make this work, so I'm curious: how are you thinking about the equity side? If the cash is constrained right now because you're managing runway carefully, I can work with that, but the equity would need to reflect the founding role more squarely. Are you able to go to 1.5 to 2 percent?"

If they resist both:

> "I understand the constraints at this stage. Can I ask — what's your rough timeline to the next raise? That would help me think about the risk-adjusted picture."

If the equity offer is already 0.25-0.5% (typical for employees, not founders):

> "For a founding engineer, I'd expect to be on the co-founder compensation schedule rather than the employee one. What did your first hire receive? I'm not asking to compare, I just want to understand how you think about founding-team equity."

---

## Equity Notes (Startup-Specific)

**What to ask for in writing before signing:**
- Vesting schedule: 4 years, 1-year cliff (standard)
- Option type: EMI options (UK) are tax-advantaged, worth confirming
- Exercise window: push for at least 5 years post-departure (not the standard 90-day cliff, which is punishing for employees who leave before an exit)
- Anti-dilution: ask what happens to your percentage in the next round. Pro-rata rights are unlikely at this stage, but good to understand the dilution model
- Cliff trigger: does it reset if the company is acquired before your cliff date? This matters a lot given Axe's trajectory

**If they offer options at last valuation:**
Ask: "What was the strike price and when was the last 409A or equivalent valuation?" A high strike price on a quickly-rising company reduces the actual equity value.

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

Hiring managers sometimes apply a "remote discount" or a "lower cost of living" discount for non-London employees. Switzerland is more expensive than London on most measures.

If this comes up:

> "I'd push back on a geography discount in either direction. Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, so if anything the adjustment runs the other way. More importantly, for a founding engineer role the value is the same regardless of where I sit. I'm not sure location-adjusted salaries make sense when the role is fully remote and the output is what matters."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have other offers in process (or want to create urgency):

> "I have a few other conversations that are moving toward offer stage. I'm more interested in Axe than in any of them because the problem and the pace are right, but I also want to be honest with you about timing. If you're able to move relatively quickly on an offer I can give you a clear answer."

If they ask what the other offer is:

> "I'd rather not share the specifics — that feels awkward for everyone. What I can tell you is that the range is consistent with what I've shared with you. If you can get to £120k and a fair equity stake, I'm not going elsewhere."

---

## Equity vs. Cash Trade-off Calculator (rough)

| Base | Equity | Implied value at 10x exit (£50M company) | Notes |
|------|--------|------------------------------------------|-------|
| £120k | 1.0% | £500k pre-dilution | Conservative |
| £100k | 1.5% | £750k pre-dilution | Reasonable founding-eng range |
| £90k  | 2.0% | £1M pre-dilution | Only worth it if conviction is high |

Note: pre-dilution. Assume 2-3 more rounds of dilution before exit. A 2% stake post-Series A may land at 1.0-1.2% at exit.

---

## Final Decision Framework

Accept if:
- Base is £100k+ (or CHF 130k equivalent), AND
- Equity is 1.0%+ with a standard 4-year vest / 1-year cliff, AND
- 5-year exercise window (or negotiate it in)

Walk away if:
- Base below £85k with no real flexibility on equity
- Equity under 0.75% for what is clearly a founding-team role
- No movement on either axis after two counter-offer rounds
