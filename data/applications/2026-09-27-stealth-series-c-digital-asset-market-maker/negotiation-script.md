# Negotiation Script: Stealth (Series C digital-asset market maker), Deputy CTO, Geneva

## Context before talking numbers

This is an executive role (ExCo-facing, deputising for the CTO with the Board) at a Series C market maker with 200+ people. The posting promises a "top-market base", "significant stock option participation" and a "highly lucrative, discretionary annual bonus". In trading firms the bonus is often the biggest line in the package, and "discretionary" means it can be close to zero in a bad year. That shapes the whole negotiation.

1. **Base is the only guaranteed number.** Push hardest there, then on a guaranteed or floored first-year bonus.
2. **Options in a private crypto firm are high-variance.** Value them conservatively, and focus on the terms (strike, vesting, exercise window, acceleration) as much as the count.
3. **Know my position.** I'm a stretch candidate on trading and regulatory experience. If they make an offer, they've decided the operating and integration profile is worth it, so I shouldn't discount myself for the gaps a second time in the numbers. I also shouldn't anchor so high it signals I misread the level.
4. **The perks** (tech budget, offsites, flexible work) are nice. Don't trade base for them.

Questions for the Talent Partner in the first call:
- "What's the base range for this role, and how is the bonus determined: is there a target percentage, and what did it pay out in the last two years?"
- "What does the option package look like: percentage of fully diluted shares or number of options, strike price, last 409A or equivalent valuation, and vesting schedule?"
- "Is there a guaranteed or minimum bonus in year one?"
- "Is any part of compensation paid in tokens or digital assets?"

## Market reference (estimates, verify before the call)

These are rough figures from general market knowledge for 2025-2026. Verify with levels.fyi, recruiter conversations (executive search firms covering Geneva trading and fintech), and ideally someone who works at a Geneva trading house or crypto firm.

| Reference | Base salary (approx.) | Variable |
|---|---|---|
| Head of Engineering / VP Eng, Swiss fintech scale-up | CHF 220k-280k | 10-25% |
| Deputy CTO / CTO, Series B-C startup, Geneva/Zug | CHF 250k-320k | 15-40% |

| Crypto market makers, senior tech leadership | CHF 250k-330k | highly variable, sometimes tokens |

Equity at Series C for a C-suite-adjacent hire is typically somewhere around 0.1%-0.5% fully diluted, depending on size and valuation. Treat that as a starting assumption only.

**My numbers**

- **Opening anchor:** CHF 300k base, bonus target of at least 40%, meaningful options.
- **Target:** CHF 270k-290k base, a guaranteed first-year bonus of at least CHF 60k, and options around 0.25% fully diluted or equivalent.
- **Walk-away:** below about CHF 240k base with no bonus floor. At that level the risk profile (discretionary bonus, crypto market cycles, illiquid options) isn't worth it compared with a Staff/Principal IC role in Geneva.

## Opening anchor script

Try to get their range first:

> "Before I give a number, can you share the range you've budgeted for the role? It'd help me understand how you're weighting base against bonus and equity."

If they push:

> "For a deputy CTO role at a Series C firm in Geneva, with Board exposure and ownership of the global engineering budget, I'm looking at around CHF 300k base. I know bonus is a big part of how trading firms pay, so I'm open on structure, but I'd want the base to reflect the fact that the bonus is discretionary."

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low

If the base comes in around CHF 220k-250k:

> "Thanks, I'm keen on the role and on working alongside the CTO. The base is below where I expected for this scope, though. You're asking for someone to run the global engineering cadence, lead a multi-year platform consolidation and represent engineering at ExCo and Board level. Can we get the base closer to CHF 280k?"

If base is capped:

> "If the base band is fixed, could we make part of the first-year bonus guaranteed, say CHF 60k-80k, and add a sign-on to cover what I'm leaving behind? That would give me some certainty while I'm still proving the value."

Other levers if base won't move:
- sign-on bonus (one-off, easier to approve)
- guaranteed first-year bonus or a written bonus target percentage
- a formal comp review at 12 months tied to the success metrics in the brief (unified cadence adopted, consolidation MVP live, data catalog with SLAs)
- a larger option grant, or an additional refresh grant after year one
- executive tech budget and training for the regulatory learning curve (DORA, MiCA courses)

## Equity negotiation (startup options)

Ask for, and get in writing:
- **Number of options and percentage of fully diluted capital**, plus the latest valuation and strike. A number of options without the denominator means nothing.
- **Vesting:** standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Ask for monthly or quarterly vesting after the cliff.
- **Acceleration:** double-trigger acceleration on change of control (acquisition plus termination or role reduction). For an M&A-heavy firm, this matters a lot.
- **Exercise window after leaving:** push for 5-10 years instead of 90 days. With Swiss tax on exercise, a short window can force a bad choice.
- **Good leaver / bad leaver terms:** what happens to vested options if I'm let go without cause.
- **Anti-dilution and future rounds:** as an employee I won't get investor-style anti-dilution, but ask about refresh grants after future rounds so the stake isn't quietly diluted away.
- **Liquidity:** any secondary sales or buyback programme? Market makers can be profitable, so a buyback is plausible.
- **Tokens:** if any comp is paid in digital assets, get the valuation method, lock-up and tax treatment clear.
- **Swiss tax:** taxation of options depends on the plan (at grant or at exercise, blocked periods, cantonal practice in Geneva). Get the plan documents and have a tax advisor look before signing.

Value the options at a steep discount (think 20-30% of paper value, at most) when comparing against a cash-heavy offer.

## Geographic discount pushback

If they suggest a lower number because of remote work or because other hubs pay less:

> "The role is based in Geneva and so am I, and Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe for housing, health insurance and childcare. Geneva trading and commodity firms pay for that. I'd expect the package to reflect Geneva rates, not a blended figure from another hub."

If they benchmark against London or Paris salaries:

> "Swiss cost of living and social charges differ enough that London or Paris numbers don't translate directly. Swiss trading and fintech benchmarks are the right comparison."

## Competing offer template

Use only if true, never invent a specific offer:

> "To be transparent, I'm also in late-stage conversations for a senior platform leadership role elsewhere, in the range of CHF [X] base plus [bonus/equity]. You're my first choice because of the scope here, and I'd like to make it work. Is there room to close the gap on base or on the guaranteed part of the bonus?"

If there's no concrete competing offer:

> "I'm talking to a few other companies about Staff and engineering leadership roles in Geneva. I'd rather decide on the merits than on a bidding war, which is why I'd like the package to be solid from the start."

## Final checks before signing

- Base, bonus target, any guaranteed portion and payout timing all in writing
- Option grant details and the plan document itself
- Notice period and non-compete terms (trading firms often have long ones; check scope and whether they're paid)
- Title, reporting line to the CTO, and Board/ExCo participation confirmed in the contract
- Pension (LPP/BVG) contribution level and whether it's above the legal minimum
