# Negotiation Script — Banque Pictet, Generative AI Engineer

---

## Market Context

Pictet is one of the largest private banks in the world, headquartered in Geneva, family-owned and not publicly listed. That structure matters for compensation: no stock options, no RSUs. The main levers are base salary, annual performance bonus, and pension contributions.

Geneva market rates for a senior GenAI Engineer (staff-level experience, 15+ years) in 2026:

- Midmarket senior AI Engineer: CHF 140,000-160,000 base
- Staff/lead AI Engineer at a major financial institution: CHF 160,000-195,000 base
- Annual bonus at private banks: typically 10-25% of base, performance-dependent
- LPP/BVG pension: banks often contribute more than the legal minimum

Private banking in Geneva consistently pays above the tech startup market for engineering talent because they compete with Bloomberg, SIX Group, and consultancies like McKinsey Digital.

---

## Opening Anchor

**Target ask: CHF 190,000-200,000 base**

Justification to give: "I've been benchmarking Geneva market rates for AI engineering roles at the staff level, and the range I've been seeing is CHF 170,000-200,000 for someone with this depth of LLM and platform experience. I'd need to be in that upper band to make the move, around 190 to 200k base."

This anchors high but is defensible. You're not being unreasonable; staff-level AI talent in Geneva commands this.

---

## If They Come In Low (CHF 140,000-155,000)

"I appreciate the offer and the time you've taken. I have to be transparent: this is below what I've been seeing for comparable roles in Geneva, and below what I need to make this work practically given the cost of living here. I've had conversations ranging from CHF 165,000 to 190,000 for roles at this level. Can we revisit the base? I'd need to be closer to CHF 175,000 to move forward seriously."

Key principle: don't reject outright. Give them a specific number to react to.

---

## If They Push Back on the Number

"I understand there are internal bands. Two things I'd ask you to factor in: one, my profile is genuinely unusual, 17 years of production systems experience plus hands-on GenAI work in regulated contexts isn't the common case. Two, Geneva cost of living has to be the baseline here, not a London or Zurich benchmark adjusted down. If CHF 175,000 base is a hard ceiling, let's talk about what the bonus structure looks like and whether we can close the gap there."

---

## Bonus Conversation

Private banks typically have more flexibility on bonus than on base (base is budget-controlled; bonus is discretionary). Push for clarity on:

- Target bonus percentage (ask for it in writing, not just "up to")
- Is it based on individual performance, team performance, or bank P&L?
- What did last year's average payout look like for engineers at this level?

Script: "Can you walk me through how bonuses work in practice? I want to understand the structure, not just the range, because for me the total comp picture is what matters."

---

## Pension and Benefits

LPP/BVG is mandatory in Switzerland, but Pictet likely contributes well above the minimum. Ask:

- What's the employer contribution rate to the pension fund?
- Is there a top-up plan (caisse de pensions complémentaire)?
- Are there other benefits specific to the role (professional development budget, conference attendance)?

This matters. A generous pension plan can add CHF 15,000-25,000/year in effective compensation that won't show up in the base number.

---

## No-Equity Pushback

If you've been in conversations with startups that offer equity, frame it this way if asked: "I've been talking to some earlier-stage companies where equity is part of the picture. Working at an institution like Pictet is a different value proposition: stability, scale, and the chance to work on AI problems that actually matter to people's financial lives. I understand the trade-off. That's part of why I'm here."

This positions you as thoughtful, not as someone who took a second-choice offer.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage (if you have one)

If you have another offer (real or in late stages), use it once, not repeatedly: "I want to be transparent: I do have another process that's moving toward an offer. I'd prefer to be at Pictet if the numbers work, but I'm going to need to give them an answer in the next ten days. Is there a way to accelerate the decision on your end?"

Don't name the company unless directly asked and you're comfortable doing so.

---

## Floor

**CHF 155,000 base + meaningful bonus structure (target 15%+)**

Below this, the total comp math doesn't work relative to your current trajectory. Be willing to walk; that's what makes a floor credible.
