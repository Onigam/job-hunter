# Negotiation Script: Artificial Intelligence Engineer, AI Platform Team (via Sigma Suisse)

## Context that shapes the numbers

- **The employer is hidden behind a recruiter.** Sigma Suisse (Plan-les-Ouates) is recruiting for "a leading international group" in Geneva. The LinkedIn listing tags the role under Banking, Investment Banking and Oil and Gas, which in Geneva usually means a bank, a private bank or a commodity trading house. Those sectors pay well and lean heavily on bonus. Find out which one before talking numbers, since the bonus culture changes the whole package.
- **Check the contract type first.** Ask whether it's a permanent hire on the client's payroll or a placement through Sigma (staffing or contractor). The numbers below assume a permanent CDI with the client. If it's a contract through Sigma, use the day-rate section.
- **Level is "Mid-Senior".** The listing is pitched below Staff. You bring 17+ years, a platform built from scratch through to an acquisition, and Senior Staff at Benevity. The main negotiation is about level as much as salary: push to be slotted at the senior or lead grade, because the salary band follows the grade.
- **No salary posted.** The recruiter knows the client's band and earns a fee that's usually a percentage of first-year salary, so a higher number is in their interest too. Use them for information.
- **Equity is unlikely.** Large groups rarely grant equity to engineers at this level. The variable part will be a cash bonus, possibly a deferred one. Most of the package talk will be about base, bonus target, and benefits.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

These are approximate ranges from public comp data and general Geneva market knowledge. Re-check on levels.fyi, Glassdoor, swissdevjobs.ch and the Michael Page / Robert Walters Swiss salary guides, and ask Sigma for the band directly.

| Benchmark (Geneva, permanent) | Base (approx.) | Variable |
|---|---|---|
| AI/ML engineer, mid-senior, large corporate | CHF 130k to 165k | 10 to 15% |
| Senior AI/ML or platform engineer, bank or private bank | CHF 160k to 200k | 15 to 30% |
| Senior/lead engineer, commodity trading house | CHF 170k to 220k | 20 to 50%+, discretionary |
| Staff/principal platform engineer, scale-up | CHF 170k to 220k | equity |

Production GenAI engineers (people who've shipped, not just prototyped) are scarce in Geneva right now, and that's the argument for the top of the band.

**Target:** CHF 185k base plus a 15 to 20% bonus target.
**Opening anchor:** CHF 200k base.
**Walk-away floor:** CHF 160k base with a real bonus. Below that, the title and level are too far from Senior Staff for it to be worth it.

Swiss offers are sometimes quoted as 13 x monthly salary. Always confirm whether a figure is annual all-in or monthly times 12 or 13.

## Step 1: with the recruiter (before the client)

"Before we go further, a few practical things. Is this a permanent role on the client's payroll, or through Sigma? Which sector is the client in? And what's the budgeted range? I'd like us to be working from the same numbers from the start."

If they ask for your expectation first:

"For a hands-on AI platform role in Geneva, given 17 years of engineering, Senior Staff at Benevity and a platform I built through to an acquisition, I'm looking at around CHF 200k base plus the group's standard bonus. If the band is in that area, I'm very interested. If it's well below, I'd want to know now so we don't waste your client's time."

## Step 2: on level (do this before salary)

"The listing says mid-senior. I've been working at Senior Staff level, setting cross-team architecture and getting standards adopted across teams I didn't manage, which is most of what this role describes: reference architectures, MCP and A2A conventions, shared libraries. I'd like to be considered at the senior or lead grade. Is there room for that in the hiring?"

## Step 3: if the offer comes in low

"Thanks for being straight with me. CHF [their number] sits closer to a mid-level band than to the scope in the brief, which is setting platform standards for the group's GenAI work, not just building features. Could we get to CHF 185k base? If the base is fixed by the grade, I'd look at the bonus target or a sign-on to close the gap."

If base truly can't move:

"Understood. Then let's look at the rest: a bonus target of 20%, a sign-on payment for the first year, and a review at six months against agreed objectives. With those in writing I can make it work."

## Step 4: pushing back on discounts

There's no geographic discount here, since the role and you are both in Geneva. The discounts you'll hear are different.

If they cite internal parity ("that's what our engineers at this grade earn"):

"I get that grades matter in a group this size. That's why I raised level first. If I'm slotted correctly, parity works in my favour."

If they point to your gap in LangGraph or formal evaluation tooling:

"Fair, and I said so upfront. What you're paying for is someone who's shipped and run production platforms that other teams depend on. Frameworks are a few weeks; that's years."

If they suggest a lower start "while you prove yourself":

"I'm fine with being measured, so let's put it in writing: a review at six months with a defined salary step if agreed objectives are met."

## Step 5: equity and long-term pay

Equity is unlikely, but ask anyway, because some groups have long-term incentive plans or deferred bonuses for senior hires.

- "Is there a long-term incentive or deferred bonus plan at this grade?"
- If the bonus is partly deferred: how much, over how long, and what happens to unvested parts if you leave or the group restructures.
- If the bonus is discretionary (common in trading), ask for the last two or three years' typical payout at this grade, not just the target.
- If it turns out to be a startup-like subsidiary with equity: standard four years with a one-year cliff, ask about acceleration on change of control, the latest valuation and the fully diluted share count. Don't count equity in the comparison against the floor.

## Step 6: benefits that carry real money in Switzerland

These are often easier to move than base.

- **Pension (LPP/BVG):** employer contribution share. Many large groups pay well above the 50% minimum; that can be worth CHF 10k+ a year.
- **Health insurance:** some groups pay part of the LAMal premium.
- **Holidays:** 25 days is common in large groups; ask for it if they offer 20.
- **Remote:** one or two days a week from home.
- **Training budget:** conferences and certifications on GenAI, which also covers the framework gap.
- **Start date:** if you're between roles, a start bonus can help with the timing.

## If it's a contract through Sigma

Approximate Geneva rates for experienced AI or platform contractors are around CHF 1,000 to 1,300 a day. Anchor at CHF 1,250, floor around CHF 1,000. As an employee of Sigma on a fixed term (not self-employed), convert back to an annual figure and compare with the permanent numbers, remembering that contractor pay has no bonus, and check how pension and holidays are handled.

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

"I want to be transparent with you. I'm in late-stage conversations for a Staff platform role at around CHF [X] base plus equity. This role interests me more because of [the hands-on scope / being in Geneva / the platform mandate], and I'd rather accept here. If you can get to CHF [target], I'm ready to sign this week."

If there's no real competing offer, don't make one up. Use market data instead:

"I'm actively interviewing for Staff-level platform roles, and the ranges I'm seeing in Geneva are CHF 180k to 220k base. I'd like this offer to be competitive with that."

## Checklist before signing

- Annual base, and whether it's paid in 12 or 13 instalments
- Bonus target, how it's decided, typical payout, deferral
- Grade or level in writing
- Employer pension share and health contribution
- Holidays, remote policy, notice period, non-compete clause (common in trading)
- Contract type: permanent with the client, not via Sigma, unless that's what you chose
