# Negotiation Script — Wave Group, AI Engineer (TypeScript)

---

## Market Context

**Posted range:** EUR 175,000–200,000 base + ~EUR 120,000 equity
**Your location:** Geneva, Switzerland (one of the most expensive cities globally)
**Role level:** Mid-Senior to Senior (their framing), but your background is Staff+

**Swiss market benchmarks (2025–2026, Geneva):**
- Senior TypeScript/Node.js Engineer: CHF 160,000–200,000
- Staff/Principal Engineer with AI focus: CHF 200,000–260,000
- Founding engineer background (acquisition at unicorn valuation): premium of CHF 20,000–40,000 typical

**EUR/CHF rate:** approximately 0.94–0.96 (CHF stronger than EUR). Their top of range (EUR 200,000) converts to roughly CHF 190,000–192,000. That lands at the lower end of Geneva-market Staff+ rates.

**Assessment:** The total comp (base + equity annualized at CHF ~30,000/yr) is competitive for a mid-senior hire but undervalued for your level. Your leverage is genuine: 17 years, founding-engineer track record, AI systems experience, and immediate availability.

---

## Opening Anchor

Use this when they ask for your expectations or if they ask you to confirm the range:

> "I've looked at the range you posted and the role scope. Given my background at Staff+ level and the Geneva cost of living, I'm looking at EUR 210,000–215,000 as a base. On the equity side I'd want to understand the structure before putting a number on it. Happy to talk through both."

Why EUR 210–215k: it's above their ceiling but not absurdly so. It signals you know your value, leaves room to land at 200–205k as a "compromise," and opens the equity conversation separately.

---

## Counter-Offer Script (If They Hold at EUR 175–185k)

If they come in at the bottom of their range:

> "I appreciate the offer. To be honest, EUR 175k puts me below what I'd need to make the move work financially given Geneva rates. I've done some benchmarking and a Staff+ engineer with a production AI and platform background is tracking closer to EUR 200k in this market. Is there room to get there on base, or would it make sense to look at the equity structure to bridge the gap?"

Then pause. Let them respond. Don't fill the silence.

If they push back that it's remote and Geneva rates shouldn't apply:

> "I hear that. Remote changes some things but not the cost of living I'm managing day to day. My tax exposure, social contributions, and baseline expenses are all Geneva-priced. That's typically something I'd expect an employer to account for at this level."

---

## If They Offer EUR 200k (Top of Range)

Accept the base directionally, pivot to equity:

> "EUR 200k on base works as a starting point. The equity is what I want to understand better before I can say yes to the full package. Can you walk me through the current valuation, the vesting schedule, and what the strike price looks like?"

---

## Equity Due Diligence — Questions to Ask

Wave Group is Series B (EUR 45m+ raised). The EUR 120k equity figure needs unpacking.

**Essential questions:**
1. Is this options or RSUs?
2. What's the current valuation used to price the grant?
3. What's the vesting schedule, and is there a one-year cliff?
4. What's the strike price (for options)?
5. Is this a percentage of the company, or a fixed euro-denominated number?
6. What was the last round's price per share, and what's the current 409A equivalent?
7. Are there anti-dilution protections (full ratchet or weighted average)?
8. Is there an acceleration clause on acquisition (single or double trigger)?

**Red flags to watch for:**
- EUR 120k quoted at current valuation with a high strike price leaves little real upside
- No acceleration on acquisition is a problem given Wave Group's trajectory
- Cliff longer than 12 months is non-standard

**Framing if they're vague on equity details:**

> "I've been on the founding side of an acquisition, so I've been through this from both ends. I'm not trying to negotiate every clause, I just want to understand what the grant is actually worth in practical terms before I sign. Can we get a call with someone who knows the cap table?"

---

## Signing Bonus (If Base Ceiling Is Firm)

If they genuinely can't move the base:

> "If EUR 200k is the ceiling on base, I'd want to look at a one-time signing bonus to close the gap. Something in the EUR 15,000–20,000 range would get us to the right place on year-one total comp."

Signing bonuses are common at Series B companies and come out of a different budget than comp bands.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage Template

If you have another offer in progress (or want to create urgency):

> "I want to be straightforward with you: I've got another process moving in parallel that's further along than I expected. I'd rather close with Wave Group if the terms work, but I need to make a decision in the next [10–14 days]. Is there any flexibility to move the offer forward or improve the package before that point?"

Use only if true or if you can make it credibly true by fast-tracking another application.

---

## Timeline and Close

If you want the role:

> "I think the fit here is real and I'd like to make this work. If we can land at [EUR 205–210k base + equity structure clarified], I'm ready to move forward."

If you need time:

> "I'd like 48–72 hours to review everything properly. I'll come back to you by [specific date]."

Never say "I'll think about it" without a concrete return date. It stalls the process and signals uncertainty.
