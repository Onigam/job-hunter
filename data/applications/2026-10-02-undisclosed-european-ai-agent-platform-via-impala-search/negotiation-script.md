# Negotiation Script: Staff Product Engineer (Forward Deployed), via Impala Search

## Context that shapes the numbers

The posting doesn't name the company. It does say: Berlin, hybrid, full time, "one of Europe's best-funded AI companies", a platform that lets business users build and manage AI agents, and "millions of customer conversations a year" handled by those agents for large European enterprises. The team is meant to grow significantly over the next year. The band is published: **€150k to €180k base, plus an annual bonus and equity.**

**Likely company: Parloa** (Berlin, AI agents for customer service, one of the larger European AI rounds). That's an educated guess based on the description, not a confirmed fact. Check it before the first call and don't name it to the recruiter until they do.

What that means for the deal:

- **The band is public, so the anchor goes at or just above the top of it.** Asking for €210k on a €150k to €180k posting reads as not having read the posting. The room to move is above the top end only if the level gets reframed (Principal or a lead role), plus the bonus, the equity and the location terms.
- **Late-stage, well-funded company.** Cash is the main lever, and equity will probably be options or a VSOP at a high valuation. That's useful, but it has far less upside than early-stage equity. Ask how it's priced.
- **Location matters most after base.** It's listed as Berlin hybrid. You're in Geneva. Decide what cadence you'd accept (for example one week in three or four in Berlin) and whether they'd employ you in Switzerland, through an EOR, or only under a German contract. Social charges, tax and who pays travel all hang on that answer.
- **Your strengths for this level:** 17+ years, TypeScript and Python in production, five years at Senior Staff, enterprise customer delivery from Alaya, and hands-on LLM integration. Their requirements (8+ years, 5+ years leading architecture, enterprise integrations) are a close fit, so there isn't an obvious gap for them to use against you.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

These are rough figures from public surveys and recruiter data. Re-check on levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Kununu, Index Ventures' OptionPlan, and the Michael Page / Hays guides for Germany and Switzerland.

**Berlin / Germany (what the company benchmarks against)**

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Senior engineer, Berlin scale-up | €85k to 110k |
| Staff engineer, Berlin well-funded scale-up | €120k to 160k |
| Staff / Principal, top-funded AI company in Berlin | €150k to 190k |
| Forward deployed / solutions architect, senior, Germany | €110k to 150k base + variable |
| Annual bonus, Staff IC | often 10 to 15% of base |

**Switzerland (your reference point)**

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Staff engineer, Geneva | CHF 180k to 230k |
| Staff / Principal engineer, Zurich (big tech or top scale-up) | CHF 220k to 280k + equity |
| Forward deployed / solutions engineer, senior, Zurich | CHF 170k to 220k + variable |

EUR and CHF are close to parity right now, so €180k is roughly CHF 170k. That sits at the low end of a Geneva Staff package, which is the argument for pushing to the top of their band and for covering travel separately.

**Your targets**

- Opening anchor: **€180k base** (top of the published band), **15% target bonus**, a meaningful equity grant, and travel to Berlin paid by the company.
- Realistic landing zone: **€170k to 180k base + ~10 to 15% bonus + equity**, with travel covered.
- Stretch: **€190k to 200k** if they'll level you as Principal or give you a lead role over the Product Engineering team.
- Walk-away floor (set your own before the call): something like **€165k base** with travel covered, or higher if they won't pay for travel.

## Opening anchor script

Use this when they ask about expectations:

> "The posting lists €150k to €180k. Given that I've got 17 years in, five of them at Senior Staff, and that I've owned enterprise delivery as a cofounder, I'd expect to be at the top of that band, so €180k base, with the bonus and equity on top. I'm based in Geneva, so I'd also want the Berlin travel handled by the company rather than out of my salary."

If they ask before sharing anything else:

> "Happy to go with the published range as a starting point. Can you tell me how the bonus and the equity are structured? That'll decide where in the band works for me."

## If they come in low

Typical low offer: €155k to 165k base, an unspecified bonus, a small equity grant.

> "Thanks, I appreciate it. I want to make this work, but €160k is mid-band, and the role as you've described it, owning the most complex deployments and mentoring the engineers who join, is the top-of-band profile. Can we get the base to €180k? If base is fixed, I'd want that made up in equity or a guaranteed first-year bonus."

If they hold firm on base:

> "Understood. Then let's look at the rest of the package: a larger equity grant, a 15% target bonus with the first year guaranteed, and travel and accommodation in Berlin covered. With those in place, I can work with €170k."

## Equity questions (ask all of them)

- Is it real options or a VSOP (virtual shares)? German VSOPs pay out only on an exit or a liquidity event, and get taxed as income, not capital gains.
- What's the strike price or base valuation, and what was the last preferred price? At a late-stage valuation, the spread is what matters, not the number of units.
- Vesting: 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- What happens on a good-leaver departure? Is there an exercise window, and how long? Push for vested units to stay with you.
- Acceleration on a change of control (double trigger at minimum).
- Are there secondary sales or tender offers for employees? Well-funded AI companies sometimes run them, and it's the only way to get liquidity before an exit.
- Anti-dilution protection is rarely given to employees; don't spend capital on it. Ask instead how many top-ups or refresh grants Staff engineers usually receive.

## Location and the geographic discount

They might argue that Geneva-based remote work should cost them less, or that a non-Berlin hire needs to take a discount for being off-site.

> "I'm not asking for a Geneva salary; I'm asking for the top of your Berlin band. Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, so I'm already taking less than the local market would pay here. What I need on top is that travel to Berlin is a business cost, not something that comes out of my pay."

If they want a German employment contract while you live in Switzerland, ask HR how social security and tax would be handled (the A1 certificate / cross-border rules) before agreeing to a number. The net result can differ a lot.

## Competing offer template

Only use this if it's true, or reframe it as your market reference without inventing an offer.

> "To be transparent, I'm in late-stage conversations for Staff-level roles elsewhere, including in Switzerland, where base is in the CHF 200k range. I'd rather join you, the problem's more interesting and the team's growing fast, but I need the package to be at the top of your band to make that work. Is there flexibility?"

Softer version without a concrete offer:

> "Comparable Staff roles in Zurich and Geneva are landing between CHF 200k and 250k base. I'm not expecting that here, but it's why I'm anchored at the top of your published range."

## Things to get in writing

- Final level and title (Staff, or Principal if they'll go there)
- Base, bonus target and how it's paid out, and whether year one is guaranteed
- Equity: type, number of units, strike/valuation, vesting, cliff, leaver terms
- Expected Berlin cadence and who pays for travel and accommodation
- Employing entity and the country of the contract
- On-call expectations for customer deployments
- Start date and notice period
