# Negotiation Script: Founding Engineer, Series A AI/robotics data startup (via Mobiskill)

## Context that shapes the numbers

The company isn't named. What the posting says: a fast-growing AI and robotics data startup, backed by a top-tier US accelerator, at Series A, deploying physical-world data collection for large AI models. The team is French-speaking (fluent French is mandatory) with Paris offices, and the role is full remote in compatible timezones. The recruiter is Mobiskill (WeFy Group), a Paris tech recruitment agency, so their fee is usually a percentage of first-year base. That gives the recruiter a mild reason to push your base up, and the client a mild reason to hold it down.

**The posting publishes the band: $80k to $100k base, "depending on profile and location", plus founding-level BSPCE or stock options.** Two things follow from that:

- **The band was written for 1 to 5 years of experience.** You have 17+. That cuts both ways: you're over-qualified for the band, but they may not have budget for more. Assume the base ceiling is soft by maybe 10 to 20%, not 50%.
- **Equity is where the real room is.** A founding engineer title at Series A with ownership of the whole software ecosystem justifies a meaningful grant, and that costs the company no cash.

Decide your floor before the client call. If a remote role at ~$100k base doesn't work for you from Geneva even with good equity, say so early. The process has four steps including a homework, so don't spend them on a package you'd turn down.

## Before the numbers: get these answers

Ask these in the client call or the founder interview, not in the offer conversation:

1. How was the $80k to $100k band set, and is it a hard cap for remote hires outside France?
2. How many people are on the engineering team today, and who reports to whom? Does "founding engineer" mean you'd lead future hires?
3. What was the Series A, roughly what amount and what post-money valuation? Without the valuation, a percentage is the only number that means anything.
4. How would they employ someone living in Switzerland: an employer of record, a Swiss entity, or a contractor agreement? This changes the gross you need (see below).

## Opening anchor

Let them name a number first if you can. If Mobiskill asks for your expectations at the screening stage:

> "I've seen the band in the posting. I'm coming from a Senior Staff role in Switzerland and I've built a platform from zero to acquisition before, so I'd expect to land above the top of it. The more important part for me is the equity and the scope. If those are right, I'm flexible on the base. Can you tell me how firm the $100k is for someone with my background?"

If the client or founder pushes for a number:

> "Around $120k base, with options in the range of 0.6% to 1%. I know that's above your posted band. The band reads like it was set for someone with a few years of experience; what you'd get is someone who's already taken a platform from blank page to acquisition, and who can hire and set up the next engineers. If the base is fixed, I'd rather move on equity than walk away over $15k."

## If they come in low

**Offer at $90k to $100k and ~0.25%:**

> "Thanks, I can see you're taking it seriously. The base is close to workable. The equity is where I'm stuck: 0.25% is what I'd expect for an early engineer, not for someone owning the whole software ecosystem. Could we get to 0.6%, or 0.5% with a refresh tied to the next round?"

**Base fixed at $100k, no flexibility at all:**

> "Understood. If the cash is fixed, then I need the rest to reflect the role: 0.75% with a standard four-year vest, a one-year cliff, and acceleration if the company is acquired and my role goes away. A title of Founding Engineer, Lead or similar, with responsibility for hiring the next engineers. And a yearly review of the base once the next round closes."

**They say the band is for 1 to 5 years and you're "over-profile":**

> "Fair, and I'd rather talk about it openly than pretend otherwise. I'm not looking for a manager title or a slower pace. I want to be writing code every day and owning the system, which is exactly this job. What I bring on top is that I've already lived the phase you're entering, including the parts that hurt. That's worth something in equity even if the base band stays where it is."

## Equity details to ask about

- **Instrument:** BSPCE only work for employees of the French company. If you're hired as a contractor or through an EOR from Switzerland, you'll likely get stock options (or BSA, which have a purchase price). Ask which one, and get the tax treatment in Switzerland checked; Swiss taxation of options granted by a foreign company has its own rules.
- **Strike price and valuation:** ask for the strike price and the latest preferred share price, so you can see the real discount.
- **Vesting:** four years with a one-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Exercise window:** BSPCE and options often have to be exercised within a short time after you leave. Ask for an extended window, ideally several years for vested options. This is often the easiest win.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (acquisition plus your role ending).
- **Dilution:** there's no standard anti-dilution for employee options. Ask instead for a refresh grant policy at the next round, and get the fully diluted share count so the percentage is real.

## Location: don't accept a "remote discount" quietly

"Depending on location" usually means a lower number for cheaper countries. Geneva is not one of those.

> "On location, Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, and if you employ me through an EOR or as a contractor, I'm also carrying Swiss social charges and insurance that a Paris employee doesn't. I'm not asking for a Geneva salary. I'm asking that location doesn't push the number below the Paris equivalent, because the cost on my side is higher, not lower."

If they offer a contractor agreement: the gross needs to be roughly 15% to 20% higher than an employee salary to cover AVS/AHV contributions, pension (LPP/BVG) and no paid leave. Say so plainly and ask for the contract rate to reflect it.

Also ask for: a budget to travel to Paris for on-sites (a monthly or quarterly allowance), and a hardware and AI tooling budget, given they want agentic engineering every day.

## Competing offer template

Use only if real, or framed honestly as an ongoing process.

> "I want to be transparent. I'm also in late-stage conversations with a Swiss company for a Staff role, with a base in the CHF 180k range. I'd rather join you; the scope and the problem are more interesting to me. But the gap is large, and I need the equity to close part of it. If we can get to 0.75% and an extended exercise window, I'm ready to sign this week."

If there's no real competing offer:

> "I'm in other processes, mostly Staff and founding roles in Switzerland, and the cash there is meaningfully higher. That's fine, I'm not trying to match it. I just need the equity to make the trade-off reasonable."

## Final checklist before signing

- Base, currency, and how you'll actually be paid (employee, EOR, or contractor)
- Grant size in percentage of fully diluted shares, strike price, vesting, cliff, exercise window, acceleration
- Title and scope, including whether you'll hire the next engineers
- Remote terms in writing: full remote from Geneva, travel budget for Paris
- Date of the first salary review, ideally tied to the next fundraise
