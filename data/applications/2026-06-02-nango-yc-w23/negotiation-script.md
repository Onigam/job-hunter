# Negotiation Script — Nango, Staff Backend Engineer

## Market Context

**Role level:** Staff Backend Engineer (equivalent to Senior Staff IC in Nango's flat structure)
**Location:** Geneva, Switzerland (remote position, but cost base is Geneva)
**Nango's posted range:** $120k-$200k USD (US market comp band)
**USD/CHF rate (2026):** ~0.90, so $120k-$200k USD = CHF 108k-180k at face value

**Swiss market rates for equivalent role:**
- Staff Backend Engineer, seed-stage startup, Geneva: CHF 160,000-210,000
- Senior Staff IC at growth-stage dev-tools company: CHF 180,000-230,000
- With founding-engineer background and unicorn acquisition: top of range is justified

**Magino's justification for top range:**
- 17+ years experience including a founding-engineer chapter
- Alaya platform acquired by $1B+ valuation company after scaling to 50+ enterprise clients
- Senior Staff track at Benevity post-acquisition (cross-team arch impact, AI tooling lead)
- Geneva cost of living: consistently top 5 most expensive cities globally
- Strong CHF creates real purchasing power gap vs. USD remote roles

---

## Opening Anchor

**Target:** CHF 200,000 base, 0.25% equity

When they ask for your number, lead with the Swiss market frame, not their USD range:

> "My reference point is the Geneva market rate for Staff engineering at a growth-stage company, which sits around CHF 190-210k. Given my background, including the Alaya founding chapter and the work at Benevity, I'd anchor at CHF 200k base. On equity, for a team of this size at this stage, I'd expect something in the 0.2-0.3% range."

Don't mention their posted USD range. If they raise it, see the geographic discount section below.

---

## Counter-Offer Scripts

**If they come in at $150k USD (~CHF 135k):**

> "I appreciate the offer. I want to be straightforward: that range is based on a US cost-of-living anchor, but I'm working from Geneva, which changes the picture meaningfully. The cost structure here is comparable to San Francisco or New York, and the Swiss market rate for Staff engineering at this level is CHF 170-210k. I'd need at least CHF 175k to make this work practically. Can we get there?"

**If they say the role is fully remote and they don't pay location-based premiums:**

> "I understand the remote philosophy, and I'm not asking for a premium. I'm asking for compensation that reflects what it actually costs to live and work where I am. A remote engineer in Geneva has the same rent and healthcare costs as an engineer at a Geneva office. The comp band that makes sense in Kansas City doesn't work in Switzerland. Can we find a number that's calibrated to European reality rather than a US baseline?"

**If they're firm on a USD range and won't move the base:**

Push on other levers in this order:
1. Equity increase: 0.1% additional if base can't move
2. Signing bonus: CHF 20,000-30,000 one-time
3. Annual equipment/home office budget: CHF 3,000-5,000/year
4. Conference and learning budget: CHF 5,000/year

> "If the base is fixed at that level, I'd want to make sure the equity reflects the gap. Could we look at 0.35-0.4% to account for the geographic comp difference? Or a signing bonus to bridge year one?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Ask for:** 0.2-0.35% (seed-stage, team of ~7, YC-backed)
**Structure:** 1-year cliff, 4-year vest, monthly vesting preferred after cliff
**Key questions to ask before accepting any equity number:**

- What's the current cap table dilution since YC and the last raise?
- What's the strike price on the options?
- Are these ISOs or NSOs? (ISOs have better tax treatment)
- Does the company have a standard option acceleration clause on acquisition?
- What anti-dilution protection is in place for common shareholders?

**If they give you a low equity number (under 0.15%):**

> "For someone joining as a staff engineer this early in the company's life, I'd expect equity to reflect the founding-stage nature of this hire. At a team of 7, the delta between 0.15% and 0.25% is meaningful in any positive exit scenario, but it's also what makes the comp package competitive against companies that can offer a higher cash base. Can we revisit the equity range?"

**Refresher expectation:** Ask explicitly for a refresher conversation at 2 years. It doesn't need to be in the offer letter, but establish it verbally.

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

Nango is a remote company and may have location-based pay bands. If they flag this:

> "I understand Nango operates remote-first, which I think is the right model. My concern isn't about working from Geneva specifically — it's about the practical math. Switzerland has one of the highest costs of living globally, and the CHF doesn't move in sync with USD or EUR. A location discount that looks like 'we pay fair remote rates' from a US perspective can translate to a meaningful real-terms cut in Switzerland. I'd rather we calibrate to Swiss market data than apply a remote discount off a US anchor."

**Data point to cite if needed:** Glassdoor and local Swiss salary surveys (e.g., Swiss ICT Salary Survey, salary.ch) show Staff Backend Engineer roles in Geneva at CHF 170-220k. These are not outliers.

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have other conversations in progress, use them:

> "I'm in late-stage conversations with two other infrastructure companies, both anchoring in the CHF 185-200k range. I'd prefer Nango — the problem you're solving is more interesting to me and the team caliber is strong. But I'd need the comp to be in that neighborhood to make it the clear choice."

If you don't have competing offers, don't fabricate. Instead, use market data as the anchor:

> "The market range I'm seeing for this level in Switzerland is CHF 175-210k. That's the frame I'm working from."

---

## Timeline and Process Notes

**Nango's hiring process (4 stages, target 2 weeks):**
1. 30-minute CTO call
2. 30-minute CEO call
3. 1-hour technical architecture interview
4. Paid full-day assignment

Don't bring up compensation before the CEO call. After the CEO call is the right moment if they haven't asked. If they ask for a number in the CTO call, defer:

> "I'd prefer to get a sense of the scope of the role before anchoring on a number. Can we revisit that after the technical round?"

**Offer deadline:** Ask for 5 business days minimum to review any written offer. If they push for faster:

> "I want to make a considered decision rather than a rushed one. Five days is reasonable for a role at this level. Is there something time-sensitive on your end I should know about?"
