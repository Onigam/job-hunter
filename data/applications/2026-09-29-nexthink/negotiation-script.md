# Negotiation Script: Nexthink, Senior Backend Engineer (Nexthink Assist)

Posted location: Lausanne, Switzerland, hybrid, permanent contract. Candidate based in Geneva.
Posted comp: "competitive compensation package", no figure given. Benefits listed: unlimited PTO on top of 25 holiday days, fitness access, public transport reimbursement, relocation help.

---

## 1. Read the situation first

**This isn't a startup.** Nexthink is a mature, privately held scale-up (Vista Equity Partners took a majority stake in 2024). There's no early-stage equity story here, so base salary and bonus carry most of the weight. Any long-term incentive will be a PE-style plan, not classic stock options, and it's worth asking about directly.

**Level is the real negotiation.** The posting says Senior. The candidate's current title is Senior Staff, with 17+ years of experience, a cofounded and acquired company, and cross-team architecture work. Getting slotted at the top of Senior, or leveled as Staff/Lead, is worth more than haggling a few thousand francs inside the wrong band. Raise this early, before any number is fixed.

**Honest gaps they'll use.** Kafka in production and "millions of users" scale aren't in the history, and recent hands-on work is more TypeScript than Java. Expect these to come up as reasons to hold the number down. The answer is scope and ownership, not pretending the gaps don't exist.

**Contract is Swiss and in CHF.** No currency or geographic discount argument should apply. Lausanne and Geneva have comparable living costs.

## 2. Market anchors (base, 2026, working estimates)

Re-check against Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, the Swiss ICT salary survey (swissICT) and any recruiter feedback before the call. Vaud typically pays a bit below Zürich for the same role.

| Level | Lausanne / Vaud, CHF | Geneva, CHF | Zürich, CHF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Senior backend engineer | 130k to 160k | 135k to 165k | 145k to 180k |
| Staff / Lead engineer | 160k to 190k | 165k to 195k | 180k to 220k |

Variable pay at Swiss software scale-ups of this size usually sits somewhere around 5% to 15% of base. Ask what the target is and what share was actually paid out in the last two years.

**Targets**

- Walk-away: CHF 155k base.
- Target: CHF 170k to 175k base, plus bonus.
- Opening anchor: CHF 185k base, justified by Staff-level scope.
- Also ask for: a level review at 12 months written into the offer, and clarity on any long-term incentive plan.

## 3. Opening the conversation

If the recruiter asks for expectations early:

> "I'd like to understand how you'd level me before we lock a number. I'm currently a Senior Staff Engineer, and I cofounded and built the platform behind a company that was acquired, so I'd expect to come in at the top of Senior or at Staff. Based in Switzerland, for that scope, I'm looking at a base around CHF 185k, plus bonus. Can you share the band you have for this role?"

If they push for one number:

> "Around CHF 185k base. I'm flexible on how it's split between base and variable once I see the full package."

If they ask for current salary: don't give it.

> "I'd rather focus on what this role is worth. My expectations are based on the Swiss market for Staff-level backend work, which is where I'd put what you've described."

## 4. If they come in low

**Scenario A: mid-band Senior offer (for example CHF 145k).**

> "Thanks, I appreciate it. I'd like to be straight with you: that's below where I am today and below the market for someone owning features from design to monitoring on a distributed system. The posting also asks me to mentor engineers and act as the link with product, which is Staff-level work in most places. I'd need to be around CHF 175k to make this move. Is there room to get there, or to look at the level?"

**Scenario B: they point at the gaps (Java recency, Kafka, scale).**

> "Fair, and I've been upfront about those. They're things I'll close in the first few months. What you'd get from day one is someone who's built a platform from nothing, run it in production, and dealt with the architecture decisions that come with growth. That's what I'm pricing."

**Scenario C: base is capped by the band.**

> "If the base can't move, can we close the gap another way? A sign-on bonus, a higher bonus target, or a written level and comp review at twelve months would all work for me."

## 5. Long-term incentives

Nexthink is PE-backed, so ask these questions rather than assuming there's anything:

- Is there a long-term incentive plan, phantom shares or management equity for engineers at this level? Who's eligible?
- What triggers a payout: an exit only, or annual vesting?
- What's the vesting schedule, and is there a cliff?
- What happens to unvested units if the company is sold again, or if I leave? Is there acceleration on a change of control?
- How is the unit value set, and is there any dilution protection?

If nothing exists at this level:

> "Understood. Since there's no equity component, I'd weight the base and bonus a bit higher. That's part of why I'm at CHF 175k rather than lower."

## 6. Location and hybrid

There's no geographic discount to push back on here, since both cities are Swiss and priced similarly. The practical points are the commute and hybrid days.

> "I'm in Geneva, so Lausanne is about 40 minutes by train. I'm happy to be in the office regularly. Could we agree on the expected number of office days, and does the transport reimbursement cover a GA or the Geneva to Lausanne route?"

If they mention the Geneva office, ask whether the Assist team can be based there part of the week.

## 7. Competing offer template

Use only with a real process in play, and never invent numbers.

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations elsewhere, with an offer around CHF [X] base. Nexthink is my preferred option because of the Assist work, so I'd rather sort out the gap with you than take the other one. Is there anything you can do on base or level?"

If there's nothing formal yet:

> "I'm talking to a few other companies in Switzerland at Staff level, so I'll have a clearer picture soon. I'd like to move with you first if we can get the numbers aligned."

## 8. Before signing

- Level and title written into the contract.
- Base, bonus target, and how bonus is actually calculated.
- LTIP terms in writing, if any.
- Notice period (Swiss standard is often three months at this level) and start date.
- Pension (LPP) contribution split. Some employers pay more than the legal minimum, which is worth real money.
- Hybrid arrangement and office days.
- Training or conference budget.
