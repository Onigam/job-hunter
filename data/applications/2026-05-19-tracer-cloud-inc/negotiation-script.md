# Negotiation Script — Tracer Cloud, Founding AI Engineer (Staff)

---

## Market Context

**Role level:** Founding AI Engineer, Staff (equivalent to Senior Staff / Principal in most orgs)
**Company stage:** Early-stage, VC-backed (likely pre-Series A or early Series A)
**Location:** London HQ, but likely remote-friendly given distributed hiring patterns

**London Staff AI Engineer market (2026):**
- Base: £130,000 to £165,000
- Equity: 0.1% to 0.5% for a founding-level hire
- Total comp: £150k to £200k+ depending on equity value

**Geneva/Switzerland market for equivalent role:**
- CHF 170,000 to CHF 220,000 base
- Geneva costs ~35% more than London on housing + taxes
- Remote from Geneva means Tracer saves London office overhead

**Key leverage points:**
- Founding engineer designation means more ownership, not just a senior hire
- Multi-agent / LLM systems expertise commands a premium right now
- The Alaya founding story and the Benevity acquisition are direct proof of value at exactly this role type
- You're available immediately with zero notice period required

---

## Opening Anchor

When they ask about expectations, don't give a range first. Ask them:

> "What's the comp structure you're working with for this role? I want to make sure we're in the same ballpark before we go deep."

If they press you to go first:

> "For a founding-level Staff role in AI systems, I'd expect something in the range of £150k to £160k base, plus meaningful founding equity. The equity piece matters a lot at this stage. What does your option pool look like for founding hires?"

If they're paying in CHF or offering a remote-friendly rate:

> "My cost base is Geneva, which runs about 30 to 35 percent higher than London on housing and tax. I'd want the base to reflect that, so CHF 190k to CHF 210k equivalent. I'm flexible on structure if the equity upside is real."

---

## Counter-Offer Scenarios

**Scenario A: They come in at £120k or below**

> "That's below what I'd need to make this work given my Geneva base. I'm not trying to be difficult, but for a founding engineer role where I'm expected to own core architecture from day one, £120k doesn't reflect the market or the scope. I could get to yes at £145k plus a meaningful equity adjustment. Is there flexibility on either side?"

**Scenario B: They offer £130k to £140k**

> "I'm close to yes on that. The base works if the equity is set up properly. Can you walk me through the option pool size, my grant as a percentage of the company, the vesting schedule, and the cliff? If the numbers make sense on the equity side, I'm in."

**Scenario C: They offer £145k+ (strong base)**

> "That works on the base side. Let's talk equity. For a founding hire, I'd want to see at least 0.15% to 0.25% of the company, standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff, and ideally some acceleration on acquisition. What are you offering?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Things to understand before signing:**
- What is the total option pool size?
- What percentage of the company do the offered options represent on a fully diluted basis?
- What is the current 409A valuation (or equivalent fair market value)?
- What is the strike price?
- Are there any preference stacks that would reduce common share value on exit?
- Is there a double-trigger acceleration clause for acquisition scenarios?

**What to ask for as a founding engineer:**
- 0.15% to 0.5% (depending on stage; earlier = higher percentage)
- 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff (standard, do not accept less)
- Double-trigger acceleration on acquisition (your options should fully vest if you're let go post-acquisition)
- 90-day post-termination exercise window, not 30-day (30-day is punitive and common at early startups)

**Pushback script if equity feels low:**
> "I understand the pool is constrained. But you're billing this as a founding engineer role, and the equity needs to reflect that. If the grant is closer to a standard senior hire, then either the title is misleading or the comp needs to compensate. I'd rather align on one or the other than have a mismatch on day one."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If they try to apply a "remote from Geneva" discount to the London rate:

> "I've seen some companies apply a location discount for remote hires, and I want to address that upfront. Geneva has a higher cost of living than London, not lower. Housing, taxes, and general expenses run 30 to 35 percent higher here. If anything, the base should trend up, not down. The offset is that you're not paying London office costs for me. I'd suggest we keep the base at London market rate and treat that as the floor."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have other offers in play (or want to create urgency without fabricating):

> "I want to be honest: I'm in late-stage conversations with two other companies, both at similar founding-level scope. I'm most interested in Tracer because of the technical problem and the approach. But I need to wrap this up within the next couple of weeks. Can we get to a term sheet by end of next week?"

If no competing offer but you want to set a timeline:

> "I have a few conversations running in parallel and I'd like to make a decision within the next two weeks. What's your timeline on your side? I'd rather keep things moving than let it drag."

---

## Remote / Work Terms to Clarify

- Is the role fully remote or is there an expectation of London presence? How often?
- If travel to London is expected, who covers it and how frequently?
- Is there a home office stipend or equipment budget for remote hires?
- What's the employment structure for Geneva-based remote employees (direct hire, EOR like Deel or Remote.com, or contractor)?

> "One thing I want to clarify early: for remote roles based in Geneva, most companies either hire through an Employer of Record or use a contractor structure. What's your current setup for non-UK remote hires?"
