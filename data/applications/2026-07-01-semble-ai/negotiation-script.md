# Negotiation Script, Semble AI / Founding AI Engineer

---

## Market Context

**Role posted:** Founding AI Engineer
**Stage:** YC Batch F25, two founders, pre-Series A, building AI agents for building-system design (fire, HVAC, plumbing, security) automation
**Location:** San Francisco, CA, US (posting requires US citizen or existing visa holder, no sponsorship implied)
**Posted range:** $150K to $250K base plus 0.80% to 1.40% equity

**Comparable YC listings, Founding/Early AI Engineer roles (2025-2026):**
- Founding Engineer, seed/pre-seed, 2-3 person team: $140K to $220K + 0.75% to 2.00% equity
- Founding AI Engineer with vision or multi-modal scope tends to price at the top of range given the narrower talent pool
- At a 2-person founding team, the third technical hire usually lands 0.75% to 1.5%, wide range because there's no comp band yet

**Switzerland market anchor, for framing base salary expectations only (this is a US-based role, not a remote Swiss hire):**
- Senior/Staff backend or platform engineer, Geneva/Zurich: CHF 150,000 to 190,000 base
- Founding-engineer-track background: CHF 190,000 to 230,000
- CHF/USD around 1.10 currently, so CHF 190K is roughly $210K USD
- Geneva cost of living is comparable to or higher than San Francisco on housing, so there's no real "geographic discount" argument to make in either direction, useful mainly to show the ask isn't inflated relative to what similar scope pays elsewhere

**Key leverage point:** Magino brings founding-engineer experience (built and scaled Alaya to acquisition by a $1B+ company) plus hands-on LLM, RAG and multi-agent workflow experience, which covers roughly half the stated scope. The computer vision half is a genuine gap and should be acknowledged rather than negotiated around, it's a factor that argues for landing toward the middle of the range rather than the top until vision work is proven out on the job.

---

## Opening Position

**Target base:** $190K to $210K
**Equity target:** 1.00% to 1.25%
**Fallback floor:** $170K + 1.00% equity before reconsidering

**Rationale to articulate:**
- 17+ years of experience, founding-engineer track record with an acquisition outcome
- Full-stack (Node/TypeScript/React/PostgreSQL) plus applied LLM, RAG and multi-agent experience covers most of the stated stack directly
- Vision is the honest gap, worth naming up front rather than overselling, and worth factoring into where in the range to land
- At a 2-person team, this is effectively a co-founder-adjacent technical hire, not a standard IC role

---

## First Offer Response

If they come in at $150K to $165K:

> "I appreciate the offer. Given the founding-engineer scope here and that I'm covering the RAG, agent orchestration and full-stack side of the roadmap on day one, I was expecting something closer to $190K. Is there room to move, either on base or by adjusting the equity to bridge the gap?"

If they come in at $165K to $185K:

> "That's in the right neighborhood. I was hoping to land closer to $195K given the scope of ownership at a two-person team. Is the base number firm, or is there flexibility on the equity side to get the full package there?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Posted range:** 0.80% to 1.40%
**Ask for:** 1.00% to 1.25%
**Framing:**

> "At this stage, equity is a big part of how I'm thinking about the offer. Given I'd be the third technical person in the building and covering half the core roadmap directly, I'd want to land at 1.1% to 1.25%, with a standard 4-year vest and 1-year cliff."

**Key terms to confirm:**
- Vesting schedule: 4 years, 1-year cliff, push back on anything longer
- Option type: ISOs, confirm strike price and current 409A valuation in writing before signing
- Exercise window post-departure: ask for a 5 to 10 year window rather than the standard 90 days, this matters more at a pre-Series A company where an exit is years out
- Preferred stock stack: ask how much has been raised and on what terms, since a heavy preference stack changes what the equity is actually worth
- Anti-dilution: not expected to negotiate at this stage, but worth understanding how much dilution is likely before the next round

**If equity is capped near 0.80%:**

> "If 0.80% is the ceiling, I'd want the base closer to $215K to reflect the full package."

---

## Visa and Relocation, Address This Early

This is the point that needs resolving before comp talk goes very far. The posting states US citizen or visa holder only, which likely means no sponsorship budget or appetite at this stage.

> "Before we get deep into numbers, I want to be upfront: I'm currently in Geneva and would need a path to work authorization in the US. Can you tell me directly whether the company sponsors visas at this stage, or whether this role requires someone who already has status?"

If they can't sponsor, this may be a hard stop regardless of comp, worth confirming in the first conversation rather than after negotiating an offer. If there's any flexibility (remote-for-now, contractor-to-hire, or a later sponsorship path once funded), get specifics in writing before investing more time.

---

## Geographic and Relocation Costs

If relocation comes up:

> "Assuming the visa path works, I'd want to talk relocation support explicitly, since a San Francisco move from Geneva isn't a small logistical lift. A relocation allowance or temporary housing stipend for the first month or two would help a lot."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in hand:

> "I have another offer in process at [$X plus Y% equity] that I'm weighing alongside this one. The technical problem here is the more interesting one, but the visa question and the comp gap both need to close before I can commit."

If no competing offer, but signaling optionality:

> "I'm in active conversations with a couple of other companies right now, so I'd like to move through this process at a reasonable pace on both sides."

---

## Things Not to Negotiate Away

- Equity vesting cliff: 1 year is standard, longer is a red flag at this stage
- Written confirmation of visa sponsorship path or lack thereof, before signing anything
- Exercise window post-departure: this matters more than people think at a pre-Series A company
- Start date: don't let urgency pressure a decision before the visa question is actually answered

---

## Close Script

Once terms are agreed and the visa path is confirmed in writing:

> "Good, I'm in. Can you send the offer letter with the final numbers and the sponsorship details in writing? Once that's confirmed I'll sign and we can start working out the relocation timeline."

Never sign on a verbal agreement, and never relocate on a verbal promise of sponsorship, get it in writing first.
