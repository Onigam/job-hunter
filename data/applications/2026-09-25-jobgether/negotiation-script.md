# Negotiation Script: Senior Product Engineer, Darkplane (Dash0, via Jobgether)

## Context
- **Who's actually hiring:** Jobgether lists the role "on behalf of a partner company". Darkplane is Dash0's product, so the employer is almost certainly Dash0: the OpenTelemetry-native observability company founded in 2023 by Mirko Novakovic (who previously sold Instana to IBM). Dash0 raised a **$110M Series B at a $1B valuation in March 2026**, led by Balderton, and reports 600+ paying customers. Confirm the employer name in the first call.
- **Setup:** remote-first, listed for Switzerland, with offices in Amsterdam, New York and Munich. No salary range is published. The benefits line mentions "competitive salary with equity", a EUR 60/month allowance, location-specific benefits and access to the founders.
- **Level gap:** the title is "Senior Product Engineer". You've been Senior Staff for five years. Don't turn down the title out of hand, since at a product-led unicorn "Senior" can pay well, but use the gap to push for the top of the band and ask where Staff sits in their ladder.
- **Where you have the upper hand:** fresh capital, a new product line (Darkplane) that needs people who can own things end to end, and a Swiss hire paid against Swiss cost of living. **Where you don't:** Go, ClickHouse and sandboxing experience aren't on your CV, so lean on your level and track record, not on the stack.
- **Employment setup:** Dash0 probably has no Swiss entity, so ask early whether you'd be hired through an Employer of Record (Deel, Remote, Oyster) or as a contractor. This changes the gross-to-net maths, pension (LPP/BVG), accident insurance and how the equity is taxed.

**What this means:** if they pay Swiss hires on a Munich band, you'll land around CHF 110k to 125k, which is a real cut for Geneva. The whole negotiation hinges on getting them to use a Swiss band.

## Your target

- **Walk-away:** CHF 160k base, an equity grant worth something real at the Series B price, and written confirmation of the employment setup (EOR with full Swiss social coverage and LPP).
- **Target:** CHF 180k base plus meaningful equity, a Staff title or a written path to it within 12 months, and scope that includes one Darkplane area end to end (for example the ingest pipeline or the evaluation runtime).
- **Opening anchor:** CHF 190k base plus equity. Justification: 17 years in production systems, a platform built from zero to an acquisition by a unicorn, Senior Staff level for the last five years, and hands-on AI-augmented engineering work that lines up directly with what Darkplane measures.

## Script: when Jobgether or the recruiter asks for expectations

> "For a senior role based in Geneva, I'm looking at around CHF 190k base, plus equity. That reflects the Swiss market for someone who's been at Senior Staff level and built a platform from scratch through an acquisition. I'm flexible on how base and equity are split, and I'd like to understand your leveling first. Where does this role sit in your ladder?"

If they push for a single number before sharing their range:

> "I'd rather not negotiate against myself. If you share the band for Switzerland, I can tell you pretty quickly whether we're in the same place."

## Script: if the offer comes in low (e.g. CHF 130k to 145k)

> "Thanks, I appreciate it, and I'm still very interested in Darkplane. The base is below what senior engineers earn in Geneva, though, and well below where I've been. Is this a Swiss band or a German one? If we can get to CHF 175k, I'm ready to move forward."

If they say base is fixed:

> "Understood. Then I'd look at the rest of the package: a larger equity grant, a sign-on bonus of CHF 15k to 20k to cover the gap in year one, and a formal review at six months tied to clear criteria, with an adjustment built in."

## Geographic discount pushback

If they suggest remote Swiss hires are paid on a European average:

> "I get why you'd want one band for everyone, but Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe. Rent, health insurance and social charges alone make CHF 130k here worth about what EUR 90k is in Munich. I'm not asking for a premium; I'm asking to be paid like the market I'm hired in, which is why the posting is listed for Switzerland in the first place."

## Equity (Series B, $1B valuation)

Ask for all of this in writing before you sign:
- **Number of options or RSUs, and the fully diluted share count**, so you can work out your percentage. A grant without a denominator means nothing.
- **Strike price versus the latest preferred price.** At a Series B unicorn the strike is usually well below the preferred price; find out what the latest 409A or equivalent valuation is.
- **Vesting:** the usual is 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Exercise window after leaving:** the default is often 90 days. Ask for 5 to 10 years, as many European scale-ups now offer.
- **Acceleration:** at minimum double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination). Given Instana's history, an exit is a realistic scenario.
- **Liquidation preferences:** ask whether the preferred stock is 1x non-participating. Anything more aggressive cuts into employee equity at exit.
- **Refresh grants:** ask how the company handles refreshers after the first grant.
- **Swiss tax:** in Switzerland, options on unlisted companies are usually taxed as income when you exercise them, not when they're granted. Check with a Geneva tax advisor before exercising, and confirm with the EOR how the grant will be reported on your salary certificate.

Script:

> "The equity is an important part of this for me. Could you share the number of options, the strike price, the fully diluted share count and the post-leave exercise window? I'd like to put a real value on it before comparing it with the cash."

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

> "I'll be transparent: I'm in late-stage talks with another company, with an offer around CHF [X] base. Darkplane is my first choice because of the product and the problem space. If we can get to CHF [Y], I'm ready to sign this week."

If there's no other offer, don't make one up. Use timing instead:

> "I'm in a few other processes and expect to decide within the next two weeks, so it would help to know where we land soon."

## Other items worth negotiating

- **Title and level:** Staff instead of Senior, or a written review against Staff criteria at 12 months.
- **Travel budget** for regular visits to Munich or Amsterdam, paid separately from salary.
- **Home office setup** on top of the EUR 60/month allowance.
- **Learning budget** (Go, ClickHouse, conferences like KubeCon or OTel Community Day).
- **Notice period and start date:** plan for Swiss notice periods if you're still employed, typically one to three months.
- **Paid vacation:** the Swiss minimum is 20 days; aim for 25.

## Red lines

- Contractor status with no social coverage, unless the day rate makes up for it by a wide margin (about +30% over the equivalent salary).
- A German band applied to a Swiss hire with no adjustment.
- Equity details that stay vague after two requests.
