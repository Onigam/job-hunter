# Negotiation Script: CTO, food-waste ML SaaS (via Impala Search)

## Context that shapes the numbers

The company isn't named in the posting. What it does say: Berlin, full time, executive level, ML demand forecasting for fresh food, "up to 30%" less waste, Germany's largest grocers as customers in several European markets, an 8-figure Series A, €2M ARR and contracts targeting €5M this year. The recruiter is Ivan Lechev, Director and Co-Founder at Impala Search.

**Likely company: Freshflow** (Berlin, founded 2021, CEO Avik Mukhija). It closed a $10M Series A in June 2026 led by Reimann Investors, with World Fund, Venture Stars, Capnamic and IBB Ventures. Public customers include Edeka, Carrefour and Intermarché across Germany and France, with plans for 30+ hires within a year and expansion into meat, bakery and warehouse replenishment. Treat this as a strong guess, not a fact, and don't name it to the recruiter until they do. Cologne-based Foodforecast also claims "up to 30%", but its €8M round is 7 figures and it isn't in Berlin.

What that means for the deal:

- **This isn't a founding CTO seat.** The company has revenue, a product and a team. Equity will be meaningful but well below founder levels, and it'll most likely be a German VSOP (virtual shares), not real shares.
- **Cash runway is decent but not lavish.** A ~$10M Series A with 30+ planned hires means the base has a ceiling. The upside lever is equity and the title/board position.
- **Location is the first question, not the last.** The role is in Berlin. Decide before the first call whether you'd relocate or need a hybrid pattern (for example one week in four in Berlin). Every number below changes depending on that answer.
- **ML depth is your negotiating weak point.** They asked for "real machine-learning or AI-engineering experience". Expect them to use that to hold the number down. Your counter is the founder-to-acquisition track record and the platform/delivery side, which is what breaks first between €2M and €5M ARR.

## Opening anchor script

Use this when they ask for expectations. Try to get their band first:

> "Before I give a number, can you share the range the board has signed off on? It'll save us both time."

If they push:

> "For a CTO seat at a Series A company with €2M ARR and contracts going to €5M, I'm looking at around €195k base and roughly 2% in equity. That reflects having built a platform from zero through to acquisition by Benevity and running architecture at Senior Staff level for the five years after. I'm flexible on how cash and equity balance out, but the total needs to reflect owning the whole technical side, including the ML team."

## If they come in low

Typical low offer: €150k to 160k base, 0.5% to 0.75% VSOP.

> "Thanks, I can see the logic. The base is below where CTOs at comparable Series A companies in Berlin land, and the equity is closer to a senior hire than an exec. If the base can't move much, I'd want the equity at 1.5% or more, with acceleration on a change of control. I've been through an acquisition as a cofounder, so that clause matters to me. Can we look at that combination?"

If they say the ML gap justifies a lower number:

> "That's fair to raise. I'm not going to pretend I've built time-series forecasting systems. What you're hiring for at €2M to €5M ARR is mostly the platform, the team and enterprise delivery, and that's exactly the stage I've already taken a company through. I'd suggest we keep the base where I proposed and tie part of the equity or bonus to milestones we both agree matter, like onboarding the next retailer on schedule."

Alternative levers if cash is stuck:

- A sign-on bonus to cover relocation or the first year's travel
- A guaranteed base review at the next funding round or at €5M ARR
- A larger ARR-linked bonus
- Board observer seat or formal executive committee membership

## Equity: questions to ask before signing

German startups almost always use VSOP, which is a cash claim on an exit, not ownership. Get the plan document and read it, or have a lawyer read it.

- **Percentage of what?** Fully diluted share count, including the unallocated pool. Ask for the current cap table summary.
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Leaver clauses:** good leaver vs bad leaver definitions are where VSOPs get unpleasant. Being let go without cause must count as a good leaver, with vested units kept.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination), at least 50% of unvested.
- **Payout events:** exit only, or also secondary sales and IPO? Is there any payout if the company is acquired for less than the preference stack?
- **Liquidation preferences:** a 1x non-participating preference is fine. Participating preferences or multiples above 1x change what your percentage is really worth.
- **Strike / base price:** VSOP units usually carry a base valuation. Ask which round it's set at.
- **Anti-dilution:** employees rarely get it. Instead, ask for a top-up grant at the next round so your stake doesn't shrink faster than the founders'.
- **Tax:** VSOP payouts are taxed as income in Germany. If you stay Swiss-resident, check how a payout would be taxed before you rely on it.

## Location and the "Geneva discount" pushback

If you stay in Geneva and commute:

> "I'm based in Geneva and I'd plan to be in Berlin regularly, at least one week in four plus key customer moments. I don't think the compensation should be discounted for that. The role's value comes from what I deliver, and I'm covering the travel time myself. What I'd ask for is a travel budget so the cost doesn't eat into the package."

If they suggest paying less because you'd be remote:

> "Location-based pay works for some roles, but a CTO is measured against the market for CTOs, not against where they sleep. Geneva is also one of the most expensive places in Europe, so a local discount wouldn't apply anyway. I'm happy to agree a clear on-site commitment in writing."

If you'd relocate: ask for a relocation package (€10k to 20k is typical), help with housing search, and a start date that allows the move.

Also check the employment setup early: a German contract with Swiss residence brings social security and tax questions (A1 certificate, cross-border rules). Ask whether they've employed someone from Switzerland before or would use an employer-of-record.

## Competing offer template

Use only if true. If there's no other offer, talk about the process instead of inventing one.

> "I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company for a senior engineering leadership role, and I'm expecting an offer in the next couple of weeks. This role is my first choice because of the problem and the stage, but the other package is meaningfully stronger on base. If we can close the gap on base or equity, I'd be ready to commit quickly."

Softer version for an active process with no offer yet:

> "I'm in other processes at a similar level, so timing matters to me. If we can agree the main terms this week, I'd prefer to wrap this up and stop the others."

## Before the call checklist

- Decide your location answer (relocate, hybrid, or neither) and your walk-away floor
- Ask Ivan for the approved band, the company name and the reporting line (CEO only, or also board?)
- Ask about team size and how ML vs engineering is split today
- Request the VSOP plan, cap table summary and liquidation preference terms before signing
- Get everything agreed, including on-site commitment and travel budget, in writing
