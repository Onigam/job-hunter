# Negotiation Script — Technical Lead Backend (DeFi Startup via ALBEDIS)

Role: Technical Lead Backend (TypeScript / Rust)
Company: Undisclosed DeFi Startup (via ALBEDIS)
Location: Geneva, Switzerland (Hybrid)
Date: 2026-06-25

---

## Market Context

**Switzerland market rates for Technical Lead / Senior Staff Backend Engineer, Geneva, 2026:**

- Senior Backend Engineer (7-10y): CHF 120,000 - 145,000/year
- Tech Lead / Staff Backend (10-15y): CHF 145,000 - 175,000/year
- Senior Staff / Principal Engineer (15y+): CHF 175,000 - 210,000/year
- DeFi/blockchain premium: +10-20% above traditional software sector

Magino profile: 17+ years, co-founder, platform engineering at scale, acquired startup. This maps to Senior Staff/Principal. Target range: **CHF 170,000 - 200,000 base**, plus equity.

**DeFi startup comp structure typically includes:**
- Base salary (CHF, paid monthly)
- Token allocation (vested, not liquid at grant)
- Optional: USDC or stablecoin bonus component
- Standard Swiss benefits: AVS/2nd pillar contributions, health insurance subsidy

Geneva cost of living note: Geneva is one of the top 3 most expensive cities in Europe. Any below-market base should be called out explicitly.

## Opening Anchor

Use this when the recruiter (ALBEDIS) asks for salary expectations. Deliver it casually, not as a demand.

**Script:**

"For a technical lead role in Geneva, given my background building and scaling platforms from zero and the seniority level this role implies, I'd be looking at a base in the range of CHF 175,000 to 195,000. That's based on what I'm seeing in the Geneva market for senior staff-level engineers with founding experience. I'm also interested in understanding how equity or token allocation is structured at this stage — that's part of the overall picture for me."

Why this works:
- Anchors high but within justifiable range
- References market, not personal need
- Opens the token/equity conversation early without desperation

---

## If They Come In Low (CHF 130,000-150,000)

This is common for early-stage DeFi startups trying to offset with equity/tokens. Do not accept on the first call.

**Script:**

"I appreciate the transparency. The base is lower than what I was expecting for this level in Geneva, and I want to make sure we find something that works for both sides. A few questions: what does the equity or token allocation look like, and what's the vesting schedule? And is there flexibility on the base, even if it means adjusting another component of the package?"

Then wait. Do not fill the silence.

If they push back that the base is fixed:

"I understand. Let me think about the overall package and come back to you. I don't want to make a decision based on the base alone if the token allocation is significant, but I'd need to understand the cliff, the vesting period, and the current valuation or FDV to assess that properly."

## Equity / Token Negotiation

For an early-stage DeFi startup, token allocation is often the main lever. Get clarity before accepting any number.

**Questions to ask:**

1. "What percentage of the token supply is allocated to the team, and how is it distributed by seniority?"
2. "What's the vesting schedule? Four-year with a one-year cliff is standard — is that what you're doing?"
3. "Are the tokens subject to a lockup after vesting, and if so, how long?"
4. "What's the current implied valuation of the protocol, and what's the FDV at current token price (if applicable)?"
5. "If the token hasn't launched yet: what's the last round's valuation, and how does that inform where you're pricing the tokens for team grants?"

**Script for negotiating token size:**

"Given the seniority of this role and the expectation that I'd be setting architectural direction for the core backend, I'd expect to be in the 0.3-0.6% range of the team token pool, depending on total supply and how the rest of the team is structured. Is that in the right ballpark for how you're thinking about this?"

## Geographic Discount Pushback

Some startups try to apply a "remote discount" even for Geneva-based roles. Push back directly.

**Script:**

"I want to flag something: I'm based in Geneva, which is one of the most expensive cities in Switzerland and Europe. If there's a market-rate conversation to be had, it should be anchored to Geneva rates, not to remote-worker rates from lower cost-of-living regions. I'd expect the base to reflect that."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in play (or want to create urgency):

**Script:**

"I want to be straightforward with you: I'm in conversations with another company at a similar level, and I expect to have a decision to make within the next two to three weeks. I'm interested in this role specifically because of the DeFi context and the Geneva location, but I'll need the overall package to be competitive to make this the clear choice. Is there room to move on [base / token allocation / signing bonus]?"

If you don't have another offer but want to create appropriate urgency, use this softer version:

"I'm at a point in my search where I'm weighing a few opportunities seriously, and I'd like to make a decision in the next few weeks rather than let things drag. What's your timeline on your end, and is there flexibility to move things forward?"

---

## Signing Bonus (Use as Fallback)

If the base is stuck and tokens are uncertain, ask for a signing bonus to bridge the gap.

**Script:**

"If the base is firm at [X], one option worth discussing is a signing bonus. It doesn't change your ongoing cost structure, and it helps bridge the gap between what I'd expect at market rate and what the role is offering. Something in the CHF 15,000-25,000 range would make the overall package more competitive."

---

## If They Ask "What Would Make You Say Yes?"

Have a ready answer. Don't stumble on this.

**Script:**

"Base at CHF 170,000 minimum, a token allocation that puts me in the senior engineer tier of the team grant structure with standard four-year vesting and one-year cliff, and confirmation of the hybrid setup in Geneva. If those three things are in place, I'm ready to move forward."

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

- Token allocation with no vesting schedule or very short cliff (under 6 months)
- Salary offered in crypto only, with no stable CHF base
- Equity described vaguely as "significant" without numbers
- Pressure to decide within 24-48 hours before you've seen full written offer
- No mention of standard Swiss employment benefits (AVS, 2nd pillar)

**If you see these:** slow down, ask for everything in writing before committing to anything verbally.
