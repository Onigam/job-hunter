# Negotiation Script: Head of Software Engineering, Adaptyv (Lausanne)

## Market context
Adaptyv is a Lausanne-based biotech/AI startup building automated lab infrastructure, likely post-seed or Series A given team size and hiring pace. Lausanne sits in the same comp band as Geneva and Zurich for senior tech talent, EPFL proximity pulls in competitive local benchmarks, but biotech startups often pay a bit under pure software startups in cash while offering more equity to compensate for the earlier-stage risk.

For a Director/VP-level, player-coach engineering lead in Switzerland at a Series A-ish startup, a reasonable range:
- **Base salary**: CHF 180,000 to 230,000
- **Bonus**: 0 to 15% target, often modest or absent this early
- **Equity**: 0.5% to 2.0%, wider range than a pure IC hire given the scope of the role (roadmap ownership, team building)
- **Benefits**: standard Swiss LPP/BVG pension contribution, relocation support if applicable (not needed here given Geneva-Lausanne proximity)

Geneva-to-Lausanne commute is roughly 45 minutes by train, so this isn't a relocation negotiation, it's a comp negotiation. Don't let that get conflated.

## Opening anchor
When asked for expectations, anchor high but justified:

"For a role that's owning the engineering roadmap and building the team from a small base, I'd expect total comp in the range of CHF 210,000 to 240,000 base, plus equity reflecting the scope, somewhere around 1 to 1.5%. I'm flexible on the base-versus-equity split depending on where you are on runway, but I want the equity number to reflect that this is a leadership hire, not an IC seat."

Justify with: 17+ years of experience, a founding-engineer track record (Alaya, built to acquisition by a $1B+ company), and direct relevance to the player-coach shape of this role. Don't lead with the acquisition story as a flex, use it as the reason the range is defensible.

## If they come in low
If the initial offer lands meaningfully below CHF 180,000 base or under 0.5% equity:

"I hear where you're starting. Given the scope here, full roadmap ownership plus hands-on shipping plus building the team, I was expecting this to land closer to what I mentioned. Is the gap coming from stage/runway constraints, or is there room to move on base if the equity number goes up instead?"

This does two things: it signals you're not walking without asking why, and it forces them to tell you whether the constraint is cash or philosophy. If it's runway, pivot to equity. If it's philosophy (they cap all offers at a fixed band), ask about the review timeline for the first comp adjustment, six months is reasonable to request in writing.

## Equity negotiation
Biotech/hardware-software hybrids like Adaptyv often underweight equity relative to pure SaaS startups because burn is higher and dilution risk from future lab infrastructure funding rounds is real. Ask directly:

- Vesting schedule: push for standard 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff, nothing longer.
- Strike price and 409A-equivalent valuation (Swiss equivalent will likely be a shareholder agreement, not options in the US sense, ask whether this is real equity or a phantom/RSU-style structure, the mechanics differ a lot for tax purposes in Switzerland).
- Anti-dilution: ask what protection exists for early hires through future funding rounds, especially since a Head of Engineering equity grant this early should carry some weight commensurate with the risk being taken on.
- If the number they offer feels low, tie it back to scope: "For a hire who's setting technical direction across the full stack, including lab-hardware integration, I'd expect equity closer to what a co-founder-adjacent hire gets, not a senior IC grant."

## Geographic discount pushback
If they try to anchor low on the assumption that "Switzerland is Switzerland" without accounting for Geneva/Lausanne cost of living specifically:

"Geneva and Lausanne run among the highest cost-of-living bands in Switzerland, comparable to Zurich. I'd want the base to reflect that rather than a national average."

Have a concrete number ready: Geneva/Lausanne rent and cost of living typically run 15 to 20% above smaller Swiss cities, so if they benchmark off a national figure, this is the correction to make.

## Competing offer angle (use only if real, or framed honestly as market activity)
"I'm currently in later-stage conversations with a couple of other Geneva-based platform and AI infrastructure teams, so I'd want to move on a timeline that respects that. If Adaptyv is the strongest fit, which right now it is given the agentic-science angle, I'd rather find a number that works than let a slower process cost you the hire."

Don't invent a specific competing number, vague market activity is honest and still creates urgency without a lie that can unravel in later conversations.

## Close
Always close a negotiation round with a concrete next step, not a vague "let me think about it":

"Let's set a date to revisit, does end of next week work for you to come back with a revised number?"
