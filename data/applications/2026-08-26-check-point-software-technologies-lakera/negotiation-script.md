# Negotiation Script: Head of Engineering, AI Security (Check Point / Lakera, Zurich)

## Market context

Check Point is a publicly traded, profitable cybersecurity company (NASDAQ: CHKP), not a cash-strapped startup, and this role sits inside a business unit built around an acquisition (Lakera), which usually means a real budget and an established comp band rather than a scrappy pre-revenue package. Zurich is one of the most expensive tech labor markets in Europe, on par with or above Geneva for senior engineering leadership. For a "Head of Engineering" title managing managers at a company this size, expect a base comfortably in the CHF 220,000 to 280,000 range, with total comp (base + bonus + equity/RSUs) landing somewhere between CHF 320,000 and 420,000 depending on level and how the acquisition equity was structured. Public company RSUs are worth more in a negotiation than early-stage options since they have a known, liquid value, so lean on that when comparing offers.

Relevant local reference points:
- Zurich cost of living is roughly 10 to 15% higher than Geneva, and materially higher than most EU tech hubs, so a Geneva-market anchor undersells this role.
- Swiss senior engineering leadership (VP/Head of Eng level) at well-funded tech companies typically clears CHF 250,000+ base before bonus.
- Security and AI are both hot skill premiums right now; a role combining both, with people leadership on top, sits at the upper end of the engineering leadership band, not the middle.

## Opening anchor

When asked for a number first, don't answer with a single figure. Say something like: "Based on what I know of Zurich comp for engineering leadership roles combining AI and security, and factoring in that this is a public company with an established comp structure, I'd expect total comp in the CHF 380,000 to 420,000 range, with base around CHF 250,000 to 270,000. I'm open to adjusting once I understand the full structure, including equity." This anchors high while signaling flexibility tied to structure, not desperation.

## If they come in low

If the offer lands meaningfully below the anchor, don't reject on the spot. Ask what's driving the number: "Can you walk me through how this offer was built, base versus bonus versus equity? I want to understand the full picture before reacting to any single piece." Then counter with specifics: "Given the scope here, managing managers and setting architectural direction for both Red Teaming and Agentic Security, I was expecting this to land closer to [X]. What flexibility exists, particularly on the equity or sign-on side, to close that gap?" Anchor the counter to scope and market data, not to personal need.

## Equity notes

Since this is post-acquisition, ask directly whether compensation includes Check Point RSUs, legacy Lakera equity that converted in the deal, or both. Key questions to ask before accepting: vesting schedule (standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff, confirm this hasn't been altered for the acquired unit), whether there's a refresh grant cadence, and whether any Lakera-origin equity had acceleration clauses that carried over. Public company RSUs are simpler to value than startup options, ask for the exact grant size in shares and the stock's recent price range so you can compute real dollar value rather than negotiating on share count alone.

## Geographic pushback

If there's any suggestion of a discount because Zurich is "just satellite" to a larger R&D org, push back with the fact stated plainly in the job posting: Zurich is described as the company's global center for AI security R&D, not a regional outpost. That framing supports a headquarters-level comp band, not a satellite-office one.

## Competing offer leverage

Even without a live competing offer, it's reasonable to say: "I'm currently in later-stage conversations with other companies in the AI infrastructure and security space, so timeline and a competitive structure both matter to me in deciding where to focus." This is honest framing if true of the broader search, and creates natural urgency without fabricating a specific offer or number.

## Closing

End negotiation conversations by restating enthusiasm for the scope of the role, not the money: "The role itself is what I'm optimizing for here, I want to make sure the offer reflects the level of ownership we've discussed." This keeps the conversation collaborative rather than adversarial while still holding the line on numbers.
