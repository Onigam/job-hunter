# Negotiation Script: Co-Founder & CTO, Omnia

## Context to keep in mind

This is a true founding-stage Swiss startup (omniacare.ch), pre-team beyond an existing co-founder handling business and operations. The role is Co-Founder & CTO, not "employee number one," which changes the negotiation entirely: the conversation isn't really about a salary band, it's about a co-founder split, and it should be treated more like a partnership negotiation than a comp negotiation. The postings for this role have been reposted repeatedly on join.com over months (multiple listing IDs, several now expired), which usually means either the search has been open a long time or the role keeps getting relisted without landing someone, both worth probing directly rather than assuming. The listings also mention a preference for an ETH/EPFL background; that's not something to over-index on in the conversation, but it's worth being ready for a moment where that gets raised, and having a direct answer ready: a founder-built, unicorn-acquired platform is a stronger signal than a degree line for this kind of seat.

## Switzerland market anchors (for framing, not for a pre-revenue seed)

- Senior Staff / Principal Engineer, Geneva or Zurich, established company: CHF 170k to 220k base, often plus bonus.
- CTO, funded Swiss scale-up (Series A or later): CHF 160k to 210k base plus meaningful equity.
- CTO, true pre-seed or seed-stage Swiss startup: often CHF 0 to 80k in real cash for the first 12 to 18 months, sometimes structured as a minimal survival salary, with the real value sitting in founder equity.
- Founder-level equity for a technical co-founder joining after the company concept exists, but before a product is live: typically 15 to 35%, depending on how much has already been built, how the existing co-founder's equity is structured, and how much personal capital either side has put in.

These numbers exist to frame the conversation, not to anchor a cash ask. At this stage the real negotiation is over the equity split and the vesting structure, not a salary number.

## Opening move: get the real stage first

Before naming any numbers, the first questions should establish what actually exists: is there a product, any revenue, any funding, any existing cap table entries beyond the founding team. Something close to:

"Before we talk structure, I want to understand exactly where things stand. Is there a product in market today, any revenue or pilot customers, and has any funding closed or been committed. And on the team side, has any equity already been allocated, to Alan or anyone else, that I'd be joining around rather than negotiating from zero."

The answers to this change everything downstream, an idea-stage company with no funding is a very different negotiation than one with a term sheet in hand.

## Equity split framing

"For a Co-Founder and CTO role where I'm building the entire technical platform from scratch, including the AI layer that's central to the product, I'd expect an equity stake in the range of 20 to 30%, vesting over four years with a one-year cliff. Where exactly that lands depends on what's already been built and what capital either of us is putting in day one. I'd also want acceleration terms if the company gets acquired or if my role changes materially after a priced round."

If they counter with a fixed low number without explaining the basis for it, ask directly what it's based on, prior contribution, capital invested, or just an anchor they picked, before responding to the number itself.

## If cash compensation is minimal or zero

"I understand cash is going to be tight or nonexistent until there's funding or revenue, and I'm fine building alongside that reality for a defined period. What I'd want in return is clarity in writing: a target runway before either of us takes a real salary, what happens if that runway is exceeded, and what the plan is for a first funding round so this isn't open-ended. I'd also want my equity vesting to start from day one of contribution, not from some later legal incorporation date, since the actual building work starts now."

## Equity mechanics to lock down

- Get the fully diluted percentage in writing, not a share count, and ask for the current cap table.
- Confirm founder vesting terms explicitly: 4-year vest, 1-year cliff is standard, and push for acceleration, at minimum double-trigger, on acquisition or on the other co-founder's departure.
- Ask what happens to unvested equity, and to the company's IP, if either founder leaves before a first raise. This should be in a founder agreement, not left informal.
- Ask directly what Alan's current equity position is and how any future hires or advisors will be allocated shares, so the eventual split isn't a surprise later.
- If a first funding round is expected soon, ask about anti-dilution protection for the founding team's shares in that round.

## Geographic discount pushback

If cash gets framed as low because Geneva has other advantages, or because a founding role "shouldn't be about the money yet":

"I'm not pushing on cash right now, I understand the stage. But I want to be clear that Geneva is genuinely one of the more expensive cities in Europe, so if cash comp becomes relevant later, on a runway plan or after a raise, I wouldn't expect a below-market number just because of where I'm based. For now the conversation that matters most is the equity split and the vesting terms, since that's what actually reflects the risk I'm taking joining pre-product."

## Competing offer script

Use only if there is a genuine competing process. Never fabricate a specific number or company name.

"I want to be straightforward that I'm also talking to another company about a funded platform role with real cash compensation. I'd rather build something as a co-founder if the equity and the working relationship make sense, so if there's flexibility on the split or on getting a founder agreement in place quickly, that's what would tip this in Omnia's direction for me."

## One thing to raise before signing anything

Ask how decisions get made between the two of you day to day, who has final say on product versus technical calls, and what the plan is if the two of you disagree on direction. This isn't a comp question, but for a two-person founding team it matters more than the equity percentage does.
