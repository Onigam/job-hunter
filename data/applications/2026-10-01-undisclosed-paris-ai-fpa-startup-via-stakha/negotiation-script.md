# Negotiation Script: First Founding Engineer, Paris AI FP&A startup (via Stakha)

## Context that shapes the numbers

This is a Paris CDI at a startup that raised a $3M pre-seed. It's founded by two former scale-up operators, one of them a repeat founder. The product is live with around ten paying French tech scale-ups. The posting is explicit: salary **up to EUR 100k**, plus a "significant" equity stake, and the role is described as first engineering hire and technical co-founder alongside the CTO. The office is in Paris, in-office by default with some flexibility, and they want someone ASAP.

What that means in practice:

1. **Cash is close to capped.** A $3M pre-seed can't pay Geneva salaries. Pushing base far above EUR 100k will mostly burn goodwill. The real negotiation is about **equity size and terms**, and whether the "co-founder" label comes with co-founder economics.
2. **Swiss rates are the reference point, not the target.** They show what Magino gives up in cash by taking this seat, and that's the argument for more equity.
3. **Location is a real cost.** Geneva to Paris is about three hours by TGV. Either relocation or a commuting pattern costs money and has tax and social security consequences that need sorting before signing.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

These are approximate ranges from public salary surveys and recruiter data, not hard figures. Re-check on levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Welcome to the Jungle, Michael Page and Robert Walters guides (CH and FR) before talking numbers.

| Benchmark | Base salary (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Senior Staff Engineer, Geneva (current reference point) | CHF 200k to 250k |
| Founding / first engineer, funded Swiss startup (Zurich, Geneva, Lausanne) | CHF 140k to 190k + equity |
| Staff Engineer, Zurich big tech | CHF 230k to 300k total comp |
| Founding engineer, Paris pre-seed / seed | EUR 75k to 105k + equity |
| Senior / Staff engineer, Paris scale-up (Series B+) | EUR 90k to 130k |
| CTO, Paris seed-stage startup | EUR 90k to 130k + 5% to 15% equity |

**Equity norms (estimates):** at pre-seed, a first engineer typically gets somewhere around 0.5% to 2%. A first engineer formally titled "technical co-founder" next to an existing CTO can reasonably ask for the top of that range or above, roughly 2% to 4%, depending on how much is already allocated to the founders and the pool.

**My target:** EUR 100k base, 2.5% to 3.5% equity on a fully diluted basis, with founder-style terms.
**Opening anchor:** EUR 105k base and 4% fully diluted.
**Walk-away floor:** EUR 90k base with at least 1.5% and decent exercise terms, plus a clear plan for location. Below that, a Swiss role pays far more for similar risk.

## Opening anchor script

Let them name structure first if possible. If asked directly for expectations:

"The posting says up to 100k, and I'm not going to pretend a pre-seed company can match what I'd earn in Geneva. For reference, Senior Staff roles there pay around CHF 200k to 250k, so I'm taking a real cash cut to do this. What makes it worth it is the equity and the co-founder part of the title. I'd be looking at the top of your salary band, around 105k, and something like 4% on a fully diluted basis. I'm flexible on how those two balance, but I want to talk about them together. How have you been thinking about the equity side?"

Then stop talking.

## If they come in low

**Script A: base is fine, equity is small (say 0.5% to 1%).**
"Thanks for being clear. That number reads like a strong senior hire, and that's fair if the role is senior hire. But the posting says technical co-founder, and the job includes setting up the agent infrastructure, the trust layer, and hiring the team. That's co-founder scope. I'd need the equity closer to 3% for that to line up with the title and with the cash I'm giving up. If the cap table can't stretch that far today, I'd want a written top-up at the seed round."

**Script B: base below 100k.**
"I understand runway matters, and I'd rather you keep money in the bank than overpay me. If base has to sit at 90k, I'd want that difference back in equity, plus a review to the top of the band once you close the seed round or hit an agreed revenue milestone. Can we put the trigger in the offer letter?"

**Script C: they say everything is standard and fixed.**
"Fair enough. Then let's focus on the terms that cost you little but matter a lot to me: the exercise window after leaving, acceleration on a change of control, and the strike price. If those are good, I can be more flexible on the percentage."

## Geographic discount pushback

This one runs the other way from usual: the role is in Paris and paid in EUR, so arguing for Geneva cash won't work. Use Geneva as the justification for equity and for location support instead.

"I'm not asking you to pay Geneva rates. I'm saying the gap between this salary and my market in Switzerland is roughly CHF 100k a year, and equity is where that gap should go. On location, I'd like to be in the office for a solid part of every week from the start. Would you cover a relocation package, or travel costs for a few months while I move?"

**Practical points to settle before signing (check with a tax adviser, rules are specific):**
- If Magino stays resident in Geneva and works in Paris, the France and Switzerland tax treaty and the cross-border worker rules (including the specific Geneva arrangements) decide where income is taxed and which country's social security applies. Commuting from Geneva is not automatically "frontalier" status and can create dual reporting. Get the answer in writing before the start date.
- If relocating to Paris, ask for a relocation allowance (EUR 5k to 10k is a reasonable ask at a funded startup), a few weeks of temporary housing, and check the French expatriate tax regime (impatriés, article 155 B CGI), which may exempt part of the income for people moving to France. Verify eligibility.
- Swiss pension (2nd pillar / LPP) assets stay put or move to a vested benefits account if leaving Switzerland; plan for it.

## Equity: what to ask, point by point

French startups usually grant **BSPCE** (stock warrants for employees). The details decide whether they're worth anything.

1. **Percentage, fully diluted.** Always ask for the percentage on a fully diluted basis and the total share count, not just a number of BSPCE.
2. **Strike price.** BSPCE strike price is usually set at the price of the last round. Ask what it is and whether a new round is coming soon (a grant before the round is cheaper).
3. **Vesting and cliff.** Standard is four years with a one-year cliff. Ask for monthly vesting after the cliff, and ask whether some vesting credit is possible given the co-founder title.
4. **Exercise window after leaving.** This is often the most important term. Many plans give only 90 days to exercise after departure, which forces you to pay cash and possibly tax early. Ask for an extended window, ideally several years for good leavers.
5. **Acceleration.** Ask for double-trigger acceleration (change of control plus termination without cause or a significant role change). Single-trigger is rare for employees, but double-trigger is a reasonable ask for a co-founder-level hire.
6. **Good leaver / bad leaver.** Read the definitions carefully. Make sure a good leaver keeps all vested BSPCE.
7. **Anti-dilution.** Employees almost never get anti-dilution protection, and asking for it can signal inexperience. Ask instead for a **refresh grant or top-up at the seed round** so the stake isn't diluted far below the original promise, and for pro-rata information rights.
8. **Tax treatment.** BSPCE gains benefit from favourable French tax treatment if conditions are met, including the holder being employed by the company for a minimum period. If Magino stays tax resident in Switzerland, the treatment is different; check before signing.
9. **Founder status.** If they really mean "co-founder", ask whether that comes with ordinary shares (with founder vesting) rather than BSPCE, and a seat in the founders' shareholder agreement. Whatever the answer, it tells you how they actually see the role.

**Script:** "The equity is the main reason I'd take this seat, so I'd like to understand it properly. Could you walk me through the percentage on a fully diluted basis, the strike price, the vesting schedule, and what happens to vested BSPCE if I leave? I'd also like to understand what 'technical co-founder' means on the cap table."

## Competing offer template (use only if real)

Do not invent a competing offer. If another process is genuinely moving:

"I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company, a Staff role with a base well above this one. Yours is the one I'd rather take, because of the product and the founding seat. I'd like to make a decision within the next two weeks. Is there room on the equity side so I can say yes without looking back?"

If there's no other offer, use the Swiss market gap and the time pressure (they want someone ASAP) instead:

"You're hiring for an ASAP start and I can move quickly. If we can agree on the equity and the location setup this week, I'm ready to commit."

## Final checklist before signing

- Base, equity percentage (fully diluted) and strike price written in the offer letter
- BSPCE plan rules received and read, including leaver and exercise terms
- Top-up or refresh at the next round written down, not verbal
- Location agreed: relocation package or travel arrangement, and remote flexibility clarified
- Tax and social security position confirmed for the chosen setup
- Start date that leaves time to give notice and organise the move
