# Negotiation Script: Smart Working Solutions, Founding Engineer (Full-Stack Generalist)

## Context to anchor on

- Sole in-house engineer on a live AI-native PSA (professional services automation) platform, remote, working directly with technical and non-technical founders.
- Company language ("once funding closes," "help decide the first supporting hire") suggests pre-seed or seed stage: revenue exists from early agency/consultancy customers, but the team is not funded for a full engineering org yet.
- This is a genuine solo-ownership role, not a padded title. That justifies both a senior base and real equity, since there is no team to share the delivery risk with.
- Magino is Geneva-based, remote-first, 17+ years experience, founding engineer at Alaya through to its acquisition by Benevity. That track record is the strongest card in this negotiation, not a generic "market rate" appeal.

## Market anchors

- US-market founding engineer benchmarks for 2026: base salary roughly $180k-$340k, with pre-seed roles skewing toward $160k-$220k base and 0.75%-2.5% equity, and seed/Series A roles toward $220k-$340k base and 0.25%-1.25% equity. Given this company appears pre-seed to early seed, expect the offer to land in the lower base band and the higher equity band.
- Swiss market anchor for a Senior Staff / founding-level engineer in Geneva: CHF 150k-190k base in a stable local company, but this is a US-style remote founding role, so the US benchmark is the more relevant reference point, not the Swiss employment-market rate. Use the Swiss figure only to push back if the company tries to apply a "cost of living in Pakistan/remote-market" discount instead of a "founding engineer, sole owner" framing.
- Total comp target: base salary at the top of whatever stage-appropriate band they cite, plus equity that reflects being the only engineer, not a first hire into an existing team.

## Opening anchor (when they ask for a number first)

"I'd rather talk about the shape of the offer than throw out a single number first. Given this is a sole-ownership role, no team to share the production and delivery risk with, and the ISO 27001/SOC 2 timeline is already on the clock, I'm thinking in the range of [base X], with equity that reflects being the founding technical hire rather than employee number five. I'm flexible on the split between the two if the reasoning behind it makes sense."

If pressed for a hard number: anchor near the top of the seed-stage band researched above, then let them react. Overshooting slightly and coming down is easier than starting low and trying to climb.

## If they come in low on base

"I hear that the budget is tight before the next round closes. I'd rather solve that with equity or a compensation review tied to funding milestones than by taking a base that's below what a sole founding engineer with this scope should be paid. Would you be open to a base closer to [target], plus a written commitment to revisit comp once the round closes?"

Fallback structure if they can't move base at all: ask for a signing bonus, an earlier equity vesting cliff, or a defined comp bump tied to a named funding or revenue milestone rather than a vague "we'll revisit it."

## Equity negotiation

- Ask for the fully diluted percentage, not just a share count. A share count means nothing without knowing the total pool.
- Standard vesting is four years with a one-year cliff. For a founding engineer joining pre-institutional-round, it's reasonable to ask for a shorter cliff (six to nine months) or partial acceleration on a change of control, given the outsized early risk of being the only engineer.
- Ask directly whether there's an existing option pool sized for future hires, and whether this grant is protected from being diluted disproportionately in the next round. If they can't answer that clearly, treat it as a flag to dig into during diligence, not necessarily a dealbreaker.
- Given the role explicitly mentions shaping the first supporting engineering hire once funding closes, ask whether that expands scope (and title/comp) once it happens. Get that in writing as a follow-up conversation, not a verbal promise.

## Geographic discount pushback

If they try to apply a lower rate because Magino is based in Geneva rather than the US:

"I get that geography sometimes factors into remote comp bands, but this role isn't scoped like a regional hire, it's the sole engineering owner of the whole platform. The scope and risk profile are the same regardless of where I'm sitting, and Geneva isn't a low-cost market either. I'd rather price this on the role than on my address."

## Competing offer as a bargaining chip (use only if genuinely applicable)

"I'm currently weighing this against another opportunity at a similar stage, and comp aside, what's pulling me toward this one is the platform itself and the directness of working with the founders. If we can land on [target] plus the equity terms above, I'm ready to commit and stop the other conversation."

Only use this if there is an actual competing process in motion. An empty bluff is easy to call and costs credibility for the rest of the negotiation.

## Closing line once terms are close

"That works for me. Can you send the written offer with the base, equity percentage, vesting schedule, and the funding-milestone comp review in writing so I can sign this week?"
