# Negotiation Script: Software Engineer, Agentic Team (Lakera / Check Point, Zurich)

## Market context

This is an IC engineering role, not a leadership title, so the anchor should sit at senior IC level rather than management bands, but the company context still matters. Lakera is now a Check Point business unit (NASDAQ: CHKP), so this isn't a cash-strapped early-stage startup negotiating against a thin runway, it's a funded unit inside a profitable public company. Zurich is one of the most expensive tech labor markets in Europe, running 10 to 15% above Geneva. For a senior individual contributor with 15+ years of experience joining at this level, expect base in the CHF 150,000 to 190,000 range, with total comp (base plus bonus plus any RSU component) landing between CHF 190,000 and 240,000 depending on how much weight the company puts on formal level versus demonstrated experience.

The posting itself reads more junior in title than Magino's background, worth naming directly in conversation rather than pretending otherwise. The negotiation lever isn't "match my last title," it's "price the actual scope of the work," since production ownership across inference pipelines, red-teaming, and data infrastructure is broad regardless of what the job title says.

## Opening anchor

When asked for a number first, avoid a single figure. Something like: "Given the scope described here, owning pieces across the inference pipeline, red-teaming, and data infra, and factoring in Zurich cost of living, I'd expect base in the CHF 170,000 to 190,000 range, with total comp somewhere around CHF 210,000 depending on the equity or bonus structure. I'm open to discussing how that breaks down once I understand the full package." This anchors near the top of the realistic senior-IC band without opening with a management-level number that would read as out of touch with the actual title.

## If they come in low

Don't reject on the spot if the number lands under the anchor. Ask first: "Can you walk me through how the offer was built, base versus bonus versus equity?" Then counter on scope, not tenure: "Given the range of what this role touches, inference performance work, red-teaming at scale, and data infrastructure, I was expecting this closer to [X]. Is there room on base or on a sign-on to close that gap?" If they push back on level or title, respond with the work itself: "I'm comfortable with the individual-contributor scope, the ask is that the comp reflects the depth of experience doing that work, not just years at this specific title."

## Equity notes

Confirm directly whether comp includes Check Point RSUs, converted Lakera equity, or both, since post-acquisition structures often blend the two. Ask for vesting schedule (standard is four years with a one-year cliff, confirm the acquired unit didn't get altered terms), refresh grant cadence, and whether any legacy Lakera equity carried acceleration clauses into the deal. Public company RSUs have a known, liquid value, so ask for exact share count and recent price range rather than negotiating on share count alone.

## Geographic pushback

If there's any framing that this role is priced against a broader, cheaper labor pool because it's "just an IC hire," push back with the fact that Zurich is positioned as the company's AI security R&D center, not a satellite office doing commodity work. That framing supports Zurich-market pricing, not a discount.

## Competing offer leverage

Even without a live offer in hand, it's fair to say: "I'm in later-stage conversations with a couple of other companies in the AI infrastructure and security space, so timeline matters to me alongside the number." Use this only if genuinely true of the broader search at the time, don't invent a specific company or figure.

## Closing

Close on the work, not the money: "The scope here, especially the agent exposure modeling piece, is what drew me in. I want the offer to reflect that this is senior-level work, whatever the title says." Keeps the conversation collaborative while still holding the line on the number.
