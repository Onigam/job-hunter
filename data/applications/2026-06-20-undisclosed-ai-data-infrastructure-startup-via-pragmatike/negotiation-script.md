# Negotiation Script — Founding Full Stack AI Engineer (Undisclosed AI Data Infrastructure Startup)

---

## Market Context

**Role type:** Founding Full Stack AI Engineer at an early-stage startup (~11 employees), fully remote EMEA.

**Switzerland market anchors (2025-2026, Staff-level full stack engineer):**
- Geneva senior full stack (5-8 years): CHF 130K-155K
- Staff/Principal Engineer (10+ years): CHF 155K-190K
- Founding engineer at Series A (with equity): CHF 140K-175K base + 0.3-0.8% equity
- Magino's profile (17 years, co-founder, AI-augmented): CHF 175K-200K+ is defensible

**Cost of living leverage:** Geneva is among the five most expensive cities in Europe. Any remote-friendly role that wants someone based in Switzerland should reflect that; a discount for remote would need to come with a clear rationale.

**Key variable:** Compensation was not disclosed. The company is early-stage and likely balancing cash constraints with equity upside. The right opening is high on base and flexible on structure, not the reverse.

---

## Opening Ask

**Anchor:** CHF 185,000 base + meaningful equity stake.

**Script (first comp conversation):**

"I haven't seen comp details in the posting, so let me share where I'm anchored. For a founding engineer role at this stage, I'm looking at CHF 185K base. That reflects my level, 17 years of experience including a founding-to-acquisition cycle, and Geneva cost of living as a reference point even for a remote role. I'm open to a conversation about structure if equity is part of the package."

**Why this works:** Anchoring high with a clear justification prevents the recruiter from anchoring you first. The "open to structure" sentence signals flexibility without conceding anything yet.

---

## If They Come In Low (Below CHF 150K)

**Script:**

"I appreciate the transparency. CHF [their number] is below where I'm benchmarking for this level, and I want to make sure we're not misaligned early. The 3+ years requirement in the posting suggests you're looking for someone with less experience than I bring; I bring 17 years, including building a platform from zero through a $1B+ acquisition. If there's flexibility on the range, I'd like to explore it. If the band is fixed, I'd want to understand the equity package before making a decision."

**Key tactic:** Don't reject it, reframe it. Force the conversation toward total comp (base + equity + potential upside), not just the base number.

---

## Equity Negotiation (Early-Stage Startup)

**At ~11 employees, reasonable founder equity for a founding engineer:**
- 0.3% to 0.7% on a 4-year vest with 1-year cliff is standard for this stage
- 0.5% is a fair midpoint to ask for
- If they offer below 0.2%, push back

**Script (when equity comes up):**

"For a founding engineer at this stage, I'd expect something in the 0.4-0.6% range on a standard four-year vest with a one-year cliff. That reflects the risk-adjusted contribution of joining this early. What does the current option pool look like, and what stage was the last funding round?"

**Follow-up questions to ask:**
- What's the current valuation cap (if SAFE) or post-money valuation?
- Is it ISO or NSO options? (ISOs preferred for US-adjacent structures)
- What's the strike price relative to the 409A?
- Is there any acceleration clause (double-trigger preferred)?
- What's the exercise window post-termination? (Standard 90 days is punishing; push for 2-5 years)

**If equity details are vague:**

"I'd want to see the cap table and option grant documentation before signing. That's not unusual for this stage, but I won't be able to evaluate total comp without it."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

Some startups offer location-adjusted pay, reducing comp for Swiss candidates since the startup is remote-first.

**Script:**

"I understand remote companies sometimes tier comp by location. My view is that the value I'm delivering doesn't change based on my timezone, and Geneva cost of living is a real factor for me. I'd rather discuss a role where the comp reflects the work than accept a discount that creates resentment down the line. I'm flexible on other dimensions, but not on a significant geographic haircut."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (real or in final stages):

"I'm in final stages with another opportunity in a similar space. It's not identical, but it's relevant. I'd prefer to join here based on what this role is, not just the number, but I'd need to know where you land before I can make a call. Can you get me a formal offer this week?"

If no competing offer yet, don't bluff. Use it only when true.

---

## Timeline and Closing

**If they ask when you can start:**
"I can move quickly. Two to four weeks from a signed offer is realistic."

**If they go quiet after the offer:**
"I want to make a decision with full information. Can you give me until [specific date, 5-7 business days out] to review the equity documentation and come back to you with any final questions?"

**Red flags that should slow you down:**
- No written equity grant before signing
- Vesting cliff longer than 12 months
- Inability to answer basic cap table questions
- Comp offer presented as "take it or leave it" with no room for discussion
