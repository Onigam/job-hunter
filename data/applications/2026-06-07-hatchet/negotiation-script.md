# Negotiation Script — Hatchet Full-Stack Engineer

## Situation Summary

- Company: Hatchet, YC W24, ~7 people, founded 2023, New York-based
- Product: orchestration platform for background tasks, workflows, AI agents (TypeScript, Python, Go SDKs)
- Role type: Full-Stack Engineer (broad scope, early-stage team)
- Traction: 374M tasks/day on Hatchet Cloud; product is real and shipping
- Location: Magino is in Geneva, Switzerland. Switzerland is unlikely to be in the default remote eligibility list for a US-based YC seed company. Resolve this before investing time on both sides.

---

## Step 0: Clarify the Location Question First

Raise this on the first call, not at offer stage.

Script:

"Quick practical question before we go further: I'm based in Geneva, Switzerland. Is that workable on your end, either as a contractor or through a payroll service like Deel or Remote.com? I want to flag it early so we don't get to the end of a process and hit a hard stop."

Two likely responses:
- "We can hire contractors internationally" — negotiate as a contractor, which changes the numbers significantly (see below).
- "We're only set up for a specific list of countries" — either pass, or ask if there's a path. Most YC seed companies at this stage have worked around it for the right person.

---

## Contractor vs. Employee

If Hatchet hires you as a contractor (the most likely structure for a Geneva-based hire):

- No employer-side Swiss social contributions, meaning you bear ~20% in AVS/LPP as a self-employed person
- No paid leave by default; negotiate this explicitly or price it into the rate
- Standard multiplier: employee equivalent × 1.25–1.35 to reach net parity

So if the role is worth CHF 180K as a Geneva employee, the equivalent contractor rate should land around CHF 225K–245K gross, or roughly $250K–$270K at current exchange rates (~0.90 CHF/USD).

## Expected Range — Full-Stack Engineer at a YC Seed Startup

No public posting is available at time of writing, so use these Geneva market anchors:

- Senior Full-Stack at a mid-size company in Geneva: CHF 130K–160K
- Senior/Staff at a well-funded scale-up or remote-first startup: CHF 160K–200K
- Early-stage startup with meaningful equity: CHF 120K–150K base + equity grant that compensates for the base discount

For a YC W24 company raising a seed round, the likely base range is $130K–$180K USD. At 0.90 CHF/USD that is roughly CHF 117K–162K. The equity component needs to close the gap between that and Geneva market rates.

---

## Equity Analysis — Full-Stack Engineer at Seed Stage

A Full-Stack Engineer hired around employee #8–10 at a seed-stage YC company typically receives 0.25%–0.75% equity. Context:

- YC invests $500K at a $5M post-money cap. Current valuation likely $5M–$20M.
- At a $10M current valuation, 0.5% = $50K today (illiquid).
- At a $100M Series A (achievable if growth continues), 0.5% pre-dilution = $500K, post-dilution roughly 0.3%–0.35% = $300K–$350K.
- At a $500M exit, diluted stake (estimate 0.2%–0.3%) = $1M–$1.5M.

Questions to ask before accepting equity terms:

1. What is the current valuation or last round price per share?
2. What is the total option pool size? What percentage of the company does it represent?
3. Standard 4-year vest with 1-year cliff, confirmed?
4. Is there an acceleration clause (double-trigger) on acquisition?
5. What is the exercise window if I leave? (90 days is standard but bad for seed-stage equity; push for 5 years minimum.)
6. What is the strike price on the options?

Double-trigger acceleration and an extended exercise window are both reasonable asks at this stage. Most founders will agree if you ask directly.

## Opening the Negotiation

### If they come in at the low end ($130K–$140K):

"I appreciate the offer. The $130K base is below where I was targeting given the seniority and the scope of the role. My research on Geneva-market comps for a senior full-stack engineer with this background puts the range at $160K–$190K. Is there room to move on base, or should we look at the total package together?"

Then stop talking. Don't fill the silence.

### If they come in mid-range ($150K–$165K):

"That's closer to where I was expecting. I want to be transparent about one piece of context: operating as a contractor out of Geneva means I absorb the employer-side social contributions myself, which is roughly 20% overhead. To get to effective parity with what $165K means as an employee, I'd need to be closer to $195K–$200K. Does that framing make sense, and is there flexibility to get there?"

### If they come in at the high end ($175K+):

"That's a solid number. Before I respond to the full package, I want to make sure I understand the equity terms. Can you walk me through the current valuation, the vesting schedule, and the exercise window? Those details are significant at this stage of the company."

---

## Counter-Offer Framework

If they push back on base, shift to total compensation:

"If the base is capped at $X, I'd want to revisit the equity component. A larger grant or a faster initial vest on the first tranche would help make the overall picture work. What's your flexibility there?"

Levers beyond base salary:
- Equity: push toward the higher end of the seed-stage range (0.5%–0.75%) given the full-stack founding scope
- Signing bonus to offset contractor setup costs in Switzerland (CHF 8K–12K is reasonable and cheap for a startup)
- Equipment budget ($2K–$3K for a remote engineer)
- Coworking or home office stipend (CHF 300–500/month for a Geneva-based remote contractor is standard)
- Vacation days explicit in the contract (25 days minimum, written not implied)
- Exercise window on options (ask for 5 years post-departure, not 90 days)

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have other offers in play:

"I want to be transparent: I'm in late-stage conversations with another company for a role at a comparable level, and their package is at [X]. Hatchet is genuinely where I want to focus, so I'd rather find a number that works for both of us than have it come down to a side-by-side comparison. What's the most you can do?"

If you don't have a competing offer, use market data instead:

"I've done research on Geneva-market and remote-startup comps for a full-stack engineer with this background and scope. The range I'm seeing is $170K–$200K for roles like this. I'm not trying to be difficult; I want to land somewhere we're both comfortable."

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

- Options quoted as a fixed share count with no disclosure of total shares outstanding (you can't calculate your percentage without that number)
- No acceleration clause of any kind
- 90-day exercise window with no willingness to extend
- Vague "standard vesting" language without written confirmation of 4-year / 1-year cliff
- Offer contingent on starting before terms are in writing

Any of these is worth slowing down for. Ask the question directly and in writing before signing.
