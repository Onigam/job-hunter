# Negotiation Script — PostHog / Technical Ex-Founder

---

## Market Context

**PostHog's stated range:** $270,750 to $296,400+, based on a San Francisco benchmark of $285,000, adjusted for location.

**What "adjusted for location" means in practice:** PostHog applies a geographic multiplier to their SF base. European candidates typically receive 80-95% of the SF rate depending on country. For Switzerland specifically, there's a strong argument against a deep discount.

**Geneva cost of living vs. San Francisco:**
- Geneva consistently ranks in the top 3 most expensive cities globally (Mercer, ECA International).
- A pre-tax salary of CHF 250,000 in Geneva leaves significantly less purchasing power than USD 285,000 in SF after Swiss taxes (federal + cantonal + social contributions).
- Switzerland's individual tax burden at this income level is real: effective marginal rates in Geneva canton can approach 40-45% on income above CHF 200k.
- There is no logical basis for a European discount in Geneva specifically.

**Switzerland market rate for this profile (ex-founder, staff+ engineering, product ownership):**
- CTO / VP Eng at Series A: CHF 200,000 to 280,000 base + equity
- Staff+ IC at product-led scale-up: CHF 180,000 to 240,000 base + equity
- Founding engineer / product engineer at funded startup: CHF 160,000 to 220,000 base + equity
- PostHog's $285k SF base converts to approximately CHF 255,000 at current exchange rates, which is at the top of the Swiss market and appropriate for this level.

## Negotiation Strategy

### Opening Anchor

Do not anchor low. If PostHog asks "what are your salary expectations?" before making an offer:

"I'm looking at the SF benchmark range, which I understand PostHog uses as its base. For Geneva specifically, I'd argue there's limited justification for a geographic discount. The cost of living here is comparable to San Francisco, and the Swiss tax burden at this income level is substantial. I'd expect to be at or near the $285,000 benchmark, ideally in CHF to eliminate currency risk."

If they push for a number: "CHF 260,000 to 280,000 base, plus meaningful equity participation."

### When They Make an Offer

If the offer lands at the top of the stated range (~$296k / CHF 265k): This is a reasonable outcome. The leverage point becomes equity.

If the offer is mid-range (~$280k / CHF 250k): Counter by noting that Geneva compensation expectations are at the top of their range, not the middle. Script:

"I appreciate the offer. Given that Geneva's cost-of-living is in the same tier as San Francisco, I'd expect to be at the top of the range rather than the midpoint. Would you be able to move to [specific number]? Alternatively, I'm happy to discuss if there's flexibility on the equity component."

If the offer is below $270k: This is a meaningful discount and worth pushing back directly:

"To be candid, that's below what I'd expect for a senior profile with a full founding-to-acquisition cycle. I've seen compensation at this seniority level in Switzerland range from CHF 220k to 280k base. I'd need to see the offer move to at least CHF 250,000 to be at a place where I'd feel the package reflects the scope of the role."

## Equity Negotiation

PostHog offers "significant equity packages" per their careers page. This is worth probing before the final offer.

**Questions to ask:**
- "What's the current strike price and the last 409A valuation?"
- "What percentage of fully diluted shares does this grant represent?"
- "What's the vesting schedule? Is there a cliff?"
- "Is there a provision for acceleration on acquisition?"
- "What's the most recent primary round price, and what's the implied dilution from that round?"

**What to look for:**
- Standard vesting: 4 years with 1-year cliff. Acceptable. Anything longer than 4 years total, flag it.
- Accelerated vesting on acquisition (single or double trigger): important given PostHog's stage and the possibility of a strategic exit.
- Early exercise option: valuable in a US structure for tax optimization (83b election). Ask if available.

**If the base is at the top of range but equity feels thin:**
"I'd take a lower base if the equity grant is materially larger. Can you share what a meaningful equity participation looks like for this role versus a senior IC package? I want to make sure I'm aligned with PostHog's long-term outcome."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

If PostHog applies a >15% discount vs. the SF benchmark:

"I understand the rationale for geographic adjustments in lower-cost markets. Geneva is not one of them. It's consistently ranked alongside Zurich and Singapore as one of the most expensive cities in the world. The Swiss franc is also a strong currency, which adds FX risk if the package is denominated in USD. I'd encourage you to reconsider the discount, or denominate the package in CHF at the SF-equivalent purchasing power."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer in hand (or are in late stages elsewhere):

"I want to be transparent: I have another conversation at [Company] at a comparable stage, with a base in the CHF 250-270k range. PostHog is my preference given the product surface area and the autonomy in the role, but I need the package to be close enough that I'm not leaving significant comp on the table."

If you don't have a competing offer, do not fabricate one. Instead:

"I've been selective in this search specifically because I want to find the right scope and team. PostHog is at the top of my list, but I'm also in conversation with a few other companies. I'd like to move quickly if we can get the package right."

---

## Timeline and Closing

PostHog's process includes a paid SuperDay worth $1,000. This is a positive signal of a company that values candidate time.

Once an offer is verbal, ask for it in writing and take 48-72 hours before responding. Use that window to:
1. Confirm the equity details in the written offer
2. Re-read the vesting schedule and any clawback provisions
3. Verify the currency and exchange rate mechanism

Do not accept verbally before seeing the written document.
