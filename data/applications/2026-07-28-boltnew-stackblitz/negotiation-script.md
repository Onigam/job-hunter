# Negotiation Script: Bolt.new (StackBlitz), Senior Applied AI Engineer

## Market anchor

This is a fully remote, US-anchored startup, not a Swiss employer, so the right comparison isn't the generic Geneva software engineer market, it's what venture-backed AI product companies pay senior applied AI/ML engineers globally. Bolt.new is past the "chatbot wrapper" stage, WebContainers is genuinely hard infrastructure, and the product already serves over a million monthly developers, which gives real negotiating weight: this isn't a pre-launch bet, it's a team that needs someone who can own agent reliability under load right now.

Generic Swiss "senior software engineer" numbers run CHF 130,000-160,000, but that band describes local product companies, not a remote AI engineering seat at a fast-growing, well-funded startup shipping agents to a large user base. With 17+ years of experience, a founding-engineer track record and an acquisition by a unicorn as a credibility marker, anchor above the Swiss-local band and closer to what US-headquartered AI startups pay senior ICs internationally:

- **Target range:** CHF 195,000-225,000 base equivalent (or the USD equivalent if paid in USD)
- **Floor (walk-away consideration point):** CHF 180,000 base equivalent
- **Stretch anchor (opening ask):** CHF 230,000 base equivalent

Ask early whether pay is denominated in USD or CHF and whether it's delivered through a Swiss entity, an Employer of Record, or as an independent contractor, since that changes both the real net number and who handles AVS and LPP contributions.

## Opening anchor

If asked for a number first, tie it to scope rather than stating a bare figure:

"Given the scope here, owning agent reliability end to end across planning, tool use and model routing for a product already serving over a million monthly developers, I'm looking at a base in the CHF 220,000-230,000 range, or the USD equivalent, plus whatever the standard equity grant looks like at this stage. That reflects 17-plus years of engineering experience, a founding background that included an acquisition, and hands-on depth in the exact Node.js layer this product runs on."

If pushed for a single number, give CHF 220,000, near the top of the target range rather than the midpoint. It's easier to come down from a high anchor than to climb back up from a low one.

## Counter-offer script if they come in low

If the offer lands below CHF 185,000 base equivalent:

"I appreciate the offer, but it reads closer to a mid-level AI engineer number than the ownership this role describes, someone accountable for agent planning, context compression and model routing in production. Based on what comparable senior applied AI roles at funded startups are paying, I was anchored closer to CHF 210,000-220,000. Is there room to move there, either on base or through a sign-on?"

If base is fixed but there's flexibility elsewhere:

"If base can't move right now, I'd want to look at a sign-on bonus or an accelerated equity refresh to close the gap. A one-time sign-on around CHF 15,000-20,000 would make the package work for me this year."

## Equity notes

Bolt.new is a venture-backed startup that's likely still years from an exit, so equity here is a real bet, not a rounding error on top of base:

- Ask directly whether the grant is options in a Delaware C-corp, and if so, get the strike price and the most recent 409A valuation so the number means something concrete rather than a headline figure.
- Ask about the vesting schedule, standard is four years with a one-year cliff, and whether there's any acceleration on a future acquisition.
- As a Swiss tax resident holding foreign stock options, get the exercise window after departure in writing (some plans force exercise or forfeiture within 90 days) and flag this early since Swiss tax treatment of foreign options differs from RSUs.
- Don't let an equity story substitute for a competitive base. Base is what compounds into pension contributions here, equity is upside on top of that, not instead of it.

## Geographic discount pushback

Some US-anchored startups apply a blanket "Europe discount" to remote hires regardless of role scope. Push back if that comes up:

"I'd expect comp here to reflect the scope of the role and the skillset it needs, not a flat regional discount. Geneva's cost of living is comparable to San Francisco, and the core collaboration window already asks me to shift my afternoon to match Pacific hours, so this isn't a low-cost, low-overlap arrangement, it's a real seat on the team."

## Competing offer template

Only use this if there's an actual second process running. An invented or vague competing offer is easy for an experienced recruiter to spot:

"I'm further along in another process right now at a similar level, so I've got a timeline I'm working against. I'd rather prioritize this one, if we can get the comp conversation wrapped up this week or next."

## Practical notes

- Negotiate total comp as one package, base, equity, sign-on, rather than line by line, it leaves more room to trade pieces against each other.
- Confirm the payment currency and legal employment structure (direct entity, EOR, or contractor) before comparing any number against the Swiss market, since net take-home varies a lot between those.
- Clarify how the 7:00-10:00 AM Pacific core window actually plays out against Geneva hours day to day, and whether that expectation is fixed or flexes with workload.
- Get the level, base, equity grant and start date in writing before signing anything.
