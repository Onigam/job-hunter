# Negotiation Script: Weflow, Senior / Principal Software Engineer (Remote)

Posted location: 100% remote **within Germany**, CET ±2h overlap.
Posted comp: **€90,000 to €110,000 base**, "varies by qualifications and experience".
Company: Weflow, Berlin, founded 2020. Revenue AI Platform on top of Salesforce. Backed by Gradient Ventures (Google) and Cherry Ventures, about $13.5M raised in total per public data, around 26 employees, 200+ customers (Retool, BenchSci, IDnow).

---

## 1. Read the situation first

**The biggest issue is not the number, it's the location clause.** The role is remote within Germany, and Magino lives in Geneva. There are three ways this can work, and each one changes the negotiation:

1. **Contractor (Swiss self-employed or via a Swiss GmbH/Sàrl).** Simplest for Weflow, no payroll abroad. Magino handles AVS/AHV, LPP and insurance themselves, so the rate has to cover them.
2. **Employer of record in Switzerland** (Deel, Remote, Oyster and similar). Weflow pays the EOR, which runs Swiss payroll. Costs Weflow roughly 15 to 25% on top of the gross salary plus a monthly fee, and Swiss employer social charges are lower than German ones, which helps the argument.
3. **German employment while living in Switzerland.** Possible in theory, messy in practice (cross-border tax and social security). Not worth proposing.

Ask about this on the first call, before any number is discussed. If Weflow can't hire outside Germany at all, nothing else in this document matters.

**The band is German, not Swiss.** €110k is at the top of the German market for a senior engineer at a seed or Series A startup, but it converts to roughly CHF 100k to 105k (check the current EUR/CHF rate), which is well below what a Senior Staff engineer earns in Geneva. Expect Weflow to push back on "Swiss rates" for a remote role. The realistic goal is the top of their band or above it, justified by the Principal scope, not full Geneva parity.

**Decide whether it's worth it.** This is a small company (~26 people) with a German band. Compared with Magino's current level, the base will almost certainly be a step down. It only makes sense if the equity is meaningful, the scope is truly principal-level (owning platform and integration architecture), or it's a deliberate move to a smaller, faster team.

**Questions for the first recruiter call:**
- Can you hire someone based in Switzerland, via EOR or as a contractor? Have you done it before?
- Is the €90k to €110k band the same for Senior and Principal, or is there a separate Principal band?
- Is there an equity component (VSOP / virtual shares), and how big is the typical grant at this level?
- What was the last funding round and when, and is another one planned?
- Is there a bonus, and what's the budget for home office, hardware and learning?

- Walk-away: €115k (about CHF 107k), unless equity is unusually strong.
- Target: €120k to €125k plus a real VSOP grant.
- Opening anchor: €130k, framed as the Principal end of the role.

**Targets, if contractor:**

- Walk-away: €750 per day.
- Target: €850 per day, or an annual retainer of about €160k for a full-time commitment.

A €110k German salary costs Weflow about €130k all in once employer contributions are added, so a contractor at €160k a year for ~210 billable days is not far off in real cost, and they skip payroll setup entirely.

## 3. Opening the conversation

If the recruiter asks for expectations early:

> "Before numbers, I'd like to sort out the setup, since I'm in Geneva and the posting mentions Germany. I'm flexible between an employer of record and a contractor arrangement. On compensation, I'm applying at the Principal end of the role: I've built a SaaS platform from zero through acquisition and I'm currently a Senior Staff Engineer. For that scope I'm targeting around €130k base, and I'm open on how it's split with equity."

If they insist on one number:

> "Around €130k base, or the contractor equivalent. I'll adjust once I see the full package, especially the equity."

## 4. If they come in low

**Scenario A: middle of the band (for example €100k).**

> "Thanks for being clear. I get that it's where your band sits for senior engineers. What I'd be bringing is the principal side of the posting: owning how the platform and the Salesforce integration layer evolve, plus the AI workflows on top. I've done that end to end once already, from a blank page to an acquisition. To make it work I'd need to be at €120k or above. Is there a Principal level that goes beyond the published range?"

**Scenario B: top of the band (€110k), with a hard ceiling.**

> "I appreciate you going to the top. If base really can't move, could we look at the other levers? A larger VSOP grant, a sign-on bonus, or a salary review after six months against agreed goals would close most of the gap for me."

**Scenario C: they refuse EOR costs and want a lower salary to compensate.**

> "I understand the EOR fee is a real cost. For what it's worth, Swiss employer social charges come in lower than German ones, so the total cost to you ends up close to hiring in Germany. If it's simpler, I'm happy to invoice as a contractor, which takes the payroll side off your plate completely."

## 5. Equity (VSOP) notes

German startups almost always grant **virtual shares (VSOP)** rather than real options. Know what you're signing:

- **What it is:** a contractual right to a cash payout at an exit (sale or IPO), equal to the share value minus a strike price. No voting rights, no dividends, usually nothing if the company never exits.
- **Ask for:** the number of virtual shares, the total fully diluted share count (so you can work out the percentage), the strike price, and the last round's price per share.
- **Reasonable size:** for a principal engineer among the first ~30 employees after a seed/Series A, something like 0.1% to 0.5% fully diluted. Below 0.1% is a token grant.
- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff is standard. Push for monthly vesting after the cliff.
- **Leaver clauses:** the most important part of a German VSOP. Check what happens to vested shares if you leave (good leaver vs bad leaver). Many plans cut vested shares if you leave before an exit, or let vested shares decay over time. Ask for vested shares to stay fully vested for good leavers.
- **Acceleration:** ask for double-trigger acceleration (exit plus termination), even partial.
- **Tax:** a VSOP payout is taxed as employment income. If contracting from Switzerland, check how Weflow's plan treats non-employees; some VSOPs only cover employees, so equity may need a separate agreement.
- **Anti-dilution:** employees don't normally get it. Instead ask how much further dilution is expected in the next round.

## 6. Pushing back on the geographic discount

Weflow will more likely say "we pay German rates" than argue for a discount below them. Response:

> "Totally fair that the band is set for Germany. I'm not asking you to pay Geneva salaries. I'm asking to be placed at the Principal level the posting describes, which is above the senior range. My cost of living is higher than Berlin's, but the case I'd rather make is on scope: you'd get someone who's already built and scaled this kind of platform."

Keep the Geneva cost-of-living point as backup, not the main argument. For a remote German startup it lands badly as the lead.

## 7. Competing offer template

Use only if true, or keep it vague and truthful ("I'm in other processes").

> "I want to be transparent: I'm at a late stage with another company, a Swiss-based role at CHF [X] base. Weflow is the one I'm most interested in because of the integration and AI work, and I'd like to make it happen. If we can get to [target] with the VSOP grant we discussed, I'm ready to decide this week."

## 8. Before signing

- Contract structure confirmed in writing (EOR entity or contractor agreement), including notice period and IP assignment.
- CET working hours overlap confirmed. Geneva is CET, so no problem.
- Off-site travel: the posting says "all expenses paid"; confirm that covers travel from Switzerland.
- Full VSOP plan document read, not just the grant letter.
- Hardware and home office budget, paid holiday (contractor: price it into the rate).
