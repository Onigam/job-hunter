# Negotiation Script, Staff Software Engineer, Infrastructure @ Ripple (Geneva)

Context: Ripple is a private company, well-funded and profitable by most public reporting, but still private, so equity here is stock options or RSUs in a company without daily liquidity. That changes the shape of the conversation: strike price, 409A-style valuation, vesting cliff, and what a liquidity event actually looks like all matter in a way they wouldn't at a public company. Cash comp still carries most of the weight in any negotiation, equity is the upside case, not the thing to bank on.

## Market anchors (Geneva, Staff+ infrastructure/platform, 2026)

- Base salary for a Staff-level infrastructure or platform engineer in Geneva generally lands CHF 170,000 to CHF 210,000, with fintech and crypto-adjacent companies often at the upper end given the talent premium for people who can operate highly available systems across multiple cloud providers.
- Total cash (base plus bonus) at a well-capitalized private company like Ripple can reach CHF 200,000 to CHF 250,000 for Staff-level infra roles, particularly given the custody and blockchain node requirements, which narrow the qualified pool considerably.
- Geneva is one of the most expensive cities in Europe, on par with or above Zurich, so there's no geographic discount to accept. If an initial number is benchmarked against a cheaper EU hub or even against Zurich, that's worth naming directly rather than letting it pass.
- Multi-cloud, blockchain infra, and custody-adjacent roles at crypto-native companies often carry a specialization premium over generic platform engineering bands. Worth asking directly whether the comp band already reflects that.

## Opening anchor

Aim for the CHF 195,000 to CHF 215,000 base range, and justify it with scope rather than just years of experience: this role spans four cloud providers, blockchain node infrastructure, and custody reliability, which is a broader and higher-stakes mandate than a typical single-cloud platform role. Something like: "Given this role covers infrastructure reliability across four cloud providers plus custody and node operations, I'd expect base in the CHF 195,000 to CHF 215,000 range, with a bonus and equity structure that reflects the operational stakes involved."

## If they come in low

Don't reject a number outright, ask what it's benchmarked against. "Is that figure based on general Staff Engineer infrastructure bands, or does it account for the blockchain and custody-specific requirements in the posting? I'd want to understand how the comp committee scoped this role before responding." That question shifts the burden back to them and often surfaces room that wasn't in the first offer.

If the number still lands low, counter on scope and honesty about the gap: "I don't come from a blockchain-native background, but I bring production Kubernetes, multi-cloud operations, and the kind of architecture judgment that held up through a company-scale acquisition. Given that trade, I'd want to close the gap on base, or make it up through bonus and equity. What flexibility is there?"

## Equity notes (private company specifics)

- Ask for the number of options or RSUs and the current 409A or internal valuation used to price them, not just a raw share count; a share count with no valuation context is not comparable to anything.
- Confirm the vesting schedule, typically four years with a one-year cliff, and ask whether early exercise is allowed if it's an option grant, since that has real tax implications in Switzerland.
- Ask what liquidity looks like in practice: has the company done tender offers before, is there any secondary market activity, or is a liquidity event tied entirely to an eventual IPO or acquisition.
- Ask whether refresh grants exist and whether they're formulaic or discretionary, since most of the long-term equity value at a private company shows up in refresh cycles, not the initial grant.
- If any part of compensation is tied to XRP or another token, get clarity on vesting, custody, and tax treatment in writing before treating it as equivalent to cash or standard equity.

## Geographic pushback

Geneva doesn't need defending as a high-cost hub, the numbers speak for themselves. If a recruiter opens with a figure that looks benchmarked against a lower-cost location, name it plainly: "That number looks like it might be benchmarked against a different market. Geneva cost of living is near the top in Europe, so I'd want the base adjusted to reflect that before we go further."

## Competing offer template (use if real)

"I want to be transparent that I'm in later-stage conversations for a comparable Staff-level infrastructure role elsewhere, with numbers in the CHF X range. Ripple is my preferred option given the scope and the technical challenge here, but I'd need the offer to be competitive with what's already on the table."

Only use this with an actual competing process in hand. A bluffed number is easy to expose if they ask for details, and it costs trust with a company worth actually joining.
