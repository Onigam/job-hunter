# Negotiation Script — Hatchet, Full-Stack Engineer (Founding Team)

---

## Market Context

**Posted range:** $160,000–$199,999 USD annually (from job listing)
**Team size:** 6–10 people, YC-backed, $5.7M seed raised
**Role level:** Founding team engineer, not a rank-and-file hire

**Switzerland reference points (2026):**
- Senior Staff Engineer in Geneva/Zurich: CHF 160,000–210,000 base
- Founding/first engineer at seed-stage startup (US-remote): $180,000–$220,000 USD
- CHF/USD: roughly 0.9 (1 USD ≈ 0.90 CHF as of mid-2026)
- At $200K USD that's approximately CHF 180K before taxes

**Key cost-of-living factor:** Geneva is consistently among the five most expensive cities in the world. Swiss taxes for Geneva canton run ~25-30% effective rate for this income level, with mandatory social contributions (AHV/AVS) adding another 5-6%. A US engineer at $180K in a lower-cost city has meaningfully more purchasing power than the same nominal figure in Geneva.

---

## Opening Anchor

If they make an offer, aim to anchor first if possible.

**Opening script:**
"Based on the role scope, founding team context, and Geneva cost of living, I'm looking at a base in the $195,000–$210,000 range. That's at the top of what you've listed, which I think is consistent with what a founding engineer with a track record like mine brings to a seven-person team."

**Why this works:** It's above their posted ceiling but not absurdly so. It gives them room to land at $185K–$195K and feel like they negotiated, while ensuring you don't open low and lose ground.

---

## If They Come In Low (below $175K)

**Counter script:**
"I appreciate you sharing where you are. My concern is that $[X] against Geneva's cost of living and tax structure puts this below what I'd net on a mid-level role at a larger company here. I'm genuinely interested in Hatchet specifically, which is why I want to make it work. Is there room to move to $190,000 as a base, and then we can structure the equity side to reflect the founding team positioning?"

**Fallback if they hold firm:**
"If the base ceiling is fixed, I'd want to revisit the equity package to make sure the total compensation reflects the founding team risk. Can we talk through the current cap table and what a 0.75%–1% grant looks like in practice?"

---

## Equity Negotiation

**Target grant:** 0.75%–1.25% of fully diluted shares
**Rationale:** At a 6–10 person company with a $5.7M seed, a founding engineer joining now is taking on meaningful risk and will have substantial impact on product trajectory. Early engineering hires at seed stage typically see 0.5%–2%. The lower end (0.5%) is for later seed hires with established options pools; 0.75%–1% is a credible ask for a founding team framing.

**Vesting structure to request:**
- 4-year vest, 1-year cliff (standard YC company structure)
- Double-trigger acceleration: full acceleration if the company is acquired AND your role is materially changed or eliminated. This matters specifically because Hatchet is acquisition-likely as an infrastructure layer.
- Exercise window: ask for 5–10 years post-termination (not just 90 days), which is now standard at employee-friendly companies

**Equity script:**
"I'd like to understand the equity structure better. What's the current fully diluted share count, and what's the option pool sitting at? I'm looking for a grant in the 0.75%–1% range given the founding team framing, with standard 4-year vest and 1-year cliff. I'd also want a double-trigger acceleration clause given the infrastructure-acquisition environment we're in."

**If they push back on the percentage:**
"I understand the pool has constraints. Even if we land at 0.6%, I'd want to make sure the vesting terms and exercise window reflect the risk I'm taking joining at seed. The 90-day exercise window after departure is a standard term I'd want to change."

---

## Geographic Discount Pushback

US companies sometimes try to apply a geographic multiplier against Geneva, arguing that salaries should reflect local market rates rather than US ones.

**Script if they raise this:**
"I understand the instinct, but the Geneva market for senior engineering talent is actually comparable to or above San Francisco rates when you account for the total cost structure. Swiss engineer salaries are benchmarked against a market where CHF 180,000–200,000 is a normal mid-senior band. Taking a geographic haircut from a US base salary would put me below what I could get at a Geneva-based product company, which doesn't reflect the founding team risk I'd be taking on."

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have another offer (or want to create urgency without fabricating one):

**Script:**
"I'm in conversations with two other companies at the moment. I'm most interested in Hatchet specifically, which is why I'm being direct with you about where I need to land rather than playing a longer negotiation game. If we can get to $190,000 base with a 0.75% equity grant, I can give you a clear answer this week."

**Even without a competing offer, timeline pressure works:**
"I have a few things moving in parallel. I want to give you a real answer rather than a prolonged back-and-forth. If we can agree on terms by end of week, I'm in."

---

## Non-Salary Terms Worth Negotiating

- **On-call compensation:** All team members are on-call on 2-week rotation. Confirm whether this is compensated or part of the base. If not compensated, ask for a rotation stipend or equivalent PTO buffer.
- **Equipment budget:** At a 6–10 person company, hardware budgets vary wildly. Ask for a dedicated equipment setup budget (CHF 3,000–5,000) if it's not included.
- **Professional development:** Conference attendance and learning budget. Ask for CHF 3,000/year.
- **Swiss social contributions:** Confirm whether salary is gross or net and how they structure payroll for Geneva-based remote contractors vs. employees. If it's a contractor arrangement (common for EU-remote), factor in the ~15% employer-side AHV contribution you'd be absorbing.
