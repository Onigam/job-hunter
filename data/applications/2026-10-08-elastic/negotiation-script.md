# Negotiation Script: Principal AI Engineer, Context Engine (Elastic)

## Context that shapes the numbers

- **Location is the first question, before any money talk.** The LinkedIn listing shows Greece, with Poland in other listings, and the posted base is **€73,300 to €115,900, with no variable component**. That's a Greek pay band. Elastic is distributed and hires in many countries, but whether it can employ someone in Switzerland for this req (directly or through an employer of record) needs confirming with the recruiter. If the answer is "Greece or Poland only", the rest of this script doesn't matter.
- **Elastic pays by location.** Expect them to say the Swiss band differs from the posted one. That's fine; it works in your favour. What you have to avoid is being hired in Switzerland on the Greek number.
- **Elastic is public (NYSE: ESTC).** Equity means RSUs with a market price, not startup options. No liquidation preferences or anti-dilution to argue about; the levers are grant size, refreshers and the vesting schedule.
- **No bonus.** With variable pay at zero, base and RSUs carry the whole package. Don't trade base for a bonus that doesn't exist.
- **Principal is a senior IC level.** You're negotiating against Staff/Principal bands, not senior engineer bands. 17+ years, a platform built from zero through an acquisition, and Senior Staff at Benevity support that.

## Market anchors (estimates, verify before the call)

Rough ranges from public comp data and recruiter conversations. Re-check on levels.fyi (filter Elastic and Switzerland), Glassdoor, swissdevjobs.ch and the recruiter's own band before quoting any of them.

| Benchmark | Base (approx.) | Total comp (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Elastic, posted band for this req (Greece) | €73k to €116k | same, no bonus |
| Staff/Principal engineer, Geneva, scale-up or mid-size | CHF 170k to 220k | + equity |
| Staff/Principal engineer, Zurich, public tech company | CHF 190k to 240k | CHF 250k to 350k with RSUs |
| Principal AI/ML engineer, Switzerland, big tech | CHF 220k to 270k | CHF 350k+ |

Geneva living costs, health insurance you pay yourself (LAMal) and a pension system tied to salary are why Swiss bands sit well above EU ones. Keep that in mind when they bring up remote parity.

**Target:** CHF 215k base + RSUs worth about USD 80k to 120k a year at grant.
**Opening anchor:** CHF 235k base.
**Walk-away floor:** CHF 185k base with a meaningful RSU grant. Below about CHF 180k base with no bonus, you're under what a Geneva Staff role pays without the equity upside.

If Elastic can only hire you through an EOR or contractor setup, add 10 to 15% to cover the benefits and pension contributions you'd be carrying yourself, and ask who pays the EOR fee.

## Step 1: confirm eligibility and band (recruiter screen)

"Before we go further, I want to check something practical. The listing shows Greece and Poland, and I'm based in Geneva. Can Elastic employ me in Switzerland for this role, and if so, what's the Swiss band for Principal? I'd rather we're working from the right numbers from the start."

If they ask for your expectation first:

"For a Principal IC role in Switzerland, I'm looking at around CHF 235k base, plus RSUs in line with Elastic's Principal grants. That reflects 17 years of engineering, a platform I built from zero through an acquisition, and hands-on work getting AI tooling into how engineers actually ship. If your Swiss band is in that area, I'm very interested."

## Step 2: if they come in low

"Thanks for being straight with me. CHF [their number] reads more like a Staff band in a lower-cost market than a Principal role in Geneva, especially with no variable pay. The scope here is owning the production loop for agents, retrieval and memory across the Context Engine, which is Principal work. Could we get to CHF 215k base? If base is capped, I'd want to make it up in the initial RSU grant."

If base truly can't move:

"Understood. Then let's look at the equity side. With no bonus, a larger initial grant or a sign-on in RSUs closes the gap for me. Could you go to [grant +30 to 40%]?"

## Step 3: pushing back on a geographic discount

If they suggest the posted Greek band, or an "EU remote" number:

"I see why the band was set for Greece. But I'd be employed and taxed in Switzerland, paying Geneva rent and Swiss health insurance, and Elastic already pays by location. The fair comparison is the Swiss band for this level, not the one for Athens."

If they say the req only has budget for one market:

"That makes sense. Is there another Principal or Staff req on the Agent Builder or Search AI side that's open to Switzerland? I'd rather find the right seat than squeeze this one."

## Step 4: equity notes (RSUs at a public company)

Ask these questions, and get the answers in writing.

- **Grant size and how it's set.** Is it a dollar value converted at a trailing average price, or a fixed number of units? A 30-day average protects you from a single bad day.
- **Vesting schedule.** Elastic has typically used a four-year schedule. Confirm whether there's a one-year cliff and whether it then vests quarterly. If there's a cliff, ask for a sign-on grant that vests in year one to soften it.
- **Refreshers.** How are annual refresh grants decided for Principal ICs, and what's a typical size? Without refreshers, total comp drops sharply in year five.
- **ESPP.** Ask whether Elastic's employee stock purchase plan is open to Swiss employees.
- **Swiss tax.** RSUs are taxed as income at vesting in Switzerland, and for cross-border cases social charges apply too. Ask whether Elastic's Swiss payroll handles withholding.

## Step 5: competing offer template (use only if true)

"I want to be transparent with you. I'm in late-stage conversations with [company] for a [Staff/Principal] role in Switzerland, with a package around CHF [X] base plus [equity]. Elastic is my first choice, because the Context Engine is the exact problem I want to spend the next few years on, and it's built in the open. If you can get to CHF [target] base and [RSU number], I'm ready to sign this week."

If you don't have a competing offer, don't invent one. Say this instead:

"I'm talking to a couple of other teams at a similar level, and I'd like to make a decision within the next two weeks. Elastic is where I want to land if the package works."

## Step 6: non-salary items worth asking for

- A home-office and equipment budget, plus a co-working allowance in Geneva if there's no local office.
- A conference or learning budget. The role is public-facing, so speaking at Elastic and AI events is a fair ask.
- Clarity on travel to team offsites, and who pays for it.
- A start date that leaves you at least two weeks between roles.

## Things not to do

- Don't accept the posted €116k ceiling as the frame for a Swiss hire.
- Don't name a number before you know whether Switzerland is even possible.
- Don't give up base for a bonus; there isn't one.
- Don't sign without the RSU grant amount, vesting schedule and refresher policy written into the offer.
