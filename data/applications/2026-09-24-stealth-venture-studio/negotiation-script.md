# Negotiation Script: Founding Full-Stack Engineer, Stealth venture studio (Agentic AI)

## Context
- The employer is a venture studio that builds pre-seed startups in agentic AI. It's still unnamed, so the first job is finding out who's behind it and how it's funded.
- The role is remote across the EU, full-time, listed as Mid-Senior. No salary, equity or stack is given in the posting.
- You're in Geneva. Switzerland isn't in the EU, so the contract will probably go through an Employer of Record (EOR) or be a contractor agreement. Ask about this early, because it changes the numbers: a Swiss EOR costs them more than an EU hire, and as a contractor you pay your own social charges (AVS/AHV, pension, no paid leave).
- The title says IC, but the scope (own the foundational architecture, work directly with founders and customers) is founding-CTO territory. That gap is the main thing you can push on in this negotiation.
- Studio equity is unusual. It might be a grant in each startup you build, a share of a studio-level pool, or both. Don't talk numbers before you understand how it works.

Sources to cite if asked: Levels.fyi (Zurich, Berlin, Amsterdam), Swissdevjobs salary data, Glassdoor Geneva, Carta and Index Ventures (OptionPlan) data on early-employee equity in Europe, Pave for seed-stage bands. These are estimates, so check the latest numbers the day before the call.

## Your target

- **Walk-away:** EUR 120k base (around CHF 112k) through an EOR, with at least 1% in each startup you build or an equivalent studio grant, and paid equipment and travel. If they want a contractor setup, CHF 900/day minimum.
- **Target:** EUR 140k base (around CHF 130k), 1.5% to 2% per spin-out you build, a standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff, and a written path to CTO at the first spin-out that reaches seed.
- **Opening anchor:** CHF 170k base (around EUR 180k), framed on 17 years of experience, a zero-to-acquisition exit as cofounder, and the fact that the scope covers the whole stack plus founder and customer work.
- Cash is likely to be tight. If it is, trade openly: every EUR 10k below target should buy extra equity or a guaranteed step-up when the startup raises its seed round.

## Script: when they ask for your expectations

"Before a number, can you tell me how equity works at the studio? Do I hold equity in the startups I build, in the studio, or both? That changes how I think about cash quite a lot. For context, I've been through zero to acquisition once as a cofounder, and the scope you're describing, owning the architecture and working straight with founders and customers, is closer to a founding CTO job than a senior IC one. On a Swiss setup, I'd be looking at around CHF 170k base, and I'm flexible on how that splits against equity once I understand the structure."

Then stop talking and let them go first.

## Counter-offer script: if they come in low

"Thanks for being straight with me. That number is below what a senior engineer earns in Geneva, and this is a founding role with production ownership across several products. I can move on cash if the equity reflects that. At [their number], I'd need [X]% in each startup I build, on a standard four-year vest, and a written step-up to [target] once that startup closes a seed round. Can we make that work?"

## Equity: what to ask for

- **Which entity.** Get a clear answer on whether the equity is in the studio, in each spin-out, or both. Per-spin-out equity is usually worth more to you, because you're building those companies.
- **Vesting.** Four years with a one-year cliff is normal. Ask for the cliff to count from your start date at the studio, not from the day a spin-out is incorporated, otherwise you could work six months on a startup before its equity clock even starts.
- **Moving between startups.** If you're moved off a startup before your cliff, ask for a pro-rata vest on what you built, or accelerated vesting on that grant.
- **Acceleration.** Ask for double-trigger acceleration (acquisition plus termination), at least on the grant of the startup you're working on.
- **Exercise window.** Push for 5 to 10 years after leaving rather than the default 90 days. Swiss tax on options can be triggered at grant or exercise depending on the plan, so ask for the plan documents and get a Swiss tax adviser to look at them before you sign.
- **Dilution.** At pre-seed you can't stop dilution, but you can ask for the current cap table, the studio's own stake (studios often keep 30% to 80%), and how much of each spin-out is kept for the founding team. If the studio holds most of the company, your percentage needs to be higher to make up for it.

## Pushing back on a geographic discount

Remote EU roles are often benchmarked on Lisbon or Warsaw pay. If that comes up:

"I get why you'd benchmark remote pay against the EU average. My costs are Geneva costs, though, and on a Swiss EOR the employer charges are what they are. More to the point, you're paying for the outcome, not for where I sit: you'd get someone who's already taken a platform from a blank page to an acquisition. I'd rather be benchmarked against the founding engineers you'd hire in Zurich or Munich than against a remote average."

If they won't move on base, ask for a higher equity grant, a signing bonus, or a paid co-working and travel budget for time with founders and customers.

## Competing offer template (use only if true)

"I want to be open with you. I'm in late stages with [company] for a [Staff / Founding / CTO] role at around CHF [X] base plus [Y]% equity. I'd rather be here, because building several agentic products from scratch is closer to what I want to be doing. If you can get to [target] with [equity terms], I can commit this week."

If you don't have a live offer, don't make one up. Say that you're talking with other teams and that you'll decide within the next two to three weeks.

## Checklist before signing
- Contract type (EOR employee vs contractor), employer entity, governing law.
- Equity plan document, which entity it's in, vesting schedule, cliff start date, exercise window.
- What happens to your equity if a spin-out is shut down or merged back into the studio.
- IP clause: make sure it doesn't cover your personal projects (e.g. Givernance) or anything built outside work hours on your own equipment.
- Written path to CTO at a spin-out, even if it's only a review point at 12 months.
- Notice period and non-compete scope (a studio non-compete can be very broad, so ask for it to be narrowed to direct competitors of the startups you worked on).
