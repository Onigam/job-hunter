# Negotiation Script: Synthesia, (Senior or Staff) Backend Engineer, AI tooling

Location: Zürich (Developer Platform team), candidate based in Geneva.
Posted comp: "EUR/GBP/CHF 130,000 base + RSUs", 25 days vacation plus local holidays.

---

## 1. Reading the posted number

The posting quotes the same 130,000 figure in EUR, GBP and CHF. That's a problem for Zürich. GBP 130k is a strong London number for this level; CHF 130k in Zürich sits at or below the market for a Senior backend engineer and well under Staff. It reads like a band floor or a single Senior anchor applied across offices rather than a Zürich-calibrated figure.

The title also says "Senior or Staff". Level is the biggest lever in this whole process, bigger than any base haggling. Everything below assumes pushing for Staff.

## 2. Zürich market anchors (base, CHF, 2026)

These are working estimates from Levels.fyi, Glassdoor and recruiter conversations; re-check them before the call.

| Level | Typical Zürich base | Strong scale-up / big tech |
|---|---|---|
| Senior backend | CHF 140k to 165k | CHF 170k to 190k |
| Staff backend / platform | CHF 170k to 200k | CHF 200k to 240k |

Well-funded AI scale-ups in Zürich tend to compete with Google, which sets the ceiling. Synthesia at a ~$4B valuation with $530M+ raised can pay at the upper-middle of this range.

**Target package (Staff):**

- Walk-away base: CHF 165k
- Target base: CHF 180k to 190k
- Opening anchor: CHF 195k base, plus a meaningful RSU grant

## 3. Opening the conversation

If asked for expectations on the recruiter call:

> "I'd like to understand the level first, since the posting covers Senior and Staff. Based on my background, 17 years in, a cofounder who built a platform through acquisition, and a Senior Staff title at Benevity, I'm looking at Staff. For a Staff role based in Zürich I'm targeting a base around CHF 195k, plus equity. I'm flexible on how that splits between base and RSUs once I understand the grant."

If they push for a single number before levelling:

> "For Staff in Zürich, around CHF 195k base. If you're thinking Senior, I'd want to talk through why before we get to numbers."

## 4. If they come in low (for example CHF 130k to 145k)

> "Thanks, I appreciate the offer. I'll be straight: CHF 130k is a solid number in London, but in Zürich it's below where Senior backend roles land, and this is a Staff-scope role by your own description, owning multi-quarter initiatives and setting technical direction a year out. To make this work I'd need the base at CHF 180k or above. Is there room to get there, or to revisit the level?"

If base is truly capped:

> "If the base band is fixed, can we look at the RSU grant, a sign-on bonus, and a written review of level and comp at six months?"

## 5. Equity (private-company RSUs)

Synthesia is private, so the RSUs aren't cash until there's a liquidity event or a secondary. Questions to ask before valuing the grant:

- **Grant size and basis.** Number of units and the price per share used to convert the grant value (latest preferred round price or a lower common valuation?).
- **Vesting.** Expect 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Ask whether vesting is monthly or quarterly after the cliff.
- **Trigger.** Private RSUs are often double-trigger (time vesting plus a liquidity event). Ask what happens to vested units if you leave before an IPO, and whether there's any expiry.
- **Liquidity.** Ask whether employees have had access to secondary sales and whether that's expected to repeat.
- **Refreshers.** Ask about annual refresh grants and the performance criteria.
- **Dilution.** Anti-dilution protection won't be on offer for employee RSUs. Ask instead about fully diluted share count, so the percentage is clear.
- **Swiss tax.** RSUs are generally taxed as income at vesting/delivery. Ask whether Synthesia handles Swiss withholding and whether a Swiss tax ruling exists for the plan.

Discount the RSU value heavily when comparing to cash offers; treat it as upside, not salary.

## 6. Pushing back on geography

If they argue that the London band applies everywhere:

> "I understand keeping bands simple, but Zürich isn't London in cost or market. Pension contributions, health insurance and rent all sit on top of base here, and Zürich engineers get benchmarked against Google and other big tech locally. A single number across currencies makes CHF the weakest of the three."

If they suggest remote from Geneva should be discounted:

> "I'm happy to be in Zürich as often as the team needs, and Geneva costs are on par with Zürich. I'd rather not have a location discount on a role I can support in person."

Also confirm the Swiss specifics: pension (BVG/LPP) employer contribution above the legal minimum, whether the base is 12 or 13 salaries, and whether travel from Geneva to Zürich is covered if regular office days are expected.

## 7. Competing offer template

Use only if there's a real process running; don't invent one.

> "I want to be transparent. I'm in late stages with another company for a Staff platform role, and their offer is in the CHF [X] range for base, plus equity. Synthesia is my first choice because the Developer Platform scope and the AI tooling focus are exactly where I want to spend the next few years. If we can close the gap on base, I'm ready to sign this week."

If no competing offer exists, frame it around the market instead:

> "I'm talking to a few companies at Staff level in Zürich and the base range I'm seeing is CHF 180k to 200k. I'd like Synthesia to be in that range."

## 8. Things to trade if base stalls

- Staff level confirmed in writing, with a clear expectations doc
- Sign-on bonus (CHF 15k to 30k) to bridge the first year
- Larger initial RSU grant
- Guaranteed comp and level review at six months
- Learning or conference budget, home office setup, travel cover for Geneva to Zürich

## 9. Close

> "Thanks for working through this with me. If we land on CHF [agreed] base at Staff, with the RSU grant as discussed, I'm ready to accept. Can you send the updated offer in writing?"
